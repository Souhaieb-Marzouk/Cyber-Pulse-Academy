# Contributor Covenant Code of Conduct

## Our Pledge

We as members, contributors, and leaders pledge to make participation in our community a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, level of experience, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, colour, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Our Standards

Examples of behaviour that contributes to a positive environment for our community include:

* Demonstrating empathy and kindness toward other people
* Being respectful of differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences
* Giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback
* Accepting responsibility and apologising to those affected by our mistakes, and learning from the experience
* Focusing on what is best not just for us as individuals, but for the overall community

Examples of unacceptable behaviour include:

* The use of sexualised language or imagery, and sexual attention or advances of any kind
* Trolling, insulting or derogatory comments, and personal or political attacks
* Public or private harassment
* Publishing others' private information, such as a physical or email address, without their explicit permission
* Other conduct which could reasonably be considered inappropriate in a professional setting

## A Project-Specific Note: This Is a Security-Education Community

This project exists to help people learn security, including people at the very beginning. Because of that, some behaviours that might pass unnoticed elsewhere are treated as unacceptable here, and they are covered by the standards above rather than being exempt from them:

* **Hostile gatekeeping.** Telling someone their question is too basic, that they should already know something, or that they do not belong here. Every person who asks a naive question in good faith is the audience this platform was built for. Driving them away is not a personality quirk; it damages the project's stated purpose.
* **"You should already know this" sneering**, in any of its forms: dismissing a contribution because the contributor is junior, answering a question with a link and no explanation, or treating confusion as evidence of incompetence. Pointing someone at a source is fine. Making them feel stupid is not.
* **Credentialism.** Ranking a contribution by the certificates someone holds, the employer on their profile, their years of experience, or whether they "actually work in security". A well-argued correction from a student and a well-argued correction from a principal engineer are worth exactly the same amount, and the review process here judges the content, not the CV.
* **Weaponising the subject matter.** Using security knowledge to intimidate, to imply that you could do something harmful, or to shut down a discussion by appeal to expertise.

The maintainers treat these as enforcement matters, not as personality clashes. If you are not sure whether something you are about to write would discourage a beginner, that is usually a good reason to rewrite it more kindly.

## Enforcement Responsibilities

Community leaders are responsible for clarifying and enforcing our standards of acceptable behaviour and will take appropriate and fair corrective action in response to any behaviour that they deem inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Community leaders have the right and responsibility to remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, wiki edits, issues, and other contributions that are not aligned to this Code of Conduct, and will communicate reasons for moderation decisions when appropriate.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies within all community spaces, and also applies when an individual is officially representing the community in public spaces. Examples of representing our community include using an official email address, posting via an official social media account, or acting as an appointed representative at an online or offline event.

For this project, "community spaces" means the repository itself: issues, pull requests, commit messages, code review comments, discussions, and any other channel operated in the project's name. It applies equally to a contributor's very first comment and to a maintainer's reply.

## Enforcement

Instances of abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behaviour may be reported to the community leaders responsible for enforcement. All complaints will be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly.

All community leaders are obligated to respect the privacy and security of the reporter of any incident.

### Enforcement Guidelines

Community leaders will follow these Community Impact Guidelines in determining the consequences for any action they deem in violation of this Code of Conduct:

#### 1. Correction

**Community Impact:** Use of inappropriate language or other behaviour deemed unprofessional or unwelcome in the community.

**Consequence:** A private, written warning from community leaders, providing clarity around the nature of the violation and an explanation of why the behaviour was inappropriate. A public apology may be requested.

**What this looks like in practice:** You receive a direct message explaining what was said and why it landed badly, with a request not to repeat it. No public callout, no locked thread, and no record beyond the maintainers' own notes. Most reports end here, and that is the intended outcome.

#### 2. Warning

**Community Impact:** A violation through a single incident or series of actions.

**Consequence:** A warning with consequences for continued behaviour. No interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, for a specified period of time. This includes avoiding interactions in community spaces as well as external channels like social media. Violating these terms may lead to a temporary or permanent ban.

**What this looks like in practice:** A written warning that states plainly what must stop and for how long, plus a defined period during which you do not participate in the project's spaces and do not contact the people who reported you. The maintainers will not mediate a private conversation between the parties.

#### 3. Temporary Ban

**Community Impact:** A serious violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour.

**Consequence:** A temporary ban from any sort of interaction or public communication with the community for a specified period of time. No public or private interaction with the people involved, including unsolicited interaction with those enforcing the Code of Conduct, is allowed during this period. Violating these terms may lead to a permanent ban.

**What this looks like in practice:** Your access to the repository's interactive spaces is withdrawn for a stated period. Pull requests you have already opened are not deleted, but they will not be reviewed while the ban is in force, and a maintainer will tell you when it ends.

#### 4. Permanent Ban

**Community Impact:** Demonstrating a pattern of violation of community standards, including sustained inappropriate behaviour, harassment of an individual, or aggression toward or disparagement of classes of individuals.

**Consequence:** A permanent ban from any sort of public interaction within the community.

**What this looks like in practice:** You are permanently excluded from the project's spaces. This is reserved for patterns and for harassment, and it is a decision the maintainers will only take together.

## Contact

Reports go to the repository maintainers through a private channel.

**If a private contact route is published** — in this file, in `SECURITY.md`, or in the repository's profile — use it directly.

**If no email address is published**, open a GitHub issue that asks for a private contact route **without describing the incident and without naming anyone involved**. A maintainer will then open a private channel with you, and the report happens there. Please do not put the details of a conduct incident into a public issue: the person reporting, the person reported, and any bystanders all deserve better than that, and a public thread makes a fair process harder.

All reports are handled confidentially. The maintainers will tell you what they intend to do, and they will not disclose your identity to the person reported without your agreement.
