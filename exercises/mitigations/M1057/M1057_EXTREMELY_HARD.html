1. What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD exam for M1057 — Data Loss Prevention (DLP), a Mitigation in ATT&CK. Here you make the calls a senior analyst, incident commander, or control owner makes — with incomplete information, conflicting signals, and real costs on every side.

The scenarios chain three or more ideas at once. A ransomware-adjacent incident where the safe answer is to pause cloud sync of a sensitive library instead of pulling the network cable. An insider who moves 50 MB a day to a personal cloud tenant and is also your best-performing executive. A litigation hold that makes your DLP platform's tidy auto-purge habit a legal problem. An approved user, an approved application, and a 40-times volume anomaly that cannot all be true at once — and the CISO needs a decision in ten minutes.

You will practise the decisions that are named in senior job descriptions: choose gather more evidence or escalate when neither action is technical; accept and state a business cost when containment is right; override a popular best practice when it is wrong in this specific context; and preserve evidence before any purge, wipe, or policy cleanup.

All scenarios stay anchored to M1057 and its technique list — T1567.004 webhooks, T1048.002 encrypted uploads, T1052.001 USB, T1537 cloud account transfers, T1020.001 traffic duplication and the rest — and to the five implementation measures. You will also rank a remediation backlog, review a policy matrix for blind spots, and brief stakeholders in plain words.

2. Keywords covered in this exam

Senior decisions

  • Conflicting signals: which one wins, and why
  • "Do nothing yet / gather evidence" and "escalate" as correct answers
  • Accepting a real cost: downtime, deal delay, executive friction

Legal and governance

  • Litigation hold vs DLP auto-purge
  • Evidence preservation before purge or wipe
  • Notifying data owners, DPO, legal, and deal teams

Chain analysis

  • Categorization → telemetry → block → scope → notify
  • CASB + DLP + egress control as one system
  • Sub-threshold exfiltration below alert limits

Coverage and attacker adaptation

  • Risk-ranking a DLP backlog
  • Policy matrix review: TLS blind spots, monitor-only rules
  • Attacker adaptations: TLS + approved SaaS, chunked webhooks
