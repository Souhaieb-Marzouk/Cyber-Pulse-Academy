1. What you will learn here

This is the HARD exam for M1057 — Data Loss Prevention (DLP), a Mitigation in ATT&CK. By now you know what DLP is and how its measures map to techniques. This exam pushes you into the uncomfortable middle ground where experienced analysts live: two answers that both look right, alerts that look alarming but are harmless, traffic that looks harmless but is a slow leak, and actions that must be the least aggressive one that still works.

You will play the roles where this mitigation is used under pressure: the SOC analyst who must decide whether a bulk HR upload is a leak or a payroll export, the incident responder who must stop an active exfiltration without destroying evidence, the detection engineer who tunes a noisy rule without losing coverage, and the GRC analyst who maps DLP activities to NIST CSF functions.

The scenarios are built on the M1057 technique list: browser uploads to web services (T1567, T1567.004), unencrypted protocol transfers (T1048.003, T1041), encrypted uploads DLP cannot read (T1048.002), USB and physical media (T1052.001, T1052), mirrored email traffic (T1020.001), cloud account transfers (T1537), and endpoint data access (T1005, T1025). You will also see where DLP is blind — encrypted channels, agentless hosts — and which compensating controls cover the gap.

Every wrong option in this file is defensible somewhere else. Your job is to find the one that is right here, and to say why in your head before you press Check. That judgement — not recall — is what separates a senior analyst from a rule-reader.

2. Keywords covered in this exam

Near-miss discrimination

  • T1567 web service upload vs T1048.003 unencrypted protocol vs T1041 C2 channel
  • Encrypted payload = no content visibility; metadata and destination still visible
  • Parent vs sub-technique rows in the M1057 table

False positives and tuning

  • Test card numbers and payroll exports that mimic PII
  • Rule scoping by group and by repository
  • Monitor mode vs enforce mode discipline

Trade-offs and gaps

  • Least-aggressive action that still contains the risk
  • Preserve evidence before you purge or wipe
  • Coverage gaps: agentless hosts, TLS tunnels, unmanaged devices

Mapping and dual perspective

  • DLP activities to NIST CSF: Identify, Protect, Detect, Respond
  • Red side: how an attacker moves data past network DLP
  • Blue side: CASB, egress allow-lists, endpoint DLP as compensation
