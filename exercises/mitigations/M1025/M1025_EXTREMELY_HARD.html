● What you will learn here

This exam runs MITRE ATT&CK mitigation M1025 — Privileged Process Integrity at senior level: chains of decisions where signals conflict, evidence is incomplete, and every choice has a price. M1025 is a mitigation — the control that keeps privileged processes (LSASS, antivirus, system services) untouchable. You know its measures already: PPL via RunAsPPL, memory protections (CFG, DEP, ASLR), code-signing validation (WDAC, AppLocker), access controls (DACLs, MIC, debug restrictions), and kernel protections (PatchGuard, SELinux, AppArmor).

Who uses this at work? You will act as the senior IR lead deciding containment while the CISO waits, the programme owner pricing the backup-agent exception against the credential-theft risk, the senior SOC analyst ruling which signal wins when the EDR and the kernel disagree, and the governance owner who keeps the exception register honest.

What will you practise here? Multi-step branches where your earlier choice shapes the next question, one configuration command under pressure (Set-ProcessMitigation scoping), timelines with realistic noise, coverage decisions per host class, and answers that are — sometimes · to gather more evidence instead of acting. Partial credit rewards the steps you reasoned correctly, even when the chain breaks later.

● Keywords covered in this exam

Decisions under pressure

  • Conflicting signals: EDR alert vs kernel refusal — which wins
  • Timestamp reconciliation; provenance of dump artefacts
  • Cost-bearing choices; gather-more-evidence as the correct answer

Techniques addressed

  • T1003.001; T1547.002 / .005 / .008; T1556.001
  • Blocked SSP loading vs denied memory access — two gate types

Programme design

  • Inventory — pilot — review — enforce · quarterly review
  • Evidence for auditors; exception register with owners and expiries

Memory protection configuration

  • Set-ProcessMitigation scoping: -System vs -Name (per-binary)
  • Per-app CFG exceptions with documented cost and expiry

Residual risk and governance

  • Vulnerable-driver blocklist as compensating control
  • Risk register, risk acceptance, review cadence

Compensating measures

  • AppLocker DLL rules and least privilege on hosts where PPL cannot run
  • WDAC code-signing validation; keeping non-DC hosts in scope

● How this exam works

  • Pass mark: 70%. Your score is the sum of exercise credits over the exercises you actually answered. PASS means you reached the mark; FAIL offers a Retake button that reshuffles everything.
  • Exercise types in this file: two multi-step branching incidents, a four-signal conflicting triage, one fake-shell configuration task under pressure (Set-ProcessMitigation scoping), a filterable night timeline, honest-limitation multi-selects, and governance fills.
  • Timer and the two pauses. A 60-minute countdown runs while you work. The manual Pause button stops the timer and hides the exercise for a real break. When you press Check, the timer stops automatically so you can study the feedback without pressure — it resumes only when you press Next (or Final Submit on the last exercise). The header always shows which pause is active.
  • Partial credit. Multi-answer exercises score per item: correct picks add, wrong picks subtract. The credit is (right — wrong) ÷ items, never below zero. Single-answer exercises are simply right or wrong.
  • Hints. One hint per exercise, and it costs 10% of that exercise's credit. The hint nudges; it never gives the answer away.
  • Skipping. Skip is free. Before you finish, a review screen lists every skipped exercise so you can go back and answer it. Skipped-and-never-answered exercises are excluded from both score and denominator — they count neither for nor against you. Skip and Hint never stop the timer; only the two pauses do.
