What you will learn here

This is the senior tier for M1030 — Network Segmentation, a Mitigation in MITRE ATT&CK. Nothing here is a definition question. Every exercise chains three or more ideas, hands you at least one contradictory signal, and asks for a decision that a senior analyst, architect or incident commander would have to defend in writing afterwards. Several correct answers cost something — user downtime, an SLA breach, four minutes of attacker dwell — and the exam wants you to accept the cost explicitly rather than pretend it is not there. At least one best practice is wrong in its context, and at least one right answer is “escalate” or “gather evidence first” rather than a technical action.

The material spans the full modern reach of the mitigation. Identity-side: T1557.001 name-resolution poisoning and SMB relay riding a flat broadcast domain, T1040 sniffing, T1482 domain trust discovery. Infrastructure-side: open VPC peerings, security groups as the cloud zone wall, T1552.007 (container API credentials behind unsegmented management paths), T1610/T1612/T1613 (container deployment and discovery), T1602 device management exposure. Resilience-side: T1489 (Service Stop) and T1565.003 (runtime data manipulation), blunted when security tooling runs on its own management network. New-surface rows the framework added: T1698 (Indirect Prompt Injection — restrict what an AI agent can reach), and T1669 (Wi-Fi Networks — keep wireless separate from wired corporate networks).

You will run programmes, not just controls: break-glass access, drift monitoring, rule recertification, tiered administration, purple-team validation, and the coverage review where a green SIEM dashboard and honest flow analytics disagree. This is M1030 as it is actually lived at scale.
