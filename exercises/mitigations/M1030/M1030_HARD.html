What you will learn here

This is the discriminator level for M1030 — Network Segmentation, a Mitigation in MITRE ATT&CK. Here the answers stop being obvious: every option will look defensible, and your job is to pick the one that is correct in this network, in this incident, for this risk. You will correlate artefacts the way a senior analyst does — flow logs against zone designs, change tickets against live intrusions, CISO demands against missing inventories — and you will meet the three classic traps: the alert that looks terrible but is the control working, the traffic that looks routine but is staging, and the correct action that is the least aggressive one on the list.

The technique set widens to the professional view of the mitigation. On the movement side: T1021.001 (RDP), T1021.006 (WinRM), T1563.002 (RDP Hijacking), T1072 (Software Deployment Tools). On the trust side: T1557.001 (name-resolution poisoning and SMB relay), T1482 (Domain Trust Discovery), T1199 (Trusted Relationship — partner and vendor paths). On the egress side: T1048.003 (exfiltration over unencrypted non-C2), T1095 (non-application layer protocols), T1571 (non-standard ports). Plus the operational rows: T1489 (Service Stop — why security tooling lives on its own management network), T1602 (device configuration exposure), and T1040 (sniffing on flat segments).

You will also do the work of the roles that own this control: network architects staging an OT segmentation programme without stopping production, control owners running rule recertification, GRC analysts mapping to ISO 27001:2022 A.8.22, NIST SP 800-53 SC-7 and NIST CSF, and incident commanders deciding what goes to IR. Every explanation ties the decision back to a task you will actually perform.
