What you will learn here

This exam takes you one level deeper into M1030 — Network Segmentation, a Mitigation in MITRE ATT&CK. Last time you met the walls; now you learn how the walls are designed, defended against erosion, and mapped to the rest of your security programme. You will connect real attacker behaviours to the exact zone rule that removes them: T1021.001 (RDP) and T1021.003 (DCOM) as remote-service paths, T1021.006 (WinRM) as the admin path, T1563.002 (RDP Hijacking) as a session-level move, and T1210 when a vulnerable service is attacked across zones. You will also meet the identity-side rows: T1098.002 account changes against domain controllers and T1482 Domain Trust Discovery, both blunted when the DCs accept connections only from an admin segment.

You will practise the work itself, as two of your future tasks do it. Detection engineers and SOC analysts read flow logs and name the technique behind the traffic, then propose the control. Network architects and control owners review firewall changes against the zone design and catch the “temporary” rule that never leaves. GRC analysts map M1030 to CIS v8 12.2 and NIST CSF PR.AC-5. Access reviewers shrink vendor VPN groups from “the whole internal range” to a real vendor zone. The building blocks stay concrete: VLANs, security groups in the cloud (VPC segmentation), jump hosts, management VLANs for SNMP (T1602), and the four-step rollout: inventory flows, define zones, allow business flows, deny-by-default with logging.

Expect two connected ideas in every exercise: identify the behaviour, then choose the measure. That pairing — technique to rule — is the core professional skill this mitigation teaches.
