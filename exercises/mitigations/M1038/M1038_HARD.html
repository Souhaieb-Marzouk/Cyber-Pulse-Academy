◈ What you will learn here

HARD level. This exam pushes your M1038 — Execution Prevention mastery into realistic territory: correlated artefacts, near-miss answers, and decisions where the obvious option is wrong. You will read tickets, AppLocker events, EDR telemetry, and policy drafts together — the way a senior analyst actually works.

The scenario company is Meridian Logistics, which runs an AppLocker pilot (enforced on servers, audit mode on workstations) and is responding to incident IR-1187, where mshta.exe and diskshadow.exe were abused across sites. Every exercise stands on its own, but together they follow the real lifecycle: read the evidence, tune the rules, contain without destroying, govern the exceptions, and only then enforce.

You will practice the judgement the M1038 page assumes: which event is a tuning task and which is an intrusion signal; why an ANY-publisher rule is worthless; why a writable-path allow is the classic flaw; why DLL rules must actually be enabled to stop T1574.001-style hijacks; and why "block mshta.exe and diskshadow.exe where not required" is the page-exact answer to the incident chain. You will also handle governance: scoped, documented, time-boxed exceptions instead of blanket approvals — and you will type one real configuration command in a safe simulated shell, as a read-only evidence task.

Who uses this level of thinking? Senior SOC analysts triaging block storms, detection engineers running pre-enforcement dry runs, incident responders turning command lines into block rules, and GRC owners defending their mappings in audits. Expect to be wrong for good reasons — every explanation tells you exactly why the near-miss option fails.

◇ Keywords covered in this exam

Every theme below is exercised by the questions in this file. The other files of this topic cover further themes — together they cover the full M1038 object.

Evidence correlation & event triage

  • 8004 WOULD BLOCK vs 8003 BLOCKED vs 8002 ALLOWED — mode-aware reading
  • Signer + path + schedule + context = response decision
  • Benign-looking storms (scheduled vendor tools) vs alarming-looking blocks
  • Dual telemetry (EDR + AppLocker) and evidence preservation during containment

Techniques in scope (from the M1038 page)

  • T1218.005 Mshta & T1047 WMI (wmic.exe) & T1490 (diskshadow.exe)
  • T1059.010 AutoHotKey & AutoIT (AutoIt3.exe)
  • T1574.001 DLL hijacking — requires DLL rules actually enabled
  • T1546.009 AppCert DLLs & T1505.004 IIS Components — in scope
  • T1546.003 WMI Event Subscription & T1003.003 NTDS — NOT in scope

Rule design & policy quality

  • Publisher rules pinned to real signers — ANY-publisher is worthless
  • Writable-path allows: the classic flaw (Downloads, AppData)
  • Script vs Msi collections; audit flag left ON means no enforcement
  • Pre-enforcement dry runs over mixed telemetry

Operations & governance

  • Rollout order: classify, tune, pilot, monitor, extend DLL last
  • Scoped, time-boxed, documented exceptions (kiosk HTA case)
  • Terminal task: read-only export of the effective policy (Get-AppLockerPolicy)
  • Framework mappings that survive audits: CIS 2.x allowlisting, NIST CSF PR.PS

◉ How this exam works

  • Goal: HARD-level mastery of M1038 — 2 to 3 interacting concepts per exercise, near-miss distractors, correlated artefacts. There are 16 exercises in this file.
  • Pass mark: 70%. ✓ PASS means at least 70% over answered exercises; ✗ FAIL means study the explanations and retake.
  • Exercise types in this file: multi-select correlation (partial credit), multiple choice, event-board triage, coverage mapping, step ordering, a three-step branching decision, and one terminal exercise — a simulated PowerShell session where you type a read-only export command.
  • Timer: the exam has a 48:00 countdown. ⏸ Manual Pause hides the exercise and stops the timer. ⏸ Check auto-pause stops the clock the instant you press Check — take the time you need on the feedback; it resumes on Next.

  • Skipping: skipped exercises wait in the review pool; you can return until the timer ends. Never-answered exercises are excluded from the score.
  • Trap expectations: at HARD level, expect at least one "looks alarming but is benign", one "looks benign but is alarming", and one case where the correct action is the least aggressive option.
