◈ What you will learn here

Expert level. This exam places you in the senior seat of Aurelia Bank’s security programme during and after incident IR-2291, where an adversary executed through mshta.exe, moved laterally with wmic.exe, and probed your recovery tooling. You will make the calls a senior engineer makes — under deadlines, with contradictory telemetry, incomplete information, and real business cost attached to every option.

The exercises chain three or more concepts at once: a go/no-go enforcement decision built from two conflicting pilot reports; coverage calls that separate M1038’s scope (code running and libraries loading) from phishing, credentials, and identity abuse; containment ordering where isolation costs revenue and memory capture cannot wait; and design reviews where the industry’s default recommendation is the wrong answer for a fixed teller image. One exercise has you type a real, state-changing command in a simulated shell — choosing the approved file among several dangerous near-misses.

You will also practice honest communication: the CTI briefing line that survives challenge, the residual-risk statement a CISO can sign, the one-sentence framework mapping an examiner cannot unwind (AppLocker/WDAC → CIS 2.x allowlisting → NIST CSF PR.PS → ISO 27001 A.8.19), and the post-incident report whose five keywords drive next quarter’s budget.

Expect the traps. At least one correct answer is "gather more evidence / escalate", at least one correct action carries real cost you must accept and state, and at least one widely-quoted best practice fails in this specific context. The M1038 page remains your anchor: block what is not required, allowlist what is, compensate with detection where necessity remains, and govern every exception.

Take the time the timer gives you — and when two signals contradict each other, name the log that settles the argument before you decide.

◉ How this exam works

  • Goal: expert-level mastery of M1038 — three or more chained concepts per exercise, conflicting signals, noise, incomplete information, and cost-bearing decisions. There are 16 exercises in this file.
  • Pass mark: 70%. ✓ PASS means at least 70% over answered exercises; ✗ FAIL means re-read the explanations — they contain the full decision logic.
  • Exercise types in this file: branching decision chains (multi-step, graded at the final Check), select-all sets with partial credit, four-artefact senior reads, containment ordering, risk ranking, coverage mapping, one fill-in-the-blank report, and one terminal exercise — a state-changing policy apply with destructive near-misses.
  • Timer: the exam has a 64:00 countdown. ⏸ Manual Pause hides the exercise and stops the clock. ⏸ Check auto-pause stops the clock the moment you press Check — study the reasoning as long as you need; it resumes on Next.
  • Partial credit: multi-item exercises score max(0, (H − W) / N); wrong picks cancel correct ones. Branch exercises grade every step at the final Check.
  • Hints: one per exercise, −10% of that exercise’s credit. Expert hints restate a constraint you may have missed — they never point at the answer.
  • Terminal exercise: the command is graded element by element. Near-miss commands that would clear or regress the policy are flagged as dangerous and penalised. The canonical command appears after checking.
  • Skipping: skipped exercises wait in the review pool until the timer ends; never-answered exercises are excluded from the score.
  • Expert expectations: at least one "correct answer is do-nothing-yet / escalate", at least one correct action with real accepted cost, and at least one industry best practice that is wrong in context.
