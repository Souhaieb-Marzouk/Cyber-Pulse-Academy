📚 What you will learn here

This MEDIUM exam takes you one step deeper into M1042 — Disable or Remove Feature or Program — a Mitigation object in the MITRE ATT&CK framework. In the EASY level you learned to recognise what M1042 is. Here you must combine two facts at once: for example, read a log, work out which ATT&CK technique is being used, and then pick the feature that M1042 would disable to blunt it. Every exercise asks you to make that little chain yourself.

Remember the map. ATT&CK techniques describe attacker behaviour; M1042 is the defensive object that says: remove or disable the unnecessary program, feature or service that behaviour depends on. The M1042 page links it to many techniques — remote services like RDP, SSH, VNC and WinRM; proxy binaries like mshta; name-resolution abuse like LLMNR; developer tools like MSBuild; Office components like VBA macros; and identity features like Entra ID app passwords. Each link comes with a short 'use' note explaining what to disable and what to watch out for.

Where you will use this at work: detection engineers turn incident lessons into hardening tickets; SOC analysts map alerts to techniques and propose the disabling action; system engineers apply the change with Group Policy, feature settings or uninstallers; GRC analysts check the change against CIS and ISO baselines and record exceptions. This exam also asks about those framework mappings (CIS v8 safeguard 4.8, the NIST CSF least-functionality idea, ISO 27001 A.8.19) and about trade-offs — why you cannot simply remove everything, and why a review always comes first for tools like PowerShell or MMC.

Take your time with each artefact: the path is not spelled out for you, but the two steps are always close together.

  • ClickOnce TrustManager setting

  • Impact review before removal (PowerShell, MMC)

Framework mappings

  • CIS v8 Safeguard 4.8 (uninstall or disable unnecessary services)
  • NIST CSF least functionality
  • ISO 27001 A.8.19 (installation of software on operational systems)

Trade-offs, coverage and gaps

  • Scope removals to hosts that do not need a tool
  • Coverage-gap thinking (covered vs still exposed)
  • Preventive vs detective vs corrective controls
  • Exception register
