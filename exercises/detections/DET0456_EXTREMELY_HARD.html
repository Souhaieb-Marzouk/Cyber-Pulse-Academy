What you will learn here

This is the expert tier for DET0456, a Detection Strategy object. Everything before this level taught you the chain: a suspicious API call, a privileged token access, a child process whose security context (SID, LogonId, integrity level) differs from its parent, and follow-on privileged actions. Here you make the calls a senior analyst, senior incident responder, or senior detection engineer makes — with incomplete data, contradicting signals, and real costs attached to every choice.

What expert work looks like on this detection, in plain words. The telemetry arrives messy: audit policies miss fields, sensors are absent on some hosts, allow-lists are stale, and attackers deliberately borrow the names of trusted tools like SCCM or backup agents. You will decide which signal wins when two contradict. You will weigh containment against business damage on a payment host during its settlement window. You will judge when the right answer is to gather more evidence instead of acting, and when a widely repeated 'best practice' is exactly wrong for the environment in front of you. You will write a real correlation query in a query editor, reason about where that correlation should physically run (endpoint EDR versus central SIEM), and keep coverage alive through a platform migration.

The roles that live at this level: the senior SOC analyst who owns the queue and defends its tuning; the IR lead who sequences volatile-evidence capture before destructive containment; the CTI analyst who states attribution confidence honestly (Turla's RPC backdoors, Lazarus's KiloAlfa, KONNI's token duplication); the detection engineer who rewrites the AN1253 correlation for a fleet. The artefacts include realistic noise and deliberate gaps — some things you will want are simply not there, and part of the test is noticing that.

There is no time pressure per exercise: the timer pauses the moment you press Check. Pressure here is in the decisions, not the clock. Read every artefact twice. The obvious answer is sometimes wrong; the correct answer sometimes has a price you must accept and state.
