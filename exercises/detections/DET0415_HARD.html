ATT&CK object: DET0415 — Application Exhaustion Flood Detection Across Platforms Object type: Detection Strategy (MITRE ATT&CK) Severity: ●HARD Exercises in this file: 15 Estimated time: ~40 min Timer budget: 40:00 Pass mark: 70%

What you will learn here

You are now at HARD level, and DET0415 stops being a definition and starts being a job. The Detection Strategy for T1499.003 — Application Exhaustion Flood lives in noisy environments: analytics fire on signals that legitimate work can imitate, and the cost of a wrong call is real — either a blind spot or an outage you caused yourself.

At this level every exercise correlates several artefacts — logs, tickets, flow records, metric excerpts, ATT&CK excerpts — and the distractors are near-misses: answers that are technically defensible in a different context but wrong in this one. Expect the three classic traps: something that looks alarming but is benign (scheduled jobs, flash crowds), something that looks benign but is alarming (a “harmless viewer” app reopening itself in a loop), and the moment when the least aggressive action is the correct one.

You will tune mutable elements without breaking coverage, rank a real alert queue, judge coverage gaps, and — in one exercise — assemble a counted, read-only evidence command for the Linux syslog from a word bank, because in production the Linux analytic AN1166 is only as good as the evidence you can pull from it. You will also re-anchor the framework facts: one analytic per platform (AN1165/66/67/68), and the single listed mitigation M1037 — Filter Network Traffic.

Who does this work? A SOC analyst triages and escalates, a detection engineer tunes and documents baselines, an incident responder correlates artefacts during the outage, a cloud on-call engineer reads CloudWatch and CloudTrail, and a GRC analyst attests the coverage. Do the work like them: read every artefact, say what each one proves, and only then decide. The explanations after each Check are written to teach the discrimination, not just the answer.

Keywords covered in this exam

  • Windows: Application log + Sysmon EC1 + perfmon
  • Linux: syslog + auditd execve + NSM:Flow

  • Cloud: CloudWatch 5xx + CloudTrail + autoscaling

False-positive engineering

  • Baseline carve-outs (backup, AV, load tests)

Near-miss discrimination

  • T1499.003 vs T1499.001/.002 look-alikes
  • Flash crowd vs flood: source diversity and error shape
  • Least-aggressive effective action

Evidence & queries

  • Read-only query discipline before reboots

Coverage gaps

  • Map a blind spot to the DC that fills it
