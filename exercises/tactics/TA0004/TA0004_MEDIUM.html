◆ About TA0004 — Privilege Escalation (read this first)

TA0004 — Privilege Escalation is a Tactic in the MITRE ATT&CK framework.

MITRE ATT&CK is a free, public knowledge base. It describes what real attackers do, step by step. ATT&CK groups attacker actions into goals called tactics. Each tactic has a short ID. TA0004 is the tactic with the goal: the adversary is trying to gain higher-level permissions.

Think about what this means. The attacker is already inside one device or one account. Their rights are small. They can maybe read their own files and run simple programs. In TA0004 they try to become much stronger: an administrator (Windows), root (Linux or macOS), a domain admin (company-wide Windows), or a cloud admin (Microsoft 365, Azure, AWS). Imagine a thief who steals the key to one hotel room. Privilege escalation is like making a copy of the master key for the whole building.

Why does the attacker want this? With more rights they can: install or change software, turn off security tools, read other people's files, create new accounts, hide better, and move to other computers. Most serious attacks fail without this step, so defenders watch it closely.

ATT&CK lists more than 40 techniques inside TA0004. A technique is the "how" behind the goal. The important families are:

  • Abuse elevation control mechanisms (T1548) — misuse built-in gates like sudo rules on Linux or UAC prompts on Windows.
  • Exploitation for Privilege Escalation (T1068) — abuse a software bug to get higher rights.
  • Process Injection (T1055) — hide malicious code inside a trusted program that already has rights.
  • Access Token Manipulation (T1134) — borrow the "identity badge" (token) of a stronger account.
  • Create or modify services, tasks, accounts, or policies (T1543, T1547, T1136, T1098, T1484) — use system settings to gain rights that last.
  • Hijack Execution Flow (T1574) — abuse how programs find their libraries, paths, or services.
  • Escape to Host (T1611) — break out of a container to control the server that hosts it.

Where you will use TA0004 in real work:

  • SOC analyst — you triage alerts such as "new local admin created" or "service installed from Temp" and decide if this is TA0004.
  • Threat hunter — you search logs for sudo misconfigurations, unquoted service paths, SUID abuse, or token manipulation.
  • Incident responder — you find out which rights the attacker got, contain the access, and keep evidence safe.
  • Threat intelligence (CTI) analyst — you map threat reports to TA0004 so leaders understand attacker goals.
  • Red teamer — you test elevation paths in your own company and report what an attacker could reach.
  • Detection engineer — you write SIEM and EDR rules that catch escalation behaviour and tune out the noise.
  • GRC analyst / control owner — you write and check least-privilege and privileged-access policies (for example, who may hold admin rights).

Tools and documents you may touch: SIEM, EDR, PAM (privileged access management), vulnerability scanners, sudoers files, Group Policy, the ATT&CK matrix, detection rules, incident reports, and risk assessments.

The risk if you ignore TA0004: an attacker reaches domain admin or cloud admin, steals sensitive data, spreads ransomware, and survives even a reinstall.

◆ How this exam works

  • Goal: master MITRE ATT&CK TA0004 — Privilege Escalation at the MEDIUM (tier 2 of 4) level. Exercises connect two ideas per step, for example: recognise the technique, then choose the right control. Hints narrow the direction.
  • Pass mark: 70%. Your final percent is computed only over exercises you actually answered.
  • Exercise types: multiple choice, multi-select, matching, drag & drop, ordering / ranking, fill-in-the-blank, log reading with a filter, spot-the-mistake, branching scenarios, and (where the tactic calls for it) a small simulated terminal.
  • Check = submit. The moment you press Check, the timer stops automatically. You can read the feedback and the explanation for as long as you want — this costs you no time. The timer starts again only when you press Next (or Final Submit on the last exercise). We call this the review pause.
  • Pause button: a manual break. It stops the timer and hides the exercise until you press Resume.
  • Hint: one hint per exercise. A hint costs 10% of that exercise's credit (not 10% of the whole exam). The hint nudges you; it never gives the answer.
  • Partial credit: exercises with several correct items are scored per item: credit = max(0, (correct − wrong) / total). Wrong selections subtract from correct ones, so "select everything" earns 0. A partly right answer still earns part of the point.
  • Skip: skipping is free. Skipped exercises wait in a review list. Before the exam ends you can return to them and answer, as many times as you need, until the timer ends or you submit. Skipped-and-never-answered exercises are excluded from the score — they count neither for nor against you.
  • Retake: if you fail, use the Retake button. Questions and answer choices are shuffled again.
