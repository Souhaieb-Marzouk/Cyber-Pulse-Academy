Meet the attacker you will study: AppleJeus (G1049)

ATT&CK OBJECT TYPE: GROUP (threat actor)

In MITRE ATT&CK, a Group is a page that describes one real team of attackers: who they are, who they work for, what they want, and how they attack. This exam is about Group G1049. Its main name is AppleJeus. Other organisations track the same team under the names Gleaming Pisces, Citrine Sleet, UNC1720, and UNC4736. One team, several names — that is normal in threat intelligence.

You will learn who these attackers are. AppleJeus is a state-sponsored team from North Korea (DPRK). It belongs to the country's Reconnaissance General Bureau and sits under the wide Lazarus Group umbrella. The team has been active since at least 2018. Imagine its job is like a bank robber, but for a government: its main mission is to generate and launder money to support that government. That is why it mostly attacks the cryptocurrency industry. It wants digital assets — coins and tokens — not customer databases.

You will learn how they get in. AppleJeus mixes two tricks. First, phishing: emails that push malicious files. Second — their signature move — fake or trojanized cryptocurrency software: trading apps and wallets that look real but steal. In their most famous operation, the 3CX Supply Chain Attack (campaign C0057), they took a two-step path: they first placed malware inside an old trading program (X_TRADER) used by one company, used that foothold to reach 3CX's build environment, and then poisoned a normal 3CX software update so thousands of customers installed the malware themselves.

You will learn where this knowledge is used at work. A SOC analyst uses it to triage alerts like a hijacked DLL or odd cookie beacons. An incident responder uses it to scope a supply chain incident. A threat intelligence analyst uses it to brief leaders and to compare evidence against this group's playbook. A detection engineer turns the group's techniques — code signing abuse, process injection, dead drop resolvers — into detection rules. A GRC or control owner uses it to justify controls like signing enforcement, multi-factor authentication on VPNs, and software supply chain risk checks. A red teamer studies the playbook to test defences against it.

Everything in this exam comes from the ATT&CK page for G1049: its aliases, its campaign, its 23 listed techniques, and its software (for example the FRP tool it dropped as MsMpEng.exe). Master this group, and you can recognise its fingerprints in real logs.
