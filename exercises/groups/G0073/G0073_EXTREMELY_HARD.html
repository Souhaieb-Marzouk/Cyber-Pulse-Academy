What you will learn here

You are now operating at the level this group's incidents actually demand. The profile is fixed in your head: APT19, China-based, also tracked as Codoso, C0d0so0, Codoso Team, or Sunshop Group; the 2017 phishing campaign against seven law and investment firms; the Forbes.com watering-hole history; Cobalt Strike (S0154) and Empire (S0363) as listed software; the Deep Panda identity question left open by the ATT&CK page itself. Here, single facts no longer decide anything. Every exercise chains three or more concepts, and at least one signal in each scenario is contradictory, incomplete, or deliberately noisy — because senior decisions are made with imperfect boards.

The decisions are senior ones. An incident lead refuses the 09:00 reimage that would erase the only memory image, and pays for that refusal with an uncomfortable executive conversation. A detection engineer declines to block a beaconing domain immediately — the textbook move — because in this house it warns the operator and burns the long-term telemetry, choosing sinkhole-and-watch instead. A CTI lead holds "moderate confidence" against a client who wants a name tonight, because identity priced above evidence is a liability. A GRC principal sequences disclosure so privilege survives first contact.

Hints here only restate a constraint you may have missed — never the answer. Several correct answers are the least dramatic option on the board; at least one is "do nothing yet — gather the evidence that decides". If a choice feels expensive, ask whether the alternative costs more.

How this exam works

  • Pass mark: you need 70% or more to pass. Exercises you answer are each worth up to 1 point.
  • Exercise types in this file: SOC alert-queue triage simulations, branching decision scenarios, single-choice questions, drag-and-drop sorting exercises, multi-select questions with partial credit, timeline ordering exercises, fill-in-the-blank exercises, matching exercises, prioritization / ranking exercises, spot-the-mistake exercises.
  • Timer: a countdown of 70 minutes starts when you press Start. The timer stops the moment you press Check, so you can read the feedback and the explanation for as long as you like. It starts again when you press Next (or Final Submit on the last exercise). The Pause button stops the timer and hides the exercise for a real break; press Resume to continue.
  • Partial credit: exercises with several correct items are scored fairly, item by item: credit = max(0, (correct items − wrong items) ÷ total items). Selecting everything does not help, because wrong items subtract from your score. The live score in the header shows your running total.
  • Hints: every exercise has one hint. A hint gives you a nudge, never the answer, and costs 10% of that exercise's credit (one hint per exercise).
  • Skips: every exercise has a Skip button. Skipped exercises are moved to a review pool without showing the answer. Before the results you can return to the pool and answer them, as many times as you want, until the timer ends or you submit. Skipped exercises that stay unanswered are excluded from your score completely.
  • Press Start when you are ready. Good luck!
