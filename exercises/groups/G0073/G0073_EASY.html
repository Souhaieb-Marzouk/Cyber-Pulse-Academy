What you will learn here

You will learn about a real attacker team that MITRE ATT&CK tracks under the ID G0073. In ATT&CK, a Group (also called a threat actor) is a card that describes one team of attackers: who they are, who they attack, which tools they use, and how they behave. The group's best-known name is APT19. Public reporting also uses the names Codoso, C0d0so0, Codoso Team, and Sunshop Group for the same team — different researchers picked different labels.

ATT&CK describes APT19 as a China-based threat group. Its job is espionage: quietly stealing information. It has targeted many industries — legal services, defense, finance, energy, pharmaceutical, telecommunications, high tech, education, and manufacturing. A well-known event from 2017 is a phishing campaign against seven law and investment firms. The group's most-used tools are Cobalt Strike and Empire: two powerful frameworks that give an attacker remote control of a computer.

Imagine you work in a Security Operations Centre (SOC), the team that watches security alerts all day. Many roles use knowledge of this group. A SOC analyst recognises suspicious emails and strange beacons in the alert queue. A threat hunter searches the network for hidden attacker activity. An incident responder cleans infected computers after an attack. A threat-intelligence (CTI) analyst writes reports about the group so others can act. A detection engineer builds alarms for the group's known tricks. A GRC analyst checks that company rules and controls would stop this kind of attack. In this easy exam you will learn the group's names, its targets, its main tools, and simple working habits — like writing indicators safely (defanging) — that analysts use every day.

Every exercise here tests exactly one small idea, and the wording guides you. Take your time, read the feedback after each Check, and you will build a solid base for the harder levels.
