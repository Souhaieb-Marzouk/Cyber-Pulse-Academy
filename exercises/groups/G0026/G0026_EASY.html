What you will learn here

You are going to work with the ATT&CK profile of a real threat actor: G0026, APT18. In ATT&CK, a Group is a profile of one threat actor: who they are, who they attack, and exactly which behaviours (techniques) they have been seen using. This exam teaches you that profile step by step.

Imagine you are a new SOC analyst. An alert fires, and a name like APT18 or Dynamite Panda appears in a report. To react well you must know what that actor tends to do: the group page says APT18 has operated since at least 2009 and has targeted technology, manufacturing, human rights groups, government, and medical organisations. It lists concrete behaviours: logging into external remote services with valid accounts, running commands with cmd.exe, keeping a foothold with a registry Run key, talking to its operators over HTTP and even over DNS, and deleting its tools and batch files when done.

People use group profiles like this every day. SOC analysts match alerts against the actor's playbook. Threat intelligence (CTI) analysts turn the page into briefings and threat registers. Detection engineers check that the actor's techniques are covered by alerts. Incident responders use the profile to know where to hunt next. GRC and control owners use it to justify controls like multi-factor authentication. The tools around it are the ATT&CK pages and Navigator, your SIEM and EDR consoles, and your own ticketing system.

Take this EASY exam to learn the shape of the page: the group's names, its targets, and its most important techniques, in short and simple scenarios. Everything in the questions comes from the G0026 page itself.
