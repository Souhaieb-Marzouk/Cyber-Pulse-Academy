What you will learn here

You will learn how T1608.004 — Drive-by Target works as a Sub-Technique of T1608 Stage Capabilities, sitting in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042) on the PRE platform — the adversary's workshop, before any victim appears. Drive-by Target means preparing an operational environment, normally a website, so that systems that visit it during the normal course of browsing get infected or quietly profiled. The visitor only has to land on the page; no extra click is needed. The key idea is pre-positioning: the trap is built and armed before the first victim arrives.

You will work the three staging methods with a professional eye: inserting malicious scripts into web pages or user-controllable content such as forum posts; modifying script files that many sites load from publicly writeable cloud storage buckets; and crafting malicious web advertisements bought through legitimate ad providers — malvertising. The staged content is usually JavaScript, sometimes wrapped in a hidden iframe. You will also see profiling scripts (the T1592 pattern, with ScanBox as the named example) measure each visitor's browser first, so the exploit is spent only where it will land.

This file is about hard judgement calls. When the prepared site is one a specific community — a government body, an industry, a region — visits regularly, the campaign is a strategic web compromise, better known as a watering hole: low victim volume, high precision. Malvertising is the opposite trade — bought scale at lower precision. You will weigh acquired infrastructure (T1583) against compromised infrastructure (T1584) and meet browser exploit kits — toolkits that fingerprint the arriving browser and serve a matching exploit. And you will learn the uncomfortable truth about upkeep: browser exploits burn fast, so staged drive-by content needs continuous maintenance or it silently stops working.

You will learn how defenders fight a PRE-phase technique. ATT&CK assigns the detection strategy DET0825 (analytic AN1957), which relies on Internet Scanning of attacker-controlled infrastructure — but most staging is invisible to victims, so real telemetry anchors downstream on T1189 Drive-by Compromise and T1203 Exploitation for Client Execution. Working defenders baseline-diff their own sites against known-good copies, watch the third-party scripts their pages load, and hunt proxy logs for visits to watered domains. The only assigned mitigation, M1056 Pre-compromise, gets its full treatment in the next file.

This is daily work for several roles. A SOC analyst reads proxy chains — trusted site, redirect hop, kit gate, payload — and decides what a 404 means. A threat hunter diffs owned pages against baselines and audits bucket ACLs. A CTI analyst tracks watered domains and the groups behind them: Transparent Tribe, LuminousMoth, Dragonfly, CURIUM, APT32 and Threat Group-3390 all stage drive-by content in documented ways you will sort and match. An incident responder pivots from a beaconed workstation back to the site that staged it. In the 15 exercises you will classify a healthcare-association case, weigh attacker trade-offs, read two proxy chains, walk one dual red/blue decision, and run two read-only terminal audits — with no exploit ever detonated.
