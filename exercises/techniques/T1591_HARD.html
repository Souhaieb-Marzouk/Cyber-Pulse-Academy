🎓 What you will learn here

This is the HARD exam for T1591 — Gather Victim Org Information. The object type stays the same at every tier: T1591 is a MITRE ATT&CK Technique in the Reconnaissance tactic, and its four sub-techniques (T1591.001–.004) are Sub-Techniques. You are past definitions now. You are past definitions now. Here T1591 stops being a card in a matrix and becomes the quiet first act of real intrusions: a group learns which department to hit, which vendor to impersonate, which site just opened, and which words the company uses for its own machines.

You will work the cases the way a senior analyst does. A job advert that seems harmless hands the attacker a location (T1591.001) and a reporting line (T1591.004) in one paragraph. A partner announcement becomes the map for a Trusted Relationship (T1199) or Supply Chain Compromise (T1195) path. ATT&CK’s procedure examples stop being trivia and become evidence: FIN7 filtering companies by revenue on Zoominfo, Kimsuky and APT28 using language models to accelerate collection, Volt Typhoon’s extensive pre-compromise preparation, Operation Digital Eye’s actors dressing their activity in the target’s own technical vocabulary.

The exam also forces the defender’s discipline. ATT&CK’s detection strategy (DET0890) is blunt: collection is often outside your visibility and noisy to chase, so detection anchors on Initial Access while mitigation (M1056) trims the public surface. You will meet the traps that catch even experienced analysts: the alarming artefact that is benign, the benign artefact that is alarming, and the moment when the least aggressive action is the correct one. Every option in a question is written to be defensible somewhere — your job is to argue why it fails here.
