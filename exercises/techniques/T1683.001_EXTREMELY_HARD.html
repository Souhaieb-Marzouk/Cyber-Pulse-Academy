ⓘ What you will learn here

This file is about T1683.001 — Generate Content: Written Content, a sub-technique of T1683 Generate Content. It sits in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042) on the PRE platform. That means the writing happens before and during an attack, outside the victim's estate. No sensor in your network sees a lure being written.

At expert level you use this object as a senior CTI analyst. You cluster lures into campaigns, map them to ATT&CK, and brief the CISO in plain language under time pressure. Two cases anchor your briefings: S9044 Kali365, a phishing kit whose AI evaluates mail and writes tailored BEC responses; and C0064, where an AI coding tool produced forged tax compliance certificates from script-collected data. You also track G1056 TeamPCP's themed repositories, G1052 Contagious Interview's fake personas, G0082 APT38's decoy support documents, G1059 NIMBUS MANTICORE's career lures and G1058 The Gentlemen's branded ransom notes.

As senior IR you make evidence-preserving decisions when the clock is short. Decoy documents, forged certificates and BEC threads all land on your desk. You contain without destroying, you export with headers and hashes, and you document every gap in the record instead of hiding it.

Detection strategy here is a design job. M1056 Pre-compromise is the only mitigation on the page: nobody can prevent a person from writing text. DET0917 and AN2060 point you downstream instead — mail gateways, brand monitoring, worker reports, out-of-band verification and takedown requests. Build defences that do not depend on atomic indicators like one domain or one address, because written content is cheap to rotate.

This exam is extremely hard on purpose. Every scenario chains three or more ideas, hides at least one conflicting signal, and forces a call in minutes. Sometimes the right answer is the least aggressive one; sometimes it is the costly one. Read every artefact, including the boring ones.
