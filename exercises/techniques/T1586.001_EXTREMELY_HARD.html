ⓘ What you will learn here

T1586.001 — Compromise Accounts: Social Media Accounts — is a sub-technique of T1586 under the Resource Development tactic (TA0042), platform PRE. It is about taking over social media accounts that already exist — LinkedIn, X, Facebook, Telegram profiles of real people — instead of building a persona from scratch (that is the sibling T1585.001). The weapon is inherited trust: the real photo, the real history and the real network come with the account, which is why adversaries hijack an existing persona rather than grow one.

In real work this technique sits on many desks. A SOC analyst reads the brand-monitor feed and asks whether a colleague's odd DM is really the colleague. An IR lead decides what to do when a hijacked account is talking to staff right now. A CTI analyst tracks a handle's history and clusters it into a campaign. A brand-risk owner weighs takedowns against amplification. A red teamer proves how far a trusted persona gets. The page's named examples are G0065 Leviathan, which used compromised social media accounts to conduct social engineering attacks, and G0034 Sandworm Team, which created credential-capture webpages to compromise existing, legitimate social media accounts; Robin Sage and the HBGary hack are the reference lessons on manufactured trust and hijacked corporate amplification.

Daily tasks: watch the AN2002 signals — personas claiming to work for your organization, and recently modified accounts making numerous connection requests to org-affiliated profiles; archive profiles before they change or go private; verify odd requests from colleagues through internal channels; file platform reports and takedown requests; push phishing-resistant MFA and carrier locks as guidance, knowing that M1056 Pre-compromise means no enterprise control stops the takeover itself on a third-party platform. The downstream use is Spearphishing via Service (T1566.003) — the persona is developed so it can be spent there.

The risk register: hijacked personas look like the real person because the real network is the payload; noise is constant (genuine recruiters, onboarding sprees, alumni posts); evidence is volatile (profiles go private, posts get removed); and every decision has a cost on both sides. This EXTREMELY HARD set puts you in the chair: conflicting signals you must resolve out loud, real costs you must accept, and defensible-but-wrong options on every screen.
