ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page covers one Sub-Technique in MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.001, Spearphishing Service. It sits under the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information. In simple words, the attacker sends messages through third-party services such as social media, personal webmail, or chat apps to trick one person into sharing information. The messages never arrive through the company mail system.

Attackers like this channel because those services often have weaker security rules than a company, and the messages can skip the corporate mail gateway completely. The attacker usually uses a fake account and a believable story, like a job offer, to build trust over days. The goal is information: details about systems, policies, tools, and sometimes credentials.

Defenders work with a hard truth here: much of this traffic is outside company visibility. SOC analysts like Maya Chen read proxy and DNS logs to spot staff talking with unknown accounts on chat platforms. Threat hunters, detection engineers, and awareness leads such as Rob Duffy work with SIEM data, proxy categories, and training material instead of a mail gateway console.

You will meet this sub-technique in daily work when a staff member reports a strange job-offer chat, when you review proxy logs for uploads to personal webmail, when you tune awareness campaigns, or when you brief the CISO about what the company cannot see.

The risk is that a friendly chat can quietly hand an attacker the map of your environment. This exam will drill the delivery channel, the fake-account signs, the social engineering levers, the reporting workflow, and the limits of detection. All examples come from a fictional insurance company, Cedarline Insurance.
