ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page covers a Sub-Technique from MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.002, Phishing for Information: Spearphishing Attachment. Its parent is T1598, Phishing for Information, which also covers phishing over services, links and voice calls. In this sub-technique the adversary sends a targeted email with an attachment and wants information back: a filled-in form returned by email, or credentials typed into a login page hidden inside the file.

Attackers like this route because a quiet email is cheaper and safer than hacking in. One reply can deliver staff names, org charts, approval workflows, signatory bank details or working credentials. Real groups such as Dragonfly, Sidewinder and Star Blizzard used attachment phishing to collect exactly this kind of material before touching a single server.

Defenders meet this technique first in the mail gateway. The gateway admin tunes SPF, DKIM and DMARC under mitigation M1054, reads gateway logs and sorts real clusters from newsletter noise. The SOC analyst triages user reports, reads Authentication-Results headers, detonates attachments in a sandbox and hunts the mail tenant for one sender hitting many mailboxes. Detection strategy DET0865 and its analytic AN1997 name the signals: single-sender clusters, spoofed senders caught by SPF and DKIM, and uncommon data flows.

You will meet T1598.002 in daily work when you triage a reported form attachment, write a hunt query against the mail tenant, tune a noisy cluster rule, brief the CISO on actor tradecraft, or explain to the help desk why a form that 'just collects data' is still a security incident.

The risk this page addresses is quiet pre-compromise access: the adversary completes reconnaissance and steps into Initial Access, often reusing your own credentials later as Valid Accounts (T1078). This exam drills two connected skills in every exercise: first identify the mechanism or signal, then decide the right classification, query or tuning action.
