What you will learn here

This is the senior-analyst level for T1599.001 — Network Address Translation Traversal (Sub-Technique of T1599 Network Boundary Bridging, tactic Defense Impairment TA0112, platform Network Devices). Here the “obvious” answer is wrong in some exercises on purpose. Real incidents do not label themselves: a loud ACL change can be perfectly sanctioned, while a quiet one-line NAT addition is the intrusion. You will learn to slow down and check context before content — tickets, baselines, subnets, clocks and asset policy decide what a log line means.

What hard looks like for this technique. An attacker with device control can leverage existing NAT instead of writing new rules, or re-flash the image (T1601.001 Patch System Image) so custom translation logic survives reboots. Their bridge may be mistaken for an Internal Proxy (T1090.001), Traffic Duplication (T1020.001) or a published service. Detection strategy DET0163 (analytic AN0465) works only as a pair: a NAT table add/update/delete in networkdevice:config, followed by NSM:Flow across address pairs the design never intended. Response must stay least-aggressive: preserve, snapshot, restrict, revert one line — and only re-flash after scope is known.

Attribution honesty. ATT&CK attributes real actors to the parent T1599 — Salt Typhoon (G1045) at telecom and ISP edge devices, APT41 (G0096), and campaign C0043 (Indian Critical Infrastructure Intrusions) — not to the .001 sub-technique directly. Reporting “Salt Typhoon performed T1599.001” over-claims; you will practise the correct wording. Controls from the five ATT&CK mitigations (M1032, M1027, M1026, M1043 Credential Access Protection, M1037 Filter Network Traffic) map to NIST ideas you already use: CM-02 baseline, CM-05 access restrictions for change, IA-05 authenticator management.
