T1222.001 · Windows File and Directory Permissions Modification

A gentle but complete exam on the way attackers change Windows file permissions to hide payloads and block defenders - and the first moves defenders make to catch and undo it.

📋 Exercises: 15⏱ Timer budget 15:00≈ Estimated time 15 min◎ Pass mark 70%

What you will learn here

  • This exam is about the MITRE ATT&CK sub-technique T1222.001 - Windows File and Directory Permissions Modification, part of technique T1222 (File and Directory Permissions Modification) under the Defense Evasion tactic.
  • You will learn what an ACL (Access Control List) is: the list on every file and folder that says who may read, write, or run it.
  • You will practise the attacker tools: icacls for deny, grant, remove, and inheritance rules; takeown for ownership; and PowerShell Set-Acl.
  • You will see why attackers do this: to make payloads hard to open or delete, and to lock out defenders and security tools.
  • You will learn the defender side: spotting icacls and takeown in command-line logs, reading Event 4670, restoring access, and applying least privilege (M1022).
  • You will also learn the boundary: attrib +h hides a file with attributes - that is a different technique (T1564.001).

How this exam works

  • The passing score is 70%. Your percent is the credit you earned divided by the exercises you answered, rounded the normal way.
  • You will meet exercise types: single choice, multi-select, drag-and-drop sorting, multi-step questions, and terminal exercises (at most two) where you type real Windows commands in a safe fake shell.
  • Multi-select and sorting give partial credit: credit = (hits − wrong) ÷ correct answers, and it never goes below zero. A partly right answer earns part of the point.
  • The timer shows your budget: 15:00 for this exam. It runs only while you answer.
  • The Pause button stops the clock by hand (⏸ Paused). When you press Check, the exam pauses itself (⏳ Auto-paused) so reading the feedback does not burn time; pressing Next starts the clock again.
  • Every exercise has one hint. Using it costs a small penalty: −0.15 on that exercise’s credit.
  • You can skip an exercise; the clock keeps running. At the end you may review skipped exercises. Skipped exercises you never answer do not count in your score or your total.
  • When you finish, your result is shown and reported to the platform once. Then you can retake the exam.
