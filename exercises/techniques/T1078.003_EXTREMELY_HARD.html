ⓘ What you will learn here

T1078.003 — Valid Accounts: Local Accounts — is a sub-technique of T1078. It covers the abuse of credentials of local accounts: accounts an organization configures for users, remote support, services, or administration on a single system or service. They are managed on the host itself, not by a domain controller. Adversaries obtain and abuse them for Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, and Stealth — Stealth is the current ATT&CK name for the defense-evasion tactic. Password reuse can make one local-admin password work on many machines, and abused locals can feed OS Credential Dumping (T1003).

In real work this technique is everywhere. A SOC analyst triages 4624/4625 logon events and asks whether a local account is really doing what its baseline says. An IR lead contains a live local-account session without destroying the evidence it generates. A detection engineer builds the DET0407 analytics: logons for accounts not typically interactive or outside business hours (AN1137), interactive or service logons outside expected context (AN1138), and rare local logons over SSH (AN1139). A CTI analyst maps campaigns: Kimsuky's GREASE adding a Windows admin account for RDP, LockBit 3.0 moving laterally with a compromised local account, Sea Turtle compromising cPanel accounts. A red teamer proves the gap: one dumped local-admin password, many hosts.

Daily tasks: read logon types and identity anchors like the built-in Administrator (RID -500), keep change records next to auth logs, plan LAPS-style unique local-admin passwords (M1027), audit Kubernetes service accounts and their API access (M1026), run M1018 hygiene queues, and weigh M1032 MFA for hosts with no domain controller behind them.

The risk you train for here: valid logons look normal, so the work is judgement under pressure — which signal wins when a documented change, a scan notice, a vendor claim and a baseline break all speak at once, and which cost you accept to keep the evidence alive.
