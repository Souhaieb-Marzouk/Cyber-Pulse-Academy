🎓 What you will learn here

This is the HARD tier for T1574.004 (the ATT&CK sub-technique for dylib hijacking on macOS). The exercises behave like real Mac casework: privileged victims, entitled apps, detection engineering, and remediation decisions where the aggressive option is not the best one.

You will work with the page's inheritance facts (a loaded dylib joins the app's address space and inherits its privilege level and resources — so privileged or entitled victims turn a hijack into privilege escalation), the page's reference “Getting Root with Benign AppStore Apps”, and the Empire (S0363) workflow: a scanner module finds vulnerable applications first, then the hijacker module builds the malicious dylib from a legitimate dylib path.

On the detection side you will assemble DET0152 / AN0435 properly: confirm the three telemetry feeds (file system changes, process execution, module loads), encode the logic, and tune with per-process historical baselines before paging anyone.

On the mitigation side you will defend the limits of M1022 (user-installed apps and /tmp-loading apps stay exposed) and run the audit workflow that finds hijack opportunities before an attacker does.

Hints remove one wrong path — they never name the answer. Read every artefact before you answer.
