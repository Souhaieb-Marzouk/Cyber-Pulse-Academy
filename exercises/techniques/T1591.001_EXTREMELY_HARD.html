🎓 What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD exam for T1591.001 — Determine Physical Locations. The sub-technique collects where the victim is — offices, plants, data centers — and, through those places, the legal jurisdiction the organisation answers to. At this level the questions are no longer "what is this artefact" but "what does this body of artefacts mean, under pressure, with costs attached".

You will hold competing explanations for the same log (a scraping pattern versus an SEO rank-tracker), decide which signal wins when a permitted surveyor van keeps appearing across client sites, and answer a CISO who wants every visitor blocked — with the option that actually changes the observed path. You will sequence evidence work before it rotates, write confidence language that survives legal review, and, at least once, tell the board that naming a partner as "compromised" is not supportable on the evidence.

The ATT&CK spine is unchanged: collection is T1591.001 (with its sibling sub-techniques for rhythm, relationships and roles); the product feeds site-directed phishing (T1566) and physical delivery paths (Hardware Additions, T1200); detection anchors downstream because the collection itself runs outside your visibility; and mitigation is M1056’s minimisation discipline — granularity and timing within the legal floor. Magic Hound’s documented procedure — location harvested from visitors to its own phishing sites — remains the standing reminder that this technique can run entirely on the adversary’s side of the glass.
