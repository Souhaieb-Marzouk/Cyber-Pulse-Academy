ⓘ What you will learn here

You will learn T1078.003 — Valid Accounts: Local Accounts — a sub-technique of T1078 (Valid Accounts). It covers adversaries who obtain and abuse the credentials of local accounts: accounts an organization configures for use by users, remote support, services, or administration on a single system or service — managed on the host itself, not by a domain controller. Every platform ships them, so the sub-technique spans Containers, ESXi, Linux, Network Devices, Windows and macOS, and it serves Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation and Stealth (the defense-evasion tactic).

As a SOC analyst you will triage the telemetry the page's detection strategy DET0407 names: local accounts that are not typically used interactively or that log on outside business hours (AN1137), interactive or service logons outside their expected operational context (AN1138), and rare local-account logons through remote mechanisms such as SSH (AN1139). As an IR responder you will contain the credential, isolate the touched hosts, preserve evidence before any rebuild, and only then apply hygiene. As a detection engineer you will tune those analytics against baselines; as a red teamer you will show which dormant or over-privileged local account would fall first.

As a CTI analyst you will map intrusions to documented operations: C0064 (AI-Assisted Breach of Mexico's Government Infrastructure) connecting through compromised local root and service accounts via an AI agent, C0062 testing harvested credentials against discovered devices, Kimsuky (G0094) adding a Windows admin account with GREASE for continued RDP access, LockBit 3.0 (S1202) chaining lateral movement on a compromised local account, and Sea Turtle (G1041) taking over cPanel accounts on hosting infrastructure.

The daily risks in this unit: dormant accounts nobody disabled, one local-admin password reused across many hosts, over-privileged Kubernetes service-account tokens, and edge devices whose account stores no domain telemetry sees. The mitigations — M1032 MFA for local accounts, M1027 unique local-admin passwords, M1026 privileged account management (including LAPS and the Kubernetes service-account audit), M1018 user account management — are the controls every scenario in this exam tests against realistic log artefacts.
