ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page is a Sub-Technique page in MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.001, Phishing for Information: Spearphishing Service. It belongs to the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information, and to the Reconnaissance tactic, TA0043. The idea in one sentence: an adversary sends messages through third-party services such as social media, chat apps and personal webmail, and tries to get a named person to hand over information. No attachment has to land in your mailbox and no code has to run for this to work.

You will learn why attackers like this channel. Third-party services often run weaker security policies than a company, and the messages can bypass corporate mail controls completely, so much of the activity happens outside the organisation's view. Attackers build rapport, pose as recruiters or IT staff from accounts they established (T1583) or hijacked (T1584), and send small, patient asks. What they get is targeting data: vendors, policies, org charts, badge details and sometimes credentials that feed the next stage.

Defenders work this technique with compensating telemetry, because the mail gateway is blind here. SOC analysts triage chat and social reports, threat hunters read CASB and proxy logs for uploads and uncommon flows, incident responders preserve chat exports, CTI analysts compare patterns with published actor behaviour, and detection engineers tune alerts that would otherwise flood. GRC owners accept the leftover risk in writing when they understand the gaps.

You will practise the tasks where this page appears at work: sorting real signals from recruiter spam, matching a persona to how it was built, deciding what a polite three-week chat is actually building, writing a read-only log query for personal-webmail uploads, and choosing the least aggressive response that still protects people and evidence.

The risk is that friendly questions finish the attacker's homework: vendor names, badge policy and reporting lines make later intrusion cheaper and safer for them. This exam drills the judgement calls: benign-but-alarming floods, calm-looking patient contacts, least-aggressive responses, and how far public reporting on actors like Scattered Spider, NIMBUS MANTICORE and UNC1549 really reaches.
