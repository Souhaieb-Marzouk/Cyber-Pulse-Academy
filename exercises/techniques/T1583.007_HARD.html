What you will learn here

You will now work T1583.007 — Acquire Infrastructure: Serverless at HARD level. The sub-technique, filed under Resource Development (TA0042) inside the parent technique T1583 — Acquire Infrastructure, describes adversaries purchasing and configuring serverless runtimes — the page names Cloudflare Workers, AWS Lambda functions, and Google Apps Script — and using them to answer infected machines directly or to proxy their traffic to a hidden command and control server. Because the traffic arrives from subdomains of major cloud providers, it blends with ordinary traffic and supports Hide Infrastructure (T1665) behaviour such as filtering out security-scanner user-agents.

HARD level means the scenarios stop telling you what matters. You get artefact sets — proxy and DNS logs, change tickets, ATT&CK excerpts, vendor emails — and several answers that are technically defensible somewhere else, but wrong here. Expect one third of the questions to have a wrong 'obvious' answer. Expect one case that looks alarming but is benign, one that looks benign but is not, and at least one decision where the correct action is the least aggressive one.

The professional backdrop stays real. The ATT&CK detection note for this technique (DET0829, analytic AN1961) says that once a serverless function is in use, detection hunts for unique characteristics of the adversary software, because the acquisition itself happens outside victim visibility. The mitigation attached to the page (M1056 Pre-compromise) is honest about the same limit. Real cases anchor the exercises: the APT41 DUST campaign (C0040) used infrastructure behind Cloudflare and Cloudflare Workers for command and control in 2023–2024, and public red-team write-ups show Lambda-based redirectors doing the same job for penetration testers.

You will pivot, discriminate between five neighbouring techniques, review a draft analytic for flaws, and argue attacker value against defender anchors. Read every artefact before answering — the shortcuts are the traps.
