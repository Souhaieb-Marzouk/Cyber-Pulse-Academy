What you will learn here

Welcome back! This is the MEDIUM file for T1608.002 Upload Tool — a Sub-Technique of T1608 Stage Capabilities, in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042) on the PRE platform. "Staging" means placing software somewhere it can be reached later, and a "tool" here is legitimate software — free or paid, open or closed source — that an adversary repurposed for malicious use. Upload Tool is the act of putting such a tool on third-party or adversary-controlled infrastructure so it stays accessible during targeting.

This file is about the decisions behind that act. Why upload your own copy instead of pointing victims at the vendor's original download page? Because a copy you own stays available and unchanged: you can pin the exact build your operators trained on, or serve a build you quietly modified — while the original site can rotate the file, throttle downloads, or log and report whoever comes to fetch it. Sometimes attackers skip the upload entirely and let the victim download straight from the original site; you will learn to spot that choice in logs too.

You will also tour the staging locations: web servers the adversary compromised (T1584 Compromise Infrastructure) or rented (T1583 Acquire Infrastructure), code repositories under attacker control, quick-provision Platform-as-a-Service apps (Heroku-class), and cloud drives or paste sites. Along the way you will place this sub-technique inside its family, from T1608.001 Upload Malware to T1608.003 Install Digital Certificate, and chain it upstream to capability sourcing (T1588) and downstream to the victim-side pull (T1105).

Real cases anchor it all. For C0022 Operation Dream Job, Lazarus Group used multiple servers to host its malicious tools. In the 2025 Poland Wiper Attacks (C0063), the adversaries staged tools and files on Dropbox and Pastebin. On the defense side you will apply M1056 Pre-compromise — the only mitigation ATT&CK assigns to this technique — through OSINT monitoring, external attack-surface monitoring, and threat-intel tracking of adversary infrastructure.

SOC analysts, CTI analysts, incident responders, detection engineers, and red teamers all touch this technique: judging pulls in proxy logs, bucketing staging locations into classes, capturing staged evidence for tickets, choosing pre-compromise controls, or staging tools lawfully inside authorized operations. Every exercise gives a hint, a full explanation, and a study pointer. Work carefully — two connected ideas hide in each one.
