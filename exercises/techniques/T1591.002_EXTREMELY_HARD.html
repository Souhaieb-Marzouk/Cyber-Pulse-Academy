🎓 What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD exam for T1591.002 — Business Relationships. The sub-technique collects the organisations around a victim — MSPs, contractors, suppliers, customers — and the supply chains and shipment paths that connect them, because those relationships hold connected, potentially elevated access. By now you can name the doors it opens: Supply Chain Compromise (T1195), Trusted Relationship (T1199), Drive-by Compromise (T1189).

What remains is the senior layer: live cases where two sources disagree (the public partner page says one thing, the internal register another), where a vendor breach and a vendor impersonation arrive in the same week, and where a procurement director wants a contract terminated mid-incident. You will weigh evidence quality, sequence evidence preservation, accept real costs (a disclosure you keep after legal review, a termination you postpone), and phrase board-level statements whose confidence language survives the post-mortem.

ATT&CK stays the anchor throughout: collection is T1591.002; delivery through a turned update pipeline is T1195; entry through a partner’s legitimate access is T1199; a lure to people remains T1566. Dragonfly’s relationship mapping, LAPSUS$’s supply-chain knowledge and Sandworm Team’s partner-site research run beneath the scenarios as the documented proof that this sub-technique is where real operations begin.

Operating rules

  • Every exercise has three labelled blocks: SCENARIO (blue), QUESTION (amber), YOUR ANSWER (green).
  • Artefact sets are large and partly contradictory; noise and missing information are intentional.
  • Hints restate a constraint you may have missed; they never name the answer. Cost: 10% of that exercise’s credit.
  • Skip and review-skipped behave as always; unanswered skipped exercises are excluded from the score.
  • Multi-answer exercises use partial credit: max(0, (H−W)/N). Selecting everything scores zero.
  • Check freezes the timer for unlimited feedback study; Next resumes it.
  • Two truths can both be real (a stale public page, a live register) — the question decides which truth answers it.
  • Some correct answers accept a real business cost. Some correct answers are "not yet".

One terminal exercise runs on a reduced per-exercise timer (90 seconds). Evidence discipline beats cleverness.
