ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page covers a Sub-Technique page in MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.001, Phishing for Information: Spearphishing Service. Its parent technique is T1598, Phishing for Information, which also covers phishing through attachments, links and voice calls. In this sub-technique the adversary sends targeted messages through third-party services such as social media, personal webmail and chat apps, and the goal is to make a person hand over information, often credentials or details about how the organisation works.

Attackers like this channel because it is cheap and quiet. Third-party services often run under a less-strict security policy than the employer has, so the messages can bypass corporate mail controls completely, and much of the activity happens outside the visibility of the target organisation. The sender identity is prepared first: accounts are established from nothing (T1583) or real accounts are compromised (T1584). A single successful chat can deliver staff names, org charts, policy details, technology choices or a working login, and a fake job opportunity is a common reason to start the conversation.

Defenders meet this technique without a mail gateway alert. The SOC analyst triages staff reports of strange direct messages, the threat hunter reads proxy and CASB logs for unusual uploads, the CTI analyst tracks fake recruiter personas, and the detection engineer tunes detection strategy DET0821 and its analytic AN1953, which watch for messages requesting information, abnormal file or data transfers, and contact from unknown accounts. The usual tools are the SIEM, proxy and CASB consoles, a sandbox and the ticketing system.

You will meet T1598.001 in daily work when you triage a reported chat from a fake recruiter, hunt a proxy log for a large upload to a chat or webmail category, write an awareness snippet about out-of-band verification, adjust connection-request settings with HR, or brief the security lead on why this analytic is loud and partly blind.

The risk this page addresses is quiet pre-compromise access: nothing has broken in yet, but every answer collected feeds later targeting and Initial Access attempts. This exam drills two connected skills in every exercise: first identify the behaviour, signal or channel, then decide the right classification, query or response step.
