What you will learn here

You will learn what T1098.002 — Additional Email Delegate Permissions is and why it matters. This object is a sub-technique in the MITRE ATT&CK framework. ATT&CK is a big catalogue of attacker behaviours. A technique describes one behaviour. A sub-technique is a more specific version of it. This one lives under the parent technique T1098 — Account Manipulation, and it is number .002 in that family.

Imagine you work on the security team of a company. One morning, an attacker who already stole an admin password quietly gives their own account the right to open a colleague's mailbox. They did not guess a new password. They did not install anything loud. They just added a delegate permission — a normal email feature that also lets an executive's assistant read their boss's mail. That is exactly what this sub-technique describes: granting extra email permission levels so the attacker can keep coming back to the same mailbox.

You will see where this fits in real work. SOC analysts read audit events like Add-MailboxPermission every day. Threat hunters look for delegates nobody asked for. Incident responders check who still has access after a hack. Detection engineers build alerts from the ATT&CK detection strategy that belongs to this page. CTI analysts report that groups like APT28 have used it. Red teamers try it in authorised tests to show why it must be watched. GRC analysts turn it into review rules, and mailbox owners live with the controls.

The risk it addresses is simple and serious: quiet, lasting access to company email. Email holds invoices, contracts, password resets and personal data. If an attacker keeps a door into one mailbox, they can read secret talks, send fake payment requests, and stay inside even after some cleanup. This exam teaches you to recognise the object, name its tactic and platforms, read one audit event, and know the first safe check a defender runs. Take your time — every hint and explanation is written in plain words.

The ATT&CK object

  • Sub-technique T1098.002 and its parent T1098 (Account Manipulation)
  • Persistence tactic — keeping access over time
  • Office Suite and Windows platforms
  • Sibling sub-techniques: T1098.001, T1098.003, T1098.004

How email delegation works

  • What a delegate permission is (a helper right on a mailbox)
  • The Add-MailboxPermission PowerShell cmdlet
  • FullAccess — the permission level used in examples
  • Google Admin console and Gmail settings for delegation

Why attackers use it

  • Quiet access without changing any password
  • Access that survives password resets of the owner
  • Leads to Internal Spearphishing (T1534) from the trusted mailbox

What defenders watch

  • Office 365 audit events for delegation changes
  • Get-MailboxPermission — the safe read-only check
  • Multi-factor Authentication (M1032) as a brake on admin abuse
