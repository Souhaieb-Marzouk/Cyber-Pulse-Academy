What you will learn here

This is the MEDIUM exam for T1685.005 — Clear Windows Event Logs. The EASY level taught you what the behaviour is; here you start combining two ideas at once — for example, recognising the behaviour and choosing the right response, or telling T1685.005 apart from its neighbours.

Quick recap in plain words. MITRE ATT&CK is the catalogue of attacker behaviours. T1685.005 is a sub-technique: a specific behaviour inside the parent technique T1685 — Disable or Modify Tools, under the tactic Defense Impairment (TA0112). It means: an attacker with administrator rights wipes Windows event logs — with wevtutil cl, with PowerShell's Clear-EventLog, through the Event Viewer window, or by deleting the .evtx log files stored in C:\Windows\System32\winevt\logs\.

Why does this matter in real work? Because a log wipe sits right at the border between two jobs. A SOC analyst must judge the 1102 alert: maintenance or attack? An incident responder must know what evidence survives the wipe — off-host copies in the SIEM, EDR process records, or nothing at all. A detection engineer must know which channels carry which signals: Event ID 1102 in the Security log, Event 104 in the System log, Sysmon process and file-delete events. A CTI analyst must map real groups — APT28, Volt Typhoon, BlackCat, ShrinkLocker — to this behaviour when writing reports.

One boundary you will practise here: clearing (T1685.005 — the log is emptied but keeps working) is different from disabling (T1685.001 — logging itself is switched off or broken). They are neighbours under the same parent, and mixing them up is the classic mistake this level will train away.

Expect realistic artefacts: EDR process tables, SIEM excerpts, change tickets, and short CTI notes. Every exercise asks you to connect two facts before answering.
