What you will learn here

This tier mirrors senior decisions. Every case chains three or more ideas from the full area of T1601.001 — Patch System Image (parent T1601, tactic TA0005 — Defense Evasion, renamed Defense Impairment in current ATT&CK), and at least one signal will contradict another. Your job is to say which signal wins and why.

The trust chain is the battlefield. A checksum only means something relative to its reference value: a stale vendor list, a replaced TFTP 'gold' image, or a self-attesting device can each make a match lie. A patched version command can report the old, higher revision. A boot-loader implant (ROMMONkit) can keep a memory patch alive across reboots — ATT&CK describes the combination, and honest reports mark it unverified until proven. The procedure example behind all of this is S0519 SYNful Knock: malware inserted into a network device by patching the operating system image.

The decisions have costs. You will weigh keeping a device online for a legal case against isolating it now, accept real downtime to preserve evidence, and on purpose pick do nothing yet / gather more evidence / escalate when acting would be worse. One widely quoted best practice is wrong in one of these contexts — find it and say why.

Who works this tier? Senior IR leads, threat hunters, detection engineers and CTI briefers — the people who own the call when a grid DMZ router, a carrier GRX box or an MSP gold image server is on the line, and the CISO wants an answer in ten minutes.
