ⓘ What you will learn here

T1078.002 — Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts — is a sub-technique of T1078. It covers the abuse of credentials for domain accounts: user, administrator, and service accounts managed by Active Directory Domain Services (AD DS), whose access and permissions are configured across systems and services in the whole domain. Adversaries obtain these credentials through OS Credential Dumping (T1003) or password reuse, then log on exactly like the real owner. The same valid credentials serve four tactics: Initial Access, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, and Stealth — Stealth is the current ATT&CK name for the defense-evasion tactic.

In real work this technique is everywhere. A SOC analyst triages logon alerts and asks whether a valid account is really its owner. An IR lead contains a compromised domain account without destroying evidence. A detection engineer builds the DET0210 analytics: multi-host, off-hours, and impossible-travel logons (AN0590), sssd and winbind use on Linux (AN0591), macOS AD-plugin logons (AN0592), and vSphere/ESXi logons with domain accounts (AN0593). A CTI analyst maps intrusions such as the SolarWinds compromise, where APT29 used domain administrators' accounts for lateral movement. A red teamer proves the gap with the same valid-credential paths a defender must close.

Daily tasks read like a control room: read 4624 logon types, 4648 explicit-credential and 4672 special-privilege events, Kerberos 4768/4769 ticket activity, and 4728 group changes; keep tiered administration (M1026), MFA (M1032), password policy (M1027), account hygiene (M1018), and MFA-push training (M1017) alive; and brief the CISO in minutes, not days.

The risk is that valid accounts look like normal work. There may be no malware to find, sessions look routine, and dormant accounts have no owner to notice. This EXTREMELY HARD set puts you in the chair: conflicting signals you must resolve out loud, real downtime costs you must accept, and defensible-but-wrong options on every screen.
