What you will learn here

This is the top tier for T1098.002 — Additional Email Delegate Permissions, the persistence sub-technique of T1098 — Account Manipulation. Nothing here is a definition check. Every exercise chains three or more ideas, plants at least one genuine conflict between signals, and often asks you to accept a real cost — downtime, business friction, or a decision made with incomplete evidence.

The scenarios are drawn from the seats seniors actually occupy. A senior incident responder holds an M&A weekend in one hand and a breakglass account in the other, deciding whether removing a delegate now warns the intruder and burns the scoping window. A principal detection engineer weighs the two analytics on the page — AN1051 on delegation changes, AN1052 on the PowerShell behind them — and explains why both are anchors. A CTI lead argues attribution from role grants, device allow-listing and OWA behaviour, with honest uncertainty. A purple team walks one scenario from both sides: which persistence to plant, and which analytic catches it. A CISO advisor explains why "rotate every password" — the most demanded action in the world — leaves this particular access untouched, and why the read-only audit search is the command that must be typed first.

You will meet irrelevant-but-realistic noise, missing telemetry you must reason around, approvals that were forged by a compromised approver, and the Default/Anonymous folder-role pair that leaves no named delegate at all. The source is the official T1098.002 page: Add-MailboxPermission, folder roles, Google Workspace delegation, detection strategy DET0373, mitigations M1026/M1032/M1042, and the documented behaviour of APT28, APT29, Magic Hound, HomeLand Justice and the SolarWinds compromise.

Keywords covered in this exam

Senior incident decisions

  • Breakglass / JIT approval versus a silent grant at 03:00 — resolving the conflict
  • Do-nothing-yet, preserve-and-escalate as the correct senior move
  • Cost-accepted containment: removing grants breaks executive assistants
  • Incomplete telemetry: no mailbox audit — reconstruct from UAL, sign-ins, message tracking

Red & blue dual view

  • Persistence that survives password resets — choosing it as an attacker
  • The stealthiest grant: Default/Anonymous folder roles
  • The analytics that catch both — AN1051 and AN1052
  • The follow-on to hunt: Internal Spearphishing (T1534)

Actor chains & attribution

  • APT29: global admin → service principal backdoor → ApplicationImpersonation + Mail.Read
  • SolarWinds C0024: Set-CASMailbox device allow-listing for ActiveSync
  • Magic Hound: read access then hundreds of mails read via OWA
  • Attribution confidence: weigh TTP overlap, tooling and targeting honestly

Safe vs destructive

  • Search-UnifiedAuditLog as the evidence-grade read-only command
  • Remove-MailboxPermission / Set-* as state-changing and alerting moves
  • Classification practice: read-only versus state-changing actions

Detection anchors

  • AN1051 — anomalous or unauthorized delegation events
  • AN1052 — PowerShell execution of Exchange mailbox-permission cmdlets
  • Grant-then-use timing as a priority signal

Framework depth

  • M1026, M1032, M1042 applied to a live incident, not a policy binder
