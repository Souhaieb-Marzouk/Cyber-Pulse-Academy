What you will learn here

This exam takes you one step deeper into T1601.002 — Downgrade System Image, a Sub-Technique of T1601 — Modify System Image. At MEDIUM level you no longer just recognise the behaviour: you combine two facts at a time. You will read two device outputs and see that the running image and the next-boot image are different. You will pick the mitigations that actually stop this behaviour, and you will type a real verification command in a simulated device shell.

Picture your workday: as a SOC analyst you correlate a TACACS+ session (the log of who typed commands on the device) with a configuration change. As a network security engineer you decide whether M1046 Boot Integrity or M1045 Code Signing protects a given device. As a detection engineer you write a rule from the pattern "image transfer → boot variable change → reload". As an incident responder you keep the device state untouched until evidence is captured. As a threat hunter you build a hypothesis from artefacts. As a GRC analyst you connect the technique to NIST 800-53 controls like SI-07. A red teamer weighing a downgrade against patching the image learns why downgrades are attractive: the old, weak image already exists — no code to write.

By the end you will connect the artefacts, the controls, and the related techniques (T1601, T1601.001, T1600) into one coherent picture — and you will know exactly what to type on the device to verify an image checksum.
