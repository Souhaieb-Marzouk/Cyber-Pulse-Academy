What you will learn here

You will learn what T1597.001 — Threat Intel Vendors is. Imagine an attacker who wants to learn about your company. Instead of reading free public news, the attacker opens a paid threat intelligence portal — the same kind of portal that security companies sell to defenders. Vendors often publish more detail than the public version: which industries are being hit, who is believed to be behind attacks, and which tricks (TTPs) worked.

This page is a Sub-Technique — a child of the technique T1597 Search Closed Sources — and it lives in the Reconnaissance tactic (TA0043) on the PRE platform, meaning it happens before any malware runs on victim systems. The vendor data is usually partly redacted: customer names and identifiers are hidden, but the trends stay visible — target industries, attribution claims, and successful TTPs and countermeasures. That is exactly what an attacker needs to plan an attack or to check whether their own campaign is being tracked (if a threat actor searches vendor data for news about their own activity, ATT&CK calls that a different technique, T1681 Search Threat Vendor Data).

In real work, a threat intelligence analyst manages vendor feeds and watches for leak risks. A GRC analyst adds redaction and access clauses to vendor contracts. A SOC analyst learns why detection here is nearly impossible (detection strategy DET0816, analytic AN1948: very high occurrence, many false positives, and it happens outside our visibility), so detection efforts focus on Initial Access. The linked mitigation is M1056 Pre-compromise: minimise the amount and sensitivity of data available to external parties.
