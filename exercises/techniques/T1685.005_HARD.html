What you will learn here

This is the HARD exam for T1685.005 — Clear Windows Event Logs. EASY taught you the concept, MEDIUM taught you to combine two facts. Here the wrong answers stop being silly: two options will often look right, and only careful reasoning picks the survivor.

The object in one paragraph. T1685.005 is a sub-technique of T1685 — Disable or Modify Tools in tactic Defense Impairment (TA0112): an attacker with administrator rights empties Windows event logs using wevtutil cl, PowerShell's Clear-EventLog or Remove-EventLog, the Event Viewer GUI, or by deleting .evtx files from C:\Windows\System32\winevt\logs\. Its sibling T1685.001 switches logging off instead of clearing it. MITRE revoked the old IDs T1070.001 and the T1562 family and moved this behaviour here, so real 2016–2024 reports cite the legacy numbers.

Where you will use this at work, at the hard level. As a SOC analyst you will separate the Sunday-night archiving job (benign, ticketed) from the 03:00 wipe by tmpadm (malicious) — same event, opposite verdicts. As a detection engineer you will reason about MITRE's detection strategy DET0532 and its analytic AN1472: a behavioural chain that links elevated privileges to clearing commands and to the resulting Security 1102, Sysmon EID 1 (process) and Sysmon EID 23 (file delete) signals, with tunables like TimeWindow, UserContext, CommandLinePattern and TargetLogName. As a responder you will sequence evidence preservation before containment. As a CTI analyst you will map old report IDs to current ones and match actors — Volt Typhoon's selective clearing, APT38's Sysmon-plus-EventLog wipe, Olympic Destroyer's fast wevtutil barrage, HermeticWiper's file overwrites — to the right nuance.

Controls matter here too. ATT&CK attaches three mitigations to this technique: M1029 Remote Data Storage (forward events off-host with minimal delay), M1041 Encrypt Sensitive Information (encrypt or obfuscate event files locally and in transit), and M1022 Restrict File and Directory Permissions (protect local event files, limit privilege-escalation). The CTID mappings explorer adds CIS Control 3.4 — Enforce Data Retention.

Rules of the game at this severity: hints remove one wrong path but never name the answer; at least a third of the exercises have a tempting 'obvious' answer that is wrong; and every exercise chains at least two to three concepts.
