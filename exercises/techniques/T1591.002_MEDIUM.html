🎓 What you will learn here

This is the MEDIUM exam for T1591.002 — Business Relationships, the sub-technique of T1591 that covers collecting the organisations around your victim: managed service providers, contractors, suppliers, customers — and the supply chains and shipment paths that connect them. You know the basics; now you connect the map to the door it opens.

The exercises rotate through three desks again. At the CTI desk you work the documented procedures: Dragonfly’s open-source relationship mapping, LAPSUS$’s detailed supply-chain knowledge, Sandworm Team’s research of the official PyeongChang Olympics partnership site. At the SOC desk you learn what relationship knowledge looks like in motion — a lookalike vendor domain, an RMM console that suddenly matters, a procurement CSV that quietly ranks above the website as ground truth. At the third-party-risk desk you review the same pages attackers read: partner directories, case studies, subprocessor lists — and you practise the mitigation trade-offs of M1056 Pre-compromise without breaking the business.

Expect two-step questions: identify the relationship artefact, then name where it leads — Supply Chain Compromise (T1195), Trusted Relationship (T1199), Drive-by Compromise (T1189) — or which sub-technique it belongs to. One exercise lets you type a real command in the simulated shell; one walks a live branch scenario. Every wrong option is wrong for a reason you should be able to say out loud.
