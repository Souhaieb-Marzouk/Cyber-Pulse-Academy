What you will learn here

You will learn what T1597 — Search Closed Sources is. Imagine an attacker who wants to attack a company like the one you work for. Before touching any of our systems, the attacker goes shopping — but not in a shop. They look for closed sources: data that is paid for, private, or not freely available. Examples are paid subscriptions to threat-intelligence feeds, private business databases, and dark web markets where stolen data is sold.

T1597 is a Technique in the MITRE ATT&CK framework, and it belongs to the Reconnaissance tactic (TA0043). Reconnaissance means "the attacker studies the target first". This technique runs on the PRE platform, which means it happens before any malware touches our machines. That is why our security tools usually see nothing while it happens.

You will see where this technique is used in real work: a threat intelligence analyst uses ATT&CK IDs like this one to write reports about what attackers know about us. A red teamer uses the same idea (in a legal, authorised way) to test what our company leaks. A SOC analyst learns why no alert fires during this phase, and why detection efforts focus on the next stage: Initial Access. A GRC analyst uses the linked mitigation, M1056 Pre-compromise, to push rules that minimise how much sensitive data is available to outsiders. The framework page also names real actors: EXOTIC LILY (G1011) searched business databases about targeted people, and its detection strategy DET0822 (analytic AN1954) explains why detection here is so hard.
