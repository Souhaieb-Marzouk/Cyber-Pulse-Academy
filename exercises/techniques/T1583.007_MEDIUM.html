What you will learn here

You will work with T1583.007 — Acquire Infrastructure: Serverless, a sub-technique of the MITRE ATT&CK technique T1583 — Acquire Infrastructure, filed under the Resource Development tactic (TA0042). In ATT&CK, this sub-technique describes adversaries purchasing and configuring serverless cloud infrastructure — the page names Cloudflare Workers, AWS Lambda functions, and Google Apps Script — that they can use during targeting.

Here is the core idea you will drill: the rented serverless runtime becomes a middleman. It can answer infected machines directly, or proxy their traffic onward to an adversary-owned command and control (C2) server, which is the behaviour ATT&CK calls T1090. Because the traffic seems to come from subdomains of huge cloud providers, it is hard to separate from ordinary traffic — which also makes it easier for the attacker to Hide Infrastructure (T1665).

At work, this page is used from several chairs at once. SOC analysts triage the beaconing that the technique eventually produces. Detection engineers tune analytics on fresh cloud-platform hostnames, guided by the ATT&CK detection idea for this sub-technique (DET0829): once the serverless function is already in use, look for unique characteristics of the adversary software, and focus detection on related lifecycle stages, because the rental itself happens outside the victim's visibility. Threat intelligence analysts anchor reports on real cases — for example the APT41 DUST campaign (C0040), where APT41 used infrastructure behind Cloudflare and Cloudflare Workers for command and control. Red teamers reuse the same pattern as a cheap, disposable redirector, as public blog posts about AWS Lambda redirectors show.
