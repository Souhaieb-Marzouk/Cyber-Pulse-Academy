What you will learn here

Welcome to the EXTREMELY HARD file for T1608.005 — Link Target. It is a Sub-Technique of T1608 (Stage Capabilities), it lives in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042) on the PRE platform, and it happens entirely before a victim is involved. The rule underneath every exercise: before the phishing message is sent, the adversary puts in place the page or file the link points to, and nothing happens until a person actively clicks or pastes the URL.

The staged destination is usually an HTML page with some client-side JavaScript (code that runs in the visitor's browser and decides what to show). Very often it is a cloned login page — a copy of a real sign-in screen that collects the credentials and one-time MFA codes people type into it. Sometimes the link points straight at an uploaded malware file. The name in the link is usually a lookalike — a homoglyph, a typosquat or a different domain ending — bought during infrastructure acquisition (T1583.001).

The wrapping is where senior work starts. URL masking means visible link text and true destination differ. Shorteners and PaaS pages (pages rented on widely trusted app-hosting providers) borrow trust so blocklists hesitate. Per-victim one-time URLs break scanning, because each address works exactly once. Here no trick appears alone: every exercise chains several inside a messy, real decision where the signals do not agree.

You will work the way senior teams work. SOC analysts triage lookalike-monitoring alerts — feeds such as RiskIQ or DomainTools watch for lookalike domains, Shodan and Censys watch the outside surface — when the tool screams and the telemetry is silent. CTI analysts map behaviour to actors and run takedowns through kit fingerprints, registrar abuse reports and intel platforms such as MISP, Recorded Future and Anomali. IR decides whether to kill a clone while harvesting is live. Detection engineers place controls from the M1056 Pre-compromise portfolio — the only mitigation ATT&CK lists for this sub-technique.

The real campaigns are your case material: NIMBUS MANTICORE (G1059) registered domains pointing at a typosquat that delivered payloads, and pre-shared credentials with spoofed login pages serving payloads only when authorized credentials are entered; the C0059 Okta panel victims were tricked into opening during social-engineering calls; Silent Librarian (G0122) cloning login pages behind a variety of shorteners. Expect contradictory signals, a costly correct action, a best practice that fails here, and one terminal decision where exactly one request is allowed.
