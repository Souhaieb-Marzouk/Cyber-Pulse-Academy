ⓘ What you will learn here

You will learn T1078.002 — Valid Accounts: Domain Accounts — a sub-technique of T1078 (Valid Accounts). It covers adversaries who obtain and abuse credentials of a domain account: the users, administrators and services managed by Active Directory Domain Services. Because a domain account works across systems and services in the domain, one valid credential can bypass access controls on VPNs, mail, remote desktop and file shares, persist like a normal user, and escalate privilege when the account is highly privileged. Much of this activity uses no malware at all, which is why it blends in with real work.

As a SOC analyst you will triage the logons: Windows 4624/4648/4672 and Kerberos 4768/4769 records, plus the cross-platform analytics AN0590–AN0593 — off-hours and impossible-travel logons, sssd and winbind use on Linux, Open Directory and Active Directory plugins on macOS, and domain-account logons to ESXi hosts. As an IR responder you will contain the account, preserve evidence before disabling anything, scope every host the identity touched, and plan ticket cleanup with krbtgt in mind.

As a detection engineer you will tune those analytics against baselines. As a CTI analyst you will map intrusions to documented operations: SolarWinds (C0024) and Operation Ghost, MidnightEclipse (C0048) and Wocao (C0014) riding compromised domain admin accounts, the 2025 Poland Wiper attacks (C0063) reaching through the FortiGate VPN with privileged accounts, Sandworm Team and Shamoon (G0034/S0140) with stolen and hard-coded domain credentials, and OilRig (G0049) retrieving domain credentials with STEALHOOK. Red teamers read the same picture to show which dormant or over-privileged domain account would fall first.

The daily risks in this unit: dormant accounts left enabled, one credential overlapping many systems, MFA push fatigue, and hygiene debt that turns a single phished password into domain-wide reach. The mitigations — M1017, M1018, M1026, M1027, M1032 — are the controls every scenario tests against realistic log artefacts.
