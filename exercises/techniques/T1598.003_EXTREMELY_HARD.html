ⓘWhat you will learn here

This page is about MITRE ATT&CK sub-technique T1598.003, Spearphishing Link, which sits under the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information, in the Reconnaissance tactic. In this attack an adversary sends an email with a link, plus friendly or urgent text, that pushes the reader to click it or copy it into a browser. The link leads to a fake page that collects information or credentials.

Attackers use this sub-technique because one click can give them a lot. They learn who is active, which staff hold which roles, and sometimes they get a working login or a session cookie. With that they can explore payment systems, map the company, and prepare a bigger attack. Kits like evilginx2 and EvilProxy can even capture one-time MFA codes in real time.

Many roles touch this problem. SOC analysts triage user reports and mail logs; threat hunters write queries over mail telemetry; incident responders revoke sessions and keep evidence; CTI analysts map actor behaviour; detection engineers tune the mail gateway; mail gateway admins run safe-link rewriting and blocks; and GRC control owners decide which risk to accept.

You will meet this sub-technique in daily work: triaging a reported message, expanding a shortened link, reading gateway logs with spf, dkim and dmarc results, writing a hunt query over mail telemetry, briefing a CISO with exposure numbers, or deciding whether to block a lookalike domain during a busy business day.

The risks are real: stolen credentials and session cookies later used with valid accounts, leaked staff and client data, and wrong decisions made under pressure. This exam runs at extreme difficulty, so it drills chained reasoning: you will weigh conflicting signals, sequence response steps, run read-only hunts, and accept or refuse costly actions with the business in mind.
