ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page covers one sub-technique in MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.001, Spearphishing Service, which sits under the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information, inside the Reconnaissance tactic. Here the attacker does not send a weapon. The attacker sends questions through a third-party service, and the goal is to make a specific person answer them.

Why do attackers use social media, personal chat apps and personal webmail? These services run under weaker security rules than a company network, so the messages often bypass corporate mail controls completely, and much of the exchange happens outside the company's visibility. What the attacker gets is valuable: program names, org charts, process details and sometimes credentials, all of which feed the next stage of the operation.

Defenders work with a hard truth: the mail gateway, the usual phishing sensor, never sees this traffic. SOC analysts, threat hunters, incident responders, CTI analysts and detection engineers lean on compensating telemetry instead: CASB logs, proxy categories, DNS logs and user reports. The official detection strategy, DET0821 with analytic AN1953, is honest about the price: a very high false-positive rate.

You will meet this in daily work: triaging a report about a fake recruiter on a chat app, writing a CASB or proxy rule, tuning noisy categories, briefing a CISO who needs a decision in ten minutes, and writing an incident record that survives review. The only mitigation listed on the page is M1017 user training, so people, not products, carry most of the defense.

The exam drills judgment under pressure: campaigns built on fake recruiter accounts and fake job offers, like the UNC1549 pattern in public reporting, with C0027 and NIMBUS MANTICORE as ATT&CK procedure examples. You will weigh conflicting signals where a small sensitive detail beats a large benign volume, price visibility gaps honestly, and accept actions that cost the business something. Sometimes the right move is to hold and escalate.
