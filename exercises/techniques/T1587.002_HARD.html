What you will learn here

This exam is about trust: why a digital signature on a program makes people and tools relax, and how adversaries exploit exactly that reflex. In MITRE ATT&CK, T1587.002 sits under T1587 (Develop Capabilities) in the Resource Development tactic on the PRE platform: the adversary creates self-signed code signing certificates before any attack traffic reaches you.

You will practise reading certificates the way a detection engineer does — thumbprint, algorithm, validity period, common name — and telling develop (the adversary creates its own certificate, T1587.002) apart from obtain (it buys or steals one, T1588.003 — note the different number). You will see why a signature proves authorship and integrity but never safety, and what that means for warnings like “Unknown publisher”.

The roles in this exam are the CTI analyst pivoting on a reused thumbprint, the SOC analyst triaging signed-but-unknown binaries, the incident responder untrusting a rogue certificate, and the GRC officer drafting the signed-code policy. Their real decisions — block, audit-mode first, allowlist, retro-hunt — are your exercise scenarios.

ATT&CK is blunt about the limit: the page lists M1056 Pre-compromise as the only mitigation and no data sources, because certificates are made far away from your estate. Detection therefore anchors on the use phase — Code Signing (T1553.002) and Install Root Certificate (T1553.004) — and on the four certificate features the page names.
