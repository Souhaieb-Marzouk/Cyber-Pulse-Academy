What you will learn here

You will learn what T1599.001 means and why attackers love it. Imagine you are the guard of a building with one door. That door has a rule: people inside can walk out, but strangers cannot walk in. On a computer network, that door is a device called a router or a firewall, and the rule is called NAT — Network Address Translation. NAT rewrites the address written on each packet, like a postman who changes the return address on letters as they pass the desk.

This page teaches one exact ATT&CK object. Its type is a Sub-Technique — a specific way of doing something broader. Its ID is T1599.001, its name is Network Address Translation Traversal, and it belongs to the parent technique T1599 Network Boundary Bridging. In the current ATT&CK matrix it sits in the tactic Defense Impairment (TA0112), and its platform is Network Devices — routers and firewalls, not laptops.

Where you will meet it at work. A SOC analyst sees it as a strange new NAT rule in a router log. A network defender uses config backups to prove someone changed it. An incident responder checks who logged in to the device. A threat hunter looks for traffic that only makes sense if a bridge exists. A CTI analyst writes reports that name T1599.001. A detection engineer builds alerts for new translation rules. A GRC analyst maps the controls that stop it — like Multi-factor Authentication (M1032), Password Policies (M1027) and Privileged Account Management (M1026). The risk it addresses: an attacker who controls one router can open a private path into the whole internal network.
