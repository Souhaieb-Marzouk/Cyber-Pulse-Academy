What you will learn here

This is the HARD tier for T1583.003 — Acquire Infrastructure: Virtual Private Server, the ATT&CK sub-technique for renting a VPS during Resource Development. At this level the answers stop being obvious. You will compare several servers that all look similar and pick the one that truly matches the adversary profile; you will meet a frightening indicator that turns out to be a researcher's scan box, and a boring-looking corporate server that is exactly what it claims to be. You will rank hunting hypotheses, criticise a detection analytic that leans on one weak field, and draft a CTI report paragraph with the correct ATT&CK names.

The artefacts are realistic: scan-platform summaries, TLS certificate panels with self-signed and CA-issued certificates, JARM-style TLS fingerprints, whois and ASN ownership data, and proxy logs with fixed beacon intervals. You will also meet more procedure examples from the ATT&CK page — BlackByte staging encryption keys on rented servers, Salt Typhoon running C2, Winter Vivern hosting scanning applications, Moonstone Sleet hosting payloads, and the ORB networks of FLORAHOX and SPACEHOP — and one terminal exercise where you choose the right tool and flags yourself to read a certificate. Every explanation tells you not just what was right, but why the near-miss answers were wrong here and defensible somewhere else.

Who uses this page at work? A SOC analyst meets the results as alerts and must keep false positives down. An incident responder uses the same artefacts to scope a case without destroying evidence. A detection engineer tunes analytics so behaviour — not one weak field — drives the alert. A CTI analyst profiles groups by their infrastructure habits. A red teamer copies the tradecraft to make exercises honest, and a GRC analyst uses the M1056 caveat to set realistic expectations with leadership. Each exercise below sits in one of those chairs.
