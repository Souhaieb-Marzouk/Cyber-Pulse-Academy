What you will learn here

You will learn about MITRE ATT&CK technique T1690 — Prevent Command History Logging. This is a Technique: a specific behaviour that an adversary performs. It lives in the Defense Impairment tactic area, TA0112. That is the part of ATT&CK that collects behaviours used to break, blind, or fool security mechanisms. A command shell normally writes the commands a user types into a history file. An attacker who switches that logging off can work on a stolen machine and leave almost no typed trace.

You will not learn this from definitions. You will act as the senior responder during one hard incident week. You will read hypervisor logs from ESXi hosts, appliance diagnostics from an Ivanti Connect Secure gateway, and shell artefacts from a build server. You will weigh live telemetry against change tickets that contradict it, decide when a popular control is wrong for one estate, and accept containment calls that cost the business real money. More than once, the right move will be to do nothing disruptive yet — capture evidence, escalate, and keep the deadline in view.

This material mirrors real work. SOC analysts tune the absence-based alerts that catch it. Threat hunters chase sessions that show activity but no commands. Incident responders run the containment decisions you will practise here. CTI analysts grade attribution with honest confidence levels. Detection engineers keep the DET0563 strategy alive across SIEM, EDR, and NDR. GRC analysts own the control choices. Red teamers show where telemetry dies first. The risk is simple: if an attacker stops command history logging, your investigation loses its best record of what they did.
