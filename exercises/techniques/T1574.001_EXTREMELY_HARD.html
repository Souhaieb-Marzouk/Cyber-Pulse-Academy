🎓 What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD tier for T1574.001 (the ATT&CK sub-technique for DLL abuse on Windows). The exercises behave like senior-analyst decisions under pressure: several artefacts at once, contradictory signals, incomplete information, and real business cost either way.

You will work one long case across an ADFS identity server, a payment host, and a fleet-wide hardening debate — deciding what wins when signals conflict, when to act and when to gather more evidence, and how the detection strategy DET0201 / analytic AN0577 is assembled end to end (module loads + file system + registry, tuned by per-application baselines).

The page's procedures appear at case depth: FoggyWeb's version.dll hijack on the ADFS service host, DarkGate's KeyScramblerE.DLL, Vidar masquerading as msedge_elf.dll, Storm-1811's 7z.DLL, and the service-riding side-loads of Operation CuckooBees (IKEEXT, PrintNotify).

Mitigations are treated as engineering trade-offs: M1044 (library-loading settings), M1013 (manifest hashes), M1038 (application control over DLL loads of legitimate software), M1047 (audit and correct), M1051 (patching). You will be asked to defend the gaps, not just recite the names.

Hints in this file only restate a constraint you may have missed. They never point at the answer. At least one exercise rewards doing nothing yet — and at least one widely-used 'best practice' is the wrong answer on purpose.
