What you will learn here

Welcome to the HARD tier of T1608.006 — SEO Poisoning, a Sub-Technique of T1608 Stage Capabilities in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042), platform PRE. The idea has not changed from the earlier files: poison the mechanisms behind search placement — purchased ads and the ranking, score and reputation computed by web crawlers — so victims find the attacker's page themselves, through their own searches. What changes here is your side of the board: how this pre-compromise staging is detected, and what honestly can and cannot be seen before any victim clicks.

Quick refresher on the rigging, in plain words. Keyword stuffing crams chosen terms into compromised sites, including hidden text people never see but crawlers still score. Link rings are dozens of cheap, freshly registered domains whose only job is to link at one target — often a typosquat, a domain one letter away from a real vendor — to fake the reputation signals crawlers count. Cloaking shows the crawler one page and people another, keyed on user agent, language or HTTP headers, usually behind evasive redirects that only the right audience is waved through.

Who works this file: the SOC analyst triaging a poisoned-result alert; the CTI analyst running brand monitoring in search results and reporting abuse to the vendor; the threat hunter sweeping our own sites for injected keywords; the IR lead pivoting after a confirmed click; the detection engineer writing the internet-scan analytic and its limits into the handbook; and the SEO or web admin, who becomes an early sensor when their pages are abused as the stuffing canvas.

ATT&CK assigns this technique the detection strategy DET0881 with analytic AN2013, and it runs on one data source: Internet Scan — external scanning of attacker-controlled infrastructure, fetching pages both as a crawler and as an ordinary user would see them. Its hard limit: the analytic depends on prior identification of infrastructure and patterns, and most staging happens outside victim visibility. That is why detection effort usually pivots downstream, to Drive-by Compromise (T1189) and client exploitation (T1203) telemetry — or, when the lure is a package search, to supply-chain telemetry.

On mitigation: M1056 Pre-compromise is the only mitigation ATT&CK assigns here — proactive work before any compromise, such as auditing public data, watching the external attack surface with Shodan/Censys-style scanners, monitoring for lookalike domains with RiskIQ/DomainTools-style services, and threat-intelligence platforms. You will also meet a documented actor: G1059 NIMBUS MANTICORE, which used SEO poisoning to deliver malware and registered dozens of domains linking to a typosquatted domain to generate link-based reputation signals, plus keyword stuffing to improve search ranking.
