What you will learn here

You will learn how attackers stop a computer from recording the commands they type — and how defenders notice it. Imagine you are a SOC analyst on a normal shift. Shells on your Linux, macOS and Windows systems normally save every typed command to a history file. If that file suddenly goes quiet while someone is still working, an attacker may be hiding their actions.

In this exam you will work with T1690 — Prevent Command History Logging. It is a Technique in the MITRE ATT&CK framework, and it sits in the Defense Impairment tactic area (TA0112). That tactic collects behaviours that break or blind security defences. This technique stops the shell from saving future commands, so the record of what the attacker typed never appears.

You will see where this knowledge is used in real work: a SOC analyst reads shell telemetry and triages missing history; a threat hunter looks for sessions with no saved commands; an incident responder checks history settings on a suspect host; a CTI analyst names the technique in reports; a detection engineer writes alerts for history that stopped; a GRC analyst writes hardening baselines; a red teamer knows why their own commands leave traces in tests. The risks are real: hidden commands, longer attacker dwell time, and weaker investigations.
