🎓 What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD tier for T1578.001 — Modify Cloud Compute Infrastructure: Create Snapshot (sub-technique of T1578, tactic Defense Impairment, platform IaaS). The technique itself is simple to say — an adversary creates a point-in-time copy of a VM disk, virtual hard drive or volume to evade defenses and bypass access restrictions — but at senior level the technique is never alone: it arrives inside pressure, ambiguity and politics.

Every scenario here is a decision under constraint. Artefacts contradict each other: a snapshot fires inside the sacred 02:00 backup window, but the identity behind it was minted twenty minutes earlier from an anonymous VPN. A share goes to an account that sits in the vendor register — whose contract expired last month. The fix that feels strongest (terminate everything) destroys the volatile evidence Legal will demand. You will be asked to state which signal wins and why, to accept the real cost of an action before recommending it, and — more than once — to recommend gathering more evidence or escalating instead of acting, because that is genuinely the senior answer.

The source material stays the ATT&CK page and its objects: analytic AN1187 (correlate snapshot creation with instance creation and mounting; weigh user behavior, IP geolocation, automation context; flag new identities, missing change control, unauthorized mounts), the mitigations M1047 Audit and M1018 User Account Management, the sibling sub-techniques T1578.002/.003/.004/.005, and the tooling anchor Pacu (S1091). Two terminal tasks test senior command judgement — one explicitly offers a destructive command as the tempting wrong answer, with a reduced per-exercise timer, because that is what a bridge call feels like.

This tier is the working reality of a senior incident commander, a principal detection engineer and a CTI lead briefing a CISO who needs a decision in ten minutes. If you can defend every call you make here, the technique is yours.

🔑 Keywords covered in this exam

Every exercise below is built from these concepts. They come directly from the ATT&CK page for T1578.001 and its linked objects.

Conflicting-signal analysis (AN1187)

  • Backup-window mimicry vs fresh-identity evidence — which signal wins and why
  • Automation context vs identity provenance (role created minutes before, from an anonymising IP)
  • Vendor-register trust vs contract staleness and unseen creation events
  • Incomplete information: cross-account reads you cannot see — reporting exposure honestly

Senior containment and cost

  • Least-costly action that stops the harm while preserving volatile evidence
  • Evidence ordering: read-only capture, share removal, identity revocation, preserve, clean, report
  • The destructive near-miss: delete/reset commands that look decisive and lose the case
  • Best-practice traps: controls that are right in general and useless at this stage

Coverage and detection engineering

  • Gap analysis over existing snapshot analytics (creation, sharing, mounting, RDS sharing)
  • Escalation into governance: M1047 Audit and M1018 User Account Management as findings
  • Executive reporting with exact ATT&CK identifiers

Tooling under pressure

  • Pacu (S1091) capability chain as the assumed attacker model
  • Read-only evidence commands vs state-changing commands on a live bridge
