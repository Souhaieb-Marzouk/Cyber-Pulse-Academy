ⓘ What you will learn here

T1683.002 — Generate Content: Audio-Visual Content — is a sub-technique of T1683 (Generate Content) in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042), platform PRE. It covers adversary creation or manipulation of audio, image, and video content: synthetic voice recordings, real-time altered audio or video during live interactions, fabricated profile photos and identity documents, and video of fabricated or impersonated individuals. Pure written content belongs to the sibling sub-technique T1683.001, and malicious code to T1587.

As a senior CTI analyst you will fingerprint visual assets — perceptual hashes, encoder quirks, icon sets — and cluster their reuse across sites and personas. Reused media is the thread that ties isolated lures into one campaign, and the report maps each cluster to T1683.002 and to the downstream technique it feeds, from phishing to financial theft, before anyone files a takedown.

As a senior IR lead you will make evidence-preserving decisions while a deepfake payment call is still live: freeze the payment rail, preserve recordings before retention purges them, and verify out-of-band with people, not pixels. The conflicting signals — a flawless call versus a broken timeline — resolve in favour of the process control, never the rendering.

As a detection strategist you will design layered controls: out-of-band call-backs, liveness checks, and a reporting culture, with AI detectors demoted to signals. Generation itself sits outside enterprise visibility, so detection effort lands downstream where the media does — at Initial Access and inside internal channels.

Takedown workflows close the loop: brief stakeholders with mapped clusters and confidence levels, file takedowns for confirmed abuse infrastructure, then keep watching the fingerprints, because reused assets tend to resurface under new names.
