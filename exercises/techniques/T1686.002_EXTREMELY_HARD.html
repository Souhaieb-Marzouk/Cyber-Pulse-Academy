What you will learn here

This is the EXTREMELY HARD tier of the T1686.002 — Network Device Firewall series. The object itself is a sub-technique of T1686 — Disable or Modify System Firewall (tactic: Defense Impairment, formerly Defense Evasion): an adversary disables network-device firewall mechanisms or adds, deletes, or modifies rules — ACLs, zones, policy entries — on routers, switches, and perimeter appliances so that previously blocked traffic flows. Sisters T1686.001 (cloud) and T1686.003 (Windows host) still matter for scope decisions.

Here you face senior-level decisions: 4+ artefacts per case, realistic noise, missing telemetry, and signals that contradict each other. The flagship incident mirrors real reporting: CVE-2024-55591, the FortiOS authentication bypass used against internet-exposed firewalls, and the 2025 Poland Wiper Attacks, whose operators modified FortiGate security settings over the native CLI and disabled network traffic logging. APT38 (ports 443/6443/8443/9443 exemptions), Cyclops Blink (iptables C2 ports), Grandoreiro (blocking a bank security tool), and Salt Typhoon (ACL additions for persistent access) all appear as intelligence anchors.

You will make the calls a senior incident commander, detection lead, and CTI analyst make under time pressure: which contradictory signal wins and why; which containment preserves evidence while production SCADA keeps running; whether a device with no logs is clean, compromised, or simply unknown; and when the correct answer is escalate and gather more evidence rather than act. At least one widely repeated "best practice" is wrong here — part of the test is recognising which one, and accepting the real business cost of the right call.

Keywords covered in this exam

Incident command under pressure

  • Conflicting signals: valid MFA logins vs administrators on leave; which evidence outranks which
  • Incomplete telemetry: nodes without auth logs are unknown, not clean and not compromised
