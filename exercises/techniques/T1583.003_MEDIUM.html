What you will learn here

You already know the basics: T1583.003 is the ATT&CK sub-technique that describes an adversary renting a Virtual Private Server during the Resource Development phase, before the attack touches the victim. In this MEDIUM exam you go one level deeper. You will combine two ideas at a time: recognising the behaviour and choosing what to do about it, reading real procedure examples from the ATT&CK page and mapping them to the right IDs, and connecting a rented server to the traffic it produces later in the Command and Control phase.

You will act in the roles that use this object every day: a SOC analyst triaging beacons towards provider-hosted servers, a CTI analyst attaching correct ATT&CK IDs to group reporting, a detection engineer working with the DET0838 scan-hunting idea, and a GRC analyst explaining why the M1056 Pre-compromise mitigation does not translate into one blocking control. You will read logs and scan-style artefacts, filter them like in a real SIEM, and type one small command in the fake shell to resolve an address back to a name. Everything uses plain language, and every wrong answer comes with an explanation you can learn from.
