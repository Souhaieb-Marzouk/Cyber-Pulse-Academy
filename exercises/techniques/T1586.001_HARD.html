ⓘ What you will learn here

You will learn T1586.001 — Compromise Accounts: Social Media Accounts — a sub-technique of T1586 under the Resource Development tactic (TA0042), platform PRE. It is about taking over social media accounts that already exist — LinkedIn, X/Twitter, Facebook, Telegram — instead of building new ones (that sibling is T1585.001). An account with a real history, real connections and real photos carries built-in trust, and that trust is the weapon: the hijacked persona is developed further and feeds later phases, most often Initial Access — Spearphishing via Service (T1566.003).

Where you meet it in real work: as a SOC analyst you triage the aftermath — a colleague's account suddenly editing its profile and sending connection requests to your staff. As a CTI analyst you track handles, avatars and bios across platforms to cluster one operation. As a brand-risk owner you run monitoring for personas claiming to work for your organisation (detection strategy DET0870, analytic AN2002), file platform reports and takedown requests, and keep corporate brand accounts on least privilege with strong 2FA. In IR you verify odd requests through internal channels before anyone opens anything. Red teamers study the same mechanics to show which of your accounts would be worth taking.

The takeover methods in scope: credential harvesting through Phishing for Information (T1598), credentials bought from third-party broker markets, brute forcing with passwords reused from breach dumps, and platform mechanics such as session theft and 2FA resets. Named examples on the ATT&CK page: G0065 Leviathan (APT40) compromised social media accounts for social engineering; G0034 Sandworm Team used credential-capture webpages against legitimate accounts. The hard limit to remember is mitigation M1056 Pre-compromise — an outsider's account on a third-party platform cannot be blocked by enterprise controls, so monitoring, verification culture and awareness do the compensating. This HARD unit tests all of it against realistic artefacts: monitor queues, profile exports, session logs and decision threads.

⚖ Keywords covered in this exam

Adversary behaviour

  • second-degree connection building — trust inherited through mutual contacts (C11)
  • multi-site personas — same actor across LinkedIn, X, Telegram (C14)
  • platform mechanics: session theft, 2FA resets, SIM-swap context (C30)
  • long-game conversations convert trust into disclosure (C13)

Real-world examples & lessons

  • Robin Sage — a fabricated persona with fake connections beat every technical control (C17)
  • HBGary — a hijacked corporate presence attacks its own audience (C18)
  • G0065 Leviathan (APT40) — compromised accounts as social-engineering infrastructure (C15)

Mitigations & defenses

  • employee MFA on personal accounts, takedown requests, platform reporting, awareness training (C24)
  • verification culture — internal-channel checks on odd 'colleague' requests (C25)
  • brand-account governance — least privilege, work/personal separation (C28)
  • AN2002 signals: employment-claim personas; recently modified accounts flooding requests (C21/C22)
