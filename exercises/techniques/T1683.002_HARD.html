ⓘ What you will learn here

You will learn T1683.002 — Generate Content: Audio-Visual Content — a sub-technique of T1683 (Generate Content) in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042), platform PRE. It covers how adversaries create or manipulate audio, image, and video to support targeting: synthetic voice recordings, real-time altered audio or video during live calls, fabricated profile photos and identity documents, and video of fabricated or impersonated individuals. Content is produced manually, with AI-assisted tools, or through third-party synthetic services — and AI lets a small team produce convincing media at scale that is harder to identify as inauthentic.

As a SOC analyst you will meet this technique where it lands, not where it is made: a cloned meeting app installed after a recruiter chat, a lookalike login screenshot in the mail queue, a suspicious 'executive' video inside a trusted internal channel. You will triage the host, preserve the artefacts, and hunt the delivery chain. As an IR lead you will handle live deepfake payment calls: freeze the rail first, verify out-of-band, and export evidence before retention purges it.

As a CTI analyst you will connect media behaviour to campaigns with documented procedure examples: Contagious Interview's AI-cloned video-conferencing apps, APT-C-36's banking login portal clones, NIMBUS MANTICORE's fake Outlook login prompts with decoy job files, and The Gentlemen's branded post-encryption wallpaper. As a detection engineer you will design the compensations the technique's only mitigation implies — content creation cannot be prevented, so you anchor on call-back procedures, out-of-band verification, liveness signals, and a reporting culture. As a red teamer you will learn why these pretexts work, so your engagements test the process controls, not just the filters.

The risk to carry through every exercise: seeing and hearing a person is not verification. The deliverables you will practice are triage verdicts, ranked response actions, policy lines, and ATT&CK-mapped hunt notes — the daily artefacts of a team that treats synthetic media as a resource-development problem with a downstream defence.
