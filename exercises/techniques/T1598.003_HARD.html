ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam page is one Sub-Technique page in MITRE ATT&CK: T1598.003, Spearphishing Link. It sits under the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information, in the Reconnaissance tactic. In this sub-technique an attacker sends a crafted email that carries a link plus text pushing the reader to click it or copy it. The link leads to a fake page that collects details the attacker wants about you or your organisation.

Attackers pick links because information is the cheapest way in. A cloned council-tax portal, a lookalike login page, a shortened URL or a QR code can capture account numbers, names, habits and passwords. That knowledge helps them pick targets, write better lures, or walk in later with stolen logins. The goal here is gathering, not breaking a machine on day one.

Defenders touch this work daily. SOC analysts triage reported mail, threat hunters run SIEM queries, mail gateway admins tune SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and incident responders contain clicks. CTI analysts map actors such as APT28 or Kimsuky, detection engineers write rules for the DET0878 strategy, and GRC owners check that the M1054 and M1017 controls really work.

You will meet this in ordinary tasks: sorting a user report, expanding a shortened link, reading gateway logs for one unusual sender, checking browser logs for homograph domains, briefing the CISO about a cloned portal, and deciding the least aggressive way to contain a click.

The risk does not stop at the click: captured credentials and session cookies often return later as T1078 Valid Accounts. This exam drills the harder skills: reading messy logs, finding the one signal inside noise, choosing controls that fit the exact abuse, and acting with the right amount of force. Hints cost a little credit, so spend them late.
