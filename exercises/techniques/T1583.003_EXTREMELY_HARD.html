What you will learn here

This is the top tier for T1583.003 — Acquire Infrastructure: Virtual Private Server, the ATT&CK sub-technique in which an adversary rents a VPS during Resource Development and later uses it for Command and Control, staging or exfiltration. Here you make the calls a senior analyst makes, with incomplete information, contradicting artefacts and real costs attached to every option. You will weigh a clean certificate on a suspicious provider against an ugly self-signed certificate on a reputable one; you will choose the evidence-preserving order of response while a production service is still earning money; you will separate one operator from two by reading scan data, certificates and registration dates together; and you will defend an attribution statement that refuses to overclaim.

The artefacts include noise on purpose: an irrelevant marketing connection, a privacy-protected registration, a missing log — exactly like real cases. The exercises assume you already know the basics: M1056 Pre-compromise and its limits, the DET0838 scan-hunting idea (listening services, certificates, SSL/TLS negotiation features), the procedure examples on the ATT&CK page such as NIMBUS MANTICORE's geolocation-reflecting Azure subdomains and the ORB control systems of FLORAHOX and SPACEHOP, and why address-based defences age out while behaviour-based hunts survive rotation. Take your time — the timer is generous, and every Check stops it so you can read the full reasoning.

Who makes these calls at work? The incident response lead owns the order of operations under business pressure. The SOC or CTI analyst owns the attribution statement and its confidence level. The detection engineer owns the hunting plan that survives infrastructure rotation. The red team lead pressures all of it with realistic adversary behaviour, and the GRC or risk owner signs off the residual risk that M1056 leaves behind. The exercises below put you in each of those chairs in turn.
