1What you will learn here

This is the HARD tier for T1647 — Plist File Modification, the macOS Technique in ATT&CK's Defense Impairment tactic (TA0005). At this level the exercises stop pointing at the answer. You get a stakeholder's demand and an artefact set — logs, tickets, signature output, ATT&CK excerpts — and you must decide what matters yourself.

The story follows a developer Mac, mbp-dev-42, where an XCSSET-style chain unfolds: an infected Xcode project is built, Info.plist keys change, a notarised Dock helper is abused, and the bundle is quietly re-signed. You will also touch Cuckoo Stealer (S1153) on a second host. These are the two real malware families ATT&CK records under T1647's procedure examples, so the scenario mirrors published behaviour, not fantasy.

You will exercise the judgement that separates senior work from keyword-spotting: near-miss commands that look almost right, alerts that look alarming but are benign, alerts that look routine but are not, and the discipline of choosing the least aggressive action that still fits the evidence. You will also tune the DET0109 detection strategy — its monitored directories, suspicious keys and time window — and weigh what mitigation M1013 (Hardened Runtime) actually does and does not stop.

Real work this maps to: an incident responder (the IR lead) structuring a macOS investigation, a detection engineer prioritising a backlog, a CTI analyst weaving procedure examples into a briefing, and a SOC analyst or team lead defending triage decisions in review. If you can defend your answers here, you can defend them on shift.
