🎓 What you will learn here

This is the HARD exam for T1591.002 — Business Relationships. The sub-technique is simple to state — collect the organisations around the victim — and hard to master, because in real intrusions the relationship map is where attacks get quiet: the trusted vendor’s identity, the MSP’s standing access, the update channel signed by someone everybody believes.

Here you work the map the way senior analysts do. You will separate Supply Chain Compromise (T1195) from Trusted Relationship (T1199) with evidence, not vibes; you will read a mail gateway log and spot the vendor costume; you will weigh the least-aggressive response when a partner compromise is suspected but unconfirmed. ATT&CK’s procedure cards — Dragonfly’s relationship mapping, LAPSUS$’s supply-chain knowledge, Sandworm Team’s Olympics partner-site research — appear as evidence to reason from, not trivia to recite.

The defender’s side gets equal weight: subprocessor pages as double-edged disclosures, third-party registers as ground truth against public pages, vendor verification protocols that survive an attacker who has read your website. Expect the HARD traps in their natural habitat: the alarming 03:00 vendor traffic that is a contracted patch window, the benign-looking Slack guest who is harvesting VPN process documentation, and the best practice that fails in this specific estate.
