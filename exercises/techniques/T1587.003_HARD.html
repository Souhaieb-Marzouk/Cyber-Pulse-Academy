What you will learn here

This exam is about the padlock and what it does not mean. In MITRE ATT&CK, T1587.003 sits under T1587 (Develop Capabilities) in the Resource Development tactic on the PRE platform: the adversary creates self-signed SSL/TLS certificates for infrastructure it controls — before any traffic reaches you. A certificate carries a key, an owner identity and a verifying signature; a self-signed one lacks the trust that a third-party certificate authority adds.

You will practise reading TLS certificates in proxy and NDR records — subject, issuer, SAN, validity, fingerprint — and telling develop (the adversary creates its own certificate, T1587.003) apart from obtain (it buys one or gets a free CA-issued certificate, T1588.004). You will weigh the trade-offs on both sides: self-signed certificates leave no transparency trail but trigger browser warnings, while CA-issued ones are logged publicly and trusted silently.

The roles in this exam are the CTI analyst pivoting on a reused fingerprint, the SOC analyst triaging self-signed outbound TLS, the incident responder who blocks infrastructure and pins known-good certificates, and the detection engineer who alerts on the highest-value follow-on tell: a root certificate being installed (T1553.004). Their real decisions are your scenarios.

ATT&CK is honest about the limit: no data sources are listed, the only mitigation is M1056 Pre-compromise, and detection focuses on related behaviours — Web Protocols (T1071.001), Asymmetric Cryptography, and Install Root Certificate. The page's procedure examples — APT29's mutual TLS, Gamaredon's certificate reuse, Storm-0501's name-mimicry — show what the develop side looks like in the wild.
