Severity: ◈ EASY

🎓What you will learn here

Welcome! This exam teaches you one MITRE ATT&CK object: T1091 — Replication Through Removable Media. In ATT&CK, a technique is a named way that attackers do something real. T1091 is the technique where an attacker puts malware on a removable device — most often a USB drive — so the malware moves to another computer when that device is used. In the ATT&CK matrix it sits in the Lateral Movement tactic and also appears in the Initial Access tactic, and it lists Windows as its platform.

Imagine an office that keeps its most important computers air-gapped — that means those computers are not connected to the office network or the internet at all. Network attacks cannot reach them. But a person can still plug in a USB drive. If the drive carries malware and Windows runs it automatically (a feature called Autorun), the malware jumps across the air gap. That is the problem T1091 describes.

You will meet this technique in many real jobs. A SOC analyst sees its alerts on a shift and writes tickets. An incident responder checks which PCs were touched by the same USB drive. A threat hunter searches logs for autorun.inf files and files copied to drives. A CTI analyst reads ATT&CK pages like this one to name attacker behaviour in reports. A detection engineer builds alerts from the detection strategy on the page. A GRC analyst writes the USB policy that lowers the risk. This exam walks you through all of those ideas in simple words.
