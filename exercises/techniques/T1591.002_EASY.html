🎓 What you will learn here

You will learn the ATT&CK sub-technique T1591.002 — Business Relationships. A sub-technique is a smaller chapter under a bigger technique. Its parent is T1591 Gather Victim Org Information, and its home is the Reconnaissance tactic. T1591.002 describes one specific collection goal: learning which other organisations are connected to the victim — partners, vendors, suppliers, contractors, managed service providers (MSPs), and customers.

Why would an attacker care? Imagine a company that quietly lists on its website: "Our IT is fully managed by Kimton RMM." That one sentence tells an attacker something precious: the company probably gives Kimton remote-administration tools and trusted logins. ATT&CK spells this out: these second- and third-party organisations have connected — and potentially elevated — network access. The same sub-technique also covers supply chains: who ships the hardware, whose software runs the victim, and which paths products travel. ATT&CK even lists a real case: before the 2018 Winter Olympics attack, Sandworm Team researched the partner organisations shown on the official PyeongChang partnership page.

You will use this sub-technique in many roles. SOC analysts see its results when a fake "vendor invoice" arrives. Third-party risk teams review exactly the pages attackers read. CTI analysts map groups like Dragonfly (open-source research on organisation relationships) and LAPSUS$ (detailed supply-chain knowledge). Red teamers plan trusted-partner paths with it. Awareness teams teach staff that "a vendor you know" is not proof the message is real.

In this exam you will learn to name what T1591.002 collects, recognise the public pages that leak it, and connect it to the attack paths it enables — especially Supply Chain Compromise (T1195) and Trusted Relationship (T1199).

Object basics

  • T1591.002 = Business Relationships (sub-technique of T1591)
  • Reconnaissance tactic (TA0043)
  • Collects: partners, vendors, MSPs, contractors, suppliers, customers
  • Why it matters: partners may hold connected, elevated access

Public pages that leak it

  • Partner / "our technology partners" pages
  • Customer case studies
  • Press releases announcing deals
  • Social media posts thanking vendors

What it enables next

  • Trusted Relationship abuse (T1199)
  • Supply Chain Compromise (T1195)
  • Drive-by Compromise via partner ad networks (T1189)
  • Vendor-identity phishing

Real procedures

  • Sandworm Team (G0034): researched the PyeongChang Olympics partner site
  • Dragonfly (G0035): OSINT on relationships between organisations
  • LAPSUS$ (G1004): detailed knowledge of supply-chain relationships

Defence & detection

  • M1056 Pre-compromise: minimise what partner details are public
  • Direct detection is hard: activity happens outside your visibility
  • Watch downstream: vendor-impersonation messages and unexpected vendor access
