ⓘWhat you will learn here

This exam is about one page in MITRE ATT&CK: the sub-technique T1598.002, Phishing for Information: Spearphishing Attachment. Its parent is T1598, Phishing for Information, which sits in the Reconnaissance tactic, TA0043. In plain words: an attacker sends a spearphishing email with an attachment so that a chosen person will fill in a form and return it, or will sign in on a fake page hidden inside the attachment. The goal is information, not code execution.

Attackers like this technique because it is cheap and quiet. One well-written email can collect credentials, organisation details, or contact lists that make every later step easier. Many lures copy a real business relationship, like a supplier questionnaire or a compliance form, and urgency or authority pushes the reply. The data collected here is used for targeting, and stolen sign-ins often come back later as valid accounts.

Defenders meet this technique in several roles. A SOC analyst triages the alert, a threat hunter looks for unusual clusters, an incident responder preserves evidence and runs containment, a CTI analyst checks actor patterns, a detection engineer writes and tunes rules, a mail gateway admin manages SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and a GRC control owner answers for the policy. The tools are the SIEM, the email gateway console, a sandbox for attachments, EDR for follow-on actions, and the ticketing system.

You will meet this technique in daily tasks: triaging reported emails, reading gateway logs and message traces, writing and tuning detection rules, deciding what to quarantine or purge, briefing the CISO when leaders demand instant action, and reading threat-intel reports about actors such as Star Blizzard and SideCopy.

The risk is clear: this is often the earliest visible step of an attack, before any Initial Access. If you delete the wrong thing you lose the evidence a regulator expects; if you wait too long, a lure stays one click away in an executive inbox. This exam drills exactly those hard calls: conflicting signals, noisy logs, missing data, and decisions that carry a signed business cost.
