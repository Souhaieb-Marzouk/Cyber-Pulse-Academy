🧭 What you will learn here

Welcome to the third tier for T1222.002 — Linux and Mac Permissions, the ATT&CK sub-technique (child of T1222) that covers changing file and directory permissions and attributes on Linux and macOS. At this level the questions stop being “what is this command?” and start being “which of two good answers is right here, and why?”. Expect near-miss options that would be correct in a different context, artefact sets that must be read together, and at least one moment where the obvious answer is the wrong one.

The behaviour you are drilling: an adversary already on a host uses chmod, chown, chattr, chflags or setfacl to weaken access controls — making payloads executable, setting SUID bits for root powers, hiding or locking files, blinding monitoring, or opening paths the ACLs had closed. ATT&CK files it under Defense Impairment (TA0005) and its detection strategy DET0351 chains process creation, syscalls, metadata and timing.

How this maps to real work: a detection engineer writes auditd analytics with arch and mode masks — and one wrong flag silences the rule. A threat hunter separates evasion (lock, hide, blind) from persistence (SUID, locked LaunchAgents). An incident responder must restore service without destroying the permission evidence — the least-aggressive action wins. A CTI analyst maps real actors to the behaviour: APT32 chmods its macOS backdoor to 755, APT38 used chmod 777, Rocke locked its files, TeamTNT used chattr, Black Basta’s binary chmods targeted files. A red teamer learns why quiet modes beat loud ones. Every explanation gives the why — read it even when you are right.
