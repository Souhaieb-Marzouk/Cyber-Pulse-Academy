What you will learn here

You will learn what T1608.004 — Drive-by Target means in MITRE ATT&CK. It is a Sub-Technique of T1608 Stage Capabilities, and it sits in the Resource Development tactic (TA0042) on the PRE platform. That means the work happens before any victim appears: the adversary prepares a website so that a later, completely normal visit can infect the visitor's browser or profile the visitor's system. Profiling here means the staged code silently reads the visitor's browser and system details first.

The heart of this technique is the word no-click. A visitor only has to land on the prepared page with a normal browser — the staged content goes to work on its own. This is the opposite of staged links, where the victim must actively click a link in a message; ATT&CK keeps that idea in the sibling sub-technique T1608.005 Link Target.

ATT&CK names three staging methods. One: inserting malicious scripts into web pages or into user-controllable content such as forum posts. Two: modifying script files that many websites load from publicly writeable cloud storage buckets — one edit, many victims. Three: crafting malicious web advertisements and buying ad space through legitimate ad providers, a trick called malvertising. Attackers often hide the payload in an iframe — an invisible window inside the page that quietly loads a second site — and the staged code is usually JavaScript.

When the prepared site is one that a specific community — a government body, an industry, a region — visits every week, ATT&CK calls the campaign a strategic web compromise, better known as a watering hole: few victims, but very well chosen. Adversaries may also stage profiling scripts first, for example the ScanBox framework, so the expensive exploit is only spent on browsers that are truly vulnerable.

This material is daily work for many roles. A SOC analyst reads proxy and web logs for unexpected external scripts after a normal page load. A CTI analyst tracks watering-hole domains and warns the community. A threat hunter scans the organisation's own sites for injected content. An incident responder cleans up after drive-by events. A detection engineer documents what staged pages look like, and a web admin hardens writeable buckets and ad slots. In this exam you will practise the fundamentals: the definition, the no-click difference, the watering-hole idea, page-source signs, the one-line mitigation M1056, the reasons for staging, the three methods, and the T1608 family.
