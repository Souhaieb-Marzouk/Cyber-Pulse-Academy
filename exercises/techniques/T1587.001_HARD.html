What you will learn here

This exam trains one narrow question: how does an adversary build its own malicious software, and what does that mean for the people who defend networks? In the MITRE ATT&CK model, T1587.001 sits under T1587 (Develop Capabilities) in the Resource Development tactic, on the PRE platform — the phase before an adversary ever touches your systems.

You will practise telling develop apart from obtain: writing a custom backdoor is T1587.001, while buying a ransomware builder on a forum is T1588.001 (Obtain Capabilities: Malware). You will read short intelligence artefacts — sample reports, build artefacts, ATT&CK excerpts — and decide what they prove, what they only suggest, and what an adversary may have placed deliberately to mislead you.

The roles that use this page daily are cyber-threat-intelligence analysts who cluster samples and track families, SOC analysts and threat hunters who blunt the deployed malware, incident responders who expect fast variants after detection, and detection engineers who write the rules. The exam walks you through their real decisions, from a first triage to a court-ready report.

ATT&CK is honest about the limit: the development itself happens outside the target’s visibility, the page lists no data sources, and the only listed mitigation is M1056 Pre-compromise. Detection therefore aims at the payload’s fingerprints — compilers, debugging artifacts, watermarks, compilation times — and at what the malware does after delivery.
