ⓘWhat you will learn here

In this exam you will learn about MITRE ATT&CK sub-technique T1598.003, called Spearphishing Link. It sits under the parent technique T1598, Phishing for Information. In plain words, an attacker sends an email with a link, plus text that pushes the user to click or copy the URL. The link leads to a fake sign-in page or a tracking tool, not to an attached file.

Attackers like links because they are cheap, quick to change, and easy to hide. A click can deliver sign-in details, confirm a live mailbox, or profile the person behind it. The attacker uses this information to plan the real attack, which often comes weeks later.

Defenders touch this every day. SOC analysts triage reported emails in the SIEM and the mail gateway console. Threat hunters expand links in sandbox tools, incident responders read portal access logs, and detection engineers tune the gateway rules. Helpdesk agents and GRC owners keep the training and controls alive.

You will meet this in daily work: triaging a user report, writing a rule for lookalike URLs, briefing staff after a click, or reading a threat-intel report. Each exercise below is a small ticket from one week at Bluefin Retail, worked by SOC analyst Leo Tran and helpdesk agent Amy Cole.

The risk is simple: one click can hand over a password or tell the attacker that your inbox is active. The exam drills the basics: the tactic and the parent technique, lure text and its levers, URL tricks and tracking pixels, safe link handling, and the calm response after a click. Master these and the harder severities will feel familiar.
