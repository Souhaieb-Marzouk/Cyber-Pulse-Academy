🧭 What you will learn here

This is the senior tier for T1222.002 — Linux and Mac Permissions, the ATT&CK sub-technique for changing file and directory permissions and attributes on Linux and macOS. Everything here mirrors a decision a senior responder, senior detection engineer or CTI lead actually makes: several concepts chained together, at least one conflicting signal you must resolve, evidence with noise and gaps, and options where two or three answers are defensible somewhere else — but only one is right in this world.

The technical core is unchanged: chmod, chown, chattr, chflags, setfacl weakening access controls — SUID doors, locked persistence, hidden LaunchAgents, blinded monitoring. What changes is the altitude. You will weigh evidence preservation against service restoration (someone always wants the host back now), decide when the correct answer is “escalate, change nothing yet”, catch the moment a popular best practice becomes the wrong move, and read the ATT&CK detection strategy DET0351 — the Linux chain AN0998 and the macOS chain AN0999 — as a working engineer, not a reader.

Real work this tier prepares you for: leading the permission-tampering workstream of an incident (capture → verify telemetry → map → restore → report with confidence levels), writing the attribution paragraph without overclaiming (the procedure table — APT32, APT38, Rocke, TeamTNT, Black Basta, COATHANGER, Bundlore, Dok — is pattern material, not proof), timing CIS-style hardening so it does not destroy live evidence, and finding the coverage gap in your macOS telemetry before an attacker does. Read every artefact. The traps are patient.
