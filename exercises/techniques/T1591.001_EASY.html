🎓 What you will learn here

You will learn the ATT&CK sub-technique T1591.001 — Determine Physical Locations. It is a child page (a sub-technique) of T1591 Gather Victim Org Information, and it lives in the Reconnaissance tactic. It describes one collection goal: learning where the victim organisation physically is — offices, plants, data centers, warehouses — including where key resources and infrastructure are housed.

Why does "where" matter to an attacker? Three plain reasons. First, lures feel local: an email that mentions the Rotterdam depot reads like it came from inside the company. Second, jurisdiction: ATT&CK notes that physical locations can indicate what legal jurisdiction and authorities the victim operates within — useful for choosing where an attack is safest or most profitable. Third, physical follow-ups: a known address supports site-focused attacks, from tailored phishing to — per ATT&CK — initial access via Hardware Additions (T1200), where a malicious device is delivered to a site.

The sources are the most public ones that exist: the contact page with the registered office, the careers page advertising the new depot, press releases about grand openings, map listings, and employees posting photos with their office in the background. ATT&CK documents a striking real case: Magic Hound collected location information from visitors to its own phishing sites — recon running on the attacker’s infrastructure, invisible to the victim.

In your work: facility security teams defend the sites this technique maps; GRC teams review how much address detail is published; CTI analysts cite it when lures quote sites; red teamers plan legal site reconnaissance with it. The mitigation is the family standard, M1056 Pre-compromise: publish deliberately, minimise what is not required.

Object basics

  • T1591.001 = Determine Physical Locations (sub-technique of T1591)
  • Reconnaissance tactic (TA0043)
  • Collects: offices, sites, facilities, where resources are housed
  • Locations can reveal the legal jurisdiction and authorities involved

Public sources of location data

  • Contact / terms pages with registered addresses
  • Careers adverts naming sites
  • Press releases about openings and construction
  • Map listings and social check-ins

What it enables next

  • Location-flavoured spearphishing (T1566)
  • Hardware Additions (T1200) — devices delivered to sites
  • Develop/Obtain Capabilities informed by jurisdiction (T1587/T1588)

Real procedure

  • Magic Hound (G0059): collected location information from visitors to its phishing sites

Defence & detection

  • M1056 Pre-compromise: minimise address detail where not legally required
  • Direct detection is hard: reading maps happens outside your estate
  • Watch downstream: lures quoting sites, unexpected visitor or delivery patterns
