What you will learn here

You will learn what T1597.002 — Purchase Technical Data is. Imagine an attacker with a wallet. They can buy information about a victim from two kinds of shops: reputable ones — paid subscriptions to scan-database feeds and data aggregation services — and less reputable ones — dark web forums and cybercrime black markets. What do they buy? Employee contact information, credentials, and specifics about the victim's infrastructure, like VPN portals or IP ranges.

This page is a Sub-Technique of T1597 Search Closed Sources, in the Reconnaissance tactic (TA0043) on the PRE platform — everything happens before any malware touches the victim. An attacker can buy data about a target they already chose, or browse purchased data to discover new opportunities. The framework page gives a real example: LAPSUS$ (G1004) purchased credentials and session tokens from criminal underground forums — session tokens are interesting because they can bypass multi-factor authentication when replayed.

In real work, an incident responder asks "where did those credentials come from?" when a login looks strange. A SOC analyst watches the next stage instead, because this one is invisible to us (detection strategy DET0880, analytic AN2012: very high occurrence and false-positive rate, outside our visibility — focus detection on Initial Access). An IAM team runs credential-exposure monitoring and fast revocation. The linked mitigation is M1056 Pre-compromise: minimise the amount and sensitivity of data available to external parties.
