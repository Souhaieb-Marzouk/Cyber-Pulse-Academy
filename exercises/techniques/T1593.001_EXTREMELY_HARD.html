💡 What you will learn here

This is part of the T1593.001 — Social Media exam series. Imagine you are a new SOC analyst. Before any alarm rings, an attacker has already started working — and one of their first tools is not malware. It is a web browser. In this exam you will learn about a real technique from the MITRE ATT&CK framework: T1593.001 — Search Open Websites/Domains: Social Media. MITRE ATT&CK is a public catalogue that describes what real attackers do, step by step. This object is a sub-technique, which means it is a specific child of a bigger technique (T1593 — Search Open Websites/Domains). Its job in the catalogue: adversaries search social media for information about a victim organisation — things like business announcements, and the roles, locations, and interests of staff.

You will see where this fits in an attack. It belongs to the Reconnaissance tactic — the "gathering information" phase that happens before the attacker touches your systems. That is why ATT&CK marks its platform as PRE: the activity happens outside your network, on sites like LinkedIn, X (Twitter), and Telegram. Attackers can work passively (just reading public pages) or actively (creating fake profiles to talk to your staff and pull out details). Real groups do this: Contagious Interview contacted victims through LinkedIn, X, and Telegram; EXOTIC LILY copied social media data to impersonate people; Kimsuky used Twitter to watch targets; and in Operation Dream Job, Lazarus used LinkedIn to pick employees to target.

This object is used in real work every day. Threat-intel (CTI) analysts map job-offer lure campaigns to it in their reports. SOC analysts learn why their SIEM cannot see the searching itself — and why detection guidance (DET0812) says to focus on later stages, like Initial Access. Brand-monitoring and IR teams hunt fake profiles that pretend to be employees or recruiters. Red teamers run a controlled social-media sweep in the OSINT phase of an engagement. HR and communications learn data minimization: the less roles, locations, and interests you publish, the less an attacker can use — because the official mitigation (M1056 — Pre-compromise) says plainly that you cannot prevent the searching; you can only reduce the amount and sensitivity of data available to external parties.

By the end of this exam you will be able to recognise this technique in evidence, name what it is and is not, place it on the ATT&CK matrix, and choose the defences that actually work against it.
