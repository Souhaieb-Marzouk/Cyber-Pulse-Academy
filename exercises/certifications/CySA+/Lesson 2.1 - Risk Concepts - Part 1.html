Certified Risk Analysis Practitioner — CRAP‑1

You have just been hired as a Risk Analyst in the Security Governance team at HeliosPay, a mid-size payment company. This exam is your first working week: a risk register, a SOC ticket queue, an incident timeline, a vendor notification and a terminal full of logs. Every exercise is real work, not a quiz.

⚑ What this exam tests

The full risk cycle from your source material: identify risk by examining processes, technologies and activities, evaluate each risk against the organization's risk appetite, and recommend how to handle it — including data breaches and unauthorized access — when resources are limited.

⏱ Time and pausing

The clock gives you 50 minutes — about 2 minutes per exercise. A thin bar under the header shows time used, not score. If you need a break, press Pause: the timer stops and the current exercise is hidden, so a pause cannot be used to answer with a clear head.

💡 Hints cost you

Stuck? Every unanswered exercise has a Hint button. A hint gives you a nudge, never the answer, and it costs −10% of your final score: two hints means 20% lost out of a maximum of 100%. Use them only when you are truly blocked.

Exam mechanics

  • 1
    25 exercises, 100 points. Branching scenarios, drag & drop, matching, timeline ordering, a log viewer with a filter box, a fake terminal where you type a real search, risk maths, and multiple‑choice as a minority of the mix.
  • 2
    Pass mark: 80 points (80%). The header shows points and score live. Nothing turns green for "passing" early — you only pass on the final screen.
  • 3
    Two kinds of scoring. Drag & drop exercises score instantly as you place items. Every other exercise has a Lock answer & grade button — only the lock reveals the explanation.
  • 4
    Every exercise is explained in plain English (B1 level): why the right answer is right, why the tempting one is wrong, and what to focus on if you missed it.
  • 5
    Full report at the end. Score, pass or fail, per‑theme breakdown, a study plan and a question‑by‑question review of your answers.
  • 6
    Retake resets and re‑shuffles everything: question order, answer options, word banks and the starting order of every sorting exercise. You cannot memorise positions.
  • 7
    Safe indicators. Every indicator of compromise is defanged: hxxps://evil[.]example, 203[.]0[.]113[.]77. This is professional habit, not decoration.
  • 8
    Keyboard and touch work too. Press Enter on a tile to pick it up, Tab to a zone, Enter to drop. Options and choices also answer on Enter.

Keywords covered — every one of them is exercised at least once

These are the valuable terms pulled from the source text. A chip turns green only when you earn full marks on an exercise that covers it, so the panel doubles as a coverage check on your own revision.

Exercise types in this exam

The counts are read from the exercise data when the page loads, so they always match what you will actually get.

Decision tree

An incident path where your choice unlocks the next step. Scored on the whole path, not one click.

Drag & drop

Sort items into threat / vulnerability / event, into risk families, and into mitigate / transfer / accept.

Timeline ordering

Rebuild an attack chain from the first scan to the confirmed data theft.

Prioritization ranking

Rank a risk register when only one control fits the budget.

Log viewer

Filter a noisy authentication stream and spot the one line that proves a break‑in.

Terminal emulator

Read the raw evidence files, then type the SIEM search that answers the question.

Matching

Match terms to their definitions, and four risks to their correct treatment.

Fill in the blank

Rebuild the company definition of risk and a board recommendation from a shuffled word bank.

Spot the mistake

Find the ticket that cannot be triaged and the register entry whose number cannot be justified.

Dual perspective

The same weakness seen from the red team (how to abuse it) and the blue team (how to catch it).

Risk calculator

Compute inherent and residual risk, then choose the control with the best return.

Choice questions

Single‑answer and pick‑exactly‑the‑right‑set questions with near‑miss distractors.
