MEDIUM practice exam — Objective 1.6: Transmission Media

30 exercises · pass mark 70% · total timer 75 minutes (2.30 min min per exercise on average).

Scope: wireless transmission media — 802.11 Wi-Fi, cellular and satellite — and wired media — fiber optic, coaxial and direct-attach copper (DAC). Every keyword of the chapter is exercised. All indicators are defanged on purpose (for example 10[.]20[.]30[.]40) — that is a real analyst habit.

How this exam works

  • Blocks: each exercise shows a SCENARIO block, then a QUESTION block, then the ANSWERS area. You never have to guess what is being asked.
  • Check validates your answer, marks each element (green correct, red wrong, amber missed or partial), shows the explanation, and pauses the timer in review mode. The exercise stays fully visible. Only Next resumes the clock.
  • Skip jumps to the next exercise and flags this one amber. Skipped exercises are excluded from scoring (neither earned nor possible points). You can return to any skipped or answered exercise until time runs out or you submit.
  • Manual Pause stops the clock but hides the exercise behind a screen so you cannot study while paused. The two pause types can stack.
  • Hint (once per exercise) reveals a nudge, never the answer, and costs −10% of that exercise's points.
  • Partial credit: multi-answer exercises (drag & drop, matching, ordering, multi-select, fills, log-line picking) score per correct element. Wrong extras earn nothing and cost nothing. Single-answer exercises are all-or-nothing.
  • Retake reshuffles questions and choices and resets everything.

Exercise types you will meet

Drag & drop · channel planner · branching scenarios · log viewer with filter · click-the-line 'spot the mistake' · matching · word-bank fills · typed answers · guided terminal · latency ranking · ordering · single-choice and multi-select questions.

Keywords covered — 1.6 Transmission Media

Every keyword below is exercised by this exam.

Wi-Fi (802.11)

  • 802.11a / b / g legacy standards
  • 802.11n (Wi-Fi 4) — MIMO, dual band
  • 802.11ac (Wi-Fi 5) — MU-MIMO downlink, 5 GHz
  • 802.11ax (Wi-Fi 6/6E) — OFDMA, TWT, BSS coloring
  • 802.11be (Wi-Fi 7) — 320 MHz, MLO
  • 2.4 / 5 / 6 GHz bands
  • Non-overlapping channels 1 / 6 / 11
  • Channel width and bonding (20–320 MHz)
  • CSMA/CA
  • DFS radar detection and AFC
  • RSSI and dBm
  • SNR and noise floor
  • Interference (microwave oven, co-channel)
  • Antennas: omnidirectional, Yagi, patch
  • Point-to-point wireless bridge
  • WPA2 / WPA3 / WEP
  • Roaming and hysteresis
  • Site survey and heat map

Cellular

  • 3G UMTS (legacy)
  • 4G LTE
  • LTE-Advanced and carrier aggregation
  • 5G mmWave vs sub-6 GHz (n77)
  • Network slicing and private 5G
  • SIM and eSIM
  • Hotspot and tethering
  • RSRP signal strength (dBm)
  • Data caps and throttling

Satellite

  • GEO / MEO / LEO orbits
  • VSAT terminals
  • C / Ku / Ka / L bands
  • Rain fade
  • Line of sight and dish alignment
  • Uplink and downlink
  • Latency by orbit (≈600 ms GEO, 20–40 ms LEO)

Fiber optic

  • Single-mode (OS2, laser, 1310/1550 nm)
  • Multimode (VCSEL, 850 nm)
  • OM1–OM5 grades
  • Modal dispersion
  • LC / SC / ST / MPO-MTP connectors
  • SFP / SFP+ / QSFP+ / GBIC transceivers
  • 1000BASE-SX / 1000BASE-LX
  • 10GBASE-SR / 10GBASE-LR
  • 40GBASE-SR4 / 40GBASE-CR4
  • Attenuation and dB loss budget
  • Bend radius and macrobend
  • Dark fiber, CWDM / DWDM
  • EMI immunity and tap resistance
  • Inspection, cleaning, OTDR testing
  • Aerial and underground plant
  • Media converter

Coaxial

  • RG-6 / RG-11 / RG-59 / RG-58
  • F-type and BNC connectors
  • 75 Ω vs 50 Ω impedance
  • DOCSIS upstream SNR
  • HFC (hybrid fiber-coax)
  • MoCA over home coax
  • Shielding and grounding

DAC & copper

  • Twinaxial DAC (direct-attach copper)
  • Passive vs active DAC
  • 10GBASE-CR
  • AOC (active optical cable)
  • Top-of-rack reach (≤7 m)
  • Cat6a / 10GBASE-T
  • Plenum-rated jackets

Cross-cutting

  • Latency by media type
  • dB vs dBm
  • Media selection: cost, EMI, distance, security
  • Media hardening: isolation, VPN, monitoring
