Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

CVSS stands for Common Vulnerability Scoring System. It is a standard way to give a software weakness a severity score from zero to ten. Analysts use the score to compare many weaknesses at once. The score describes the flaw itself, not how much the flaw matters to your company.

Why should you care?

Most teams have more weaknesses to fix than time to fix them. A shared score helps different tools and vendors describe severity in the same words. But a number by itself can mislead you. A high score on an unreachable system may be less urgent than a medium score on a public server. Use the score as one input, and never as the whole decision.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. An analyst studies how an attacker reaches the flaw, over the network or only on the machine.
  2. They record what access an attacker needs before the flaw can be used.
  3. They note what the flaw affects, such as data secrecy, data honesty or uptime.
  4. Those answers together produce a base score somewhere between zero and ten.
  5. Extra settings adjust the score for how easy the flaw is to use in practice.
  6. A local adjustment raises or lowers the score for the value of the affected system.

Real-world example

Imagine a hospital triage desk with a long queue of patients. A nurse gives each patient a number that describes how serious the injury looks. That number helps the team decide who is seen first. But the nurse also knows the number is not the whole story. A broken finger on the only surgeon in the building matters more than the same finger on a visitor. The number from the triage rule is the base score. The local judgement about the person is the environmental view. If you read only the number, you may treat the wrong patient first. Teams that rank work by score alone make exactly that mistake.

Key takeaways

  • A high severity score does not always mean the most urgent work for you.
  • Combine the score with what you know about the affected system.
  • Record the metrics you used, so another analyst can repeat your score.

In more detail

The Common Vulnerability Scoring System (CVSS) is an open framework for describing the technical severity of a software vulnerability on a scale from zero to ten. It does not measure business risk on its own; it scores qualities such as how a flaw is reached, what access it requires, and what it affects. A base score captures those intrinsic traits, while temporal and environmental metrics let an organisation adjust for exploit maturity and for how much the affected asset matters locally. The frequent mistake is treating the base score as a decision: a very high score on an isolated test box is not the same problem as a moderate score on an internet-facing payment service. Scoring is a shared language for comparison, not a substitute for asset context and business impact.

What you will practise

  • Explain what the Common Vulnerability Scoring System measures and what it leaves out.
  • Describe how base metrics and local adjustments differ in purpose.
  • Use a severity score together with asset criticality to set priorities.
  • Identify situations where a score alone would give a misleading answer.
  • Explain a vulnerability score to a non-technical colleague in plain words.
