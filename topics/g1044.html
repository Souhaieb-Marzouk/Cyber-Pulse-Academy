G1044 APT42

G1044Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

APT42 is a group of spies who attack through messages and fake websites. APT means advanced persistent threat, which is a team that keeps trying for a long time. Experts link this group to Iran and to other names, including Charming Kitten and Mint Sandstorm.

Why should you care?

This group does not smash down the door. It talks to you first and earns your trust. A single stolen password can open mail, cloud files and chat history all at once. People who study politics, write for newspapers or defend human rights are common targets. Learning the pattern helps you doubt the polite request that arrives on a very busy day.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. They pick people whose mail or contacts are interesting to a government.
  2. They build a fake identity that looks like a colleague, a journalist or a recruiter.
  3. They talk with you for days or weeks, using small requests to look harmless.
  4. They send a link to a page that copies a real login screen.
  5. They use the stolen password to read mail and set a rule that hides their access.
  6. If that fails, they try a small hidden program or a tool on your phone.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital night desk with one visitor badge. A stranger in a delivery uniform chats with the clerk for a week, always friendly, always brief. One night he asks the clerk to open the drug store room because his hands are full. The badge looked real and the request was small. The trust came first, the theft came later, and the clerk never had to be forced.

Key takeaways

  • Trust building is part of the attack, so be careful with slow, friendly strangers.
  • Turn on multi factor authentication, because a stolen password alone should not be enough.
  • Review mail rules and app permissions, since attackers hide inside them.

In more detail

APT42 is an Iranian state linked espionage group. Public reporting associates it with targeting that supports Iranian government interests, and researchers note overlaps with activity also tracked as Charming Kitten and Mint Sandstorm. Its tradecraft is social rather than purely technical: long term credential harvesting against cloud mail and collaboration services, extended rapport building with journalists, academics, policy staff and dissidents, and occasional in person meetings. Campaigns commonly run from attacker controlled domains and, more recently, from hosted services, which lets phishing links appear on trusted platforms and slip past mail filtering. Where password theft fails, reporting describes custom backdoors and mobile malware being used instead. Evidence is thin and distributed: authentication logs from unexpected countries, mailbox rules, OAuth consent grants, proxy and sign in logs, and the phishing domains themselves. Attribution rests on infrastructure reuse and malware analysis and is published with varying confidence rather than as proven fact.

What you will practise

  • Define social engineering and explain how long term rapport building enables credential theft.
  • Identify cloud authentication artifacts that reveal an account takeover, such as mail rules and consent grants.
  • Explain how hosting phishing pages on trusted services weakens link based email filtering.
  • Describe why state linked espionage targeting follows certain professions and communities.
  • Assess the confidence level of public attribution for an espionage group and state its limits.
