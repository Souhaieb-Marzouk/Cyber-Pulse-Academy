T1568.002 Dynamic Resolution: Domain Generation Algorithms

T1568.002Command and ControlExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Dynamic resolution means malware works out its contact address instead of storing one. This version does it by generating many possible domain names from a changing value. The malware tries them one after another until one of them works. The attacker only needs to own one of those names at a time.

Why should you care?

Static blocklists fail against this method, because the names are not known in advance. The malware can keep trying for months and reconnect after every takedown. It also creates a lot of noise in name resolution, and that noise is a useful early warning if you watch for it. Ignoring the signal means losing your easiest chance to spot an infection.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The malware and the attacker's server share the same domain generating routine and the same inputs.
  2. The routine uses a changing value, such as the current date or a hidden number, as its starting point.
  3. It produces a list of names for today, and the malware asks the name system about each one.
  4. Most of those names do not exist, so the name server answers that there is no such name.
  5. The attacker registered only one or two of them, and that answer begins the control channel.
  6. Defenders spot a burst of failed lookups from one computer and then a single success.

Real-world example

Think of two people who use the same puzzle book. Every day they solve the same puzzle and get a different word, then meet wherever that word points. Only a few of those words are real places. A watcher cannot guess tomorrow's word, but they can notice that one person keeps asking for directions to places that do not exist. That is the clue the malware leaves.

Key takeaways

  • Many failed name lookups followed by one success is the pattern to hunt for.
  • Look at the resolved domain's age and reputation, not only at the name itself.
  • Per host baselines turn a noisy signal into a usable alert.

In more detail

Domain Generation Algorithms (DGAs) are routines that deterministically produce large lists of candidate domains from changing inputs such as the date, a counter or a seed value. The implant queries the candidates until one resolves, and the adversary pre registers only the few names for the current period, so the rendezvous point is cheap to maintain and impossible to block with a static list. Generation styles leave fingerprints. Character based DGAs emit high entropy strings that rarely resemble words, while dictionary DGAs concatenate real words into combinations that are unusual in a different way, and both often favour long labels and less common top level domains. Behavioural telemetry is the strongest signal: many non resolving queries in a short window followed by a single success, lookups from a host or process with no business reason to make them, and a spike in NXDOMAIN responses for one client. A defender separates this from legitimate activity using per host baselines, the reputation and registration age of the domain that did answer, and correlation of the resolving query with the connection and process that follow.
