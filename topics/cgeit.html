Simple definition

Certified in the Governance of Enterprise IT (CGEIT) is an ISACA certification about running technology in a controlled way. It is not a hands on technical exam. It is for people who decide how technology should be used, funded and checked in a company.

Why should you care?

Companies spend large amounts of money on technology. Leaders must show that this spending is planned, safe and useful. This certificate proves you can set up that control system. It is often asked for in senior roles, such as IT governance manager or audit director. It also helps you talk to business leaders and not only to engineers.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Read the official ISACA exam outline and note the five domains and their share of the exam.
  2. Study common governance ideas, such as direction setting, monitoring and assurance.
  3. Learn one widely used framework, for example Control Objectives for Information and Related Technologies (COBIT).
  4. Study how organisations assess and treat technology risk, and how they measure value.
  5. Practise questions that describe a business problem and ask for the best management action.
  6. The exam is multiple choice, and it expects you to choose the answer that fits governance best, not the most technical fix.

Real-world example

Imagine a bank that wants to move its customer system to the cloud. The engineers can list the technical steps. The governance question is different. Who approves the spend, what happens if the supplier fails, how do we measure success, and who reports back to the board? A CGEIT holder is the person who designs those rules before the first server moves. That is why the exam asks about decisions and control, not about server settings.

Key takeaways

  • CGEIT is a senior certification, so it makes most sense after several years of IT or audit work.
  • The exam rewards the answer that fits good governance, even when a clever technical fix is offered.
  • Learn one governance framework well instead of skimming several at once.

In more detail

Certified in the Governance of Enterprise IT (CGEIT) is an ISACA certification for experienced professionals who direct, manage and assure information technology (IT) from a governance point of view. It sits at a senior level and suits IT managers, risk and compliance leads, auditors, architects and consultants who already hold responsibility for how technology serves business goals. It proves to an employer that you can shape an IT governance framework, manage IT resources and risk, and measure whether IT is delivering value. The exam is multiple choice and covers five domains, and ISACA also expects qualifying work experience. Typical roles after certification include IT governance manager, IT risk manager, enterprise architect and audit director. Exam domains and objectives

  • Explain how an enterprise governance of IT framework is set up in a real organisation
  • Describe the duties of a board and of senior management when they direct technology
  • Assess and treat technology risk in line with the organisation's risk appetite
  • Plan and manage technology resources, including people, money and suppliers
  • Define measures that show whether technology is delivering business value
  • Judge how new technology and regulation change an existing governance approach
