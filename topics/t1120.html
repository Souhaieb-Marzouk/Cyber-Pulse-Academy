T1120 Peripheral Device Discovery

T1120DiscoveryExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Peripheral devices are the extra pieces of hardware connected to a computer. Examples are a keyboard, a printer, a camera, a card reader or a memory stick. Peripheral device discovery means an attacker lists these devices to learn what the computer can do. It is a way of looking around before deciding what to steal.

Why should you care?

The devices attached to a computer tell an attacker what is possible. A camera means video can be taken, and a printer means documents may sit nearby. Removable storage may be a way to move data out of the building. This step is easy to miss because listing devices is a normal thing for many programs to do.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker runs a command or script on a host that they already control.
  2. The tool asks the operating system for a list of connected devices and their types.
  3. On Windows this may use setup interfaces, the registry or device management tools.
  4. On Linux and macOS the same details come from system files and command line utilities.
  5. The attacker notes devices that are useful, such as cameras, printers, card readers or removable drives.
  6. The list shapes the next step, for example audio capture or copying data from attached storage.

Real-world example

Think of a burglar who gets into a hotel room and first walks around to see what is there. A laptop on the desk, a safe in the wardrobe, a camera on the wall. A spare key card lies by the bed. They take nothing yet. The walk around tells them which rooms deserve a second visit and which tools to bring next time.

Key takeaways

  • Device lists are gathered before collection, so this step is an early warning sign.
  • Inventory tools query devices too, so check who ran the query and from where.
  • Cameras, card readers and removable drives are the devices worth protecting most.

In more detail

Peripheral device discovery is the enumeration of hardware attached to, or reachable from, a compromised host: keyboards, printers, cameras, smart card readers and removable storage. The purpose is situational awareness rather than immediate theft. A printer implies document stores and possibly a capture path, a smart card reader implies credential material, and a camera or microphone implies audio and video collection; removable drives suggest both a data source and a possible transfer route. On Windows the details come from setup interfaces, the registry and device management tooling, while Linux and macOS expose them through system files and command line utilities. Telemetry is thin because inventory and patching agents query the same interfaces constantly. The differentiators are the parent process, the account making the query, the time of day, and a burst of device queries that no known management tool can account for.

What you will practise

  • Define peripheral devices and give three examples of them.
  • Explain why an attacker lists devices before stealing any data.
  • Describe how device information is obtained on Windows, Linux and macOS.
  • Explain why device enumeration is difficult to separate from normal administration.
  • Describe which discovered devices could lead to later data collection.
