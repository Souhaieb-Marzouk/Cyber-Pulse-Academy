DET0376 Behavioral Detection Strategy for Network Service Discovery Across Platforms

DET0376Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Service discovery means checking which computers are switched on and which network doors are open on them. Attackers do this to choose a target. This detection counts connection attempts from one source to many destinations in a short time. It raises an alert on the pattern, not on a specific tool.

Why should you care?

A scan is often the first sign that someone is inside your network or testing it from outside. It also tells you which of your own systems an intruder can already reach. Left unseen, the scan is followed by attempts against whatever it found. Seeing the scan gives you a short but real chance to block the source.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Collect firewall and router logs for accepted and denied connection attempts.
  2. Collect network flow records that summarise connections between addresses and ports.
  3. Group the events by source address and by destination address over a short time window.
  4. Count distinct destination ports per destination, and distinct destinations per source.
  5. Compare the result with a list of approved scanners and with the normal rate for that network segment.
  6. Alert when a source goes well above its baseline, and include the top ports it tried.

Real-world example

Think of a person walking down a street of flats and knocking on every door to see which ones open. No door was forced, and nothing was taken, but the behaviour tells you what happens next. The doorbell log is the flow record.

Key takeaways

  • Scanning is a pattern of many destinations in a short time, not a single event.
  • An approved scanner list is the difference between constant noise and useful alerts.
  • Internal scans matter as much as external ones, because they show an intruder already inside.

In more detail

This strategy detects network service discovery, where an adversary probes hosts and ports to build a map of what is reachable and what is listening. Rather than alerting on a tool name, it looks at the shape of the traffic: one source contacting many destination ports on one host, or many hosts on a small set of ports, within a short window. Telemetry comes from firewall and router logs that record denied and allowed connection attempts, network flow records such as those from a flow export protocol, endpoint network connection events that show a short lived connection pattern, and cloud network logs for security group and load balancer probes. It fails most often because vulnerability scanners, monitoring agents and inventory tools scan the same estate on schedule, so an approved scanner inventory and a per source baseline are essential before the rule can be trusted.

What you will practise

  • Explain what network service discovery is and why it precedes exploitation.
  • Choose flow or firewall telemetry that captures both allowed and denied connection attempts.
  • Write a rule that detects a high count of distinct destination ports from one source.
  • Build an approved scanner inventory and use it to suppress expected scans.
  • Distinguish an internal scan by a compromised host from your own scheduled assessment work.
