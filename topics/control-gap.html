Control Gap

GRC & ComplianceVery hardKeyword

A control gap is the measurable difference between the protection an organisation believes it has and the protection that is actually implemented, operating and effective. Three distinct failure modes hide inside that single phrase, and separating them is the core skill. A design gap exists when no control addresses the risk at all, for example no privileged access review for a tier-zero identity store. An implementation gap exists when a control is defined but not deployed everywhere it is needed, such as endpoint detection present on workstations but absent on the servers that hold the crown jewels. An operating effectiveness gap exists when the control is deployed but does not function as intended, which covers disabled logging, alert rules that never fire, exceptions that quietly became permanent, and configuration drift after a change. Practitioners find gaps through several converging sources: control testing and walkthroughs, framework crosswalks against a target baseline, attack-path or red-team findings, audit and assessment reports, incident and near-miss analysis, and telemetry coverage checks that compare an asset inventory against the systems actually reporting logs. Ranking gaps requires more than counting, because a missing detection on an identity provider that can reach everything matters more than a missing hardening baseline on an isolated lab. Good gap registers record the evidence, the affected scope, the residual risk, the remediation owner, the acceptance criteria and the verification method that will prove closure, and they distinguish genuine remediation from documentation that merely restates the intent.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to separate design, implementation and operating effectiveness gaps when reviewing a control failure.
  • You will be able to build a telemetry coverage comparison between asset inventory and systems actually reporting logs.
  • You will be able to derive gaps from framework crosswalks, control testing and red-team findings rather than opinion.
  • You will be able to rank gaps by reachability and business impact instead of by raw count.
  • You will be able to write remediation entries with owners, acceptance criteria and a defined verification method.
  • You will be able to detect where a compensating control or accepted exception has become a permanent silent gap.
