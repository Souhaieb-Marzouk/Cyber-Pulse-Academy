SOAR

SOC & Detection EngineeringVery hardKeyword

Security Orchestration, Automation and Response is the discipline and tooling category concerned with turning detection output into repeatable action. Where a SIEM answers what happened, a SOAR platform asks what should happen next and then does as much of it as is safe without a human. Three capabilities are usually bundled: orchestration, meaning integration with other systems through APIs and playbooks; automation, meaning execution of those playbooks with minimal analyst input; and response, meaning the enrichment, containment and case-management actions that result. Typical first automations are unglamorous and high value: enriching an alert with asset ownership, user directory data, threat-intelligence reputation and recent sign-in history; de-duplicating and grouping related alerts into one case; notifying the right on-call queue with a consistent summary; and posting a structured timeline into the ticketing system. The hard part is not the integration, it is the decision boundary. Automations that take destructive action such as disabling an account, isolating a host or blocking an indicator can cause outages and can be abused by an adversary who learns what triggers them, so mature teams stage actions by confidence and reversibility, require approval for anything irreversible, and log every automated decision with its inputs for later review. SOAR also institutionalises response quality: a playbook is a documented, versioned procedure, so junior analysts execute the same steps as seniors, and post-incident review can show exactly which step failed. Common pitfalls include automating a process nobody had defined, brittle integrations with no error handling, and relying on threat-intelligence indicators that expire before the playbook runs.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to distinguish orchestration, automation and response capabilities and map each to concrete SOC workflows.
  • You will be able to design a playbook that enriches an alert before any containment action is considered.
  • You will be able to classify response actions by reversibility and confidence, and place approval gates where an automated mistake would cause outage or data loss.
  • You will be able to write idempotent automation steps so that a retried playbook does not duplicate tickets, notifications or containment actions.
  • You will be able to instrument playbooks with logging that records inputs, decisions and outcomes for after-action review.
  • You will be able to evaluate a SOAR integration for failure handling, rate limits, credential storage and blast radius before enabling it.
