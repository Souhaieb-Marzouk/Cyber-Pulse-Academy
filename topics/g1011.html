G1011 EXOTIC LILY

G1011Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

EXOTIC LILY is the name for a group that gets into companies with convincing email. It then sells that access to other criminals. Public reports say it has been active since about 2021 and mostly targets organisations in Europe and North America. It focuses on getting in, not on stealing money itself.

Why should you care?

The group hides inside trusted services. The message arrives from a real file hosting site, so it looks normal to both filters and people. Detection tools that only check a sender's reputation will miss it. Once the attackers have a working login, they hand it to ransomware crews. The harm then grows far beyond the first infection. Awareness training that teaches users to trust any well known link will not help here.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers pick a target company and learn the names of people who work there.
  2. They create an email address that looks almost identical to a real employee's address.
  3. They write a short business email that asks the reader to open a shared file.
  4. They upload the file to a well known hosting service, so the link looks trustworthy.
  5. The file hides a loader, which is a small program that fetches a bigger tool later.
  6. The loader installs attacker tooling, and the access is then handed to another group.

Real-world example

Imagine a courier who wears the uniform of a well known delivery firm and carries a real company clipboard. The guard at the gate checks the logo, sees it is genuine, and lets him in. The attackers copy that trick with email. The uniform is the trusted hosting service. The clipboard is a stolen employee name. The guard is the mail filter that trusts only familiar names.

Key takeaways

  • A link to a genuine service can still deliver a harmful file, so judge the request, not just the domain.
  • Confirm unusual file sharing requests using a phone number you already have on record.
  • Block or alert on shortcut, script, and disk image files arriving through web mail.

In more detail

EXOTIC LILY is a financially motivated intrusion set that public reporting links to the wider Wizard Spider ecosystem and to the TrickBot and Conti operations. It has been active since around 2021. Its signature is initial access by impersonation: members pose as employees of real companies, contact targets from lookalike email addresses, and point them at files hosted on legitimate services such as file sharing and collaboration platforms. Because the hosting domain is genuine and trusted, mail filtering and domain reputation checks often pass the message through. The delivered files have included archive and disk image formats with shortcut or script files inside, and loader families such as BazarLoader and BumbleBee have been reported as the next stage, usually followed by commercial penetration testing and command and control frameworks. Targets lean toward English speaking organisations in Europe and North America. Clusters like this are rebranded and remixed often, so a shared name may cover several teams rather than one fixed roster.

What you will practise

  • Explain why hosting a payload on a legitimate service defeats naive link reputation checks.
  • Describe the access broker model and how initial access is sold to ransomware operators.
  • Identify the file types and packaging tricks commonly used in these email campaigns.
  • Define what a loader does and why it is small on purpose.
  • Draft an email rule or user brief that addresses impersonation of known colleagues.
  • Explain why cluster names in this ecosystem are unstable and should not be treated as fixed identities.
