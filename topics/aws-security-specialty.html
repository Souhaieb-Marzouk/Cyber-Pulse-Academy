Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

The AWS Certified Security - Specialty exam is an advanced test for people who protect workloads on AWS (Amazon's cloud platform). It covers identity and permissions, encryption, network isolation, logging and incident response. It suits engineers who already have cloud experience and now own the security of whole accounts.

Why should you care?

In the cloud you rent computers by the minute, and anyone with the wrong permission can copy your data out quietly. A single open storage bucket or a leaked access key has caused very costly breaches. This exam exists so employers can trust someone to close those gaps. Without that care, speed in the cloud becomes a serious risk.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Start with identity. You give each person and each program only the permissions it truly needs.
  2. Then you protect the data. You turn on encryption so files and backups are useless if stolen.
  3. Next you separate the network. You put systems in their own private zones and control what may reach them.
  4. You switch on logging, which records every important action taken in your account.
  5. Then you watch the findings. Services raise a flag when something looks wrong or unsafe.
  6. Finally, you practise the response. You rehearse isolating a stolen key and keeping evidence safe.

Real-world example

Think of renting space in a huge shared warehouse. You get your own locked cages, your own keys, and your own alarm panel. The building owner keeps the walls and roof in good repair. Everything inside your cages is your job. If you leave a cage open, nobody will stop a stranger walking off with your boxes. AWS works like that shared building.

Key takeaways

  • In the cloud, you share the building but you own what is inside your rooms.
  • Permissions are the main door. Give the smallest set that still lets work happen.
  • Turn on logging before you need it, because you cannot investigate what you never recorded.

In more detail

AWS Certified Security - Specialty (SCS-C03) is an advanced, scenario-driven certification that validates a practitioner's ability to secure cloud solutions on AWS. It is aimed at security engineers and cloud security architects with three to five years of experience securing cloud workloads, and who own security outcomes rather than individual services. The exam is organised into six content domains: Detection (16%), Incident Response (14%), Infrastructure Security (18%), Identity and Access Management (20%), Data Protection (18%), and Security Foundations and Governance (14%). Questions are written as short architectures or incident narratives and frequently hinge on a single stated constraint - eliminating long-lived credentials, minimising operational overhead, or satisfying an audit - so the whole stem matters more than recall of service feature lists. Fifty scored questions (plus fifteen unscored) are delivered as multiple choice, multiple response, ordering and matching items, scored against a scaled pass mark of 750 out of 1,000 with no per-domain minimum, so strength in one domain offsets weakness in another. Hands-on practice changes the outcome materially: writing and debugging identity-based and resource-based policies, tracing why a request was allowed or denied, operating AWS KMS key policies and rotation, configuring CloudTrail and VPC Flow Logs, and rehearsing the containment and evidence-preservation steps of an incident response.

Exam domains and objectives

  • Detection: designing and troubleshooting the logging, monitoring and alerting that surfaces control-plane and data-plane events, and tuning findings so real signal is not buried in noise.
  • Incident Response: preparing runbooks and automation for compromised credentials and workloads, containing and isolating affected resources, and preserving evidence in a form that survives later review.
  • Infrastructure Security: protecting the network edge and compute workloads, scanning and patching what runs, and segmenting networks so that traffic is permitted or prevented exactly as required.
  • Identity and Access Management: building least-privilege access with authentication and authorization controls, temporary credentials, federation and permission boundaries, and diagnosing why a request is allowed.
  • Data Protection: controlling data in transit, at rest and across regions, and managing the keys, secrets and credentials that protect confidential material.
  • Security Foundations and Governance: centrally deploying and governing accounts, enforcing secure deployment through policy and infrastructure as code, and evaluating compliance across an AWS organization.
