G0063 BlackOasis

G0063Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

BlackOasis is a spy group that uses surveillance software, which is software normally sold to police and intelligence services. Public reports say it attacked activists, journalists and politicians in the Middle East. It has been linked in reporting to products made by a company called Gamma Group.

Why should you care?

This group shows that surveillance tools do not stay in the hands of the people who bought them. When such software leaks or is resold, ordinary people can be watched. For a defender, the lesson is that an attack may not look like a virus at all. It may look like a document that opened normally, then quietly copied everything. That makes user awareness and patch speed genuinely important, because the entry point is usually an out of date program.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers choose a target, often a person whose messages or documents would be valuable.
  2. They send a message with a document that exploits a known weakness in a reader program.
  3. When the document is opened, the spy program installs itself without any visible warning.
  4. The program copies files, messages and recordings of the victim's activity.
  5. It then contacts a server run by the attackers to receive new instructions.
  6. To stay in place, it adds entries that restart it automatically when the machine boots.

Real-world example

Picture a hospital night desk with a locked drawer of patient files. A visitor arrives with a form that needs a signature. While staff are busy reading the form, the visitor quietly takes photographs of the drawer contents. Nothing is broken, and the form was real. BlackOasis works in the same way. The document is the form that keeps you busy, and the copying happens while you are reading it.

Key takeaways

  • Commercial surveillance software is what this group is best known for using.
  • Documents and reader programs are common entry points, so keep them updated.
  • Startup entries and quiet outbound connections are key clues on a victim machine.

In more detail

BlackOasis is a cyber espionage group tracked as G0063 and publicly reported since around 2016 for deploying commercial surveillance software against activists, journalists and political figures in the Middle East and neighbouring regions. Reporting associates the group's toolset with surveillance products marketed by the Gamma Group, and the activity is notable because it shows how lawful interception or monitoring software sold to governments can be repurposed for targeted intrusion. Delivery has been observed through spear phishing messages and documents exploiting vulnerabilities in common reader applications, after which the payload installs itself, gathers documents and communications, and beacons to attacker controlled infrastructure for tasking. Defenders often spot the activity through email gateway detections, Office and browser exploit telemetry, unusual persistence entries in the startup folders or registry, and outbound connections to hosting ranges with no business purpose. Attribution of the group's clients or sponsors is an assessment based on infrastructure and targeting, and public reporting presents it as such rather than as a settled fact.
