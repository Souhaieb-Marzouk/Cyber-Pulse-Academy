Kerberos

Identity & AccessExtremeKeyword

Kerberos is a network authentication protocol built on symmetric cryptography and a trusted third party, in which a client proves its identity to a service without sending a password across the network. The key distribution centre, composed of the authentication service and the ticket granting service, issues a ticket granting ticket after the client authenticates, and the client then presents that ticket to obtain service tickets for individual resources. Tickets carry a validity window and are encrypted with keys derived from account passwords or machine secrets, which is why Kerberos security is inseparable from the hygiene of those secrets. In Active Directory the protocol underpins almost all domain authentication, and its abuse paths dominate enterprise intrusions: requesting service tickets and cracking them offline because a service account password is weak, forging tickets when a service account key is stolen, relaying authentication to a service that does not enforce mutual verification, and taking advantage of delegation configured too broadly. Defensive practice centres on detecting anomalous ticket requests, especially bursts of service ticket requests with unusual encryption types or for services that a principal never normally contacts, and on eliminating the conditions that make those requests valuable. That means long random passwords for service accounts managed automatically where possible, removal of unnecessary privileged service principals, restriction of delegation, and monitoring of the domain controller security log for the ticket and credential events that indicate a principal is attempting to obtain material it should not need.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to describe the role of the authentication service, ticket granting service and service tickets in a Kerberos exchange.
  • You will be able to explain how ticket lifetime, renewable tickets and encryption type downgrades affect exposure.
  • You will be able to identify risky service principal configurations, including weak service account secrets and unnecessary delegation.
  • You will be able to build detection logic for anomalous service ticket requests and unusual encryption type selection.
  • You will be able to explain relay and coercion risks against services that do not verify authentication properly.
  • You will be able to prioritise remediation for Kerberos-related findings by blast radius rather than by count.
