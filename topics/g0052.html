G0052 CopyKittens

G0052Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

CopyKittens is a name for a spying group that public reports link to Iranian interests. It targets government offices, universities, defence companies and technology firms, mainly in the Middle East. The group sends emails with documents that look useful, and it builds its own set of spying tools. Different security companies group the same activity under different names.

Why should you care?

Universities and research centres often hold valuable ideas but have light security. A single opened document can give an attacker a foothold that lasts for months. Because the group builds its own tools, ordinary antivirus products may not recognise them at all. Staff awareness and careful email filtering carry much of the protection. Early detection also matters, because this group tends to return to the same targets.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Researchers or officials receive an email that looks relevant to their daily work.
  2. The attachment asks the reader to allow content, which starts a small script.
  3. That script installs a backdoor which starts again every time the computer restarts.
  4. The backdoor receives commands from a server that the group replaces often.
  5. Operators collect documents and passwords, then search for other computers to reach.
  6. If the first attempt fails, they send a new lure to the same target later.

Real-world example

Imagine a small research library inside a university. It holds original papers that nobody outside has read. The door is open during the day and the staff are helpful. A visitor asks for one photocopy, then returns every week with a new request. Each visit takes away a little more knowledge. CopyKittens approaches its targets in that same slow and repeated way.

Key takeaways

  • Research and academic organisations hold valuable data and are often lightly defended.
  • Custom malware may not match any known virus signature.
  • Repeated attempts against the same target are normal, so stay alert.

In more detail

CopyKittens is an espionage cluster described in public reporting as active for many years, with campaigns against government, defence, academic and technology targets in Israel and the wider Middle East. Public reporting associates the group with Iranian interests, an assessment built on targeting patterns, tooling and infrastructure rather than a definitive public finding. Tradecraft includes spear-phishing with document lures, a custom malware framework with several backdoor components, and infrastructure that is rotated between campaigns. Defenders see malicious document execution, new persistence mechanisms and outbound connections to short-lived servers, often on consumer-grade hosting. Two misunderstandings are common: that the group only targets one country, and that custom malware must be technically exotic, when reporting describes functional rather than advanced tooling. Because several vendors track overlapping activity under different names, campaign attribution should carry the confidence its source states.

What you will practise

  • Describe the target sectors and region reported for CopyKittens.
  • Explain how repeated spear-phishing supports long-term access.
  • Explain why custom tooling weakens signature-based detection.
  • State attribution with the same confidence level used by the source.
  • Outline controls that protect research and academic environments.
