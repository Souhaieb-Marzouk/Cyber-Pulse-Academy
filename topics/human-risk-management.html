Human Risk Management

Security AwarenessHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Human risk management means understanding how people's everyday habits can cause a security problem. It looks at what staff really do, not only at what a training course says. The aim is to help people make safer choices with better tools and clearer rules. It is not about blaming anyone for an honest mistake.

Why should you care?

Most incidents start with a person doing something ordinary, such as opening a file or approving a payment. Training once a year does not change daily habits. If you blame people, they stop reporting problems, and you learn about attacks much later. Measuring human risk shows which teams need help and which tools are hard to use. Then you can fix the cause instead of repeating the same course.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The team collects signals such as reporting rates and simulation results over time.
  2. It groups those signals by role and team, so the risk picture is realistic.
  3. It looks for barriers, like a process that pushes people to take shortcuts.
  4. Small targeted actions follow, such as coaching or a simpler approval step.
  5. The team measures again later to see whether the behaviour really changed.
  6. Leaders are asked to model safe habits and to protect people who report.

Real-world example

Think of a busy kitchen where cooks must wash their hands. A poster on the wall does not make anyone wash. Managers made it easy by placing sinks near every station and by praising staff who remind each other. They watched how often the soap ran out, instead of blaming one cook after a bad night. Human risk management works the same way in an office. You change the kitchen, not the cook.

Key takeaways

  • Measure behaviour over time, not attendance at one yearly training course.
  • Make the safe choice the easy choice, and most people will follow it.
  • Never punish someone who reports a mistake, or reporting quietly stops.

In more detail

Human risk management is the practice of measuring and reducing the chance that ordinary working behaviour leads to a security incident, rather than treating staff as a blameworthy weakest link. It replaces the annual awareness course with a continuous, evidence-led programme: phishing simulation results, user reporting rates and time to report, help-desk attempts at social engineering, near-miss reports, policy exception requests, data-handling telemetry and control failures are combined into risk signals grouped by team, role and task. Interventions are then targeted at the behaviour and at the system around it, such as simplifying a payment approval step that people work around, giving engineers a safe place to move data, or coaching the group that actually faces the risk. The mistakes are reporting only click rates, which measures the simulation rather than the risk, and punishing reporters, which suppresses the very reports that make an intrusion visible early. The objective is fewer successful attacks, not better scores.

What you will practise

  • Explain what human risk management means and how it differs from awareness training.
  • Describe the signals that can be combined into a picture of human risk.
  • Identify the everyday behaviours that create the most risk in an organisation.
  • Choose interventions that change the process instead of blaming the individual.
  • Explain why a blame-free reporting culture lowers real security risk.
