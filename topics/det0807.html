DET0807 Detection of Identify Roles

DET0807Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Attackers want to know who works at your company and what each person does. A name and a job title is enough to write a believable message. This detection looks for unusual searching of your staff directory and for bulk lookups of people on public sites. It catches the research step before the phishing starts.

Why should you care?

A phishing email that names your manager and your project is far more convincing than a generic one. Attackers also look for the person who resets passwords, because that role can unlock accounts. Knowing the team structure tells them who reports to whom and who is likely to trust whom. This research is cheap, and it makes every later step more likely to work. Detecting it gives your team a chance to warn the specific people who are being studied.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Your identity provider records directory searches and profile views, including the account and the search terms.
  2. The rule counts those searches per account over a period of time.
  3. It compares the count with what is normal for that person's role, such as a recruiter or a help desk agent.
  4. It also looks at web requests to people search and professional networking sites from corporate devices.
  5. It checks email gateway logs for messages or lookups that walk through many staff addresses in sequence.
  6. Accounts or sources that go far beyond the normal pattern are grouped and raised for the security team.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital sign in sheet at the staff entrance. A visitor signs in once and goes to see one patient. That is normal. Another visitor signs in and then asks the receptionist for every nurse on the night shift. They also ask for the phone numbers. The receptionist would not hand that over and would remember the request. This detection watches for that request. It does not matter whether the request comes from outside the building or from an account inside.

Key takeaways

  • Names and job titles are the raw material of convincing phishing, so collecting them is a real event.
  • You must know which roles legitimately search the directory heavily, or they will drown the alert.
  • A role based alert lets you warn the exact people being researched, which is often more useful than blocking.

In more detail

This strategy detects the collection of employee roles and responsibilities, ATT&CK sub technique T1591.004, which adversaries use to choose targets for phishing, help desk impersonation and business email compromise. The adversary looks for names, job titles, team structures, reporting lines and contact details on professional networking sites, company pages, conference agendas and job postings. Detectable telemetry comes from identity and directory services rather than the endpoint: search and profile view records in the identity provider or employee directory, bulk query patterns from a single account, outbound web requests to professional networking and people search domains, and email gateway logs showing directory style harvesting. Detection is built around behaviour that a normal employee never generates, such as an authenticated account enumerating the directory far beyond its role, or an external source resolving a large number of staff addresses in one session. The strategy fails because directory searches are among the most common actions in any company, because recruiting and sales teams query these sources legitimately every day, and because an unauthenticated adversary can learn most of it from public data without touching any of your systems.

What you will practise

  • Explain how role information supports targeting in phishing and impersonation attacks
  • Identify identity provider and directory telemetry that records search and profile view activity
  • Establish a normal search volume per role using historical directory activity
  • Detect bulk enumeration of staff records by a single account or external source
  • Separate recruiting, sales and help desk activity from suspicious collection patterns
  • Convert a role reconnaissance alert into targeted warning and awareness actions
