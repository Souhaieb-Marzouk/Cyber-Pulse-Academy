T1125 Video Capture

T1125CollectionExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Video capture means using a computer camera or webcam to record images or video. An attacker can take short clips or single pictures without the user knowing. The files are stored and sent away later.

Why should you care?

A camera shows what is in front of the screen. That can include documents, whiteboards, other screens and the people in the room. Laptops and monitors often have cameras built in, so nothing extra is needed. Pictures can be taken slowly over many days, so no single moment looks strange. Most users never think about the camera as a risk.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Malware asks the operating system for access to the camera device or its media interface.
  2. Some tools instead attach to a video call application that already has the camera open.
  3. The tool records a video clip, or takes single pictures at set time intervals.
  4. Files are written to disk, often with a temporary or harmless looking name.
  5. The attacker may delete the files after sending them, which leaves very little behind.
  6. Recording often starts and stops based on events, such as movement or a scheduled time.

Real-world example

Imagine a small shop with a camera behind the counter. The owner uses it to watch the till. If somebody else gains access to that camera, they can see the till, the safe and everyone who comes in. The camera never changes and the shop never looks different. Only the recordings show that somebody else was watching.

Key takeaways

  • A camera can be used with no new hardware and no obvious change on screen.
  • Camera in use indicators and application permissions are the fastest checks.
  • Still images taken over time can leak as much as a long video and are easier to hide.

In more detail

Video capture is the recording of images or video through a built in camera, an attached webcam, or a video calling application. The adversary uses the same media interfaces that legitimate software uses, so the collected material may be a clip, a burst of still frames taken at intervals, or a single snapshot. Artefacts are limited: a process holding the camera, temporary or deceptively named media files, and a camera in use indicator appearing while no call is running. The technique is separated from screen capture because it uses the physical camera rather than the display output, though the two are frequently deployed together. On Windows and macOS the practical control point is the per application camera permission, and detection depends on correlating camera access with the user's real conferencing activity, the parent process, and the file paths where images are written.

What you will practise

  • Explain how malware records video or takes pictures through a camera.
  • Describe how a video call application can be abused instead of the camera device.
  • Explain how video capture differs from screen capture.
  • Identify process, file and permission evidence of camera use.
  • Describe which controls limit an application's access to the camera.
