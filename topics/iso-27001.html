ISO 27001

GRC & ComplianceHardKeyword

ISO/IEC 27001 is the international standard that specifies requirements for establishing, implementing, maintaining and continually improving an information security management system. It is a management-system standard rather than a control catalogue: it tells an organisation how to govern security through leadership commitment, documented scope, risk assessment, risk treatment, internal audit, management review and corrective action, while the accompanying guidance in ISO/IEC 27002 describes control objectives an organisation may select from. Certification is granted to the management system by an accredited certification body, not to a product, a data centre or a specific application, and it is issued against a declared scope and Statement of Applicability. For the security practitioner the practical consequences are concrete. Risk assessment must be repeatable and produce traceable treatment decisions; controls must be documented with owners, evidence and review cycles; Annex A control themes now cover organisational, people, physical and technological domains, and each selected or excluded control needs a justification. Auditors sample evidence rather than trust assertions, so operational artefacts such as access reviews, change tickets, incident records and supplier assessments become the real currency of the audit. Surveillance audits occur at least annually and a recertification audit happens on a three-year cycle, which means compliance is a continuous programme rather than a project with an end date. The standard pairs well with ISO/IEC 27017 and 27018 for cloud services and with ISO 27005 for risk methodology.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to explain how a risk assessment and risk treatment plan feed the Statement of Applicability and the audit programme.
  • You will be able to define an ISMS scope that matches the organisation's real legal, contractual and technical boundaries.
  • You will be able to map Annex A control themes onto existing operational controls and produce clause-level evidence.
  • You will be able to prepare for a stage 1 documentation review and a stage 2 evidence-based certification audit.
  • You will be able to run an internal audit and management review cycle that generates genuine corrective actions.
  • You will be able to identify the common failure modes where certification is achieved but security practice does not improve.
