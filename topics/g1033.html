G1033 Star Blizzard

G1033Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Star Blizzard is a name for a group of attackers that public reports link to Russia. The group wants information, not money. It sends careful messages to people who work with policy, news, or charity organisations. The messages link to a fake login page that copies the password.

Why should you care?

One stolen password can open a whole mailbox, and a mailbox holds years of secrets. This group does not need to install anything on your computer. It only needs you to type your password into a page that looks correct. If your account has a second check, such as a code, the trick becomes much harder. This is why login safety matters as much as antivirus software.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The group studies a target and learns about their work, contacts, and interests.
  2. It sends a message that looks like it comes from someone the target knows.
  3. The message holds a link, which opens a page that copies the real login.
  4. The victim types the password and the second code into the fake page.
  5. The attackers then use that session to read mail without logging in again.
  6. They add mail rules that hide warnings and move replies out of sight.

Real-world example

Think of a hotel reception desk where guests sign a book. A clever visitor replaces the book with a copy that looks exactly the same. Guests still sign in, and the receptionist still smiles. Later, the visitor uses the copied signatures to enter rooms that were never booked for him. He does not break any lock; he simply borrowed the trust of the front desk. Fake login pages steal trust in the same quiet way.

Key takeaways

  • Check the web address before typing a password into any login page.
  • Turn on a second sign-in check, such as an app code, on every account.
  • Review mailbox rules and forwarding settings every few months.

In more detail

Star Blizzard is a Russian-nexus espionage cluster that public reporting, including statements from United Kingdom and United States authorities, links to a Russian intelligence service. The group's tradecraft centres on credential theft rather than malware: carefully researched messages to defence and policy specialists, journalists, and non-governmental organisations, with links to fake sign-in pages that copy the real service and capture both passwords and session cookies. A stolen session lets an intruder read mail without authenticating again, and reporting describes mailbox rules that hide replies or move messages into folders nobody reviews. Campaigns have also reached targets through messaging applications, where a link arrives from a compromised contact's account. Evidence is strongest in identity provider logs, including unexpected sign-in locations, token reuse, new forwarding or delegation settings, and device registrations. Attribution appears in government statements, but organisations should still verify their own telemetry rather than rely on a name.
