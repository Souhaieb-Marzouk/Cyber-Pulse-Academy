G0033 Poseidon Group

G0033Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Poseidon Group is a criminal group that public reports describe as stealing data and money from businesses. It started with carefully written emails that carried harmful files. Then it stayed hidden inside the network for a long time. Reporting says it also tried to persuade employees to help from the inside.

Why should you care?

Most teams plan for attackers outside the network. This group shows that an insider can also be part of the problem, sometimes after pressure or persuasion. A long quiet stay also means the first alert and the final theft may look unrelated. If you only review single events, the full story stays hidden. Watching longer patterns matters just as much as blocking the first email.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers study a company and choose employees who can open doors or reach valuable data.
  2. They send email that looks normal, carrying a file that quietly installs hidden software.
  3. With that foothold they watch the network for weeks and learn how the business works.
  4. They collect documents, customer lists and financial information while staying very quiet.
  5. Reporting describes attempts to persuade or pressure staff into helping with the theft.
  6. Finally they move money or large amounts of data, often through several accounts and services.

Real-world example

Imagine a night-shift cleaner who is given a small favour and a little money by a stranger. Months later he is asked to leave one side door unlocked. Nobody broke anything, and the stranger never climbed a wall. Security that ignores people and relationships misses half of the picture.

Key takeaways

  • Insider risk is real, so access should be limited and unusual behaviour noticed.
  • Long intrusions are best spotted by looking at patterns over time, not single events.
  • Watch large outbound data transfers, because theft often shows up at the end of a campaign.

In more detail

Poseidon Group is a financially motivated intrusion set that public reporting described as targeting businesses in several countries and sectors, including energy, manufacturing, finance, media, technology and services. Reporting characterises the group as starting with spearphishing messages that delivered a first-stage implant, after which the operators spent an extended period inside the network before attempting theft. The distinctive element in that account is a stated practice of recruiting insiders: the attackers reportedly used stolen data to pressure or persuade employees into helping, turning a technical intrusion into a human one. The long dwell time is the main defensive lesson, because the initial alert and the eventual theft may be months apart. As with any single-vendor report, tool names and claims belong to that reporting rather than settled fact.

What you will practise

  • Describe the Poseidon Group as reported and the sectors it was said to target.
  • Explain why a long dwell time complicates detection and investigation.
  • Explain how an insider can change the risk picture for an organisation.
  • Identify the controls that limit the damage a single account or employee can cause.
  • Identify the evidence sources that would reveal bulk data leaving a network.
  • Attribute specific claims about the group to the reporting rather than stating them as fact.
