Risk Register

GRC & ComplianceHardKeyword

A risk register is the controlled record of identified risks that an organisation has decided to track, each entry capturing the risk statement, the affected assets and business processes, the threat and vulnerability that make it credible, the existing controls, the assessed likelihood and impact, the resulting inherent and residual rating, the risk owner, the treatment decision and the target date. It is the operational heart of a risk management programme because it converts assessment into accountable action: an entry without a named owner and a due date is an observation, not a managed risk. Practitioners should treat the register as a living engineering artefact rather than an annual spreadsheet. Good registers express risk in a consistent grammar such as cause, event and consequence, so that two analysts score similar situations similarly; they attach evidence such as detection coverage gaps, vulnerability findings, control test results and near-miss incident data; and they record risk acceptance explicitly, with the accepting authority, the rationale and the expiry. Risk scoring should be calibrated against real incident and loss data where it exists, because uncalibrated five-by-five matrices drift toward subjective optimism. Registers also need lifecycle discipline: risks are reviewed on a defined cadence, escalated when treatment slips, closed with evidence, and linked to the frameworks in use, whether ISO 27001 clause six, NIST SP 800-30 or a sector regulator's expectations. Effective registers are visible to engineering, not locked in a GRC tool only compliance staff can open.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to write risk statements in a consistent cause, event and consequence structure that different analysts score the same way.
  • You will be able to attach control evidence, detection coverage and vulnerability data to support likelihood and impact ratings.
  • You will be able to assign risk owners with authority to accept or treat, and set meaningful target dates.
  • You will be able to document formal risk acceptance with rationale, accepting authority and review expiry.
  • You will be able to run a review cycle that escalates overdue treatment and closes risks only with evidence.
  • You will be able to calibrate a risk matrix against incident and loss data rather than unfounded opinion.
