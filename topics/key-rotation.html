Key Rotation

CryptographyVery hardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Key rotation means replacing a cryptographic key with a new one on a schedule or after an incident. The old key is retired once nothing uses it. Rotation limits how much damage a stolen key can do. Even if an attacker copied the key, it stops working after the change.

Why should you care?

Keys do not last forever. People leave, laptops are lost, and logs sometimes leak a secret. A key used for years is a long opportunity for an attacker. Rotation shortens that window and is also required by many security standards. The hard part is not making the new key. It is finding every system, script and device that still uses the old one.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. List every place the key is used, including apps, build pipelines, backups and partner systems.
  2. Create the new key in an approved place, such as a key manager or hardware device.
  3. Give the new key to every consumer while the old key still works.
  4. Wait through a short overlap period so nothing breaks during the change.
  5. Confirm from logs that every consumer has switched to the new key.
  6. Disable and delete the old key only after you are sure nothing needs it.

Real-world example

Think of changing the locks on an office door. You tell every employee and cleaner about the new key before the old one stops working. You keep both keys valid for a week, so nobody is locked out. Then you check the door logs to confirm everyone used the new key. Only then do you throw the old key away. If you skip a step, people stand outside.

Key takeaways

  • Rotate keys on a schedule and immediately after any suspected leak.
  • Find every consumer first. Most rotation outages come from a forgotten script.
  • Accept both keys during a short overlap, then retire the old one with evidence.

In more detail

Key rotation is the scheduled or event-driven replacement of cryptographic keys, secrets and certificates so that the exposure window of any single key is bounded. Rotation limits the blast radius of an undetected compromise, satisfies compliance requirements that demand defined cryptoperiods, and reduces the value of a stolen key to an attacker who may already have exfiltrated it. A workable rotation process has four parts: generating new key material in an approved location, distributing it to every consumer without downtime, accepting both old and new material during an overlap window, and retiring the old material with proof that nothing still depends on it. The hard part is almost never the cryptography; it is discovery of every place a key is used, including configuration files, environment variables, CI pipeline secrets, cached tokens, backups, third-party integrations and human-held copies. Rotation that breaks production is the reason teams avoid it, so mature organisations automate issuance, treat keys as short-lived credentials, prefer key derivation per message or per tenant over one long-lived shared key, and monitor decryption or authentication failures that would reveal a consumer missed a rotation. Emergency rotation after a suspected compromise is a different exercise from scheduled rotation, since it must assume the attacker holds the old material and can observe the overlap window.

What you will practise

  • Design a key rotation policy covering generation, distribution, overlap acceptance and retirement with evidence.
  • Enumerate every consumer of a key across services, pipelines, devices, backups and third parties before rotating.
  • Automate rotation using a secrets manager or KMS while preserving availability for dependent systems.
  • Distinguish scheduled rotation from emergency rotation after suspected compromise and adjust the process accordingly.
  • Detect incomplete rotation through authentication, decryption and signature verification failures in telemetry.
  • Define cryptoperiods for different key types and defend those choices in an audit or control review.
