Risk Exception Management

GRC & ComplianceHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Risk exception management is a formal way to accept a rule you cannot follow. A company states which security rule it cannot meet right now. Instead of silently ignoring the rule, someone writes down the reason, the risk and the date it must be fixed. A responsible person approves it. The record is kept and reviewed.

Why should you care?

Real systems sometimes cannot meet every rule. A hospital machine may run old software that a supplier no longer updates. Saying no is not always possible, but forgetting is dangerous. A written exception keeps the problem visible instead of hidden. It also proves that a person with authority accepted the risk on purpose.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Someone finds that a required control cannot be met and raises a request.
  2. The request states what cannot be done, why, and which system is affected.
  3. The team lists compensating controls, which are other steps that reduce the danger.
  4. A risk owner, a person accountable for that area, reviews and approves or refuses it.
  5. The exception gets an expiry date and a clear condition for review.
  6. The list of open exceptions is reported and checked at every review cycle.

Real-world example

Think of a shared flat where the front door lock is broken. The tenants cannot repair it tonight. So they agree on temporary measures instead. The chain stays on, and one person checks the door before bed. The landlord is called in writing. They write the arrangement on the noticeboard with a repair date. What they must never do is simply forget that the lock is broken.

Key takeaways

  • An exception is a temporary, approved and written acceptance of risk.
  • Every exception needs an owner, an expiry date and a review condition.
  • Keep one central list, because exceptions buried in email are never revisited.

In more detail

Risk exception management is the governance process by which an organisation formally records that a specific control requirement cannot be met, for a defined period, and accepts the resulting risk with someone accountable for the decision. It is distinct from ignoring a finding, and distinct from remediation: the exception is a document, not an outcome. A workable record identifies the requirement, the system or process in scope, the business reason, the compensating controls that reduce exposure, the residual risk stated in whatever terms the organisation uses, a named owner and a named approver, and an expiry date plus the condition under which the risk must be revisited. In practice it turns up wherever a mandated control collides with an operational constraint: a legacy application that cannot support multifactor authentication, a patch that would break a supplier product, a payment environment where a rule cannot be applied as written. It also feeds the risk register and appears in audit and assurance reporting. The mistake people usually make is granting exceptions permanently and only tracking them in email, so the accepted risks outlive their justification and nobody can produce a current list.

What you will practise

  • Explain what a risk exception is and how it differs from ignoring a finding or remediating it.
  • List the essential elements of an exception record, including owner, approver and expiry.
  • Describe the role of compensating controls in reducing the residual risk of an exception.
  • Explain how exception records feed risk registers, assurance work and audit reporting.
  • Identify failure modes such as permanent exceptions, missing reviews and undocumented approvals.
