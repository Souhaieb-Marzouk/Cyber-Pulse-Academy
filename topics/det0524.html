DET0524 Traffic Signaling (Port-knock / magic-packet → firewall or service activation) – T1205

DET0524Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This detection looks for a secret knock on the network. A program on an infected host sleeps until it hears a special pattern of packets. Then it wakes up and starts working. The alert fires when such a pattern is followed by a new open port or a firewall rule change.

Why should you care?

A silent implant is hard to find, because it does nothing until someone calls it. Traffic signalling lets the attacker choose when the implant is active. The implant can then stay quiet through most of your monitoring. The knock often looks like harmless failed connections to closed ports. Only the change that follows the knock shows the real intent. Linking the two gives you a rare view of a hidden entry point switching on.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Network sensors log every connection attempt with the source, the destination, the port and the connection state.
  2. The rule looks for several refused connections from one source. They arrive at different ports inside a short window, in a fixed order.
  3. At the same time, host or gateway logs are checked for packet filter changes. A new accept rule is the clearest example.
  4. The rule joins the two streams by source address and by time. It keeps pairs that happen within a few minutes.
  5. Known knock configurations used by administrators are filtered out by source and destination pair.
  6. Surviving pairs become an alert that shows the port sequence and the rule that was added.

Real-world example

Think of a closed shop with a back door. The owner knocks three times, then once, and a helper inside opens the door. To anyone walking past, the knocks mean nothing, and the shop still looks shut. The useful clue is not the knocking on its own. It is the door opening right after a strange pattern that no normal visitor makes.

Key takeaways

  • The knock is weak evidence on its own; the state change that follows it is the real signal.
  • Correlate network flow records with firewall rule or service state changes by source and time.
  • Approved port knocking setups exist, so allow them by address pair before you alert.

In more detail

This detection strategy looks for traffic signalling, the use of a special sequence of network packets to wake a hidden service or open a path through a packet filter. A single magic packet, a fixed order of connection attempts to closed ports, or a crafted field value can tell a dormant implant that it is safe to start, which keeps the implant quiet during routine monitoring. Packet filter rule changes, firewall logs that show a short ordered sequence of refused connections followed by a rule commit, and flow records with repeated refused connections in a fixed order supply the evidence. The main failure mode is treating the knock itself as an attack when it may be an approved administrative access control, so the rule must be tied to what happens after the sequence.

What you will practise

  • Describe how a packet sequence can activate a dormant service or open a firewall path
  • Identify the flow record fields used to spot an ordered sequence of refused connections from one source
  • Explain how to correlate a knock sequence with a packet filter rule change or a new listening service
  • Distinguish approved administrative port knocking from malicious traffic signalling
  • Choose a correlation window and explain the coverage it costs
  • Decide on a severity for signalling that leads to a hidden remote access path
