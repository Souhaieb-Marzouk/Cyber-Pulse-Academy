Cloud Shared Responsibility Model

Cloud SecurityHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

The shared responsibility model is an agreement about who protects what in the cloud. The provider protects the buildings and the machines that run the service. You protect your own data, your user accounts, and your settings. The exact line moves depending on the kind of service you buy. Reading that line carefully is part of your job.

Why should you care?

Many people believe the cloud provider handles all security, and that belief causes breaches. If you leave a storage area open, the provider will not close it, because it is your setting. If you never patch the operating system of your rented server, nobody else will do it for you. Knowing the split tells you which tasks belong on your own checklist. It also helps during a review, when an auditor asks who is responsible for each control.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The provider always secures the physical buildings, the hardware, and the software that runs the cloud itself.
  2. For rented servers, you take on the operating system, the applications, and every patch and setting above it.
  3. For managed platforms, the provider patches the platform, and you still protect your code and your data.
  4. For ready made software services, the provider runs almost everything, and you manage accounts, data and sharing.
  5. In every model, your identity settings, your access rules and your data stay your responsibility.
  6. You confirm the split in the contract and the provider's documentation, because it differs between providers.

Real-world example

Think of renting a flat. The owner repairs the roof, the pipes and the front door lock. You lock your own door, decide who gets a key, and keep your valuables safe. If you leave the window open in the rain, the owner is not responsible for the ruined carpet. The cloud works the same way. The provider owns the building, and you own what happens inside your flat. The exact duty list depends on how much of the flat came furnished.

Key takeaways

  • The provider secures the cloud itself; you secure what you put inside it.
  • The dividing line moves with the service type, so check it for each service you use.
  • Your data, your identities and your configuration remain yours in every model.

In more detail

The shared responsibility model describes how security duties are divided between a cloud provider and its customer, and it is the mental model that most cloud security failures trace back to. The provider is responsible for security of the cloud: facilities, physical hardware, the hypervisor, and the internals of the managed services it operates. The customer is responsible for security in the cloud: data classification, identity and access management, configuration of every resource it creates, and the patching of anything it deploys on top. The boundary moves with the service model, so a customer running virtual machines owns the guest operating system, a customer using a managed platform owns its application and data, and a customer using finished software mainly owns accounts, data and sharing settings. It surfaces in contracts, in compliance scoping, and in incident arguments about who was meant to patch what. The mistake is reading the model as a liability transfer: an exposed storage location or an unpatched guest system is the customer's finding regardless of what the contract says.

What you will practise

  • Explain what the shared responsibility model means for a cloud customer.
  • Describe how responsibility shifts between infrastructure, platform and software services.
  • Identify the duties that always stay with the customer, such as data and identity protection.
  • Give examples of incidents caused by misunderstanding the split.
  • Locate the authoritative description of the split for a given provider and service.
