DET0886 Detection of Spearphishing Voice

DET0886Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Voice phishing is a phone call from a criminal who pretends to be someone you trust. The caller asks for a code, a password, or approval on your phone. This detection looks for the results of those calls, such as a sudden password reset or a new phone added to an account.

Why should you care?

Technical controls are good at stopping links and files. A friendly voice on the phone walks past all of them. The person on the call is often helpful and in a hurry, which is exactly what the caller wants. Because there is no malware, nothing technical looks wrong until an account is used in a strange way. Pairing call records with account changes is the only reliable way to see it.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The service desk writes a ticket for each call, with the caller, the request, and the verification steps used.
  2. The identity system logs security changes, such as a multi-factor reset or a new device enrolment.
  3. The detection joins those two streams using the user name and a short time window.
  4. It marks cases where the account is valuable, the caller was not verified, or the change was rushed.
  5. Extra signals, such as a new mail forwarding rule, raise the score further.
  6. The analyst calls the user back on a known number and reverses any change that was not genuine.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital sign-in sheet at the staff entrance. Anyone can write a name in it. If a stranger signs in as a doctor, walks to the drug store, and takes a box, the sheet alone would not stop them. But if the store log shows a removal one minute after an unverified sign-in, the security team has a story to investigate.

Key takeaways

  • The phone call itself is invisible, so you detect the account changes that follow it.
  • Joining service desk tickets with identity events is the heart of this detection.
  • Verification rules for the service desk are a control, not just a detection improvement.

In more detail

This strategy covers voice phishing, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK T1598.004 (Phishing for Information: Spearphishing Voice). The adversary telephones a target while posing as information technology support, a bank, or an executive, and talks the person into revealing a code, approving a multi-factor prompt, or resetting a credential. Almost none of this happens in your logs, so the detection is built from the side effects: help desk ticket records, identity provider events such as multi-factor authentication resets and new device enrolments, new mail forwarding rules, and unusual authentication attempts that follow a call. The pattern worth alerting on is a high-value identity change that occurs shortly after an inbound call to a service desk, especially when the caller identity was not verified or the change happened outside normal process. The main failure mode is that legitimate password resets are extremely common, so without pairing the call record with the identity event the rule becomes a reset notifier rather than a detection.

What you will practise

  • Define voice phishing and describe the identity changes adversaries request during a call
  • Identify the service desk, identity, and mail telemetry needed to detect the aftermath of a call
  • Correlate an inbound call record with a multi-factor reset or device enrolment inside a time window
  • Rank alerts by account value and by whether caller verification was recorded
  • Recommend service desk verification steps that both reduce risk and improve detection quality
