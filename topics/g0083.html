G0083 SilverTerrier

G0083Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

SilverTerrier is a name for a broad community of criminals, mostly based in Nigeria, who attack companies by email. They do not work as one team. They share tools and advice with each other. Their main trick is business email compromise, where they pretend to be a manager or supplier and ask for a payment. One security company gave the group this name in a series of reports.

Why should you care?

This kind of fraud does not need to break a computer. It only needs someone to believe a message and approve a payment. Losses are paid out by the victim, and recovery is hard once money leaves. Because the criminals share tools, blocking one program does not help much. Good checks on payments and on mailbox access reduce the risk far more directly.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers collect names and email addresses from company websites and social media.
  2. They send a message that looks like it comes from a manager or supplier.
  3. Some attacks trick the reader into entering a password on a fake page.
  4. With that access they read the real emails and learn about unpaid invoices.
  5. They send a new message with changed bank details for the expected payment.
  6. Staff pay the invoice, and the money reaches an account the criminals control.

Real-world example

Think of a shop that always pays its supplier by post. A stranger learns the supplier's name and writes a letter on matching paper, saying the bank details have changed. The shop pays as usual, because the letter looks right and the amount is expected. Nobody checked by phone. The trust in the supplier's name was the whole attack.

Key takeaways

  • Always confirm changes to bank details with a known phone number, never by reply email.
  • Protect mailboxes with strong sign-in checks, because reading mail reveals your plans.
  • A shared toolkit means blocking one program rarely stops this kind of fraud.

In more detail

SilverTerrier is a label that Palo Alto Networks applied to a large, loosely connected community of Nigerian cybercriminals rather than to a single hierarchical group. Reporting described hundreds of individuals sharing tooling, tutorials and infrastructure to run business email compromise, a fraud in which attackers impersonate a trusted colleague or supplier and redirect a payment. The activity is unusual because the participants reuse commodity malware, free hosting and stolen credentials at high volume, and because the same tool may appear across many unrelated actors. For defenders, the practical consequence is that blocking one family of tools changes little, while mailbox protection, payment verification by phone and monitoring of suspicious sign-ins address the fraud directly. Attribution by country or nickname should be treated with caution, since participants cooperate loosely and public reporting covers only part of the activity.
