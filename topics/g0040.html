G0040 Patchwork

G0040Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Patchwork is a name that security researchers use for a group that carries out long-term spying attacks. It tries to break into computers that hold valuable information, such as government offices, research centres and defence teams. Researchers describe its tools as a mix of code taken from public projects and stitched together, which is where the name comes from. Other names for related activity include Dropping Elephant and Operation Hangover.

Why should you care?

Groups like this do not attack everyone. They choose a small number of organisations and then stay quiet for a long time. If you work in a government office, a research team or a large company, an email that looks ordinary may be the first step of an intrusion. Spotting odd behaviour on one laptop early can stop a whole campaign before data leaves. Ignoring strange document prompts or unexpected new scheduled tasks makes the attackers' job much easier.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers first study a target organisation and note which people hold useful information.
  2. A fake email arrives that looks like a news article, a job offer or a shared document from a colleague.
  3. When the reader opens the attachment and allows the content, a small script starts running on the computer.
  4. That script fetches more files from an internet address the attacker controls and starts them in the background.
  5. The operator then reads documents, captures screenshots and sends the stolen data back over the same connection.
  6. The intrusion stays quiet, so it can remain in place for months until someone notices unusual network traffic.

Real-world example

Imagine a busy office building with one shared mailroom. Most letters are normal bills and newsletters. One day a letter arrives that looks like a harmless catalogue, but inside the cover there is a short instruction to leave the side door unlocked. The clerk follows it because the letter looks routine. That is how a document lure works. The email is the envelope, the attachment is the instruction, and the small script is the person who unlocks the door from the inside.

Key takeaways

  • Treat unexpected documents as suspicious, even when the sender name looks familiar.
  • Report strange prompts from office software instead of clicking through them.
  • Quiet intrusions usually begin with one ordinary-looking email.

In more detail

Patchwork is an espionage cluster that public reporting tracks under several names, including Dropping Elephant, Operation Hangover and Monsoon. Rather than relying on expensive zero-day exploits, reporting describes long-running spear-phishing that delivers malicious documents, scripts and copied code, which is where the descriptive name comes from. Targets reported across campaigns include government bodies, defence organisations, think tanks and academic institutions, with a concentration of activity in South Asia. Defenders usually encounter it through email gateway detections, unusual script execution from office applications, and periodic outbound connections from user workstations to small hosting providers. Two misunderstandings are common: that skilled espionage always needs advanced exploits, and that a group reusing public code is therefore unsophisticated. Attribution of this activity is a vendor assessment made with moderate confidence, and different companies group the campaigns differently.
