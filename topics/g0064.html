G0064 APT33

G0064Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

APT33 is a spy group that public reports link to Iran. The letters APT stand for advanced persistent threat, which means a patient attacker who stays in a network for a long time. Security companies also call this group HOLMIUM, Elfin or Peach Sandstorm.

Why should you care?

APT33 targets companies that build aircraft, defence equipment and energy systems. A spy who reads those files can learn about technology, contracts and safety plans. The group also matters because its methods are not exotic. Job offers, fake websites and simple password guessing do most of the work. That means the best protections are ordinary ones. Strong passwords, multi factor login and careful staff who question unexpected messages make a real difference here.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers first study the target company, reading websites, job posts and public documents.
  2. They build fake recruitment pages or send job offers to employees who might be curious.
  3. A link or attachment installs a program that gives the attackers a quiet way back in.
  4. They try small lists of common passwords against many accounts, a trick called password spraying.
  5. Once inside, they create new accounts and scheduled jobs to keep their access alive.
  6. They then collect documents about projects, contracts and technology, and send them out.

Real-world example

Imagine an airport queue where a staff member is offered a better job by a friendly stranger. The stranger only asks for a work email address to send the contract. That email later installs a spy program on the staff laptop. Nothing about the conversation felt like an attack. APT33 works this way. The bait is opportunity, not fear, and that is exactly why it succeeds so often against careful, ambitious people.

Key takeaways

  • Job themed phishing is a common and effective lure from this group.
  • Password spraying uses a few common passwords across many accounts, so lockouts and alerts help.
  • Public reporting attributes the group to Iranian interests with varying levels of confidence.

In more detail

APT33, tracked as G0064 and known in public reporting as HOLMIUM, Elfin and Peach Sandstorm, is an espionage group active since at least 2013 and assessed by multiple vendors to operate in support of Iranian state interests, with that attribution presented as an assessment rather than a settled fact. Its targeting has concentrated on aviation, aerospace and energy organisations, particularly in the Middle East, the United States and South Korea. The group is best known for extensive reconnaissance of victim organisations before intrusion, for spear phishing campaigns that deliver malicious links or attachments with job themed lures, and for custom tooling used for credential theft, command execution and persistence. Reporting has also described use of password spraying, in which a small number of common passwords is tried against many accounts, and abuse of legitimate remote access for later stage access. Investigators find traces in email and web proxy logs, authentication records showing widespread failed logins, endpoint telemetry of scheduled tasks or added accounts, and network beacons to small hosting providers.

What you will practise

  • Explain what APT33 is, including the aliases used for it in public reporting.
  • Describe the reconnaissance and job themed phishing that characterise its initial access.
  • Explain how password spraying differs from brute force attacks and how to detect it.
  • Identify the persistence techniques the group uses to keep access after a reboot.
  • Explain why aviation, aerospace and energy organisations are attractive espionage targets.
