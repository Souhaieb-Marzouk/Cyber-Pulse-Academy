T1583.008 Acquire Infrastructure: Malvertising

T1583.008Resource DevelopmentExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Malvertising means using paid online ads to spread malicious software. The attacker buys advertising space, then puts harmful content or links inside it. This sub-technique is about buying those ads as part of the preparation for an attack. The parent technique covers acquiring infrastructure in general.

Why should you care?

People trust ads that appear above real search results. A fake ad for a well-known program can lead to a copy that contains malware. Victims may not need to click anything at all if the ad carries code that attacks the browser. Legitimate websites carrying the ad look blameless.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker opens an advertising account and funds it, sometimes with a stolen card.
  2. They create ads that copy the name and look of popular, trusted software.
  3. They use the network's targeting to reach chosen countries, industries, or job roles.
  4. Automated review traffic is sent to harmless pages, so the ad passes inspection.
  5. Real visitors who match the target profile are routed to a clone site or a harmful file.
  6. Domains and ads are replaced quickly to survive takedown and reputation checks.

Real-world example

Picture a post office with a wall of small adverts. Someone pins an advert that looks exactly like the official passport service. Travelers queue at the wrong counter and hand over documents and fees. The wall is genuine, and the staff never check who pinned the card.

Key takeaways

  • Results at the top of a search page can be paid adverts rather than real matches.
  • Software should be downloaded from the maker's own site, not from an advert.
  • Repeated brand-name downloads from fresh domains are worth investigating as one campaign.

In more detail

Malvertising is the purchase of online advertising space that is then used to deliver malicious content to selected victims. Under Acquire Infrastructure, the acquired asset is an ad placement and its audience targeting, not a machine or a domain. Ads are bought to sit prominently in search results or on popular sites, often spoofing well-known brands, and clicks may lead to cloned download pages offering trojanised installers. Targeting features of the advertising network let the operator reach specific industries, regions, or software users, borrowing the trust that users place in search engines and reputable publishers. Some campaigns need no click at all, because the ad creative itself carries code that exploits the browser. Evasion is built into the delivery: automated review traffic is routed to harmless pages while genuine clicks from validated targets receive the malicious content, so the ad network's own checks may never see it. Detection therefore leans on endpoint and proxy evidence such as browser processes spawning installers, downloads from newly registered domains appearing in referral chains, cloned software branding, and correlation between ad impressions and subsequent infections.
