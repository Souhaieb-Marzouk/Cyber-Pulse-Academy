M1020 SSL/TLS Inspection

M1020Enterprise MitigationVery hardMitigation

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

SSL and TLS are the locks that protect traffic on the internet, such as the lock in your browser. SSL/TLS Inspection means a security device opens that traffic inside your company and reads it. Then the device locks the traffic again.

Why should you care?

Attackers now hide almost everything inside encrypted traffic, so an unreadable network is a blind network. Inspection lets your tools see malware downloads and stolen data leaving. Without it, your defences only see addresses and names, never content. The cost is that inspection must be planned carefully, because it can break services and expose private data.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Your device connects to the security tool instead of the real website, because network settings point it there.
  2. The tool shows the device a certificate from your own internal authority, which the device already trusts.
  3. The tool decrypts the traffic and checks the content against your security and data rules.
  4. Clean traffic is re-encrypted in a second connection from the tool to the real website.
  5. Policy exceptions skip inspection for sensitive categories such as health, banking, and legal services.
  6. The tool logs what it inspected and what it blocked, and your team watches for broken applications.

Real-world example

Imagine a postal room that opens every parcel, checks it, and seals it again. This catches dangerous items that a sealed box would hide. The room needs its own official stamp, so senders trust the resealed parcel. Some letters are legally protected and must pass through unopened. The room also slows every delivery and holds a copy of everything for a moment. SSL/TLS Inspection is that postal room for your network traffic.

Key takeaways

  • Inspection gives visibility, but it also weakens end to end privacy, so decide the rules first.
  • Protect the internal certificate authority, because it can pretend to be any site.
  • Expect some applications to break, and keep a clear list of traffic that is never inspected.

In more detail

SSL/TLS Inspection is the controlled breaking of an encrypted connection inside your own network so that security tools can read what the traffic actually contains. The mechanism is a trusted man in the middle: the inspection device presents a certificate signed by an internal authority the client already trusts, terminates the client session, inspects or logs the plaintext, then opens its own session to the real server and re-encrypts. It sits inline at the network boundary or in front of a proxy, and it is what allows intrusion prevention, data loss prevention, malware analysis, and content filtering to work at all on modern traffic. Evidence appears as certificates issued by the internal authority, TLS handshake and decryption events, certificate validation errors on clients, a rise in blocked or inspected sessions, and application failures caused by pinning. The common misunderstandings are that inspection is free, universal, and invisible. It costs latency and hardware, it breaks applications that pin certificates or use mutual authentication, it creates a powerful internal authority whose private key must be protected, and it turns the inspecting device into a place where sensitive personal data exists in readable form. Many organisations therefore exclude banking, healthcare, and legal categories by policy, and some jurisdictions restrict the practice outright.

What you will practise

  • Explain how TLS interception works and why clients accept the inspecting certificate.
  • Identify which security controls depend on decrypted traffic to function.
  • Describe the operational and legal limits of inspecting encrypted traffic.
  • Explain why certificate pinning and mutual authentication break under inspection.
  • Plan policy exceptions for sensitive or legally protected categories.
