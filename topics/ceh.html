CEH EC-Council CEH

CEHEC-CouncilVery hardCertificationUnofficial Study Resource

The EC-Council Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) is a vendor-specific, knowledge-based credential that certifies a practitioner can operate like an attacker inside an authorised engagement, using the same reconnaissance, enumeration and exploitation tooling as an adversary in order to find weaknesses before someone hostile does. It suits network and system administrators, SOC analysts, auditors, developers and career changers who need a working command of offensive tradecraft rather than a single toolchain, and it assumes reasonable grounding in networking, operating systems and defensive controls. To an employer the certification signals familiarity with a broad, structured methodology spanning network, web, wireless, mobile, cloud, IoT and operational technology targets, together with an understanding of authorisation, scope and the legal and ethical boundaries of testing. The knowledge exam is a proctored, primarily multiple-choice instrument drawn from a large item pool in a single timed sitting, with scenario items that ask the candidate to choose the appropriate technique, tool category or countermeasure, and the terminology of commercial tools appears throughout. EC-Council also offers a separate practical examination in which candidates work through challenge scenarios against live systems within a fixed window, and many candidates sit both. Because multiple-choice testing rewards recognition over recall, self-study alone tends to plateau; candidates who build isolated lab ranges, run the tools against deliberately vulnerable systems they own, and read the resulting packets and logs retain the material, recognise the phrasing used in items, and finish the sitting with time to spare.

Exam domains and objectives

  • Ethical and Legal Framework: covers authorisation, scope definition, rules of engagement, and the professional and legal limits placed on offensive testing.
  • Footprinting and Reconnaissance: covers passive and active information gathering against an authorised target, including open-source, DNS and infrastructure profiling.
  • Scanning and Enumeration: covers host discovery, port and service identification, and extracting account, share, service and directory detail from reachable systems.
  • Vulnerability Analysis: covers identifying and validating weaknesses, interpreting scanner output critically, and ranking issues by realistic business impact.
  • System Attack Techniques: covers password, privilege and access attacks against authorised hosts, including how small weaknesses are chained into a foothold.
  • Malware and Payload Concepts: covers how malicious software is categorised, delivered and controlled, and how defenders recognise it without handling live samples.
  • Sniffing, Session and Denial-of-Service Concepts: covers traffic interception, session handling weaknesses and resource-exhaustion attacks at a conceptual and defensive level.
  • Web, Database and Application Attacks: covers server and application weaknesses, injection classes, and the controls that reliably prevent them.
  • Wireless, Mobile, IoT and Operational Technology: covers the attack surface of radio, handheld, embedded and industrial environments and the controls specific to each.
  • Cloud and Container Attack Surface: covers identity, storage and configuration weaknesses in hosted environments and the shared-responsibility questions they raise.
  • Cryptography: covers encryption, hashing, PKI and common implementation mistakes, and how cryptography both protects a target and undermines it when misused.
  • Evasion and Defence Awareness: covers how attackers avoid detection and how firewalls, IDS and monitoring are tuned and evaded in practice.
