T1592.001 Gather Victim Host Information: Hardware

T1592.001ReconnaissanceExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This sub-technique focuses on the physical machines rather than their software. It covers the make and model of servers, laptops, and network devices. It also covers extra security hardware, such as fingerprint readers or dedicated encryption cards.

Why should you care?

Some attacks only work against certain hardware. Knowing the exact model tells an attacker which ones will fit. Extra security hardware is also worth knowing about, because it shows which data is well protected. If you publish equipment lists, you hand over this detail for free.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker reads job adverts that list the servers, laptops, and network devices you use.
  2. They collect equipment names from assessment reports, resumes, and purchase documents that leak.
  3. They scan hosts and read banners and user agent strings to identify model families.
  4. They look for signs of extra security hardware, such as smart card readers or encryption modules.
  5. They compare the details with public lists of known weaknesses for that equipment.
  6. They use the result to plan a hardware attack or a delivery of tainted devices.

Real-world example

Think of a locksmith called to open a flat door without a key. The locksmith first looks at the keyhole and the brand stamped on the lock face. Different brands need different tools and different amounts of time. Knowing the brand saves a wasted journey. Attackers study hardware in exactly that way. They want the make and model before choosing a method. Extra items such as a fingerprint reader tell them the door is better protected than most. Hardware gathering is the locksmith's first glance, but done from far away.

Key takeaways

  • Keep an accurate asset list, because you cannot protect hardware you have not recorded.
  • Avoid publishing exact device models and versions in job adverts, reports, and resumes.
  • Track who may add devices to your network, since hardware can arrive from outside.

In more detail

This sub-technique narrows host information gathering to physical equipment: the make, model, and version of servers, workstations, laptops, and network devices on specific hosts, plus the presence of additional components that signal stronger protection, such as card or biometric readers and dedicated encryption hardware. The distinction from the parent matters because it is about the machine itself rather than the software running on it, and because some of the most useful indicators of defensive maturity are hardware facts. Adversaries assemble it from active scanning of hostnames, server banners, and user agent strings, from phishing for information, and heavily from documents that leak equipment detail such as job adverts, network maps, assessment reports, resumes, and purchase invoices. The purpose is selection: matching methods to known device families, anticipating that some hosts are better protected, or planning compromise through the hardware supply chain or direct hardware additions. Detection is largely asset-management and physical in nature, since procurement records, device connection logs, and inventory reconciliation expose unexpected hardware far more reliably than network signatures.

What you will practise

  • Explain what hardware gathering collects and how it differs from general host information gathering.
  • Identify documents and public sources that disclose equipment models and versions.
  • Describe how scanning techniques identify hardware families remotely.
  • Explain why the presence of security hardware is itself useful information to an adversary.
  • Recommend asset management and publication practices that reduce hardware detail leakage.
