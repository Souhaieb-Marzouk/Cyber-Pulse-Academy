G0028 Threat Group-1314

G0028Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Threat Group-1314, or TG-1314, is a spying group that public reports say has targeted defence companies since about 2012. It starts by sending carefully written emails with harmful attachments. After that, it keeps hidden software running inside the network for a long time. Researchers track it as a single activity group.

Why should you care?

Defence and engineering companies hold information that other countries want. A single opened attachment can give an intruder months of quiet access. The worst damage is often the documents that leave the network, not the computer that stopped working. Blocking one email does not end the campaign. That is why long-term monitoring and fast removal both matter.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers research the company and write to employees whose work gives them access to useful files.
  2. The email carries an attachment or link that appears to be a normal business document.
  3. Opening it runs hidden software that records what the user types, including passwords.
  4. That software connects to an external server, so the attackers can give new instructions at any time.
  5. They add more tools, create fresh ways to run them, and keep access even if one part is removed.
  6. They collect documents and send them out slowly, so the traffic looks unremarkable.

Real-world example

Imagine a sealed, official-looking letter delivered to an office. The envelope is opened by the right person at the right desk, exactly as the sender planned. Inside is not a letter but a tiny listening device. It stays in the room for a year and nobody questions it, because it arrived looking official. That is how a well-made phishing message works.

Key takeaways

  • One successful phishing message can give an intruder access for many months.
  • Detecting what software does is more reliable than only blocking names you already know.
  • Credentials are a favourite target, so unusual login patterns deserve attention.

In more detail

Threat Group-1314, usually shortened to TG-1314, is an espionage cluster that public reporting describes as active since at least 2012, mainly against the defence industrial base of the United States and its partners. Documented tradecraft begins with targeted phishing carrying malicious attachments, followed by custom backdoors and keylogging tools that collect credentials, documents and correspondence over long periods. Researchers note that the group adapted its tooling after public disclosure, abandoning malware families that had become easy to detect and replacing identified infrastructure. That adaptation is the analytic point: signature-based detection of a named family ages badly, while behavioural detection of credential access and outbound command-and-control traffic stays useful. The phishing message is only the first minute of a campaign that continues quietly for months.
