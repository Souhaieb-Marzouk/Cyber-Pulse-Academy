Case Management

SOC & Detection EngineeringHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Case management means keeping all the information about one security problem together in one place. Alerts, notes, evidence, and decisions are joined into a single record. That record follows the work from the first warning until the problem is closed. Several people can work on it and see the same history.

Why should you care?

One attack can create many alerts on many machines. Without a shared record, several analysts repeat the same work. Important details get lost in private messages and email. When a manager asks what happened, nobody can give a full answer. A single clear record saves time and protects the company during review.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. An alert arrives from a security tool and someone decides how serious it is.
  2. Related alerts and evidence are joined into one case with a single owner.
  3. The analyst records findings, actions, and decisions in the case as they work.
  4. If the problem grows, the case is escalated with all its history attached.
  5. Evidence is stored carefully with notes about who collected it and when.
  6. When the case closes, the record is kept so lessons and reviews can use it.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital night desk. When a patient arrives, staff open one paper file. Every nurse and doctor writes their observations in that same file. The next shift reads the file and continues without asking the patient everything again. Tests, medicines, and decisions are all in one place. The file travels with the patient.

Key takeaways

  • Group related alerts into one case so the investigation has a single history.
  • Record what you decided and why, not only what you did.
  • Keep evidence and its details together so later reviews can trust it.

In more detail

Case management in a security operations centre is the discipline of grouping related alerts and evidence into a single investigation record that carries the work from first detection through triage, escalation, containment, closure, and lessons learned. Its purpose is to prevent duplicate effort and lost context: one phishing campaign that fires alerts on twenty mailboxes should be one case with twenty affected assets, not twenty disconnected tickets, and the case record should preserve the timeline, the evidence and its provenance, the decisions taken with reasons, and the outcome for later review and audit. A workable process defines severity and escalation paths, named roles such as first responder and case owner, service targets for each stage, and a handover convention so an analyst starting a shift can continue an investigation without re-deriving it. Case management is also where the chain of custody begins, because evidence copied out of a system without recording who collected it and when becomes far less useful if an incident later reaches legal or regulatory review. The common mistake is treating a ticket as a case: a queue shows that work happened, whereas a well-kept case record shows what was decided, what evidence supported it, and whether the same weakness will be hit again.

What you will practise

  • Explain what case management means in a security operations centre.
  • Describe the difference between an alert, a ticket, and an investigation case.
  • Outline the stages a case passes through from detection to closure.
  • Explain why a timeline, decisions, and evidence provenance are recorded in a case.
  • Describe how escalation paths and service targets make case handling consistent.
  • Explain how closed cases support reporting, learning, and regulatory review.
