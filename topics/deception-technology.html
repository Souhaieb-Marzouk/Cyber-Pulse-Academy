Deception Technology

Security ArchitectureHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Deception technology places fake systems and fake files on a network to catch intruders. These decoys look real, but no honest user has a reason to touch them. When someone does touch one, the security team gets a strong warning.

Why should you care?

Security teams see thousands of alerts, and most of them are harmless. A warning from a decoy is different, because normal work never touches it. That makes it easier to react fast and to trust the signal. Decoys can also slow an attacker down and push them away from real systems. Without a trap like this, an intruder may move around for weeks before anyone notices.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The team creates fake servers, shared folders, files and login details that look real.
  2. These decoys are placed where an intruder would search, mixed with real assets.
  3. Small clues, called breadcrumbs, lead an intruder from real systems toward a decoy.
  4. Real users never touch the decoys, so any contact is suspicious by design.
  5. The system records the action and alerts the security team at once.
  6. The team follows a written plan to check the host and contain the intruder.

Real-world example

Think of a shop that expects burglars. The owner puts a small amount of marked money in the till and a fake safe in an obvious place. Real staff know the safe is empty, so they never open it during the day. If the alarm rings at night, the owner knows the visitor was not a member of staff. The marked notes help the police follow the money afterwards. The plan works because honest people stay away from the trap. The owner also knows what to do when it triggers.

Key takeaways

  • Decoys work because normal users never touch them.
  • Write the response plan before you deploy the decoys.
  • Use deception alongside monitoring and segmentation, not instead of them.

In more detail

Deception technology places believable but fake assets on a network, such as decoy servers, file shares, credentials and documents, so an intruder who is already inside reveals themselves by touching something no legitimate user has a reason to touch. Alerts from these lures are high fidelity because the decoys carry no production traffic, which lets defenders skip a long triage queue and act on a signal that is almost always malicious. Modern platforms blend real and fake data, plant breadcrumbs that steer an attacker away from a real host and toward a decoy, and cover endpoints, identity and cloud workloads as well as the network. The common mistake is deploying decoys without a playbook: if nobody knows what a hit means or who responds, the alert is wasted. Deception complements detection and segmentation, and it does not replace either.

What you will practise

  • Explain what deception technology is and why its alerts are high fidelity.
  • Describe the kinds of decoy that can be deployed across network, endpoint and cloud.
  • Explain how breadcrumbs influence an intruder's movement toward a decoy.
  • Identify the operational readiness needed before decoys go live.
  • Distinguish deception from prevention controls and state how they combine.
