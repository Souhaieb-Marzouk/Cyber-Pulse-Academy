G0137 Ferocious Kitten

G0137Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Ferocious Kitten is a name researchers use for a group that spies on people rather than on companies. Public reports link it to Iranian interests, and its targets have included people inside Iran and members of diaspora communities. It starts conversations on social media and messaging apps.

Why should you care?

This group does not attack your office network. It makes contact with you as a person, often as someone who shares your views or your worries. That means a firewall cannot protect you. The programs it sends can watch your screen, record what you type and listen through your microphone. For activists, journalists and researchers, that is a direct threat to personal safety.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker studies the target's public posts, friends and interests for weeks.
  2. They create a profile that appears to share the same cause or profession.
  3. They make contact and build trust through normal, friendly conversation.
  4. They send an archive with a real photo or document plus a hidden program.
  5. The victim opens the visible file, and the hidden program starts in the background.
  6. The program records typing, screen activity and sound, then uploads what it finds.

Real-world example

Picture a community meeting where people exchange leaflets. A newcomer hands you a leaflet that looks right and a memory stick with more information. You take both home and plug in the stick, because you trust the room. The leaflet was real, but the stick had a second purpose. That is how contact-based attacks work: the trust comes from the cause, not from the technology.

Key takeaways

  • Treat unexpected files from online contacts as dangerous, even when the person seems genuine.
  • Use a separate device for sensitive activism or journalism work when possible.
  • Cover your camera and review which apps can use your microphone.

In more detail

Ferocious Kitten is an espionage cluster that public reporting associates with Iranian interests and that has been described targeting individuals inside Iran as well as regional and diaspora communities, which distinguishes it from clusters that focus on foreign government networks. Reporting describes extensive reconnaissance of the target before contact, including review of social media and public profiles, followed by social engineering on messaging platforms where the operator poses as an activist, a journalist or a member of an opposition group. Payloads have been reported as archive files containing an executable accompanied by decoy documents and images, so the victim sees a plausible file while a background process establishes access, and the resulting backdoor provides file collection, screen capture, keylogging and audio capture. The group's choice of domestic and diaspora targets means traditional network perimeter controls are largely irrelevant, because contact starts on consumer platforms. Attribution statements carry limited confidence in public reporting and the tooling is comparatively simple. Evidence sits in chat platform records, download and execution chains on the endpoint, and uploads of collected material.

What you will practise

  • Explain why person-focused espionage cannot be stopped by perimeter network controls.

  • Identify the capabilities a typical information stealer provides to an operator.
  • Explain how decoy documents hide a malicious executable in an archive.
  • Recommend practical protections for high-risk individuals such as journalists and activists.
