Incident Response Playbook

SOC & Detection EngineeringHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

An incident response playbook is a written, step-by-step plan for handling one type of security incident. It starts at the first alert and ends when the case is closed. It lists who does what, in which order. The goal is that any analyst on shift handles the incident the same good way.

Why should you care?

During a real incident, people are stressed and the clock is running. Without a written plan, they guess, and they forget steps. A good playbook also slows you down in the right place. It reminds you to gather information before you take a big action that could knock a system offline.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The playbook opens with a trigger: the exact alert or situation that starts this procedure.
  2. It names the roles involved and who has authority to approve risky actions.
  3. It lists the information to collect first, such as who owns the machine and how critical it is.
  4. Then it gives ordered steps with the exact commands or queries to run.
  5. At each decision point it states the criteria for each branch, so choices are not guesswork.
  6. It ends with closure rules: what evidence to save and what must be recorded.

Real-world example

Think of the safety card in an aeroplane seat pocket. It does not explain how planes fly. It shows you, in order, what to do if the cabin loses pressure. You read it once when calm, and then you follow it when you are not calm. An incident playbook is that card for a security team. It turns panic into a sequence of small, clear steps.

Key takeaways

  • Write a playbook for each alert type that happens often.
  • Always gather information before you disable an account or isolate a machine.
  • Give every playbook an owner and a review date, because systems change.

In more detail

A playbook in security operations is a documented, versioned procedure that specifies how a defined class of alert or incident is handled, from the first acknowledgement through to closure and follow-up. It exists so that response quality does not depend on which analyst is on shift. A usable playbook is more specific than a policy and more procedural than a runbook index. It states the trigger conditions that invoke it, the roles and who has authority to act, the required inputs such as the alert payload and enrichment sources, the ordered steps with the exact queries and commands to run, the decision points with explicit criteria for each branch, the containment options available and their blast radius, the evidence to preserve and where, the escalation path with contact conditions and timeframes, and the closure criteria including what must be recorded. Two design principles separate playbooks that survive contact with reality from those that do not. First, enrichment precedes action: gather asset ownership, identity privilege, business function and recent related activity before disabling an account or isolating a host, because the outage can be worse than the incident. Second, every irreversible step has a named approver and a documented reversal path. Playbooks also decay. Infrastructure changes, log sources move, contact lists age and query syntax drifts, so each playbook needs an owner, a review date, and validation through tabletop exercise or live-fire test. Response metrics such as time to detect and time to contain are only meaningful when the underlying procedure is consistent and actually followed, which is the operational argument for maintaining them.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to define the required sections of an incident response playbook, from trigger conditions through closure criteria.
  • You will be able to write ordered response steps with explicit decision points and branch criteria instead of vague instructions.
  • You will be able to place enrichment before containment and justify the ordering in terms of outage risk.
  • You will be able to identify every irreversible action in a procedure and assign an approver and a reversal path to each.
  • You will be able to specify the evidence to preserve at each stage and the handling requirements that keep it usable.
  • You will be able to validate a playbook through tabletop exercise and record the gaps the exercise exposes.
  • You will be able to maintain playbooks against infrastructure drift with owners, review dates and change triggers.
