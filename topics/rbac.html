Role-Based Access Control

Identity & AccessHardKeyword

Role-based access control (RBAC) is an access control model in which permissions are assigned to roles rather than directly to users, and users receive permissions only through the roles granted to them. The model is usually decomposed into users, roles, permissions, operations and objects, with role hierarchies allowing a senior role to inherit the permissions of a junior one and constraints preventing conflicting combinations from being held simultaneously. In practice RBAC is the default model for Active Directory groups, Linux groups, Kubernetes role bindings, cloud IAM policies and nearly every commercial application, and it succeeds or fails on the quality of role design rather than on the model itself. Roles that accumulate permissions over years, roles that are granted for convenience rather than function, and role explosion where hundreds of near-duplicate roles exist because exceptions were encoded as new roles rather than handled as exceptions, all produce effective privilege that far exceeds intent. Role mining and periodic recertification are the corrective processes: derive candidate roles from observed entitlement usage, consolidate what overlaps, and require an accountable owner to affirm every assignment on a schedule. The persistent gap is that RBAC answers whether a subject holds a permission but not whether the request is appropriate for the moment, which is why organisations layer attribute-based or context-based conditions over role assignment for sensitive operations. Segregation of duties and least privilege are both expressed through RBAC, so careful attention to role-to-permission mapping and to the aggregation of inherited roles is where real assurance comes from.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to model users, roles, permissions and role hierarchies for a representative application.
  • You will be able to explain how role inheritance and the aggregation of multiple roles produce unintended effective privilege.
  • You will be able to run role mining and recertification to consolidate role explosion and remove unused entitlements.
  • You will be able to design segregation-of-duties constraints that keep conflicting duties out of one identity.
  • You will be able to review cloud IAM policies and Kubernetes role bindings for overly permissive or wildcard grants.
  • You will be able to explain the limits of RBAC for context-sensitive decisions and when to add attribute-based conditions.
