TA0001 Initial Access

TA0001EnterpriseExtremeTactic

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Initial Access is the moment an attacker first gets a way into your network. It is how someone who was outside your systems becomes someone inside them. Common routes include a convincing phishing email, a guessed or stolen password, or a weakness in a service that faces the internet.

Why should you care?

Everything an attacker does later depends on getting in first. If you can spot and block this step, the rest of the attack never starts. It is also the step that touches systems you already watch, such as email, passwords and remote access. Most break-ins here look like normal work, so context matters more than any single alert.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. An attacker studies your company first, looking for staff names, email addresses and any service you expose to the internet.
  2. They pick the easiest path, often a phishing message with a link or attachment, or a login page they can guess against.
  3. A single click, a reused password or an unpatched service gives them a working foothold on one machine or account.
  4. They then confirm the access works, often by signing in again quietly or checking what the account can reach.
  5. From that small start, they move toward the systems and data they actually want.

Real-world example

Think of a large office building. Attackers cannot walk through the front desk, so they look for a side door left open. One might pose as a delivery driver and ask a staff member to hold the door. Another finds a window that no longer locks. Once inside, they are just a person in the building, and nobody questions them. Initial Access is that first open door.

Key takeaways

  • Initial Access is the first step an attacker needs before anything else can happen.
  • Most entry attempts arrive by email or by a stolen password, not by clever code.
  • Watch your internet-facing services and your sign-in records very closely.

In more detail

Initial Access is the tactic that describes how an adversary first gains a foothold in a target network, and it is the point at which preparation on attacker-controlled infrastructure converts into presence on victim systems. The behaviours grouped here include sending phishing messages with malicious attachments or links, abusing valid accounts whose credentials were guessed, sprayed, stuffed, or purchased, exploiting public-facing applications such as web servers, VPN concentrators, and file transfer services, and using trusted external remote services with legitimate credentials. Telemetry defenders can rely on is unusually rich for this tactic because most of these paths touch perimeter and identity systems: web and reverse proxy logs, WAF alerts, VPN and remote access authentication records, mail gateway verdicts, endpoint process creation for parent processes that are email clients or browsers, and cloud identity sign-in logs showing impossible travel or unfamiliar user agents. The difficulty is not a shortage of data but a shortage of discrimination. Failed authentication attempts are constant, exploit probes against exposed services are relentless, and a successful credential-based intrusion can look exactly like an employee working from a new location. Detection therefore depends on correlation, baseline behaviour, and knowledge of which of the organisation's own assets are genuinely reachable from the internet, plus rapid triage of the small number of events that combine an unusual source, an unusual identity, and a subsequent post-access action.

What you will practise

  • Explain how Initial Access converts off-target preparation into on-target presence across phishing, valid accounts, exploitation, and external remote services.
  • Identify the highest-value telemetry sources for this tactic and the specific fields that make an event actionable.
  • Correlate perimeter exploitation attempts with subsequent authentication and endpoint activity to confirm whether access was actually obtained.
  • Distinguish credential-based initial access from legitimate user behaviour using device, location, and post-sign-in activity context.
  • Prioritise exposed assets and remote access services by business impact so that triage effort follows real risk.
  • Build a repeatable first-hour triage checklist for a suspected initial compromise.
