DET0855 Detection of Business Relationships

DET0855Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A business relationship is any company that you work with, such as a supplier or a partner. Attackers study these links before they strike. They want to know who you trust, so they can pretend to be that company. This detection looks for signs that somebody is mapping those links or copying a partner name.

Why should you care?

Most people trust a message from a known supplier. An attacker who learns the name of your supplier can send invoices, files or links that look normal. A smaller supplier often has weaker security than you do, so the attacker may enter your network through them. Your own logs rarely show the research stage of that plan. What you can see is a strange domain asking about your partners. You can also see mail that arrives from a name almost like the real one.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Your web server writes a log line each time somebody requests a page from your public site.
  2. The detection counts requests that touch partner, supplier and vendor pages from one source address.
  3. Separately, a monitoring feed reports new certificates issued for names that resemble your partners.
  4. Your mail gateway extracts the sender domain from every inbound message and compares it to an approved list.
  5. When a look alike partner domain appears in mail, or one web source reads only partner pages, the rule alerts.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital sign in sheet at the reception desk. A visitor writes a name and the person they came to see. A thief can read that sheet and learn which doctor works with which clinic. The thief then buys a white coat and says he is from that clinic. Nothing on the sheet was secret, but the pattern of visits was valuable. Your detection acts like a receptionist who notices the same stranger reading the sheet every morning. The receptionist cannot arrest anyone, but she can write down the time and tell security to watch the door.

Key takeaways

  • Attackers study your partners so they can borrow the trust you give them.
  • A look alike sender domain is often the first clue you can actually see.
  • Public partner pages are not secret, but the pattern of access still matters.

In more detail

This detection strategy looks for adversary collection of information about your business relationships, meaning the suppliers, vendors, partners and contractors that your organisation depends on. An attacker who maps those relationships can send a convincing message that appears to come from a trusted partner, or can aim at a smaller supplier whose access controls are weaker. The telemetry that helps is web server access logs for partner and supplier pages, certificate transparency monitoring for look alike domains, domain registration monitoring and inbound mail gateway records. The strategy fails in practice because most of this collection happens on somebody else's infrastructure, so you usually see only the last step, when a look alike partner domain sends mail to your staff.

What you will practise

  • Explain why an attacker maps your suppliers and business partners.
  • Identify the log sources that record collection against partner pages.
  • Describe how to compare a sender domain with an approved partner list.
  • Build a query that flags look alike partner names in inbound mail.
  • Recommend controls that reduce the risk of supplier impersonation.
