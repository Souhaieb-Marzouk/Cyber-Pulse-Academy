G0071 Orangeworm

G0071Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Orangeworm is a name used for a group of attackers that mainly targets hospitals and health companies. Public reports describe it since around 2015. It is known for staying inside a network for a long time and copying large amounts of data. The reports do not confirm who runs the group.

Why should you care?

Hospitals run equipment and software that cannot easily be stopped for updates. Patient care comes first, so security work is often delayed. A patient record also contains identity details, insurance information and medical history. If that data is copied, the harm continues long after the systems are cleaned.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker gains a first foothold, often through a supplier or a weak remote service.
  2. A backdoor is installed on a clinical or administrative computer to keep access.
  3. The attacker steals administrator credentials and then signs in to other systems at will.
  4. Shared folders and management tools are used to move deeper into the network.
  5. Large volumes of system and patient data are copied first to an internal staging area.
  6. Data leaves slowly, and the attacker stays quiet so that daily work continues normally.

Real-world example

Imagine a hospital where the maintenance team carries a master key to every ward. The key is convenient, because staff must reach equipment quickly at any hour. Now imagine that the key is copied and never reported missing. Someone can walk through every ward at night, read every chart and never break a single lock. That is how this activity spreads through a hospital network. The credentials are the master key, and the paperwork in every room is the reason the visitor keeps coming back.

Key takeaways

  • Protect administrative credentials, because they open far more doors than a single exploit.
  • Ask your suppliers how they secure remote access into your network, and write the answer down.
  • Segment clinical networks so that one infected workstation cannot reach every ward.

In more detail

Orangeworm is a tracked activity set first described in public reporting around 2015 and best known for targeting the healthcare sector, including hospitals, clinics, insurance providers and the technology suppliers that serve them, with most reported victims in the United States and Europe and a smaller number in Asia. The group's hallmark backdoor, publicly referred to as Kwampirs, has been reported in connection with these intrusions, alongside heavy use of stolen administrative credentials and lateral movement across shared network resources. A notable trait is the willingness to copy large amounts of system and configuration data rather than only personal records, which suggests reconnaissance of the estate and of the supplier relationships inside it. Public reporting has also described access obtained through software and service supply chains, where a compromised vendor of medical imaging or related software provides a route into many customer networks at once. Evidence includes the backdoor on clinical workstations and servers, service creation for persistence, authentication from unexpected hosts, large internal file transfers, and unusually long dwell times. It is worth being precise about what is and is not known: the group's motivation and sponsorship remain unconfirmed in public sources, and treating it as a purely criminal ransomware crew misreads the observed behaviour, which is quiet, persistent and collection oriented.
