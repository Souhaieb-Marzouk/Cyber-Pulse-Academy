GDPR

GRC & ComplianceVery hardKeyword

The General Data Protection Regulation is the European Union legal framework governing the processing of personal data, and it applies to organisations established in the EU as well as organisations outside the EU that offer goods or services to, or monitor the behaviour of, people in the EU. Its obligations are built on principles rather than prescriptive controls: lawfulness, fairness and transparency; purpose limitation; data minimisation; accuracy; storage limitation; integrity and confidentiality; and accountability, where the organisation must be able to demonstrate compliance rather than merely assert it. For security practitioners the regulation matters in four operational areas. Article 32 requires appropriate technical and organisational measures proportionate to risk, which is where encryption, pseudonymisation, access control, resilience and testing of those measures land. Articles 33 and 34 set breach notification duties, with a seventy-two hour window to the supervisory authority once the controller becomes aware, so incident response runbooks must be able to answer what data, how many people, what categories and what residual risk within hours. Chapter IV introduces the data protection impact assessment for high-risk processing and the records of processing activities that underpin it. Chapter V governs transfers outside the EU and drives data residency, standard contractual clauses and transfer impact assessments. The security team's artefact trail, including detection records, containment timelines and post-incident reviews, is frequently the evidence that a supervisory authority examines.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to map the data protection principles onto concrete engineering and operational controls.
  • You will be able to determine what Article 32 appropriate measures mean for a given processing activity and risk level.
  • You will be able to run a breach assessment that produces the information a seventy-two hour notification requires.
  • You will be able to support a data protection impact assessment with accurate data flow and risk evidence.
  • You will be able to explain how international transfer restrictions change architecture and vendor selection.
  • You will be able to identify where a controller and a processor hold different obligations in an incident.
