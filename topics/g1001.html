G1001 HEXANE

G1001Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

HEXANE is a name used in public research for a group that spies on companies in the Middle East. It has targeted phone operators, oil and gas firms and government offices. Spying here means stealing information quietly over a long period of time.

Why should you care?

Telephone operators and energy companies hold data that matters to governments, and they also run services people depend on every day. The group is patient, so it may sit inside a network for months before anyone notices. Phishing messages that look like job offers or business requests are hard to spot, because they match normal email. If you ignore quiet, low volume activity, you may miss the whole intrusion.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The group sends emails that look like job offers or business requests to selected employees.
  2. A malicious document or link runs a small program that collects basic details about the computer.
  3. The program reports back, and the attacker decides whether this victim is worth more effort.
  4. A stronger backdoor is delivered, giving the attacker steady control of the machine.
  5. The attacker steals credentials and explores other systems using accounts that already exist.
  6. Communication is slow and rare, so it looks like normal background traffic on the network.

Real-world example

Imagine a hotel reception desk that receives a call from someone claiming to be a recruiter. He asks friendly questions about the manager, the shifts and the computer system, then calls again a week later. Each call is short and polite, so the staff think nothing of it. Over months he learns the whole layout of the hotel. HEXANE behaves like that caller. It asks small questions, waits, and returns. The damage comes from patience, not from a single loud action.

Key takeaways

  • Treat job offers and business requests in email as untrusted until you verify the sender.
  • Low and slow network traffic still needs monitoring, because quiet attacks last longer.
  • Public reporting links this cluster to Iranian interests with moderate confidence only.

In more detail

HEXANE is a suspected state linked espionage cluster that public reporting has followed since roughly 2018, most often in connection with telecommunications operators, oil and gas companies, aviation and government targets across the Middle East. Reporting describes a phishing first pattern in which recruitment and business themes carry malicious documents or links, followed by information gathering utilities that profile the host before a more capable backdoor is delivered. The operators have been reported to use both custom and repurposed tooling, to maintain long beacon intervals, and to reuse established command and control infrastructure across campaigns. Defender visible evidence therefore clusters in mail telemetry, document execution chains, periodic low volume outbound traffic to repeated destinations, and credential use from unexpected networks. A common misunderstanding is that a modest technical footprint means a modest threat: persistence and patience, not sophistication, is what makes this cluster effective. Public research associates the activity with Iranian interests at moderate confidence and links overlapping campaigns to several aliases, so conclusions should stay hedged.

What you will practise

  • Describe the phishing first intrusion pattern reported for this cluster and the reconnaissance stage that follows it.
  • Explain why low volume, long interval command and control traffic is difficult to detect with threshold based alerting.
  • Correlate mail telemetry, document execution evidence and authentication records into a single intrusion timeline.
  • Assess the confidence level behind public attribution of this cluster to a state sponsor and communicate it honestly.
  • Recommend monitoring improvements for sectors such as telecommunications and energy that face this targeting.
