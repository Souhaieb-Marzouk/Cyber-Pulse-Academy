DET0550 Detecting Suspicious Access to CRM Data in SaaS Environments

DET0550Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A CRM is software that stores customer details, deals and contact history. This detection watches for people or programs pulling large amounts of that data out of the platform. It looks at who exported records, from where, and how much they took.

Why should you care?

Customer lists are easy to sell and hard to replace. An attacker with a working CRM login can copy years of sales data in a few minutes. The platform may look healthy while this happens, because the account is valid. Catching the export while it runs gives you a chance to revoke access early.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The CRM platform writes an audit event for every sign in, every API call and every report or export action.
  2. A SIEM, which is a central system that collects and searches log events, brings those events into one place.
  3. The rule filters for export, report and bulk read actions rather than normal browsing.
  4. It compares the number of records read with the normal amount for that user and that hour of the day.
  5. It adds context, such as a new source address, a new device, or an unusual hour in the user's local time.
  6. When the volume and the context both look wrong, the rule raises an alert for the identity team to review.

Real-world example

Imagine a public library where each visitor may read books on site. A visitor who photocopies the whole reference section in one night is not reading, they are copying. The library sign in sheet plus the photocopier counter is what proves it. In a CRM, the sign in event and the export record play exactly those two roles.

Key takeaways

  • CRM exports are normal, so the signal is volume, timing and source address, never the export itself.
  • Turn on the tenant audit log before an incident, because the events cannot be recovered later.
  • Tie every export alert to an account owner so someone can confirm whether the action was expected.

In more detail

This strategy covers Data from Information Repositories: Customer Relationship Management, meaning the theft of customer records, sales pipelines and contact data held in a SaaS CRM platform. It matters because CRM data is often the most valuable commercial asset a company holds and is frequently the reason attackers target business applications at all. The enabling telemetry lives in the SaaS audit trail: sign in events, API request logs, report and list view export records, bulk record read counts, permission and role changes, and connected application authorisations. The main way it fails in practice is that CRM platforms record that an export happened but not what was inside it, so analysts must lean on volume, timing, source address and the exporting account, and the data is only available if the tenant audit log or event monitoring product is enabled and retained.

What you will practise

  • Describe how SaaS CRM audit trails record sign ins, API calls and record exports.
  • Distinguish legitimate sales reporting from bulk extraction using record counts and source context.
  • Correlate CRM sign in events with permission changes and connected application authorisations.
  • Explain why tenant audit logging must be enabled and retained for this detection to work at all.
  • Write tuning thresholds based on a per user baseline of normal export volume.
  • Identify which stakeholders to involve when a CRM data extraction alert fires.
