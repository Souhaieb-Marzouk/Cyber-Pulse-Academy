DET0873 Detection of Establish Accounts

DET0873Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Account establishment means an attacker creates new accounts to use during an attack. They may register free mail accounts, cloud tenants or service logins. This detection looks for the creation and first use of those accounts before they are used against you.

Why should you care?

Every later step needs an identity the attacker controls. Phishing needs a mailbox. Data theft needs storage. Remote control needs a login. Those accounts leave creation and first use records. If you spot a burst of new accounts from odd sources, you may be seeing the start of a campaign. That warning gives you time to block the source before the attack arrives.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Identity providers and mail services log when an account is created, including the source address, the device and the name chosen.
  2. Those records are collected into your SIEM or reviewed through the provider console.
  3. The rule counts how many accounts appear in a short window from one address, one device or one payment or verification method.
  4. It also checks the name and domain pattern against real company information, looking for lookalike names.
  5. When a burst matches, the alert lists the accounts, the shared attributes and the first sign in of each one.

Real-world example

Think of a library card desk. One person joining is normal. Twenty people joining in ten minutes, all giving the same postal address and all asking for a card in a similar name, is a pattern worth a phone call to the branch manager.

Key takeaways

  • Account creation is preparation, so it is worth detecting before the accounts are used.
  • Volume, shared attributes and lookalike naming are the three strongest signals.
  • Most of this telemetry lives with the provider, so collection and review must be arranged in advance.

In more detail

This detection strategy covers account establishment, where an adversary creates the email, cloud and service accounts that carry a later operation. It matters because these accounts sit at the centre of phishing, data staging and command and control, and because signup behaviour is one of the few places where infrastructure preparation becomes visible to defenders. The telemetry that makes it possible includes provider signup and audit logs, single sign on provisioning records with source address and device, and mail gateway entries for new sending domains. The strategy fails in practice because new accounts are created in huge numbers for good reasons, so the rule must distinguish automation with a change record from bursts that share one source, one naming pattern and one payment method.

What you will practise

  • Explain how attacker controlled accounts support phishing, staging and remote access
  • Identify the account creation and first use events available from your providers
  • Describe the shared attributes that reveal a coordinated signup burst
  • Recognise lookalike domains and names that imitate your organisation
  • Write a rule that counts new accounts per source address or verification method
