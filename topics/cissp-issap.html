Simple definition

The Information Systems Security Architecture Professional (ISSAP) is an advanced certification from ISC2. It is only open to people who already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP). It focuses on designing security for large systems, rather than on running them day by day.

Why should you care?

Architects decide how a whole system will be protected before it is built. Those choices are hard and costly to change later. This certificate proves you can make them well. It is a strong signal for senior roles and for consultancy work. It also deepens the knowledge you already proved in the core CISSP exam.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Check that you hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) and meet the rules for taking a concentration exam.
  2. Read the official ISC2 exam outline and list the domains with the tasks under each one.
  3. Study security architecture models and the frameworks that large organisations use.
  4. Go deeper on cryptography, identity and access management, and network design.
  5. Practise scenario questions that ask which design best meets a stated business need.
  6. The exam is multiple choice and is separate from the core exam, so plan several months of study.

Real-world example

Imagine a company that wants one login for staff, partners and customers. A junior engineer may reach for a single large directory. An architect asks harder questions. How do we prove who a partner is, what happens when their system is offline, and how do we remove access the same day a contract ends? The architecture answer often combines several services with clear trust rules. The ISSAP exam tests that kind of thinking.

Key takeaways

  • You must already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) before you sit this exam.
  • The questions are deeper, so study design trade offs and not only definitions.
  • Learn to justify a design to a business audience as well as to engineers.

In more detail

The Information Systems Security Architecture Professional (ISSAP) is a concentration exam from ISC2 for holders of the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) who specialise in security architecture. It sits at an advanced level and suits senior architects, security engineers and consultants who design controls for large or complex organisations. It proves to an employer that you can turn business needs into an architecture, choose cryptographic and identity solutions, and defend design choices to executives and auditors. The exam covers six published domains, including identity and access management, security architecture models, and the security operations and governance that support them; it uses multiple choice items written at a much deeper level than the core CISSP exam. It leads to roles such as security architect, enterprise architect, principal consultant and technical security lead. Exam domains and objectives

  • Translate business and regulatory needs into security architecture requirements
  • Compare architecture models and choose one that fits the organisation
  • Choose cryptographic controls and explain the trade offs to decision makers
  • Design identity and access management for staff, partners and customers
  • Plan security monitoring, response and recovery as part of the architecture
  • Assess how a design change affects risk, cost and compliance
