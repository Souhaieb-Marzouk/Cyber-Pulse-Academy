Simple definition

This is a professional certificate about information security risk management, based on a published international standard, ISO 27005. Risk management means deciding what could go wrong, how likely it is and how much harm it would cause. The lead part means you learn to guide the whole process, not just fill in a form.

Why should you care?

Every company has more weaknesses than time or money to fix. Risk management is the skill of choosing the work that matters most. This certificate proves you can make those choices in a way that auditors and managers accept. It fits analysts, auditors and security officers who want to move into governance and risk roles. It also pairs well with ISO 27001, because risk assessment sits at the heart of that standard.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Learn the shared vocabulary first, including asset, threat, vulnerability, likelihood, impact and risk owner.
  2. Study one complete risk method from start to finish, so you can see how the steps connect.
  3. Practise on a small case study and write a short risk register with a score for each entry.
  4. Compare treatment options such as reducing, avoiding, sharing and accepting a risk, and justify each choice.
  5. Ask a colleague to challenge your ratings, because exam scenario questions often test whether your reasoning holds.
  6. Sit the exam, which mixes definition questions with case study questions about a risk programme.

Real-world example

Imagine you are planning a long car journey with your family. You look at the weather, the age of the tyres and the length of the drive. You decide some risks are worth taking and you reduce the others. So you buy new tyres and skip the mountain road. You also accept the risk of a short delay. Nobody stops every possible accident, but a careful driver reduces the worst ones. Security risk management works in exactly the same way.

Key takeaways

  • Risk work is about choosing what not to fix as much as choosing what to fix.
  • You must be able to explain the reason behind every score you write down.
  • Check your provider's current exam and assignment format before you book a date.

In more detail

The ISO/IEC 27005 Lead Risk Manager is a professional certification for people who run information security risk management rather than merely take part in it. It is built on the guidance in ISO/IEC 27005 and is delivered by accredited training bodies, so the standard is exam code 27005 rather than a vendor exam number. The level sits between practitioner and manager: candidates are expected to know the vocabulary of risk, to apply a repeatable method, and to explain their decisions to auditors and executives. The exam is normally a written paper of multiple choice and scenario questions, and some providers add a practical assignment based on a case study. It suits security analysts, risk officers, auditors and consultants who already work with ISO/IEC 27001 and now need the deeper risk skills. Typical next roles are information security risk manager, governance risk and compliance manager, and security consultant. Exam domains and objectives

  • Explain the concepts, terms and principles that information security risk management uses
  • Establish context and scope so that risk decisions match the organisation and its obligations
  • Identify, analyse and evaluate risk with a method that another person could repeat
  • Select and document risk treatment options, including the acceptance of residual risk
  • Describe how risk is communicated to owners, executives and other interested parties
  • Monitor, review and improve a risk management process over time
