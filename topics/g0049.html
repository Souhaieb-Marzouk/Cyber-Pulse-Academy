G0049 OilRig

G0049Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

OilRig is a name for a spying group that public reports link to Iranian state interests. It mainly targets government and energy organisations in the Middle East. The group writes its own tools and also uses features that already exist in the operating system, so it leaves fewer obvious files behind. Other security companies call the same activity APT34 or Helix Kitten.

Why should you care?

Built-in tools are already on your computers, so you cannot simply block them. The group also works through trusted suppliers to reach their clients, which spreads the damage further than expected. If your staff discuss work with strangers on social media, that conversation can become the entry point. Watching how internal tools are used is far more useful than watching only for known malware files.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Operators find employees through public profiles and start a work-related conversation online.
  2. They send a file or link that appears to be a document, a tool or a job offer.
  3. Once opened, a small script runs and connects back through ordinary network services.
  4. In some cases the connection hides inside name lookups, which almost every network allows.
  5. The group collects passwords and mailbox rules, then uses them to reach other systems.
  6. They may use a partner company as a bridge into the real target's network.

Real-world example

Picture a large office where every visitor signs in at the front desk. Now imagine someone who never enters the building at all. They telephone the front desk and ask for a package to be passed upstairs. The desk follows normal procedure, and the package reaches the right floor. OilRig often works through a trusted partner in the same way, letting someone else carry the package inside.

Key takeaways

  • Built-in administration tools can be abused, so monitor how they are used.
  • Name resolution traffic deserves attention in network monitoring.
  • A supplier can carry an intrusion into your network without knowing it.

In more detail

OilRig, tracked by vendors as APT34, Helix Kitten, COBALT GYPSY and Hazel Sandstorm, is an espionage group whose activity public reporting associates with Iranian state interests. Targets concentrate in the Middle East, particularly government, energy, telecommunications and financial organisations, and the group has also used third parties as stepping stones into their clients. Reported tradecraft includes custom backdoors, heavy use of built-in scripting such as PowerShell, credential harvesting, and command and control carried over domain name system queries so that the traffic resembles ordinary name resolution. Operators are reported to approach employees directly through social media and messaging platforms. Detection depends on unusual name resolution patterns, script execution logs, and mailbox rules created without the user's knowledge. Attribution rests on targeting, tooling and infrastructure, and should be presented with the confidence its source states.
