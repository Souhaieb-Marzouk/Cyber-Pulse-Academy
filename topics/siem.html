SIEM

SOC & Detection EngineeringVery hardKeyword

A Security Information and Event Management platform is a centralised system that ingests logs and telemetry from across an estate, normalises them into a common schema, correlates events over time, and evaluates detection rules to raise alerts that a security operations centre can triage. In practice a SIEM is three coupled capabilities: collection and parsing, a searchable hot and warm store with a query language, and a rules and correlation engine with alerting, case management and reporting on top. Its value depends far less on the vendor than on data quality and coverage. A rule is only as good as the fields it can rely on, so normalisation decisions, timestamp handling, and reliable identity resolution across endpoints, identity providers, cloud control planes and network sensors determine whether detections are precise or noisy. Detection engineers therefore treat the SIEM as a product with a content pipeline: they map log sources to what MITRE ATT&CK techniques they can observe, write and version rules, measure precision and recall against known-good and known-bad telemetry, and retire content that only produces fatigue. Common failure modes are predictable. Ingesting everything without enrichment produces volume without context; ignoring time zone and clock skew breaks correlation windows; broad rules with single-event triggers flood analysts; and quiet log sources create blind spots that adversaries exploit precisely because nobody alerted. Effective operators tune the platform deliberately, using allow-lists with expiry dates, entity risk scoring rather than raw severity, and periodic purple-team validation to prove that a technique which should be detected actually is.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to explain how a SIEM pipeline collects, parses, normalises and stores telemetry, and identify where parsing errors silently break downstream detections.
  • You will be able to assess log source coverage against MITRE ATT&CK techniques and state honestly which techniques your estate cannot currently observe.
  • You will be able to write and tune correlation rules that use entity context, thresholds and time windows rather than single-event triggers.
  • You will be able to diagnose false-positive floods by tracing an alert back through normalisation, enrichment and rule logic to its root cause.
  • You will be able to design retention and hot/warm/cold tiering that balances investigation needs, cost and compliance obligations.
  • You will be able to validate detection content with controlled test telemetry and record the evidence that a rule fires when it should.
