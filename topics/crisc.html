CRISC ISACA CRISC

CRISCISACAExtremeCertificationUnofficial Study Resource

The Certified in Risk and Information Systems Control credential is ISACA's qualification for practitioners who own technology risk and the control environment that answers it, spanning both IT risk identification and the design, monitoring and maintenance of controls. It is aimed at risk managers, control and compliance leads, security and audit professionals who want to move from operating controls to governing risk, and business-facing technologists responsible for a risk register or regulatory obligations. To an employer it certifies that the holder can identify and evaluate IT risk in business context, set risk appetite with stakeholders, choose proportionate control responses, measure whether those controls actually reduce exposure, and report risk in terms that executives, auditors and regulators can act on. The exam is a single multiple-choice assessment of roughly one hundred and fifty questions with a four-hour allowance, using a scaled score model with a minimum scaled score of 450 out of 800, and it consistently rewards the answer that treats risk as an ongoing managed process with clear ownership and documented decisions rather than a one-off technical remediation. Its domains cover governance, risk assessment, risk response and reporting, and information technology and security, with questions presented as business scenarios in which several answers are technically plausible and only one respects the risk framework, tolerance and accountability model. Hands-on practice changes results because the exam tests decision quality: classifying an inherent risk, arguing a treatment option against tolerance, detecting a control that exists on paper but cannot be evidenced, and building metrics that show residual risk trending rather than simply counting open findings.

Exam domains and objectives

  • IT Risk Governance: Establish and maintain a risk governance framework that defines risk appetite, tolerance, roles, accountability and escalation paths across the enterprise.
  • IT Risk Governance: Align IT risk management with enterprise risk management, legal and regulatory obligations, and business strategy, and communicate the aggregated risk profile to oversight bodies.
  • IT Risk Assessment: Identify IT risk events and contributing factors across processes, projects, assets and third parties, using techniques such as threat and vulnerability analysis and scenario analysis.
  • IT Risk Assessment: Analyse and evaluate inherent and residual risk using qualitative and quantitative methods, prioritise exposures, and document the analysis so that it can be reviewed and relied upon.
  • Risk Response and Reporting: Select and justify risk response options including avoidance, mitigation, transfer and acceptance, and ensure remediation, ownership and deadlines are agreed with accountable parties.
  • Risk Response and Reporting: Monitor and report risk and control status with meaningful metrics and key risk indicators, and validate that reported improvements are supported by reliable evidence.
  • Information Technology and Security: Design and implement controls that address identified risk across identity and access, change, operations, data protection and third-party arrangements.
  • Information Technology and Security: Assess whether controls are designed effectively and operating as intended, and maintain them as business, threat and technology conditions change.
  • Information Technology and Security: Evaluate the risk implications of emerging technology, architecture change and outsourcing decisions before commitment, and integrate risk considerations into project and change lifecycles.
  • Control Ownership and Culture: Embed risk awareness and control accountability into business and technology teams so that risk decisions are made and recorded at the right level.
