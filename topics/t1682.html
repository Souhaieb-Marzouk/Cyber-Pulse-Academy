T1682 Query Public AI Services

T1682ReconnaissanceExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is when attackers use public artificial intelligence services, such as chat tools, to research their targets. They ask the tool to gather and summarise information that is already public. The result is a fast, organised picture of a person or company. They then use that picture to plan an approach.

Why should you care?

Research that once took days now takes minutes, so attackers can prepare many more personalised approaches. A message that mentions your manager's name, your supplier and your current project is far more convincing than generic spam. The attacker can also gather personal details that are used to pressure or blackmail someone. Because the research happens on the attacker's own account, you will not see it in your logs. Your best defence is limiting what is public and helping staff expect a well-informed approach.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker chooses a target organisation and collects names, projects and suppliers from public sources.
  2. They paste that material into a public AI service and ask it to summarise and organise the information.
  3. The tool identifies key people, their likely roles and the technologies the organisation appears to use.
  4. It can also suggest which business relationships could be used to create a believable reason to make contact.
  5. The attacker asks for contact details, drafts of messages for each person, and any sensitive facts useful for pressure.
  6. The finished approach arrives looking carefully researched, because it is.

Real-world example

Picture a door-to-door salesperson with perfect recall. Yesterday he read every public notice board in the street. Today he knocks on your door and mentions your dog, your builder and your extension. Nothing he says is secret. He simply gathered what was already on display, and that is why you trust him enough to keep listening.

Key takeaways

  • Public AI tools let attackers research targets far faster than before.
  • The research happens on their systems, so you see the approach, not the preparation.
  • Reduce public detail about staff and projects, and expect personalised approaches.

In more detail

Query public AI services covers using hosted artificial intelligence tools, particularly large language models, as an analysis layer on top of public information. Where classic open source research means reading pages and databases by hand, this technique uses a model to synthesise, aggregate and summarise material at scale, and to turn scattered facts into a usable picture. Documented uses include shortlisting individuals or organisations worth targeting, mapping an organisation's structure and key personnel, working out which technologies a target runs, and researching business relationships in order to build a believable pretext for a social engineering approach. The same capability supports collecting contact details to seed phishing or phishing for information, and assembling derogatory or sensitive material that can be used for extortion or coercion. Defensively, this activity is almost entirely outside your visibility. Queries are made from the actor's own accounts, often from infrastructure unrelated to the target, and traffic goes to a public provider rather than to you. What you can watch is the consequence rather than the cause: reconnaissance-shaped requests arriving at your public properties, new account registrations that reuse profile details published elsewhere, phishing messages whose phrasing and targeting suggest automated research, and staff whose publicly documented roles match the individuals contacted. The practical controls are reducing the public information an organisation exposes and training people to expect well-researched, personalised approaches.

What you will practise

  • Explain how publicly available AI services support target research.
  • Describe the kinds of public information an adversary can aggregate with these tools.
  • Explain how this research strengthens social engineering and phishing approaches.
  • Recognise why this reconnaissance is largely invisible in defender telemetry.
  • Identify practical controls such as limiting public organisational detail and staff awareness.
