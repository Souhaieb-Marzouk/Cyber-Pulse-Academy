CHFI EC-Council CHFI

CHFIEC-CouncilExtremeCertificationUnofficial Study Resource

The EC-Council Computer Hacking Forensic Investigator (CHFI) is a vendor-specific credential that certifies a practitioner can investigate digital evidence in a forensically sound manner, from identification and seizure through acquisition, analysis and reporting, so that conclusions hold up under scrutiny in a disciplinary, regulatory or legal proceeding. It targets law enforcement and military investigators, incident responders, CSIRT and SOC staff, internal auditors, and IT professionals who are handed a compromised or suspect machine and asked what actually happened. To an employer the certification signals disciplined evidence handling, including chain of custody, write blocking, hashing for integrity verification and respect for order of volatility, alongside platform artefact knowledge across Windows, Linux and macOS and an expanding set of network, cloud, email, mobile and IoT sources. It also evidences awareness of anti-forensic behaviour such as timestomping, data hiding and log manipulation, and of the techniques used to detect and defeat it. The exam is a proctored, primarily multiple-choice instrument delivered from a large item bank in a single timed sitting, framed around investigation scenarios that ask the candidate to pick the correct acquisition method, artefact location, tool capability or analysis step, with vendor tool terminology appearing throughout. Practical experience is decisive: candidates who have imaged a disk, recovered deleted files, parsed a memory capture and built a timeline from real artefacts answer from memory of the work, while candidates who only read the modules tend to confuse similar tools, artefact paths and acquisition sequences under time pressure.

Exam domains and objectives

  • Forensic Readiness and Investigation Process: covers the phases of an investigation, evidence identification, documentation, and the constraints imposed by jurisdiction and organisational policy.
  • Evidence Handling and Chain of Custody: covers seizing, labelling, transporting and storing exhibits so that integrity and admissibility are preserved end to end.
  • Disk and File System Fundamentals: covers storage geometry, partitioning and file system structures, and where the metadata that supports a defensible timeline is stored.
  • Data Acquisition and Duplication: covers live versus dead acquisition, write blocking, imaging formats, sparse and compressed captures, and integrity verification by hashing.
  • Anti-Forensics and Artefact Recovery: covers recognising timestomping, hidden, slack and encrypted data, log tampering, and methods for recovering obscured evidence.
  • Windows Forensics: covers registry hives, event logs, prefetch, shellbags, jump lists and similar artefact families, and what each can prove about user and program activity.
  • Linux and macOS Forensics: covers system and authentication logs, shell history, package and service state, and platform-specific indicators of execution and persistence.
  • Network and Log Forensics: covers packet capture, flow records, proxy, firewall and authentication logs, and correlating network evidence with host artefacts.
  • Web, Email and Database Investigations: covers tracing application and mail-borne activity, header and server log analysis, and reconstructing transactions from server-side stores.
  • Malware and Memory Forensics: covers triaging malicious code by behaviour, volatile memory acquisition, and extracting artefacts such as injected regions and resident network state.
  • Mobile, Cloud and IoT Forensics: covers acquisition limits on handheld and embedded devices, evidence held by hosted services, and the shared-responsibility questions that follow.
  • Reporting and Expert Testimony: covers writing defensible findings, presenting timelines and opinion clearly, and withstanding cross-examination on method and interpretation.
