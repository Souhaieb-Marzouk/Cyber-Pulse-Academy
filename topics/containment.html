Containment

Incident Response & ForensicsVery hardKeyword

Containment is the incident response phase in which a team deliberately limits the spread, impact and persistence of an active intrusion while preserving both the evidence and the business functions needed to recover. It is a risk decision made with incomplete information: the analyst usually knows that at least one host or identity is compromised and does not yet know the full scope, so containment trades some operational disruption for a reduced chance that the adversary moves laterally, exfiltrates data or re-establishes access from a foothold nobody has found. Short-term containment is applied within minutes to hours and is usually blunt - network isolation of an endpoint, disabling an account, blocking a command-and-control destination at the egress proxy or DNS resolver, revoking active sessions and tokens, or rotating exposed credentials. Long-term containment keeps the environment stable while eradication and recovery are planned, often by segmenting affected subnets, applying temporary compensating controls, or standing up clean rebuild infrastructure. Effective containment depends on decisions made long before the incident: who has authority to take production systems offline, how isolation is technically performed without tipping off the adversary, and how forensic images and volatile data are captured before a rebuild destroys them. Containment carries risk of its own, because an attacker who detects isolation may accelerate destructive action, so timing and sequencing matter. Teams document every containment action with a timestamp, an owner and a rationale, because those records support later scoping, legal hold and regulatory reporting.

What you will practise

  • Define containment in terms of the spread, impact and evidence tradeoffs it is intended to manage.
  • Differentiate short-term and long-term containment and select the appropriate approach for a given intrusion stage.
  • Identify the pre-approved authority, contacts and tooling required to isolate a production system without delay.
  • Sequence containment actions so that volatile evidence is captured before rebuilds, reboots or log rotation destroy it.
  • Recognise the operational and adversary-detection risks created by isolation, account revocation and traffic blocking.
  • Document containment decisions so they support later scoping, legal hold and regulatory notification.
  • Evaluate whether a proposed containment action is proportionate to the scope of compromise that has actually been confirmed.
