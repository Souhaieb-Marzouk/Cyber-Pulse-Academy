T1683.002 Generate Content: Audio-Visual Content

T1683.002Resource DevelopmentExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Audio-visual content means fake sound and fake pictures made by an attacker. It includes copied voices, altered live calls, and invented profile photos. Forged identity papers and fake videos belong here too. Some of it is edited by hand, and some comes from AI tools. The aim is to make the victim trust what they see and hear.

Why should you care?

We naturally trust a familiar voice or a face on a screen more than an email. A short voice message can push an employee to move money during a live call. These fakes are now cheap and fast to make, so they appear in everyday fraud, not only in rare cases. Teams that rely on voice alone for approval lose their safety check.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker collects samples of a target person's voice or face from public videos and recordings.
  2. They use AI or editing tools to build a copy that can say new sentences.
  3. They contact the victim by phone, voice message, or video call using the fake identity.
  4. They keep the call short, and apply pressure so the victim acts before checking.
  5. They may also create fake photos or identity papers to support the story.
  6. They ask for money, a password reset, or another action that is hard to undo.

Real-world example

Picture an airport queue where staff check passports. A traveller hands over a document with the right photo, the right stamps, and the right paper. The officer is busy, and the queue is long. Nothing is broken and no alarm sounds. The identity was simply manufactured, and the queue moved on.

Key takeaways

  • A familiar voice on a call is not proof of identity, so verify with a second channel.
  • Ask for a callback on a known number when money or access is requested.
  • Keep approval rules that do not depend on one person's voice alone.

In more detail

Audio-Visual Content is the media-focused part of Generate Content. Adversaries produce synthetic voice recordings, manipulate audio or video during live calls, fabricate profile photographs and identity documents, and create video showing people who never said or did those things. Production ranges from manual editing through AI-assisted generation to paid synthetic media services, and the goal is often a single convincing moment during a voice or video interaction rather than a mass campaign. The practical effect is that the victim's own ears and eyes become the weak control, which is why voice cloning is frequently paired with help desk calls and executive impersonation. Artefacts that support detection are largely provenance and context: media metadata and content credentials, frame and audio artefacts, mismatched lighting or lip timing, an unexpected channel change mid-conversation, and a request that would normally require written approval being made verbally.
