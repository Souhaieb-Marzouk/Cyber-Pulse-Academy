Shift Handover

SOC & Detection EngineeringHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Shift handover is the short briefing between one group of security staff and the next. The leaving team explains what is open, what they did and what should happen next. The arriving team takes over the work.

Why should you care?

An investigation can take many hours, but a shift ends after eight or twelve. If the story is not passed on, the next team starts from nothing. Important clues are forgotten and a slow attack continues unnoticed. A clear handover keeps one case moving forward instead of restarting it.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The leaving team updates each open case with its status and the actions taken.
  2. They write down the working theory and the next decision that is needed.
  3. They note anything they silenced on purpose, such as a known noisy scanner.
  4. They name the owner of each case and the time it should be checked again.
  5. Both teams meet briefly, and the arriving team asks questions about the cases.
  6. The arriving team accepts the list and confirms who is handling each item.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital at the change of shift. The night nurse does not simply walk out. They stand beside the day nurse and point at each patient. They explain what changed, which medicine is due and which family is worried. The day nurse asks questions and writes notes. If that conversation were skipped, the day nurse would read the chart and miss the small detail that matters. A security shift handover is the same conversation, with cases instead of patients and alerts instead of medicines.

Key takeaways

  • Write the handover down, because a spoken briefing leaves no evidence.
  • Name one owner and one next check time for every open case.
  • Pass on deliberate suppressions so the next team does not re-investigate noise.

In more detail

Shift handover is the structured transfer of operational context from one security operations shift to the next, and it is a control in its own right rather than an administrative courtesy. A monitoring team works in rotation, so an investigation that starts at the end of one shift continues in the hands of people who did not see the alert arrive. A good handover passes forward the open incidents with their current status, the actions already taken, the working theory, the next decision point and the person accountable for it, plus any noise the outgoing team has deliberately suppressed and the tickets that are waiting on a third party. It also carries forward context that no ticket field captures, such as a planned change window or a known noisy scanner. In practice this appears as a written turnover log, a short verbal briefing at the boundary, and a shared queue with clear ownership. The common mistake is a verbal, unstructured pass-down that mentions only the loudest alert, so the quiet but important item dies between shifts and no one can reconstruct who knew what.

What you will practise

  • Explain why shift handover is a control and not just a courtesy.
  • Describe the information an open case needs before it is passed to the next shift.
  • Draft a written handover note that records owner, status and next action.
  • Identify context that is easy to lose, such as change windows and known noise.
  • Recognise the failure pattern of a purely verbal and unstructured pass-down.
