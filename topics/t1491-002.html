T1491.002 Defacement: External Defacement

T1491.002ImpactExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is defacement of content that the public can see, usually a public website. An attacker replaces the real page with a message, a political statement, or an offensive image. This sub-technique is the outside facing side of the wider defacement technique, which you should learn first.

Why should you care?

A public website is your shop window, and everyone can see the change. Customers may stop trusting you with their details or their money. News and social media can spread the image before your team even knows. Some attackers use the changed page to trick visitors into downloading harmful software. Even a short public change can cause long lasting damage to your name.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker takes over the publishing path, such as a content management account or a web server file.
  2. They find the page that visitors see first, usually the home page and its images.
  3. They upload or edit content so the public page shows their own message.
  4. They may leave the rest of the site working so the change is not noticed by staff straight away.
  5. Visitors see the new page immediately and often share screenshots with others online.
  6. The attacker may also post proof themselves to claim responsibility for the intrusion.

Real-world example

Think of a post office with a large sign above the door showing the opening times. A vandal cannot get into the safe behind the counter, so instead they climb up at night and change the sign to say the office is closed forever. The post office still works inside, but the whole street now believes it has shut. The message on the outside did more damage than any theft would have.

Key takeaways

  • You may hear about a public defacement from outsiders before your own monitoring fires.
  • Protect the accounts and files that publish public content as carefully as servers.
  • Keep an offline copy of the site so the real content can be restored within minutes.

In more detail

External defacement changes content that people outside the organisation can see, most often a public website, and is used to deliver a political message, embarrass the victim, or claim credit for the intrusion. The delivery mechanism varies: a compromised content management account, a web application weakness that allows file writes, a publishing pipeline that accepts attacker content, or redirection of traffic so a different page is served. Significant cases are treated as public events, so the first reliable indication is often a report from outside rather than an internal alert, which makes external content monitoring comparable to brand monitoring. The technical evidence is the web request sequence before the edit, content management audit records, file integrity alerts in the web root, and deployment history showing a release that no engineer created. It can also be staged deliberately as a distraction or as preparation for later compromise of visitors.

What you will practise

  • Define external defacement and explain the goals behind it.
  • State how this sub-technique differs from the wider defacement technique.
  • Describe the common ways an attacker gains the ability to change a public site.
  • Identify the records that reveal how and when a public page was changed.
  • Explain why external defacement is often detected from outside the organisation.
  • Explain how a defaced site can be used to attack the visitors who see it.
