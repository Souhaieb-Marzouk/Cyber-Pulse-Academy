G0034 Sandworm Team

G0034Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Sandworm Team is a name for a set of attacks that public government reports link to Russian military intelligence. It has spied on governments and also destroyed data at energy and media organisations. It is known for malware that can talk to industrial control systems. Different vendors call it different names.

Why should you care?

Most attackers want money or information. This group also destroys systems and makes recovery difficult. Its tools have been aimed at equipment that runs power grids and factories. A failure there can affect everyday life, not just one company. Even if you do not run industrial equipment, your suppliers might.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers gather information about a target, including how its networks and equipment are built.
  2. They get a first foothold, often through stolen credentials, weak remote access or a supplier.
  3. They spread inside and separate parts of the network so defenders cannot see the whole picture.
  4. They disable backups and security tools to make a later recovery much harder.
  5. They deploy destructive software that erases data or interferes with industrial equipment.
  6. Effects may appear suddenly, but preparation inside the network often lasted for months.

Real-world example

Imagine someone who does not rob the bank but instead removes the batteries from every alarm and hides the spare keys. When the fire starts, nobody can call for help and nothing works. That is what destructive intrusion looks like: the damage is planned long before anything visible happens.

Key takeaways

  • Destructive attacks are planned quietly, so detecting preparation is the best chance to stop them.
  • Backups must be separate and tested, because attackers often target recovery systems first.
  • Separating office networks from industrial networks limits how far an intruder can reach.

In more detail

Sandworm Team, also tracked as Seashell Blizzard and APT44, is a Russian state-linked activity set that public reporting attributes to Russian military intelligence. Its documented history is unusually broad: espionage against government, military and political targets, destructive operations against energy and utility organisations, attacks on media, and disruptive malware known as Industroyer, built to speak industrial control protocols. Reporting also describes supply chain abuse, including compromise of widely used network management and accounting software to reach many victims at once, plus destructive wipers designed to make recovery hard rather than to hold data for ransom. The group combines reconnaissance of operational technology environments with malware able to interact with industrial equipment. Attribution appears in public statements with stated confidence levels.

What you will practise

  • Describe Sandworm Team and the range of operations attributed to it in public reporting.
  • Explain what operational technology is and why it needs separate protection from office networks.
  • Explain why attackers disable backups before destructive operations and how to counter that.
  • Identify supply chain and third-party access risks highlighted by documented campaigns.
  • Describe the evidence that would show preparation for a destructive attack.
  • Present public government attribution with its stated confidence level.
