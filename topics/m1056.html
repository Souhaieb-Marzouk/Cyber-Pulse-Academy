M1056 Pre-compromise

M1056Enterprise MitigationVery hardMitigation

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Pre-compromise work happens before an attacker gets in, while they are still studying you. It means reducing what a stranger can learn about your systems and your staff. It also means watching for signs that someone is preparing an attack. You are making the research step slower and noisier for them.

Why should you care?

Every attack starts with research, and much of that research uses information you publish yourself. Job adverts name your tools, social posts name your staff, and old documents leak your network details. An attacker who knows your systems chooses a sharper, cheaper attack. Removing that information blurs their picture and buys you time. Watching for impersonation domains and scanning lets you act before the real attempt arrives.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. List what the public internet shows about you, such as domains, certificates, staff roles and supplier names.
  2. Remove or reduce unnecessary detail from websites, documents, job posts and social media.
  3. Register and monitor similar looking domain names that an attacker might use to imitate you.
  4. Watch scanning and probing activity against your exposed services to see who is looking around.
  5. Reduce your exposed surface by retiring forgotten services and limiting what must be public.
  6. Brief staff, suppliers and new joiners about the information they share on your behalf.

Real-world example

Imagine a shop window on a busy street. Everything inside is visible to anyone walking past. You decide what goes in the window and you keep the back room out of sight. You also watch for someone photographing the window and for a fake sign with your name on it. The street itself is public, but your display is your choice.

Key takeaways

  • Your public information is part of your attack surface, so treat it as something to manage.
  • Watch for impersonation domains and probing activity, because reconnaissance is visible.
  • You cannot hide everything, so aim to reduce detail and detect interest instead.

In more detail

Pre-compromise mitigations are the defensive work done while an adversary is still in the reconnaissance and resource development phases, before any access to your estate exists. The mechanism is denial of information and friction against preparation: minimising what public sources reveal about technologies, staff, suppliers and mail infrastructure, registering and monitoring look-alike domains, watching certificate transparency and domain registration data for typosquatting, and limiting how easily an adversary can acquire or stage the infrastructure a campaign needs. In real environments this appears as external attack surface management, brand protection, takedown requests, perimeter hygiene on internet facing services, and internal awareness that travel, hiring and public announcements are themselves disclosures. Evidence is mostly passive and long lived: certificate and registration records, search engine and social media exposure reports, phishing domain alerts, scanner telemetry and takedown tickets.

What you will practise

  • Describe the reconnaissance and resource development phases and what defenders can do during them.
  • Explain how publicly available information shortens an adversary's preparation work.
  • Outline an external attack surface reduction and monitoring routine.
  • Identify the records that reveal adversary interest, such as scanning and domain registration data.
  • Explain how to brief staff and suppliers about information disclosure.
