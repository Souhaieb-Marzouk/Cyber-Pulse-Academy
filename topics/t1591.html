T1591 Gather Victim Org Information

T1591ReconnaissanceExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This technique is about learning how a target organisation works. The attacker studies its departments, its daily business, and who does what. The aim is to understand people and processes, not computers.

Why should you care?

Attacks often succeed because a message looks believable. Details about your teams and projects make a fake email convincing. Knowing who approves payments or holds administrator rights tells an attacker who to target. This kind of research also leaves very few traces in your technical logs.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker reads your public website, press releases, and reports to learn your business areas.
  2. They study employee profiles on social media to find names, teams, and reporting lines.
  3. They note who mentions administrator tools, finance systems, or sensitive projects in public posts.
  4. They copy your writing style, logos, and signature formats for use in later messages.
  5. They may create fake accounts and contact staff, pretending to be a recruiter or a partner.
  6. They use what they learn to write messages that look normal to the people receiving them.

Real-world example

Picture a new starter on their first day in a large company. They walk past the noticeboard and read the team photographs, the project posters, and the birthday list. Within an hour they know who runs finance and who approves purchases. Nobody objects, because that information is on the wall for everyone to see. An attacker does the same thing from outside the building. Your website, social media posts, and conference talks are the noticeboard. Each detail seems harmless on its own. Together they tell a stranger exactly who to write to, and what to say.

Key takeaways

  • Treat everything staff publish about their role as information an attacker will read and reuse.
  • Limit public detail about who holds privileged, financial, or executive support roles.
  • Tell staff how to report unusual recruiting or supplier messages sent to them.

In more detail

Gathering victim organisation information is collection about how a target business is structured and operated: divisions and departments, the nature of its daily business, and the roles and responsibilities of key employees. It is the human counterpart to network and host reconnaissance, and it feeds social engineering rather than exploitation directly. Adversaries satisfy most of it from open sources, including social media, victim-owned websites, regulatory filings, press releases, conference talks, and leaked internal documents, and supplement it through phishing for information when public detail is not specific enough. The output shapes believable pretexts, supports the creation or compromise of accounts that impersonate staff, and identifies which external relationships are worth pursuing. Technical telemetry is scarce because the collection happens outside your estate, so the observable signals are indirect: out-of-band messages to staff, unusual profile activity impersonating your brand, and directory or address-pattern probing that precedes targeted mail. Detection therefore leans on human reporting and on correlating access attempts with known recruitment or sales activity.

What you will practise

  • Describe the categories of organisational information that adversaries collect before targeting.
  • Explain how open sources such as social media and regulatory filings expose internal structure.
  • Describe how collected detail is used to build believable social engineering pretexts.
  • Explain why organisation gathering produces little technical telemetry and what indirect signals remain.
  • Recommend awareness measures that help staff recognise and report elicitation attempts.
