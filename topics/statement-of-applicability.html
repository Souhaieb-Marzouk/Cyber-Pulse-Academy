Statement of Applicability

GRC & ComplianceVery hardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A statement of applicability is a document that lists every security control in a chosen set. For each control it records whether you include it, why you decided that, and where it is implemented. It links your risk assessment to the controls you truly run. Auditors use it as a map of your evidence.

Why should you care?

When an auditor asks why you skipped a control, a vague answer damages trust. This document gives a written reason for every choice, so nothing looks accidental. It also stops duplicated effort, because you can see which risks already have a control and which have none. Over time it becomes a short inventory of your whole control estate.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. List every control in the catalogue that you are working from.
  2. For each one, record include or exclude and give a short reason.
  3. Point to real evidence, such as a policy section or a system setting.
  4. Name the person who owns each control and note its current status.
  5. Check that every treated risk links back to at least one control.
  6. Keep versions, so you can show what the document said during a past audit.

Real-world example

Think of a school trip consent form. A parent ticks each activity their child may join and writes a reason if they say no. The teacher can then plan safely and explain every choice later. A statement of applicability is that form for security controls. Skipping a control is allowed, but you must write down why, and someone must be able to check that reason.

Key takeaways

  • Every control entry needs a decision, a reason and a real reference.
  • Exclusions must be justified by scope or risk, not by convenience.
  • Every treated risk should point to at least one control.

In more detail

A Statement of Applicability is the document that records, for every control in the applicable catalogue, whether the organisation includes it, why that decision was made, and where the implementation lives. In an ISO/IEC 27001 management system it is a mandatory artefact that ties the risk assessment and risk treatment process to the controls actually operated, and it acts as the table of contents an auditor follows through the rest of the evidence. A defensible entry contains the control reference and title, an inclusion or exclusion decision, the justification for that decision, the implementation status, a reference to the specific implementation such as a policy section, system configuration or process document, and the control owner. Exclusions are where most documents fail. An organisation cannot legitimately exclude a control simply because it is inconvenient; the justification must show that the control is not applicable to the scope, that the risk it addresses does not exist in the scope, or that an alternative treatment reduces the risk to an acceptable level. Practitioners should keep the statement traceable in both directions: every selected control should map back to at least one assessed risk, and every treated risk should map forward to at least one control or a documented acceptance. Version control matters because the document is audited against a period, and reviewers should be able to reconstruct what the statement said when a given audit took place. It is also useful beyond certification as a concise inventory of the control estate.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to build a complete statement that traces every selected control back to an assessed risk.
  • You will be able to write exclusions that are genuinely justifiable rather than convenience-driven.
  • You will be able to record implementation references that point to verifiable configuration, policy or process evidence.
  • You will be able to assign control owners and implementation status that survives an auditor walkthrough.
  • You will be able to maintain version control so a past audit period can be reconstructed.
  • You will be able to use the statement as a control inventory for coverage and gap analysis outside certification.
