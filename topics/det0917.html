DET0917 Detection of Written Content

DET0917Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Written content means text: email, chat messages, documents and online profiles. This detection looks for text written by artificial intelligence (AI) at machine speed to trick people. It finds campaigns, not one message.

Why should you care?

Most attacks start with words, such as a message that asks you to sign in somewhere. Writing those messages used to take time and skill. A machine can now produce a huge number of good ones. The volume makes each message harder to check by hand, so you need pattern based detection.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. You collect text metadata from mail, chat and document stores. You keep the sender and the timing.
  2. You compare texts with each other to find groups that are almost the same.
  3. You check grammar, tone and length for unusual uniformity across many messages.
  4. You score the text with a language model that estimates machine authorship.
  5. You check the account history. New accounts that send a lot are more interesting.
  6. You alert on the campaign and show several examples side by side.

Real-world example

Think of a market stall selling cakes. One cake that looks perfect means nothing at all. But the seller produces two hundred perfect cakes in five minutes. Every cake is exactly the same size. The single cake was never the clue. The speed and the sameness were.

Key takeaways

  • Machine written text is best caught in groups, not one message at a time.
  • Near duplicates with small changes are a strong sign of automated generation.
  • Text scoring is only evidence. Account history and volume carry more weight.

In more detail

This detection strategy focuses on written content produced by generative artificial intelligence (AI) for use in deception, such as phishing mail, fake persona profiles, fraudulent invoices, chat messages and documents. It covers technique T1683.001 and matters because text is the cheapest content to generate, so it is the channel where machine scale appears first and in the largest volume. The telemetry that makes it possible is mail body and header metadata, stylometric scoring, text similarity and embedding comparisons across a corpus, document authorship metadata, and outbound requests to public AI writing services from the estate. In practice the strategy fails because generated prose is intended to read naturally, employees use assistants for legitimate work, and text classifiers produce false positives on non native speakers and on heavily templated business writing. The dependable signals are volume, repetition with small variations, and the relationship between sender and recipient.

What you will practise

  • Explain why written text is the most common channel for machine generated deception
  • Measure near duplication and small variation across a corpus of messages or documents
  • Apply language model scores to text while stating their accuracy limits clearly
  • Correlate text similarity findings with newly created accounts and first contact recipients
  • Read document authorship and revision metadata to look for signs of automated production
  • Produce a campaign level alert that presents representative samples rather than every match
