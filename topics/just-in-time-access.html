Just-in-Time Access

Identity & AccessVery hardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Just-in-time access means you get powerful rights only when you need them. You ask for the right, explain why, and someone or something approves it. The right is removed automatically after a set time, so it never stays with you.

Why should you care?

Administrators often keep powerful rights all day, every day. If someone steals that account, the thief gets those rights too. Just-in-time access shrinks that window to a few hours. It also creates a clear record of who asked for what and why. That record is gold during an investigation.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. You request a powerful role and write a short reason for needing it.
  2. The system checks your request against the rules and your ticket reference.
  3. An approver agrees, or the system approves automatically for routine work.
  4. The role is switched on for a fixed time, often with a stronger sign-in.
  5. When the time ends, the system takes the role away without human effort.

Real-world example

Imagine a hotel master key. The manager does not carry it all day long. When a guest is locked out, the manager signs the key out at the front desk and returns it after the job. The log shows the time, the reason, and the room. If the key ever goes missing, staff know exactly who had it and when.

Key takeaways

  • Standing privilege is the problem; time-limited elevation is the fix.
  • Every request should carry a reason, an approver, and a short expiry time.
  • Watch for activations outside working hours or repeated renewals.

In more detail

Just-in-time (JIT) access is an access management pattern in which elevation to a privileged role or entitlement is granted only for a bounded period, on request, and only after the requester has satisfied whatever approval, justification and authentication conditions the organisation defines. It replaces standing privilege, where an administrator holds powerful entitlements permanently and therefore holds them equally at three in the morning during a phishing campaign and during a planned maintenance window. A JIT workflow typically has four phases: request, where the user states the scope and reason and often a ticket reference; approval, which may be automatic when the request matches a pre-approved template or manual when it does not; activation, where the entitlement is attached for a fixed duration and is frequently accompanied by step-up authentication and a session recording; and expiry, where the grant is removed automatically by the platform rather than by human discipline. The security gain is measurable, because the window during which a stolen credential is useful narrows from indefinite to hours, and the population of accounts holding privileged rights at any instant becomes small enough to enumerate and monitor. The pattern also produces high-quality telemetry that rarely exists elsewhere: every request, justification, approval decision, activation and expiry is logged, so defenders can alert on activations outside change windows, on self-approvals where separation of duties is required, on repeated activations that suggest a workflow is being used as permanent access, and on direct use of the underlying privileged account that bypasses the broker entirely.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to describe the request, approval, activation and expiry phases of a just-in-time access workflow.
  • You will be able to design approval policies that keep routine work fast while reserving manual review for high-risk elevation.
  • You will be able to enforce maximum activation duration, step-up authentication and scope limits for each privileged role.
  • You will be able to detect activation anomalies such as out-of-window elevation, self-approval and repeated renewal.
  • You will be able to identify bypass paths where a privileged credential is used directly instead of through the broker.
  • You will be able to measure the reduction in standing privilege achieved after a JIT rollout.
