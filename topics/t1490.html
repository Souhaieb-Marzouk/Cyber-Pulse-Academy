T1490 Inhibit System Recovery

T1490ImpactExtremeTechnique

Inhibiting system recovery is the removal or disablement of the mechanisms that would let an organisation restore systems and data after destructive activity. It is the step that converts a recoverable outage into an existential one, and it is usually executed shortly before or alongside mass encryption or wiping activity. The mechanism targets every layer of resilience. On endpoints and file servers, native utilities that manage volume shadow copies and system restore points can delete recovery data, and boot configuration or recovery partitions can be modified so that built in repair options no longer work. Backup catalogues, backup files and backup agents can be deleted, stopped or reconfigured to point at storage that has been wiped, and backup infrastructure is often attacked directly because it holds credentials and trust relationships for everything else. In virtualised environments, hypervisor snapshots and datastores are removed. In cloud environments, snapshot deletion, backup vault tampering, retention policy changes and lifecycle rule manipulation achieve the same result through authenticated control plane calls. The telemetry is administrative and therefore easy to overlook. Endpoint and file server logs show shadow copy, restore point and backup service operations, often with the calling process, account and command line. Backup product audit logs record job deletion, catalogue changes and retention edits, and a sudden drop in successful backup jobs is itself a signal. Hypervisor and cloud audit logs record snapshot and vault operations together with the caller principal. Investigators distinguish malicious recovery inhibition from routine operations by correlating it with encryption, credential access and defence evasion in the same window, by checking whether the operation was scoped far more broadly than normal, by examining the account that performed it and whether that account was recently created or compromised, and by confirming the absence of a change record. Legitimate backup rotation and storage reclamation follow schedules and retain older copies, whereas malicious removal tends to be comprehensive and immediate. Monitoring that treats backup and snapshot deletion as high severity events in their own right is the most reliable control.

What you will practise

  • Explain how recovery inhibition targets endpoint, backup, hypervisor and cloud layers of resilience.
  • Identify the administrative artefacts that reveal shadow copy, snapshot, backup job and retention policy tampering.
  • Treat sudden degradation in backup success rates and catalogue integrity as a detection signal.
  • Differentiate scheduled backup rotation and storage reclamation from comprehensive malicious removal.
  • Correlate recovery inhibition with concurrent encryption, credential access and defence evasion activity.
  • Recommend monitoring, immutability and out of band recovery design that keeps restoration possible during an incident.
