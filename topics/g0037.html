G0037 FIN6

G0037Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

FIN6 is a criminal group that steals payment card numbers. They break into shops, hotels and online stores to reach the computers that handle card payments. Security companies also call this group ITG08, Skeleton Spider or Magecart Group 6.

Why should you care?

Card data is money. A single stolen batch can be sold and used for fraud long before the shop notices. The group also harms trust. Customers blame the shop, not the attacker, and the shop pays for fines, card replacement and investigations. FIN6 shows why payment systems deserve stronger protection than ordinary office computers, and why a stolen password on one laptop can put every card in the business at risk.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The group sends phishing emails with harmful attachments or links, hoping an employee will open them.
  2. Once inside, they use off the shelf tools (ready made software anyone can buy or download) to explore the network.
  3. They steal usernames and passwords, including stored ones, to reach servers with more power.
  4. They move quietly to the computers that read cards or run the online shop, called point of sale systems.
  5. They copy card details from that system and send the data out to servers they control.
  6. In later campaigns, they also encrypt the network and demand payment, a crime known as ransomware.

Real-world example

Imagine a busy restaurant where the waiter takes your card to a small terminal by the till. Now imagine a thief who never enters the dining room. He gets a job as a delivery driver, learns the staff entrance code, and one night walks to the till area and quietly copies every card receipt in the drawer. The restaurant only learns about it months later when banks report strange charges. FIN6 behaves like that thief. They stay away from the front of the shop and go straight to the till.

Key takeaways

  • Payment terminals and online checkout servers are the real target, so protect them first.
  • Stolen passwords are the group's main key, so use multi factor login everywhere.
  • Detecting ready made attack tools on your network is as important as spotting viruses.

In more detail

FIN6 is a financially motivated intrusion set tracked as G0037 and known in public reporting under the aliases Magecart Group 6, ITG08, Skeleton Spider, TAAL and Camouflage Tempest. The group has been active since at least 2015 and built its reputation on stealing payment card data from retailers, hospitality chains and online checkout environments, then selling or monetising that data. Its tradecraft relies on spear phishing and credential theft to obtain an initial foothold, followed by off-the-shelf remote access and penetration tooling to escalate privileges, harvest credentials from memory and move laterally to point of sale systems and e-commerce servers. Later reporting describes a shift toward ransomware deployment, often after data theft, which makes the group relevant to both payment security and extortion response teams. Investigators reconstruct activity from email gateway records of malicious attachments, Windows event logs showing new services and scheduled tasks, endpoint telemetry of credential dumping utilities, point of sale application logs, and network flows to infrastructure that never appears in corporate asset inventories.
