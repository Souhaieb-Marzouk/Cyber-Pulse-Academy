G0085 FIN4

G0085Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

FIN4 is a name for attackers who went after confidential business information rather than money in a bank. Public reporting described them targeting public companies and their lawyers. They stole email passwords and then read other people's mail. Their aim seemed to be learning about company news before the market did. They avoided the noisy attacks that most people expect.

Why should you care?

Email holds plans, prices and private arguments, so it is valuable on its own. If an attacker reads your mailbox for months, they learn how you decide and who you trust. Business deals can be damaged, and insider trading rules can be broken. This kind of theft often leaves no broken file, only unusual sign-ins. Protecting mailboxes and reviewing sign-in records is therefore a core control, not a small detail.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers pick companies with valuable secrets, such as drug research or deals.
  2. They then send messages that push members of staff toward a fake sign-in page.
  3. The stolen passwords let them open the mailbox from their own personal computers.
  4. They then read the messages and look for negotiations, financial results and legal advice.
  5. They sometimes send messages that look exactly like a reply from a colleague.
  6. They then keep watching for months, and they gather more credentials from the contacts.

Real-world example

Think of a locked briefcase that carries a company's secret plans. A thief does not take the briefcase. He copies the key, opens it at night, photographs the papers, and puts everything back exactly where it was. The owner never notices a missing item, and the copies are worth more than the briefcase itself.

Key takeaways

  • Email content can be the goal of an attack, not only a step toward another system.
  • Watch sign-ins from new devices and places, especially for executives.
  • Strong sign-in checks make stolen passwords much less useful.

In more detail

FIN4 is a financially motivated intrusion set that FireEye reported in 2014, describing a sustained campaign against publicly traded companies, their advisers and law firms, with the apparent goal of obtaining confidential information about upcoming market moving events. The operators did not deploy destructive malware or ransomware; they concentrated on stealing credentials for corporate email and then used those mailboxes to monitor and influence conversations between insiders, lawyers and advisors. The activity is remembered for its narrow focus and its patience, with victims in healthcare, pharmaceuticals and financial services, and for very low technical noise that left few traces beyond authentication records and mail access patterns. Attribution to a financially motivated group with possible state interests was offered with caution, and the case remains a good illustration of how mail access itself can be the objective rather than a stepping stone.

What you will practise

  • Explain why a mailbox can be a valuable target on its own.
  • Describe the signs of unauthorised mailbox access in authentication records.
  • Explain how stolen credentials support long term, quiet monitoring.
  • Explain the business risks of leaked confidential company information.
  • Outline controls that protect mail accounts at an organisation.
