T1496 Resource Hijacking

T1496ImpactExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This technique means an attacker uses your computers and network to make money for themselves. Common examples are using your machines to mine cryptocurrency, or using your internet connections as a relay for other people's traffic. You keep paying the bill while they keep the profit.

Why should you care?

Your systems slow down, and your cloud bill can grow very quickly. Mining makes processors hot, which shortens the life of the hardware. Proxying means your network address appears in other people's crimes, which can damage your reputation. Because nothing is stolen and no data is lost, the activity can run quietly for months before anyone notices. Rising costs are often the first clue.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker finds a way in, such as weak credentials or a service that is open to the internet.
  2. They install a small program that uses the processor, graphics card, or network connection.
  3. The program contacts the attacker infrastructure to receive work and to report results.
  4. It starts automatically after a reboot so the income continues without the attacker.
  5. It may limit its own use of the processor so users do not complain too early.
  6. Some versions remove other attackers programs so they do not share the profit.

Real-world example

Think of a laundrette where customers pay for the machines by the hour. A stranger works out how to start every machine without paying, then quietly runs their own laundry business on your electricity all night. Your machines are busy, your meter is spinning, and the stranger is collecting the money. Nothing in the shop has been stolen, which is exactly why it goes unnoticed for so long.

Key takeaways

  • Track your normal processor use and cloud spending so sudden rises stand out.
  • Attackers profit from your bill, so cost anomalies are a real security signal.
  • Watch for new cloud instances and workloads that nobody on your team created.

In more detail

Resource hijacking converts a victim estate into a source of profit. The attacker does not steal data so much as rent out capacity that belongs to someone else: processor and graphics cycles for cryptocurrency mining, network bandwidth resold through residential proxy networks, or messaging and cloud service quotas used to send bulk spam or generate paid traffic. The operations are economically motivated, so persistence and quiet operation matter more than speed, and the tell is usually a resource profile rather than a malicious file. Harvesting is often automated, scanning for exposed service interfaces, weak credentials or containers with no resource limits, and can affect a single host or an entire cloud subscription. Detection leans on sustained resource saturation compared with a learned baseline, connections to pool or proxy infrastructure, unusual cloud spending, and the sudden appearance of workloads or instances that no engineer can account for.

What you will practise

  • Define resource hijacking and explain the attacker motive behind it.
  • List several forms of resource hijacking beyond cryptocurrency mining.
  • Describe how computing, bandwidth and messaging resources can each be abused.
  • Identify the resource and cost telemetry that reveals hijacked capacity.
  • Explain why hijacking can continue unnoticed for a long time.
  • Describe how to distinguish legitimate heavy workloads from attacker activity.
