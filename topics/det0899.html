DET0899 Detect Social Engineering

DET0899Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Social engineering means tricking a person into doing something, such as opening a file or paying an invoice. This detection looks for messages and contact patterns that try to do that. It catches the attempt, not a software bug.

Why should you care?

Firewalls and antivirus tools do not stop a polite or frightening message. The attacker only needs one person to trust it. These messages often arrive first, before any tool is touched. If you spot the pattern early, you can warn people and block the sender before anyone acts.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. You collect mail gateway data. This includes the sender, the reply address and the subject.
  2. You check the sender's domain and its age, and whether it is close to a domain you trust.
  3. You check whether this sender has written to this person before. A first contact is a signal.
  4. You look at the message body for pressure words or for a payment or password request.
  5. You watch endpoints for the moment a user opens an attachment or follows a link.
  6. You combine these signals and send the highest scoring messages for human review.

Real-world example

Imagine a hospital reception desk. A stranger in a white coat smiles and says he is the new doctor. Nobody checked the badge. The coat and the confidence were the whole trick. Social engineering works like that: a friendly, urgent story is used instead of a forced door.

Key takeaways

  • Social engineering attacks people, so your people are also your sensors.
  • A first contact between sender and recipient is one of the strongest signals you have.
  • Lookalike domains and pressure wording together beat either signal alone.

In more detail

This detection strategy covers social engineering activity: email, messaging and voice contact that persuades a person to take an action rather than exploiting a technical flaw. It covers technique T1684 and matters because the human channel bypasses controls entirely, and because the first warning is often a message that looks almost legitimate. The telemetry that makes it possible is mail gateway metadata and headers, sender and domain reputation, first contact relationships between sender and recipient, collaboration platform message events, and endpoint process creation when a document or link is followed. In practice the strategy fails because the malicious content is designed to look normal, and because the same pattern is produced by legitimate suppliers, recruiters and bulk mail. The workable approach scores several weak signals together, such as a new sender, a lookalike domain, urgency wording in the subject and an unusual request, and then confirms with human reporting.

What you will practise

  • Define social engineering and distinguish it from technical exploitation of a software weakness
  • Identify the mail and collaboration metadata fields that indicate a first contact or a lookalike sender domain
  • Compare a sender domain against protected brand names using edit distance rather than exact matching
  • Correlate a suspicious message with the endpoint event that followed it, such as attachment execution or link redirection
  • Describe how user reports and mailbox rule indicators feed a social engineering detection programme
  • Set escalation criteria for invoice fraud, credential requests and executive impersonation
