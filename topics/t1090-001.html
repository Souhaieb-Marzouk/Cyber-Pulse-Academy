T1090.001 Proxy: Internal Proxy

T1090.001Command and ControlExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is a proxy placed inside your own network. One compromised computer acts as the middle point, and the other infected computers talk to it. Only that one computer needs to reach the internet. Unlike the parent technique, which covers middle machines of any kind, this sub-technique is about relays inside your network.

Why should you care?

Traffic between internal computers is usually trusted and rarely inspected. Using it hides attacker control traffic in plain sight. It also means fewer connections leave your network, so outside monitoring sees less. If you clean up one infected machine, the others can still reach the internet through a different relay. That is why internal relays are hard to remove completely.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker infects several computers and chooses one of them to act as the internal relay.
  2. The other infected computers connect to that relay instead of reaching the internet directly.
  3. The relay forwards their traffic to the real control server outside the network.
  4. Connections often use common internal protocols, such as file sharing, to blend in.
  5. If the relay is removed, another infected computer can take over the same role.
  6. Defenders look for unusual host-to-host connections and one external address serving many peers.

Real-world example

Think of a large office where staff are told not to call head office directly. Instead, each floor sends messages to one person at the front desk, who passes everything on. The phone company sees only one number dialling out. If that person leaves, another takes over the same job. An internal proxy is that front desk for malicious traffic.

Key takeaways

  • Watch internal host-to-host connections, not only traffic that leaves your network.
  • One outside address fed by many internal hosts is a strong clue.
  • Finding every peer matters, because relays can be replaced.

In more detail

An internal proxy relays command and control traffic between systems that are already inside the compromised environment, so only one host needs to reach the internet while the remaining implants communicate with that host. Adversaries favour peer-to-peer protocols such as SMB, the Windows file sharing protocol, and ordinary remote service ports, because that east-west traffic already exists in most networks and seldom raises suspicion. The result is fewer outbound connections, redundancy when a relay host is cleaned or taken offline, and an east-west path that is much harder to trace back to the operator. Detection looks at host-to-host connections that do not match normal service use, repeated relay chains across subnets, and a single external destination receiving traffic from many internal peers that do not normally talk to each other.

  • Describe why removing a single relay does not end the activity.
