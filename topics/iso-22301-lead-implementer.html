Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is a professional certificate about ISO 22301, the international standard for business continuity. Business continuity means keeping the important parts of a company working when something goes badly wrong. The word implementer means you learn how to build that system, not how to inspect somebody else's.

Why should you care?

Companies lose money and trust when a fire, flood, cyber attack or power cut stops their work. This certificate shows an employer that you can plan for those days and lead the response. It fits people who already work in risk, operations or information security. It often helps you move from a technical job into a management or resilience role.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Learn the vocabulary of the standard first, such as business continuity, recovery time objective and management system.
  2. Study the planning cycle in order, because the standard expects you to know how each step feeds the next one.
  3. Practise on a real organisation, even a small one, and write down which services are critical and who owns them.
  4. Draft the core documents yourself, including an impact analysis, a risk assessment and a continuity plan.
  5. Test your draft plan in a table top exercise. Then explain what failed and how you would fix it.
  6. Sit the exam, which mixes knowledge questions with scenario questions about an implementation project.

Real-world example

Think of a family bakery that loses power for two days. A careless owner waits for the lights to come back on. A careful owner knows the chilled stock must move to a neighbour's fridge within two hours. Two staff members hold spare keys, and customers can keep ordering by phone. The second owner did not stop the power cut, but kept almost all the income. A continuity plan is a written promise about what happens first, second and third.

Key takeaways

  • Continuity planning keeps services running during a crisis, while security work tries to stop the crisis.
  • You must read the standard itself, because the exam tests how well you understand its requirements.
  • Check with your chosen training provider whether the exam is a written paper, an assignment, or both.

In more detail

The ISO 22301 Lead Implementer is a professional certification for people who build and run a business continuity management system, which is the set of plans that keeps an organisation working during disruption. The programme sits at practitioner to manager level and is delivered by accredited training bodies rather than by the standards bodies themselves. It rewards candidates who already work in risk, resilience, operations or facilities management and who now want to prove they can set up the system, not just audit it. The exam is usually a written paper of multiple choice and scenario questions, and many providers also require a practical assignment in which you draft part of a continuity plan. It leads to roles such as business continuity manager, resilience officer, risk and compliance analyst, and information security officer with a continuity remit. Exam domains and objectives

  • Explain the purpose and structure of a business continuity management system
  • Plan an implementation project that fits the size, culture and risk appetite of a real organisation
  • Carry out a business impact analysis and record the recovery targets that come out of it
  • Select continuity strategies and solutions that can meet the agreed recovery targets
  • Run exercises that test plans and turn the lessons learned into improvements
  • Describe how internal audit, management review and corrective action keep the system alive
