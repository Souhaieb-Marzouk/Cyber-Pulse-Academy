Shared Responsibility Model

Cloud SecurityHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

The shared responsibility model describes how security duties are split between a cloud provider and you, the customer. The provider secures the cloud itself, such as buildings, hardware and the network. You secure what you put in the cloud, such as your data, your accounts, your settings and your code.

Why should you care?

Many people assume the provider handles all security, and that belief causes breaches. The cloud provider does protect the underlying platform, but it cannot know whether your storage should be public or your staff should have admin rights. Most cloud incidents happen in the customer's part of the stack. Knowing where the line falls tells you which work is still yours.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Start by listing the services you use, because the split differs for each one.
  2. For each service, find the provider's own description of who does what.
  3. Sort the duties into groups: physical, platform, operating system, identity, data and configuration.
  4. Write next to each duty the name of the team that owns it and the evidence they keep.
  5. Check the split again whenever you add a new service or a new feature.
  6. During an incident, use the list to know who to call and what logs to expect.

Real-world example

Renting a flat is a good comparison. The landlord keeps the building safe: the roof, the wiring, the front door lock and the fire alarms. You keep your own flat safe: you lock your door, you decide who gets a key, and you insure your belongings. If you leave your door open, that is not the landlord's fault.

Key takeaways

  • The provider secures the cloud; you secure what you put inside it.
  • The split changes with each service, so check it every time.
  • Write down who owns each duty before you need it in an incident.

In more detail

The shared responsibility model is the contractual and architectural division of security duties between a cloud service provider and its customer, in which the provider secures the cloud itself while the customer secures what they put in the cloud. The split is not uniform: for infrastructure as a service the provider owns the physical facilities, hosts, hypervisor and network fabric, while the customer owns the guest operating system, patching, identity, data and application; for platform as a service the provider absorbs the runtime and middleware, leaving the customer identity, configuration and data; for software as a service the provider operates nearly the whole stack and the customer retains responsibility for accounts, access grants, data classification and how the service is used. Security professionals rely on the model because most cloud incidents occur in the customer's portion of the stack, particularly identity misconfiguration, overly permissive policies, exposed storage and unpatched workloads. Applying it demands more than reading a marketing diagram: teams must map each control in their own framework to a named owner, record the evidence source, and confirm the split for every managed service they adopt, since the boundary shifts service by service and even feature by feature. Auditors expect this mapping to be documented and traceable, and incident responders need it to know who to contact and what telemetry the provider will and will not expose.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to explain how responsibility shifts across IaaS, PaaS and SaaS and why the boundary differs per managed service.
  • You will be able to map a control framework to provider and customer ownership and identify which side supplies the evidence.
  • You will be able to recognise the misconfiguration classes that repeatedly fall on the customer side of the line.
  • You will be able to use provider documentation to settle disputed ownership during an incident or audit.
  • You will be able to brief engineering and procurement teams on the security work they still own after adoption.
