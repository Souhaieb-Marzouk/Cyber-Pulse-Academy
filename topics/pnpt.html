Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

PNPT is a practical certification from TCM Security about penetration testing. A penetration tester is paid to attack a company's own systems with permission. The tester then reports what they find. This exam gives you a fake company network to attack over several days. You then write a report and talk about it with an assessor.

Why should you care?

Employers value this certification because it mirrors real work rather than a quiz. It proves you can plan an attack, keep going when something fails, and explain your findings clearly. The report and debrief show you can talk to clients, which many technical people find hardest. It is also one of the more affordable practical certifications, which makes it a good first step. It leads into junior penetration testing and red team roles.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Start with the fundamentals: how networks work, how to scan for services, and how to use Linux well.
  2. Learn hacking tools in a lab you own. Never practise on systems you do not have permission to test.
  3. Study Active Directory, which is the Microsoft system most companies use to manage users and computers.
  4. Practise writing reports as you learn. A finding without clear evidence and advice is not useful to a client.
  5. In the exam you receive a target network and five days to gather information, exploit it, and take control.
  6. You then have more time to submit a written report, followed by a live debrief where you explain your work.

Real-world example

Think of a building surveyor inspecting a large office. The surveyor does not simply find one cracked wall and leave. They walk every floor and note each problem. Then they hand the owner a report with photos and repair advice. A penetration test works the same way. OffSec and other exams often test a single technique. PNPT asks you to survey the whole building, then explain what you found to the owner face to face.

Key takeaways

  • Report writing is graded, so practise it from your first lab session.
  • You need patience for a multi day assessment. Plan your time and take good notes.
  • The live debrief is part of the exam. Be ready to explain your decisions out loud.

In more detail

TCM Security PNPT, the Practical Network Penetration Tester certification, is a hands on credential for people who want to prove they can run a full penetration test from start to finish. It suits career changers entering offensive security, junior penetration testers, and students who learn best by doing guided labs. The exam is a five day practical assessment against a corporate network, followed by a report and a live debrief with an assessor, so writing and explaining your work counts as much as exploitation. TCM Security publishes per domain weightings and the chapter list below follows them. Passing proves to an employer that you can perform reconnaissance, exploit a network, attack Active Directory, and deliver a professional report, and it leads to roles such as penetration tester, red team analyst, and security consultant. Exam domains and objectives

  • Gather information about a target network by using open source and active methods
  • Exploit vulnerable services to gain an initial foothold on a host
  • Attack an Active Directory environment and reach domain administrator level access
  • Escalate privileges and move between hosts without losing your access
  • Collect and organise evidence that supports every finding in your report
  • Write a professional penetration test report aimed at both technical and business readers
