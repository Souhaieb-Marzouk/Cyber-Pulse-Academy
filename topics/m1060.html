M1060 Out-of-Band Communications Channel

M1060Enterprise MitigationVery hardMitigation

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

An out of band channel is a second way to talk that does not use your normal network. It is used when that normal network is broken or under attack. Examples include mobile phones, a separate chat app, or a dial in number. The point is that the backup path still works when the main one does not.

Why should you care?

During a serious incident, attackers often sit inside email and chat systems and can read your plans. If your team coordinates there, the attacker learns what you are doing. Ordinary outages can also cut off your usual tools at the worst moment. A prepared second channel lets the right people talk, decide and act. Without one, response slows down exactly when speed matters most.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Decide who must be reachable during a major incident and keep their contact details current.
  2. Choose separate paths, such as mobile numbers, a stand alone messaging app or a dial in bridge.
  3. Keep the instructions and contact list offline, so they are usable when systems are down.
  4. Test the channel on a schedule and during exercises, not only when a real incident starts.
  5. Agree simple rules for what may be shared on the channel and how decisions get recorded.
  6. Afterwards, move the record of decisions back into your normal systems for the report.

Real-world example

Think of a lifeboat drill on a ship. The main radio is in the control room, so the crew also carries whistles, flares and a spare handheld radio. When the bridge loses power, the crew can still call for help and agree on a plan. Nobody waits for the drill day to find out whether the flares work.

Key takeaways

  • Assume the network you are defending is the network an attacker can read.
  • Keep channel details offline and tested, because a plan behind single sign-on may be unreachable.
  • Write decisions down during the incident and move them into your records afterwards.

In more detail

An out-of-band communications channel is a pre-arranged way for an incident response team to talk that does not depend on the network being defended. During a serious intrusion the primary estate is exactly what is compromised, so email, chat and voice over internet protocol cannot be trusted for coordination and may be monitored or tampered with by the adversary. The mechanism is to prepare separate paths in advance, such as a dedicated conference bridge, encrypted messaging on personal or separately managed devices, mobile numbers, satellite or radio links, and a physical meeting point, together with a directory and a call tree that work without the corporate network. In real environments these arrangements are rehearsed during exercises and stored offline, since a plan locked behind single sign-on on the compromised network is unavailable when needed. Evidence includes the contact roster, bridge details, test records and exercise logs showing the channel worked.

What you will practise

  • Explain how to rehearse a backup channel during exercises.
  • Identify the records that show an out of band plan is maintained and tested.
