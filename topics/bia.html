Business Impact Analysis

GRC & ComplianceVery hardKeyword

A business impact analysis is the structured study that determines how the loss or degradation of each business process, and the resources that process depends on, would affect the organisation over time. It is the analytical foundation of business continuity and disaster recovery planning because it produces the numbers that drive every later decision: the maximum tolerable period of disruption, the recovery time objective, the recovery point objective, the minimum acceptable service level and the priority order for restoration when resources are scarce. A competent analysis works process-first rather than asset-first. It starts from the organisation's products and services, breaks them into business processes, identifies the people, applications, data, facilities, suppliers and infrastructure each process needs, and then models impact across several dimensions: financial, regulatory and legal, operational, customer and reputational, and safety where relevant. Impact is assessed over a time axis because most disruption is tolerable for minutes and intolerable after days, and that curve is what makes a recovery objective defensible rather than arbitrary. Practitioners should insist on named process owners providing input, because a BIA produced solely by the security team will encode technical assumptions that no part of the business has agreed to. The output feeds continuity strategies, dependency mapping, third-party risk assessment, recovery testing scenarios and the risk register, and it should be refreshed when the business changes rather than on a fixed multi-year cycle alone.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to derive business processes and their dependencies from products and services rather than from an asset list.
  • You will be able to model impact across financial, regulatory, operational, customer and safety dimensions.
  • You will be able to build an impact-over-time curve that justifies a maximum tolerable period of disruption.
  • You will be able to run structured interviews with process owners that produce evidence rather than reassurance.
  • You will be able to derive recovery priorities and dependencies that shape continuity and recovery strategies.
  • You will be able to spot where an untested dependency or single supplier invalidates a stated recovery objective.
