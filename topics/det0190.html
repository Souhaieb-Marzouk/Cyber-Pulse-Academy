DET0190 Detect MFA Modification or Disabling Across Platforms

DET0190Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This detection watches for changes that turn off or weaken the second step of a login. That second step is often a code from a phone app or a text message. Attackers remove it so a stolen password is enough on its own. The rule looks at who made the change and which account was affected.

Why should you care?

MFA is the reason a stolen password often fails to give access. Remove it and the password alone opens the door. Attackers who already have a password will try this quickly. The change is recorded in the identity system, so it can be caught. A fast alert here can prevent a full account takeover.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. An account holder or administrator changes an MFA setting in the identity system.
  2. The identity provider writes an audit record with the actor, the target and the time.
  3. The record shows what changed, such as a factor removed or a policy exclusion added.
  4. The rule checks whether the actor is allowed to make that change for that target.
  5. It also checks the source address and whether the actor is a helpdesk or an administrator.
  6. Unusual combinations raise an alert with both accounts and the change listed.

Real-world example

Think of a bank vault that needs a key card and a fingerprint. One morning the fingerprint reader is switched off for a single safe deposit box. The guard sees the change in the maintenance log. The request came from a staff member who does not manage that box. The vault was not opened, but the missing second check is the story.

Key takeaways

  • Removing MFA converts a stolen password into full access.
  • Track the actor and the target account together, since both are needed for context.
  • Self service resets are normal, so bulk or helpdesk changes need their own rules.

In more detail

This strategy detects changes to multi factor authentication settings that weaken an account, covering factor removal, factor re-registration to an attacker controlled device, per user MFA disablement and policy exclusions. It matters because MFA is the control that most often stops credential theft from becoming account takeover, so removing it is a high value step for an adversary who already holds a password. Detection relies on identity provider audit records for authentication method changes and policy edits, correlated with the actor, the target account and the source address. The main way it fails is context: self service factor resets, lost phone recovery and bulk policy maintenance all produce the same records, so the discriminator has to be who changed what for whom, not the change type alone.

What you will practise

  • Explain how MFA removal converts credential theft into account takeover.
  • Identify the identity provider audit records for factor removal and policy changes.
  • Correlate the acting account with the target account and the source address.
  • Separate self service factor resets from administrative changes to other accounts.
  • Recognise conditional access policy exclusions as a quieter way to bypass MFA.
  • Describe how to alert on factor re-registration to a new device after a password reset.
