G0027 Threat Group-3390

G0027Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

Threat Group-3390 is a long-running intrusion set that public reporting, including work published by several vendors and by MITRE ATT&CK, associates with activity widely assessed by third parties as linked to a Chinese state-interest nexus, though no government has formally indicted or sanctioned the activity under this name, so the sponsorship claim rests on vendor analysis rather than official attribution. The cluster is tracked under many overlapping names, including Emissary Panda, Lucky Mouse, Bronze Union, Earth Smilodon, and APT27, and different publishers have drawn the boundaries of those labels differently over time, with some activity reassigned between clusters as new evidence emerged. Reporting describes targeting of government ministries and agencies, defence and aerospace organisations, energy and critical infrastructure operators, gaming and technology companies, and organisations in the Middle East, Central Asia, Southeast Asia, and Europe, with additional victims reported in North America. The group is notable in open reporting for combining web-facing exploitation with heavy use of publicly available remote access tooling, which lowers the cost of operations and blurs the line between targeted intrusion and commodity compromise. Described tradecraft includes exploitation of public-facing applications for initial access, abuse of external remote services including web shells and remote management interfaces, credential dumping and reuse of valid administrator accounts, lateral movement over standard administrative protocols, scheduled task and service-based persistence, and exfiltration staged to attacker-controlled or legitimate third-party web services. Several reporting streams describe usage of custom downloaders alongside commodity tooling and an interest in harvesting intellectual property and email. Defenders should note that the group's reliance on administration-facing protocols means detection strategies centred on endpoint malware alone will miss significant portions of the intrusion lifecycle, and that the use of legitimate remote services makes allow-listing decisions consequential.

What you will practise

  • Explain how web shell persistence on an internet-facing server is detected and how to prioritise containment once found.
  • Detect abuse of external remote services and administrative protocols that blend with legitimate management traffic.
  • Analyse the risk of credential dumping and administrative account reuse when designing tiered administration controls.
  • Build detection logic for scheduled task and service creation used for persistence and evaluate its false positive profile.
  • Explain how staged exfiltration to legitimate third-party web services evades protocol-based egress controls and what replaces those controls.
  • Describe how cluster boundaries shift as vendors reassign activity and why that matters for indicator sharing.
  • Prioritise detection effort across web-facing exploitation, identity abuse, and lateral movement for an organisation in a targeted sector.
