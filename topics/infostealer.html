Infostealer

Endpoint & MalwareVery hardKeyword

An infostealer is malware whose primary purpose is to harvest and exfiltrate credentials and other valuable data from an endpoint quickly and with minimal footprint. Unlike ransomware, which seeks to disrupt, an infostealer is designed to be quiet: it typically runs for seconds or minutes, collects what it can, sends it out, and often removes itself. The target set is broad and highly specific at the same time. Browser stores are the richest source, including saved passwords, session cookies and authentication tokens, and the theft of live session cookies is particularly damaging because it lets an intruder reuse an already authenticated session and sidestep multi-factor authentication entirely. Beyond browsers, stealers target cryptocurrency wallet files, messaging and file-transfer application sessions, saved credentials in mail and remote-access clients, keylogging of anything typed, and bulk collection of documents and configuration files from user profiles. Distribution is usually opportunistic and consumer-oriented: cracked software, fake installers, malicious advertising, game cheat utilities and community-shared files, which is why an infostealer detections often first appear on a personal or unmanaged device before the stolen credentials are used against a corporate service. For defenders the implications are architectural rather than purely endpoint-focused. Stolen session cookies and tokens invalidate the comfortable assumption that multi-factor authentication makes credential theft survivable, so detection must include impossible-travel style session anomalies, first-time device and user-agent combinations, and token reuse from unexpected network locations. Response to a confirmed infostealer infection requires mass credential rotation, revocation of active sessions and refresh tokens, not just removal of the file.

What you will practise

  • Explain why infostealers prioritise browser credential and session-cookie theft over persistence or disruption.
  • Describe how stolen cookies and tokens let an attacker bypass multi-factor authentication at the session layer.
  • Identify high-value local data stores beyond browsers, including wallets, messaging clients and saved remote-access profiles.
  • Recognise the common distribution channels that place stealers on unmanaged or lightly managed endpoints.
  • Build detections for stolen-session reuse, including device, user-agent and network-location anomalies.
  • Run a post-infection response that revokes sessions and rotates credentials rather than only removing malware.
