G0093 GALLIUM

G0093Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

GALLIUM is a name for a spying group that goes after telephone and internet providers. Public reports describe activity from around 2012, with victims in Southeast Asia, Europe and Africa. The group stays on important servers for a very long time. It often uses the same administrative tools that your own engineers use. Another common name for the activity is Granite Typhoon.

Why should you care?

Telecom networks carry calls, messages and internet traffic for millions of people. Access to those systems can reveal who talks to whom, not just what a single file says. The group hides among normal administration work, so it is easy to miss. Protecting management interfaces and reviewing administrator actions are therefore essential. It also shows why long term access is more dangerous than a quick break-in.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers study a telephone or internet provider and all of its public facing services.
  2. They gain a first foothold through stolen credentials or an exposed management service.
  3. They then use the provider's own administration tools to move quietly around the network.
  4. They then collect account data and many details about the provider's own customers.
  5. They then keep working access so that they can return after small cleanups.
  6. They extend reach to other systems that share trust with the first one.

Real-world example

Imagine the staff who maintain the lifts in a tall building. They have keys to every floor and a list of who lives where. A stranger who copies their keys can visit any apartment without breaking a lock. The residents see nothing unusual, because lift maintenance happens every day. Controlling those keys is the whole defence.

Key takeaways

  • Systems that hold records about many people are high value targets.
  • Administrator tools need logging, review and tight access control.
  • Durable access survives quick cleanups, so rotate credentials after an incident.

In more detail

GALLIUM, also tracked as Granite Typhoon, is an espionage cluster that public reporting from Broadcom and other vendors links to activity against telecommunications operators and, in some cases, their customers, with victims reported in Southeast Asia, Europe and Africa from about 2012 onward. The operators were notable for maintaining long term access to high value servers and network equipment, for using living off the land techniques with existing administration tools, and for developing tooling that targets remote access and the systems that manage subscriber data. Attribution to a state linked nexus with an interest in telecommunications intelligence was offered as an assessment rather than a proven fact, and reporting has noted overlaps with other clusters, which complicates separation. The practical consequence for defenders is that telecom infrastructure demands strict control of management interfaces, credential rotation and monitoring of legitimate administrative tooling, because those are precisely the channels this activity relies on.
