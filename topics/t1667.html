T1667 Email Bombing

T1667ImpactExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Email bombing means flooding someone's mailbox with a huge number of messages on purpose. The attacker often signs the target up for many mailing lists at once. The mailbox fills with junk until real messages are impossible to find. Nothing is broken, but the person cannot work normally.

Why should you care?

A buried inbox hides the messages that warn you about a real attack. A password reset alert, a bank fraud notice or a help desk reply can all disappear under thousands of newsletters. Attackers use this cover story, then phone the victim pretending to be support and ask for a code. That is how credentials get stolen and ransomware gets installed. It is also a simple way to harass a person or slow down a whole team.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker collects the target's email address, usually from a public website or a leaked list.
  2. They use an automated tool to submit that address to many websites that send newsletters or confirmation mail.
  3. Because those sites do not check that the owner agreed, the signups are accepted and the messages start arriving.
  4. Thousands of messages land in a short time, pushing genuine mail far down the inbox list.
  5. While the victim and the mail team struggle with the flood, the attacker sends a fraud message that blends in.
  6. The attacker then calls the victim, pretends to be support, and guides them into sharing a code or granting access.

Real-world example

Imagine a hospital night desk with one tray for urgent patient notes. Someone quietly subscribes that desk to five hundred free magazines. By morning the tray is a mountain of paper. Buried near the bottom is the one note that says a patient is deteriorating. The clerk cannot find it in time. Worse, a stranger then walks up and says they are from the magazine company. He offers to help sort the pile if the clerk just signs this form.

Key takeaways

  • A sudden flood of newsletters is often cover for one important hidden message.
  • The follow-up phone call matters more than the flood, so never share codes with callers.
  • Alert on inbound volume spikes next to credential or payment events.

In more detail

Email bombing is a volumetric technique against a human inbox rather than a service. The adversary subscribes the target address to large numbers of newsletters, signup forms and mailing lists that do not use confirmed opt-in, so the victim receives thousands of messages within minutes. Some variants simply send bulk mail directly. The operational goal is cover: the flood buries password reset alerts, help desk tickets, invoice threads and fraud notifications, so a genuine malicious message or a business email compromise thread is never read. Because the inbound traffic is largely legitimate mail from real senders, spam filtering usually cannot block it, and blocking rules risk rejecting wanted mail. Mail gateway and SMTP logs show a sharp spike in inbound volume, many new sending domains, and a high ratio of messages with no prior history to the receiving domain; mailbox audit logs show an outbound recovery or password reset request issued during the spike. The classic follow-up is a voice call from someone posing as support, so the pattern to hunt is a volume spike plus a credential or payment event inside the same short window.

What you will practise

  • Define email bombing and explain why spam filters rarely stop it.
  • Describe how automated signups to unverified mailing lists create the flood.
  • Explain how the flood hides security alerts and business email.
  • Recognise the combination of a mail spike and a follow-up phone call as one campaign.
  • Identify the log sources that reveal inbound volume anomalies and mailbox activity.
