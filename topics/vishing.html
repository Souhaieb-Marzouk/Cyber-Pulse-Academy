Vishing

Security AwarenessHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Vishing is a phone call made by a criminal to trick you into giving away information or money. The word joins voice and phishing. Phishing is a fake message that pretends to come from someone you trust. In vishing the fake message arrives as a live voice instead of written text.

Why should you care?

A live caller can answer your doubts and sound completely convincing. Callers can fake the number shown on your screen, so it may look like your own bank. Staff who are careful with email may still trust a friendly voice. One call can lead to a payment, a password or access to a system. Training helps, but clear rules and independent checks protect you far more.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker picks a target and learns names, roles and recent events from public sources.
  2. They call and pretend to be somebody trusted, such as support staff or a bank officer.
  3. They create urgency with a problem that seems to need an answer immediately.
  4. They ask for small pieces of information first, then build up to larger requests.
  5. They may ask you to read out a code or approve a login prompt on your phone.
  6. They may also fake the caller number so the display shows a real company.

Real-world example

Picture a quiet office at five in the afternoon. The phone rings and a calm voice says it is the bank. The voice claims a suspicious payment is leaving your account right now. The voice asks you to confirm a code sent to your phone. Because you are worried and the voice is kind, you read the code out. One simple rule would have saved you: hang up and call the bank using the number on your card.

Key takeaways

  • A displayed caller number can be faked, so never trust it as proof of identity.
  • End the call and dial back on a number you find yourself.
  • Never read out a login code or approve a prompt for somebody who called you.

In more detail

Vishing is voice phishing: a social engineering attack in which a caller, or an automated call, impersonates a trusted person or organisation to obtain information, money or access. It works by combining live conversation with pressure, so the attacker can adapt to objections and exploit authority, urgency and fear in ways a written message cannot. In real environments it appears as calls claiming to come from information technology support, a bank fraud team, a supplier accounts department, or an executive asking for quiet help, and it is often paired with email or text messages that make the story credible. The mistake defenders make is treating it as an awareness poster problem: technical and procedural controls, such as verifying through a number the recipient looks up independently, phishing resistant multi factor authentication, and documented payment change procedures, reduce impact far more than warnings alone.

What you will practise

  • Explain what vishing is and how it differs from phishing by email.
  • Describe how attackers build a believable story before a call.
  • Explain why urgency and authority are effective in a live conversation.
  • Describe the callback verification rule and when to apply it.
  • Explain how a real time login code can be relayed during a call.
  • Identify procedural controls that reduce the impact of a successful call.
