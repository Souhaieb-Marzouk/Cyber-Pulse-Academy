T1078 Valid Accounts

T1078Initial AccessExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Valid accounts means an intruder signs in with real usernames and passwords instead of breaking software. The credentials may be default logins on a new device, passwords stolen earlier, or genuine cloud and company accounts. Every login succeeds, so the activity looks like normal work. This is why the technique is so hard to spot.

Why should you care?

Most security tools look for strange software, not for a correct password. An intruder with a working account can read mail, open files and change settings while every door opens politely. Default passwords still shipped on devices are a common way in. If you do not compare logins with how an account is normally used, a break-in can look like a busy employee.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The intruder gets credentials, from default settings, a previous breach or a purchased list.
  2. They sign in at a console, over remote desktop, through a web portal or by an interface key.
  3. An interface key is a long secret token that lets software call a service without typing a password.
  4. The login succeeds, so the system records a normal and fully authenticated session.
  5. The intruder then acts as that user, within whatever access the account already has.
  6. Analysts look for logins that are valid but unusual for that person, place and device.

Real-world example

Picture a house where the owner hides a spare key under the flowerpot. A visitor finds it and lets themselves in. Nothing is broken, no alarm sounds, and the door closes gently behind them. They can open the fridge and read the post. The only clue is that the burglar alarm was never armed by anyone who lives there.

Key takeaways

  • A correct password is not proof of a legitimate person.
  • Change default credentials before any device is placed on the network.
  • Judge logins by context: which account, from where, on which device, at what time.

In more detail

Valid Accounts describes adversary use of legitimate credentials or existing accounts to gain and keep access rather than exploiting a software flaw. The accounts involved may be default or built-in accounts shipped with an operating system, application or appliance, local or domain accounts whose passwords were obtained earlier in an intrusion, or cloud, SaaS and federated identities issued to real users or service principals. The technique is attractive precisely because the resulting activity is authenticated and therefore blends into the very large volume of normal sign-in and administration traffic. Mechanism varies by target: an interactive logon at a console or over remote desktop, a remote service session over SSH or server message block, an API call authenticated with a stolen token, or a service account used to run scheduled work. The artefacts that make investigation possible are concentrated in identity and access telemetry, including authentication logs, sign-in records carrying location, device and client application, token issuance and refresh events, Kerberos ticket requests, privilege role assignments and group membership changes, alongside the endpoint and network records of what the authenticated session then did. Distinguishing misuse from normal work depends on context: impossible travel, sign-ins from hosting or anonymising infrastructure, first-time combinations of device and application, dormant accounts that suddenly become active, service accounts authenticating interactively, and privilege use far outside an established baseline. Because the credentials are genuine, the decisive question in an investigation is rarely whether authentication succeeded but whether the account, the source and the behaviour are consistent with how that identity is normally used, and whether the same session also produced actions that no business process explains.

What you will practise

  • Explain how default, local, domain and federated accounts differ as footholds and what each leaves in identity telemetry.
  • Interpret sign-in, token and Kerberos records to identify authentication that is valid but inconsistent with an identity baseline.
  • Recognise the behavioural signatures of dormant, service and shared account misuse.
  • Correlate identity evidence with endpoint and network activity to confirm what an authenticated session actually did.
  • Prioritise credential hygiene controls such as multifactor authentication, just-in-time access and privileged access management against observed gaps.
  • Document a valid-account incident timeline from first anomalous sign-in through credential rotation and containment.
