Business Email Compromise

Email SecurityHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Business email compromise is a scam that uses a company email account to trick people. The attacker either breaks into a real mailbox or pretends to be someone you trust. They then ask for money or for secret information. The message looks normal because it comes from a known name.

Why should you care?

Staff are trained to act quickly on emails from managers and suppliers. A single payment to the wrong bank account can be very large and hard to recover. These scams often skip normal virus checks because there is no harmful file. Companies also lose trust with customers and partners after such an event. Clear payment rules stop most of these losses.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker studies the company online and learns who approves payments and invoices.
  2. They send a fake login page by email, hoping one employee enters their password.
  3. With that stolen account, or a very similar fake address, they read old email threads.
  4. They copy the usual tone and send a request for payment or for private data.
  5. They add pressure, such as a deadline, so the employee skips the normal checks.
  6. The money is sent, and the attacker moves it on quickly before anyone notices.

Real-world example

Imagine a shared flat where everyone trusts a note left on the kitchen table. A stranger learns the house style and leaves a note asking for the rent to be paid to a new account. It looks exactly like the usual note. The flatmate pays without calling to check. The lesson is simple. Money requests need a check you can trust.

Key takeaways

  • Always confirm a change of bank details by calling a number you already know.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication so a stolen password is not enough.
  • Publish email authentication records so others can spot fake messages from your domain.

In more detail

Business email compromise is a fraud category in which an attacker abuses a trusted email identity, either by taking over a real mailbox or by impersonating one closely enough to be believed, and uses that trust to move money or data. Because the message comes from a genuine account or a convincing lookalike, it often passes ordinary spam and authentication checks, and the ask is usually a payment to a changed bank account, a change of payroll details, or the release of sensitive records. The typical path starts with reconnaissance on public sources to learn reporting lines and who authorises payments, then a credential phishing page, session theft, or an unenforced authentication policy that permits domain spoofing. The attacker reads existing threads to copy tone, timing, and signatures before inserting a request, and pressure or a familiar executive voice is used to bypass the usual verification habit. An email gateway may detect the initial credential phishing, but the later fraudulent request can look entirely legitimate, so controls such as multi-factor authentication, out-of-band verification of payment changes, and mailbox audit trails matter more than content filtering alone.

What you will practise

  • Explain what business email compromise is and how it differs from ordinary phishing.
  • Describe how a mailbox takeover leads to fraudulent payment requests.
  • Explain why payment and payroll detail changes need out-of-band verification.
  • Identify detection opportunities such as mailbox rules, logins, and forwarding changes.
  • Describe the email authentication records that make domain spoofing harder.
  • Outline the first response steps when a fraudulent payment is discovered.
