DET0870 Detection of Social Media Accounts

DET0870Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A social media account compromise means someone else gains control of a company or staff account. The attacker can then post messages or send direct messages that look official. This detection looks for the first sign of that takeover in login records.

Why should you care?

People trust messages from an account they already follow. That trust is exactly what an attacker wants. A single post can send many staff or customers to a fake login page. The takeover also damages the brand and takes time to undo. Login records usually show the takeover before any post appears.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The platform or the single sign on provider writes an audit record when someone signs in or changes account settings.
  2. Those records are sent to your SIEM, together with the risk score the provider gives to each sign in.
  3. The rule looks for a failed sign in burst followed by a success from a new address, a new device or an unusual country.
  4. It also watches for changes right after that sign in, such as a new recovery address, a new application approval or a new administrator.
  5. When the pattern appears, the alert names the account, the address and the change so the owner can be reached at once.

Real-world example

Think of a till receipt at a shop. The receipt shows who opened the register, at what time and what changed in the drawer. If a register is opened at three in the morning by a staff card that belongs to someone on holiday, the receipt is enough to start asking questions.

Key takeaways

  • A takeover shows up as an unusual sign in long before the first malicious post.
  • Risk scoring from the identity provider is a strong signal, but it must be routed into your SIEM.
  • Fast containment matters more than perfect detection here, because each minute of access can reach the audience.

In more detail

This detection strategy covers the compromise of social media accounts, where an adversary takes over a corporate or employee social identity to support later phishing and impersonation. It matters because a post or message from a trusted company account reaches customers and staff who would ignore the same message from an unknown sender, and because account takeover is visible in login telemetry long before the abuse becomes public. The telemetry that makes it possible is the platform audit log, the identity provider sign-in log with risk annotations and the email security gateway report of messages sent from your own domain. It fails in practice because many organisations do not queue social platform audit logs into the SIEM, so the only signal left is the phishing report that arrives after the abuse.

What you will practise

  • Explain why a trusted social identity is valuable to an adversary
  • List the audit events that show a social account has changed hands
  • Correlate identity provider risk scores with account setting changes in the same session
  • Configure alerting that reaches the account owner and the communications team quickly
  • Define a containment checklist for a confirmed social account takeover
