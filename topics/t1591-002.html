T1591.002 Gather Victim Org Information: Business Relationships

T1591.002ReconnaissanceExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This sub-technique is about mapping the other companies your organisation works with. It covers suppliers, contractors, and support firms. It also covers where your hardware and software come from before they reach you.

Why should you care?

Attackers often enter through a partner instead of your front door. A support company with remote access may be easier to compromise than you are. Knowing your suppliers also helps an attacker fake an invoice or a delivery. If you cannot list your partners, you cannot watch what they do on your systems.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker reads case studies and partner pages that name your suppliers and contractors.
  2. They follow social media posts that mention joint projects, shared events, or new contracts.
  3. They identify support firms that hold remote access to your systems and data.
  4. They trace where your hardware and software come from before it reaches you.
  5. They may contact staff, pretending to be a supplier, to confirm which systems are shared.
  6. They use the relationships to send believable invoices, software updates, or access requests.

Real-world example

Think of a small bakery that sells bread to a chain of cafes. The bakery buys flour from a mill and hires a mechanic to service the ovens. The cafe chain's logo appears on the bakery window as a proud partnership. A stranger reads that window and learns three things at once. They know who supplies the flour and who repairs the ovens. They also know which cafes will accept the bakery name as a reason to open the back door. Business relationship gathering is reading that window. Attackers follow the story between companies to find the weakest link in the chain.

Key takeaways

  • Write down your critical suppliers and how each one connects to your systems.
  • Require strong checks before anyone acts on a supplier request for access or payment.
  • Remember that a partner's weak security can quickly become your incident.

In more detail

This sub-technique covers collection about the organisations a victim depends on and is depended on by: managed service providers, contractors, and partners that hold connected and often privileged network access, plus the supply chains and shipment paths that deliver hardware and software. It frequently overlaps with network trust dependencies, but the emphasis here is commercial relationship and supply path rather than the resulting network path. Adversaries gather it from partner and case study pages, award notices, joint marketing material, social media, and filings, and they confirm specifics by eliciting answers from staff who believe they are helping a real supplier. The value is that a weaker partner becomes an entry route, and a mapped supply path becomes a target for interference or impersonation. Victim telemetry is largely limited to authentication from partner address ranges, vendor portal activity, and payment or change requests that arrive outside normal channels, so ticket correlation and supplier allow-lists drive detection, not signatures.

What you will practise

  • Explain what business relationship gathering collects and why partners attract adversaries.
  • Identify public sources that disclose suppliers, contractors, and supply paths.
  • Describe how supply chain detail enables impersonation and interference.
  • Recognise telemetry that reveals misuse of legitimate partner access.
  • Propose controls that keep third-party relationships inventoried and verified.
