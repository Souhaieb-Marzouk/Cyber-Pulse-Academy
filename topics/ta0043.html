TA0043 Reconnaissance

TA0043EnterpriseExtremeTactic

Reconnaissance is the set of adversary activities that gather information about a target before and alongside intrusion attempts, and it is the one tactic where most of the work can happen entirely outside the victim's visibility. Attackers enumerate publicly reachable services, harvest employee names and email addresses from social media and conference sites, fingerprint technology stacks from HTTP responses and TLS certificates, review job postings for named products, and search breach data and code repositories for credentials that were never meant to be public. Active variants cross into the target's own address space: port and service scanning, directory brute forcing, DNS zone transfer attempts, and probing of VPN or webmail portals that leave firewall, load balancer, and web server logs. Telemetry available to defenders is thin and noisy. Internet-facing sensors, honeypots, passive DNS, certificate transparency monitoring, and brand monitoring can reveal scanning or credential-harvesting infrastructure, while email security gateways may show early directory-harvest attempts or spoofed display names. Detection is difficult in isolation because scanning traffic is indistinguishable from vulnerability scanners, search engine crawlers, and researchers on most networks, and because the highest-value reconnaissance often produces no packets to the target at all. Effective defence is therefore mostly preparation: reducing the public attack surface, keeping an accurate external asset inventory, monitoring certificate and DNS changes, and correlating low-severity internet telemetry so the earliest stage of a campaign is not silently discarded.

What you will practise

  • Explain how the Reconnaissance tactic maps to adversary information gathering before initial access, and why most of it never touches victim-owned infrastructure.
  • Identify the telemetry that genuinely exists for this tactic: internet-facing scan logs, honeypot hits, passive DNS, certificate transparency records, email gateway anomalies, and brand monitoring feeds.
  • Distinguish opportunistic scanning noise from targeted enumeration using source reputation, timing, target selection, and request content.
  • Correlate several low-severity external observations, such as certificate issuance for a lookalike domain plus a login-attempt spike, into one campaign hypothesis.
  • Assess how much of an estate is discoverable from the public internet and prioritise attack-surface reduction accordingly.
  • Write and tune triage logic that suppresses crawler and vulnerability-scanner noise without discarding targeted probing.
