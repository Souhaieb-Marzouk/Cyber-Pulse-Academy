T1585 Establish Accounts

T1585Resource DevelopmentExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is when an attacker creates accounts on websites and services before an operation. The accounts may look like ordinary people or fake companies. The attacker builds a story around them so they seem real. This is preparation, not an attack on a victim yet. The accounts are the tools they will use later.

Why should you care?

A message from a brand new account is easy to ignore. A message from an account with a history, photos and shared contacts looks normal. Attackers know this, so they spend time building believable identities. These accounts are then used for phishing, fake job offers or file sharing. Because the accounts belong to real services, blocking them by domain does not work. You need to judge the account, not the website.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker registers one or more email accounts, often with providers that ask few questions.
  2. They add profile details such as a name, a job, a photo and a location.
  3. They connect the account to other accounts to create a believable network.
  4. They keep the account active for a while so it does not look new.
  5. They use free trials and cloud sign-ups to obtain storage or computing power.
  6. Later they use this identity to approach targets without raising any suspicion at all.

Real-world example

Imagine you want to join a members-only club. The doorman checks whether other members know you. A stranger with no history is turned away. So before you apply, you join a gym, a book group and a running club, and you get to know a few members. When you finally reach the door, three people say they know you. An attacker does the same thing online. They build a small, believable history so the first contact seems friendly and safe.

Key takeaways

  • A friendly profile is not proof that a real person is behind it.
  • Check how old an account is and what it did before contacting you.
  • New accounts that ask for files or payments deserve extra care.

In more detail

Establishing accounts is the deliberate construction of identities the adversary will use later, and it sits in resource development because the work happens before any victim is touched. A usable persona needs more than a registration: a plausible name, a work history, a profile photo, posts, and connections to other accounts, so a target who searches for the sender finds corroboration rather than an empty shell. Email accounts are the most reusable asset, because nearly every other service, including domains, cloud tenants and code repositories, verifies identity through a mailbox. Defenders observe this indirectly, through account age, sending history, domain reputation, and the mismatch between an account's claimed past and its actual footprint.

What you will practise

  • Explain why adversaries build personas before attacking a target.
  • Describe the steps used to make a new account look established.
  • Identify signals that show an account has little real history.
  • Relate account creation to later phishing and infrastructure acquisition.
  • Apply verification habits when a new online contact asks for action.
