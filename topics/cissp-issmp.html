Simple definition

The Information Systems Security Management Professional (ISSMP) is an advanced certificate from ISC2. It is open only to people who already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP). It tests whether you can manage a programme, a budget and a team. It does not test whether you can configure a firewall.

Why should you care?

Most security careers slow down when the work stops being technical. This certificate shows an employer that you can run a whole programme. It is a strong signal for manager, director and governance roles. It also builds on the leadership side of the CISSP. The core exam only touches that side lightly. That makes it useful for a job where you own policy and people.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Check that you already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP). This exam is only open to holders.
  2. Read the official ISC2 exam outline and list every domain. Then write down the tasks under each one.
  3. Study security governance, risk management, and contingency planning, which carry the largest share of questions.
  4. Learn how to build a business case and set a budget. Then learn to report risk to senior leaders.
  5. Practise scenario questions where several answers are defensible and you must pick the best management choice.
  6. The exam is multiple choice, so plan several months of study around your current job.

Real-world example

Picture a ransomware attack at a hospital. A team lead asks which backup to restore first. A security manager asks different questions. Who decides whether to pay? Who talks to patients and regulators? How long can each service stay down? Who signs off when the recovery plan changes? The ISSMP exam lives in that second set of questions. It rewards people who can hold a plan together while everyone else is under pressure.

Key takeaways

  • You must already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) before you sit this exam.
  • The questions test management judgement, so study decision making and not only definitions.
  • Expect to justify spending, staffing and risk decisions to a senior audience.

In more detail

The Information Systems Security Management Professional (ISSMP) is an advanced concentration exam from ISC2 for people who already hold the Certified Information Systems Security Professional (CISSP) and want to be judged on management rather than hands on engineering. It suits security managers, programme leads and consultants who own budgets, policy and staff. It proves to an employer that you can build a security programme, lead a team, argue for funding and keep the organisation running through a serious incident. The exam is multiple choice and tests judgement at the level of a manager, so the questions read like the decisions you make in a weekly leadership meeting rather than like configuration tasks. It leads to roles such as security manager, security programme director, governance lead and chief information security officer. The domain structure below follows the published objectives; ISC2 has refreshed this outline, so check the vendor's current version and weighting before you book. Exam domains and objectives

  • Build a security programme that matches the organisation's strategy and risk appetite
  • Lead and organise a security team, including hiring, training and measuring performance
  • Run enterprise risk management and explain the results to senior leaders
  • Use threat modelling to shape which controls the organisation funds first
  • Write and test contingency plans that keep critical services running during an incident
