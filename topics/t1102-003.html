T1102.003 Web Service: One-Way Communication

T1102.003Command and ControlExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is command and control where the attacker only sends orders through a normal website. The infected computer reads those orders and does the work. Its results do not go back through that same website, if they are sent anywhere at all.

Why should you care?

Traffic that only reads from a public service looks even more ordinary than a two-way conversation. The website that carries the orders never sees anything strange being posted, so it holds no obvious signal. If results travel through a second service, you must connect two different sets of records to see the whole picture. Blocking one part of the chain may not stop the tasking.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The implant watches one public service, such as a page, feed or shared document.
  2. When the content changes, the implant treats the new text as its instructions.
  3. It runs those instructions on the victim host, quietly and in the background.
  4. Output may be sent later through a different service, or through the first control channel.
  5. For simple orders, such as delete a file, no output is sent back to the attacker at all.
  6. So the read side is visible in web records, while the result side may be hidden elsewhere.

Real-world example

Picture a shared flat where instructions arrive in the letterbox. You read the note, do the shopping and never post a reply. Your friend simply leaves the next note the following day and assumes the job was done. If a receipt is needed, you leave it somewhere else entirely, at a different address that your friend checks separately.

Key takeaways

  • Tasking and results are split, so one service may show only the quiet read side.
  • The implant is the better detection target than the website, because its polling habits stand out.
  • Connect records from more than one provider to see the full path between tasking and results.

In more detail

One way communication uses a public web service to deliver tasking without returning results over that same channel. The implant periodically fetches content from the platform and treats it as instructions, but the command output either travels over a separate channel, such as a different service or the original payload channel, or is not sent at all because the adversary only needs to push a command. This split reduces the volume and symmetry of traffic, so the pattern looks closer to ordinary content retrieval than to a conversation, and the service that carries tasking may never see any suspicious write from the victim. The trade off is weaker operator feedback and slower tasking cycles, which attackers accept when staying quiet matters more. Correlation across two providers is the main defensive lever: a host that reads from one platform and shortly afterwards writes to an unrelated one, repeatedly and from the same process, is the strongest clue.

What you will practise

  • Explain how one way communication delivers instructions without returning output over the same service.
  • Describe why an adversary might accept slower tasking in exchange for lower traffic volume.
  • Identify the process and network evidence that shows an implant polling a public platform.
  • Explain how pairing records from two different services can reconstruct a split command channel.
  • Compare one way and bidirectional web service channels in terms of visibility and risk.
