Threat Hunting Hypothesis

SOC & Detection EngineeringExtremeKeyword

A threat hunting hypothesis is a falsifiable statement about adversary behaviour that is expected to be present in an environment if a specific technique is being used, written so that telemetry can confirm or refute it. It is the element that separates hunting from browsing dashboards. A usable hypothesis names the behaviour rather than a tool, states the observable artifacts that behaviour would create, identifies the data sources required to see those artifacts, and defines in advance what evidence would count as a hit and what would count as absence. A well-formed example is that an adversary using valid accounts for lateral movement will produce authentication events from atypical source hosts or at atypical times for that identity, which can be tested against identity logs joined to asset inventory. The discipline around the hypothesis is as important as its content. Before hunting, the analyst verifies the required log source is actually collected, parsed and retained for the period of interest, because a great many hunts end with the discovery that the question cannot be answered with current telemetry. During the hunt, findings are documented as they are made, including negative results, since knowing that a technique was sought and not found is itself informative for coverage decisions. After the hunt, hits are converted into durable value: a new or tuned detection rule, a logged coverage gap with an owner, or a data source onboarding request. Hunting without that loop produces interesting sessions and no lasting improvement, which is why mature programmes track hunts by hypothesis, data source, technique coverage and number of detections produced rather than by hours spent.

What you will practise

  • You will be able to write a falsifiable hunting hypothesis that names behaviour, observable artifacts, required data sources and refutation criteria.
  • You will be able to derive hypotheses from ATT&CK techniques, threat intelligence reporting and known gaps in existing detection coverage.
  • You will be able to verify before hunting that the required telemetry is collected, parsed and retained for the relevant time window.
  • You will be able to execute a structured hunt and document hits and negative results as you go.
  • You will be able to convert hunt findings into durable outputs such as new detection rules, tuning changes or data source requests.
  • You will be able to prioritise a hunt backlog by technique risk, current coverage and the cost of answering the question.
  • You will be able to report hunt outcomes in terms that inform coverage decisions rather than only describing the investigation.
