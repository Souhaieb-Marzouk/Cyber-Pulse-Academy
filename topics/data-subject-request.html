Data Subject Access Request

Data Protection & PrivacyHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A data subject access request is a request from a person. They ask to see the personal data a company holds about them. The company must usually reply within a set time and explain how the data is used. The same rules often let the person ask for corrections or deletion.

Why should you care?

People have a right to know what is stored about them. Ignoring a request can lead to complaints and fines. Answering well also builds trust, because customers see that the company takes their data seriously. The hard part is finding every copy, because data spreads across many systems. A late answer costs more than the request itself, so a clear process matters.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The request arrives, and the company records the date and the person's details.
  2. The team checks the person's identity without asking for more than it needs.
  3. They search every system that may hold the data, including email and backups.
  4. They gather the records together, then remove any information about other people.
  5. They send the answer in a clear format, with an explanation of how the data is used.
  6. They record what was found and what was removed, for future reference.

Real-world example

Imagine a clinic reception desk where a patient asks for a copy of everything the clinic holds about them. The receptionist cannot answer alone. The notes live in the appointment system, the paper file, the billing office and the doctor's inbox. One person is made responsible for collecting them all by a set date. Before sending anything, the team removes details about other patients that appear in the same notes. The patient then receives one clear pack. Handling a data request works the same way: one owner, every shelf checked, and a firm date.

Key takeaways

  • Give every request one owner and one deadline.
  • Search the awkward places too, such as email, logs and backups.
  • Remove other people's details before you send the answer.

In more detail

A data subject access request (DSAR) is a request from an individual asking an organisation to confirm whether it holds personal data about them and to provide a copy, together with related rights such as correction, deletion or objection. Handling one is mostly a discovery problem: the data typically sits across a customer database, a ticketing system, email, backups, analytics exports and third party processors, so the organisation needs a documented route to search each source and to log what it found. Identity verification is required so that one person cannot obtain another person's records, but it must not become an unreasonable barrier, and the response clock usually starts when the request arrives, whichever team receives it. The common mistake is searching only the obvious customer system and returning an incomplete answer, or missing the legal deadline because no owner was assigned.

What you will practise

  • Explain what a data subject access request is and which rights it relates to.
  • Describe the systems that must be searched to answer a request completely.
  • Explain how identity verification balances privacy against unreasonable barriers.
  • Identify the operational failures that make a request late or incomplete.
  • Describe how a redaction decision is made and recorded.
