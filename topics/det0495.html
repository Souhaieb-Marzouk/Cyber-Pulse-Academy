DET0495 Detection Strategy for Financial Theft

DET0495Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Financial theft means an attacker steals money, not just data. This detection watches payment systems and finance computers for the steps of that theft. It looks for new payees, changed bank details and unusual payments. The goal is to catch the fraud before the money leaves the company.

Why should you care?

Money lost to fraud is often gone for good, because bank transfers complete in minutes. One changed bank account number on a supplier invoice can cost more than a full data breach. An attacker needs only one mailbox rule and one approved payment to succeed. Logs alone cannot stop this, so the detection pairs records with a fast human check.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Payment and enterprise resource planning systems write an audit record for each important action.
  2. These records include the user, the payee, the amount and the time of the change.
  3. The rule builds a normal pattern for each finance user over several weeks.
  4. A new payee followed quickly by a large payment breaks that pattern and is scored.
  5. Mailbox rules that hide bank messages are added as supporting evidence.
  6. The alert goes to the fraud team with the account number and the approving user.

Real-world example

Picture a shop till that prints a receipt for every sale. A dishonest cashier cannot simply take notes from the drawer, because the till records each one. Instead he adds a fake supplier to the payment list and pays that supplier for goods that never arrive. The receipt looks completely normal, but the supplier list changed. This detection watches the supplier list, not the drawer.

Key takeaways

  • Watch the change to the payee record, because the payment itself often looks normal.
  • Correlate mailbox, endpoint and payment logs to see the whole story.
  • Set a fast human check for any new payee with a large first payment.

In more detail

This strategy detects financial theft, where an adversary uses a valid account or a compromised business process to move money or redirect a payment. Typical activity includes creating a new payee, editing bank account details on a supplier record, approving a large first payment and hiding the confirmation replies with a mailbox rule. Visibility comes from payment platform audit records, enterprise resource planning change logs, mailbox configuration audit entries and endpoint process activity on finance workstations. It fails in practice because finance staff create payees and release payments every day, so the detection lives or dies on peer baselines and on catching the rare combination of a brand new payee and an immediate large payment.

What you will practise

  • Describe the common steps in a business email compromise payment fraud.
  • Name the audit records a payment platform produces for each action.
  • Build a baseline of normal payee creation for finance staff.
  • Correlate mailbox rule changes with payment events on the same account.
  • Design an escalation path that reaches the fraud team within minutes.
