Simple definition

The ECIH is a certification from EC-Council about incident handling. Incident handling means the steps you follow when a company is attacked or a system breaks its security rules. The exam tests whether you know how to prepare for attacks and how to respond when they happen. It sits between entry level training and specialist forensics work.

Why should you care?

Every company needs people who stay calm when a security alarm fires. This certification shows an employer that you understand a structured response process and not just a list of tools. It helps you move from a support desk role into a security operations centre. It also gives you a shared language for working with managers and lawyers during a real breach. The exam is well known, so recruiters recognise it on a CV.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Start by learning the vocabulary. Learn what an event, an incident and a breach mean, because the exam uses these words precisely.
  2. Study the incident handling lifecycle as a loop. It runs from preparation through identification, containment, eradication, recovery and lessons learned.
  3. Work through the attack categories in the syllabus. They include malware, email attacks, web attacks and network attacks.
  4. Practise on a home lab. Follow a written runbook and write a short incident report for each exercise you finish.
  5. Review the official exam blueprint. Book the exam when your practice scores are steady rather than lucky.
  6. The exam itself is a proctored knowledge test. It uses multiple choice questions, including scenario items about a described incident.

Real-world example

Picture a finance team member who opens an email attachment at nine in the morning. By lunchtime, files on a shared drive are renamed. A pop up on the screen then demands payment. An incident handler does not simply delete the email. First they confirm what happened. Next they stop the spread by isolating the machine and remove the harmful software. Then they restore clean files from backup. Finally they write down what worked, so the next case is faster. That ordered thinking is what the ECIH exam measures.

Key takeaways

  • Learn the vocabulary first, because the exam grades precise wording.
  • Write a practice runbook and an incident report, not just study notes.
  • Check the current EC-Council blueprint before you book a date.

In more detail

The EC-Council Certified Incident Handler (ECIH, exam code ECIH) is an intermediate-level certification for security analysts who deal with live incidents. It suits SOC analysts, help desk engineers moving into security operations, and administrators who must contain a breach before it spreads. It proves to an employer that you can follow a recognised incident handling process, from preparation through containment, eradication and recovery to lessons learned. The exam is a knowledge test of multiple choice questions with scenario items, sat at a testing centre or online with a proctor. It leads to incident response analyst, SOC analyst and incident handler roles. Check EC-Council's current exam blueprint before booking, because the domain list below follows the published objectives and is revised from time to time. Exam domains and objectives

  • Explain the difference between a security event, an incident and a breach using the words correctly.
  • Describe the incident handling lifecycle and the activities performed at each stage.
  • Identify the common attack categories that cause incidents and the evidence each one leaves behind.
  • Outline the first response steps for a malware, email, web or network incident.
  • Describe how to preserve evidence so that later investigation stays trustworthy.
  • Produce a short incident report that states impact, actions taken and lessons learned.
