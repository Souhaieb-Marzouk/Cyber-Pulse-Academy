Operational Technology Security

OT & ICS SecurityHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Operational technology means the computers and devices that control real machines and physical processes. Examples include pumps in a water plant, robots in a factory and signals on a railway. OT security is the work of keeping those systems safe, running and free from tampering. It is different from normal computer security because stopping the process can be dangerous.

Why should you care?

If a control system is changed or stopped, water may not flow. Power may fail, or a machine may move in the wrong way. A delay is not the only risk, because people can be hurt. These systems often last for many years, so old and unsupported devices are normal. Attackers know that many of them were never built with security in mind. Protecting them keeps services running and keeps workers safe.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. First you list your devices and decide which ones directly touch the physical process.
  2. Next you separate the control network from the office network and from the internet.
  3. You control remote access, so a vendor can work only with approval and only for a limited time.
  4. You harden the engineering laptops, because they are the machines that can reprogram controllers.
  5. You keep offline copies of controller settings, so you can restore a device after tampering.
  6. You watch the control network for unexpected changes and practise what to do in an emergency.

Real-world example

Think of a hospital intensive care unit. You would not install a new software update on a ventilator just because a patch is available. You would ask the maker, plan the change, and keep a spare device ready. The room is also locked, and visitors are escorted. The aim is not to make the machine modern, it is to keep the patient alive while it runs.

Key takeaways

  • In OT, safety and continuous operation come before confidentiality and convenience.
  • Segment control networks from office networks, and control every path in from outside.
  • Never apply an information technology fix to industrial equipment without the vendor and engineers.

In more detail

Operational technology security covers the protection of systems that interact with the physical world, including industrial control systems, building management, medical devices and transport signalling, where the governing priority is safe and continuous operation rather than data confidentiality. The engineering reality that drives every control decision is that these environments were designed for availability and determinism, with long equipment lifetimes, vendor-specific protocols that often lack authentication, and change windows measured in months. Security work therefore proceeds by segmenting control networks from business networks and from the internet, enforcing strictly one-way or tightly brokered data flows where information must cross, controlling removable media and vendor remote access, hardening and monitoring the engineering workstations that can reprogram controllers, and keeping tested offline backups of controller logic. Governance bodies such as NIST, the International Society of Automation and the International Electrotechnical Commission publish guidance in this area, and the widely referenced Purdue model provides a layered reference for network zones. The mistake beginners make is to copy information technology controls directly: aggressive patching without vendor approval, scanning tools that crash fragile devices, or antivirus agents that break real-time performance, all of which can cause the outage the security programme was meant to prevent.

What you will practise

  • Define operational technology and give examples of systems it includes.
  • Explain why availability and safety take priority over confidentiality in control environments.
  • Describe network segmentation between control zones, business networks and external access.
  • Explain why engineering workstations and removable media need special protection.
  • State why standard information technology tools and patching practices can endanger industrial devices.
