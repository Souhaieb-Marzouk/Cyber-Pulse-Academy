G1009 Moses Staff

G1009Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Moses Staff is the name for a group of attackers. Public reports say it has targeted organisations in Israel since around 2021. Its attacks are destructive. It locks up files so nobody can use them, and it also publishes stolen information in public. It behaves less like a thief selling a key. It acts more like someone smashing a shop window for everyone to see.

Why should you care?

Most attackers want to be paid, so they keep a way to unlock your files. This group does not offer that. The damage is the point, which means backups are your only real recovery. If your backups sit on the same network and use the same administrator accounts, they can be destroyed too. A destructive attack can also leak customer and staff data at the same time. You then face privacy harm and disruption together.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers first find a way in, often through an unpatched public facing service or a stolen credential.
  2. They run a small remote access tool so they can return later without alerting anyone.
  3. They spend time inside the network, checking which systems hold the most important information.
  4. They copy files out to storage they control, so the data can be published later.
  5. They run an encryption tool that locks files and leaves no usable recovery key.
  6. They publish samples of the stolen data through public channels to press their message.

Real-world example

Think of a warehouse where the alarm rings only when someone carries goods out of the front door. One night the intruders photograph every shelf. They weld the shutters closed from the inside and post the photos on the notice board outside. Nothing was sold and no ransom note explains how to reopen the shutters. That is the difference between ordinary ransomware and a purely destructive operation.

Key takeaways

  • Keep at least one recent backup that is offline or in a separate account with different credentials.
  • Test restoring from backup regularly, because an untested backup is only a hope.
  • If a service faces the internet, patch it quickly and watch its logs for unusual access.

In more detail

Moses Staff is a destructive intrusion set tracked under names including DEV-0500 and Marigold Sandstorm in public reporting. It has been observed targeting organisations in Israel since around 2021. Its distinguishing feature is what it does after access: instead of stealing data quietly, it encrypts files without leaving any working recovery path, and it publishes stolen material through its own public channels. Analysts have also seen it deface websites, which points to disruption and messaging rather than a normal extortion business model, because no real decryption service is offered. Public reporting has discussed possible Iranian state links, but that attribution is made with limited confidence and is not something to state as fact. For defenders the practical signals are custom tooling such as the StrifeWater remote access trojan and the PyDCrypt disk encryptor, plus heavy use of legitimate remote access and file transfer utilities that blend into ordinary administrative activity.

What you will practise

  • Explain how a purely destructive operation differs from a normal ransomware extortion attempt.
  • Describe why an offline or isolated backup changes the outcome of a destructive attack.
  • Recognise the role of legitimate remote access and transfer tools in an intrusion chain.
  • Summarise how public attribution of this group is reported, including its stated confidence level.
  • Explain what data theft alongside destruction means for privacy and disclosure duties.
  • Outline the first four hours of a response when destructive encryption is suspected.
