T1496.004 Resource Hijacking: Cloud Service Hijacking

T1496.004ImpactExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Cloud service hijacking means an attacker takes over a company's cloud service and uses it for their own work. They may send spam through your email service or use your paid model service. You receive the bill while they keep the benefit.

Why should you care?

Cloud services often bill for what they use, so misuse creates very large unexpected charges. Service quotas can be consumed, which stops your own applications from working. Your company name may be blamed for spam sent to customers and partners from your trusted service. Because the attacker works through your own accounts, many security tools see the activity as normal use.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker gains access through stolen keys, weak settings, or a compromised application.
  2. They use your messaging service to send spam or phishing mail and texts at scale.
  3. Sometimes they first switch on a service inside your cloud account, then use it.
  4. For artificial intelligence services, they set up a reverse proxy that hides real users.
  5. Your account pays for every message and for every model request they make.
  6. Usage rises sharply, quotas fill, bills grow, and your own workloads slow down.

Real-world example

Think of a shared flat with a single electricity account. Someone copies the meter key and runs a workshop from the flat all night. The bill stays in your name. Nothing is broken and no door is forced. Every charge looks legitimate on the statement. Cloud service hijacking is the same trick applied to a cloud account, where the attacker simply spends your allowance.

Key takeaways

  • Attackers abuse services you already pay for, so no malware is needed.
  • Cost spikes, quota use, and new credentials are the first visible signs.
  • Lock down keys, review enabled services, and set budget alerts.

In more detail

Cloud service hijacking is resource theft performed through software as a service rather than through malware on a host. After gaining access to a company's cloud tenant, an adversary reuses the platform's own capabilities: messaging and email APIs such as AWS SES, SNS, SendGrid, or Twilio become a bulk spam and phishing delivery engine, and hosted artificial intelligence services are reached through reverse proxies so the victim account pays for model inference. Where the abused service is part of a larger cloud platform, the adversary may first switch the service on for the tenant, which makes the enablement call itself an early indicator. Detection lives almost entirely in provider and identity telemetry: audit logs showing new access keys or roles created from unfamiliar addresses, sudden increases in outbound message volume, sending quotas consumed within minutes, unexpected region usage, and cost anomalies that break the normal relationship between spend and customer activity. Legitimate marketing has a known owner and pattern; hijacked sending is abrupt, comes from fresh credentials, and does not map to any business campaign.

What you will practise

  • Explain what cloud service hijacking is and why it targets existing accounts.
  • Describe how messaging services are reused to distribute spam and phishing.
  • Outline how hijacked artificial intelligence services are reached through proxies.
  • Identify identity and audit log signals that indicate unauthorised service use.
  • Explain the financial and availability impacts of consumed service quotas.
  • Describe controls such as key rotation and budget alerts that limit exposure.
