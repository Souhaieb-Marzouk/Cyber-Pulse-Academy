T1110.001 Brute Force: Password Guessing

T1110.001Credential AccessExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Password guessing means an attacker tries many possible passwords against a login until one works. They do not know the real password in advance. They can guess by hand, with a script, or with a list of passwords that leaked from other sites.

Why should you care?

Anyone who can reach a login page can try this, so exposed services attract constant attempts. A weak or reused password can turn a failed attack into a full break in. The attempts also fill your logs, and repeated failures can lock out real users. The difficult part is telling an attack apart from one tired person typing the wrong password.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker picks a service they can reach, such as remote desktop or a webmail page.
  2. They send a password attempt and read whether the login was accepted or refused.
  3. Broken authentication tries one common password across many accounts in turn.
  4. Password spraying tries a few likely passwords, such as Season plus a year, spread over time.
  5. The attempts are slowed down to stay under lockout limits and avoid clear spikes.
  6. When one attempt succeeds, the attacker logs in and usually stops guessing on that account.

Real-world example

Someone stands at the door of a block of flats and tries the same few keypad codes on every buzzer, one flat at a time. Most buzzers refuse, and the person quietly moves on. Eventually one door clicks open. The neighbours never notice, because each buzzer only failed a few times.

Key takeaways

  • Watch for many failed logins from one source, or a few failures spread across many accounts.
  • Account lockout and login throttling make guessing slow, so keep both switched on.
  • Guessing attacks a live service; cracking happens offline on stolen hashes and is a different problem.

In more detail

Password guessing is the online half of brute force: the adversary has no valid credentials and repeatedly submits candidate passwords to a live authentication service until one is accepted. Because each attempt has to reach a real service, the method is slow and noisy compared with cracking stolen hashes, and the failure records it creates are the primary detection surface. Targets are usually remote management and mail interfaces, including SSH, Telnet, FTP, SMB, LDAP, Kerberos, RDP, database listeners, VNC, SNMP and web based administration pages, as well as cloud and single sign on portals. Attackers rarely try every account at once. They rotate passwords against a single account, rotate accounts on a single password, or pace attempts to stay under lockout thresholds, sometimes drawing candidates from breach lists or organisation specific words. Defenders should expect many failed authentications across several log sources, often mixed with non standard client behaviour, and should treat any following success as the moment that matters.

What you will practise

  • Define password guessing and contrast it with offline password cracking.
  • List the network services and cloud portals that attackers commonly target with guesses.
  • Recognise broken authentication and password spraying patterns in authentication logs.
  • Explain how lockout thresholds and attempt pacing change the evidence an attack leaves.
  • Correlate failed authentication events with a later successful logon from the same source.
