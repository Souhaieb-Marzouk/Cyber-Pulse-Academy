G0130 Ajax Security Team

G0130Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Ajax Security Team is a name researchers give to a group that spies on organisations in the Middle East and elsewhere. Public reports link it to Iranian interests. The group has been active for many years under several campaign names.

Why should you care?

This group has been tracked for over ten years, which shows that some attackers are patient and long lived. It also approaches people directly on social media, so technical controls alone will not stop it. Older descriptions of its tools may not match what it uses today. That is why you should always confirm current behaviour from recent reports rather than trusting one old summary.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The group studies the target's website, staff and public activity to find a weak point.
  2. It attacks an internet-facing service or sends a convincing login request.
  3. After gaining access, it installs a backdoor that starts automatically on the machine.
  4. It collects documents and login details from the systems it can reach.
  5. It also contacts people directly on social networks to gain trust.
  6. It replaces its infrastructure often, so blocking known addresses has a short life.

Real-world example

Imagine a shop that has been burgled several times by the same crew over ten years. Each time, the crew changes its van and its tools, but the way it studies the shop does not change. If you only memorise the old van, you will miss the next visit. That is why defenders describe behaviour and patterns, not just the last set of tools an attacker used.

Key takeaways

  • Long-running groups change tools, so track behaviour and update your knowledge.
  • Keep internet-facing services patched and monitored, since that is a favoured entry point.
  • Staff who are publicly visible may be approached directly, so teach them to verify strangers.

In more detail

Ajax Security Team is a long-running espionage cluster that public reporting associates with Iranian interests and that researchers have tracked through several campaign names against Israeli, United States, Saudi and other regional targets. The group's reported activity spans more than a decade, and the tradecraft follows the wider pattern of Iranian-nexus operations: reconnaissance of the target's public presence, credential phishing or exploitation of internet-facing services, and the use of custom backdoors and information stealers for collection, with earlier reporting also describing destructive capability. A notable reported characteristic is the use of social media and messaging to identify and approach individuals, including researchers and journalists who might not be reachable through corporate email. Attribution to a specific national sponsor is stated with moderate confidence in public reporting and the group name covers activity that other vendors describe under different labels, so incidents should be assessed on behaviour. Evidence typically includes web server exploitation attempts, newly created accounts, persistence through scheduled tasks or services, and outbound traffic to infrastructure that changes frequently. A common misunderstanding is that a decade-old group must be inactive or that its older tools still describe its current capability.
