T1114.002 Email Collection: Remote Email Collection

T1114.002CollectionExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is email theft directly from the mail service rather than from one computer. The attacker signs in to the mailbox with stolen access and copies messages from there. Sub-technique version of email collection, focused on servers and cloud mail platforms instead of the device.

Why should you care?

It differs from the parent's local form because the attacker never needs to touch your device or copy any file. Your endpoints look clean, so nothing on them explains the loss. A token or forwarding rule can keep access alive after a password change. Mail platforms carry so much routine traffic that quiet, targeted searching is easy to miss.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker obtains working credentials or a token for a mailbox or mail platform.
  2. They authenticate directly, sometimes through a mail protocol rather than a browser.
  3. Search queries pick out messages by keyword, sender, date or attachment type.
  4. Results are exported, downloaded or copied into an attacker controlled mailbox.
  5. A rule or delegate permission then keeps copies flowing automatically.
  6. Only service side records show the access, so endpoint monitoring sees nothing.

Real-world example

Instead of reading letters left in the hallway, someone becomes a temporary mail clerk at the depot. Every envelope passes through the clerk's hands, and copies are made quietly in the back office. The residents' own letterboxes remain untouched, so no one at home suspects anything at all.

Key takeaways

  • Remote collection leaves evidence in service audit logs rather than on the endpoint.
  • Review sign in records, mail search and export audit entries, and forwarding rule changes together.
  • Sub-technique note: the parent covers device level theft as well as this server side form.

In more detail

Remote email collection is the server side half of email theft. Instead of reading files on a workstation, the adversary authenticates to a mail platform, whether an on premises Exchange deployment, a hosted mail service or a cloud productivity suite, and retrieves messages from there. Access may use stolen user credentials, an application or OAuth style token, a service account, or an existing mail protocol session such as a direct client connection that avoids the web interface entirely. Because the traffic is ordinary mail protocol or web mail traffic from a legitimate account, the mailbox is the collection point and nothing needs to be written to the victim's device. Detection lives on the platform: sign in records showing unfamiliar clients, new source addresses or impossible travel, mailbox auditing entries for search and export operations, and configuration changes such as forwarding rules, delegate permissions or new application registrations. Automation makes broad keyword searching against many mailboxes cheap, so both the rate and the breadth of queries matter.

What you will practise

  • Explain how adversaries collect email directly from mail servers and cloud mail platforms.
  • Describe the credential and token types that grant remote mailbox access.
  • Identify platform audit records for sign ins, mail searches, exports and rule creation.
  • Explain why endpoint monitoring alone cannot reveal this form of collection.
  • Distinguish legitimate mail client behaviour from automated or unexpected mass searching.
