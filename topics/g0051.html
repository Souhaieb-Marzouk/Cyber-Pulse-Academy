G0051 FIN10

G0051Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

FIN10 is a name for a criminal group that attacks companies for money. Public reports describe it targeting gaming and casino businesses in North America. It often gains access through remote desktop access that is open to the internet. Once inside, it steals data and then demands payment to stay quiet.

Why should you care?

Extortion does not always need to lock your files. Simply copying business or customer data and threatening to publish it can be enough to force a payment. Exposed remote desktop services are one of the most common ways in, and they are easy to forget about. If nobody reviews remote login records, an attacker can work for days without being noticed. Strong access control and monitoring remove most of the opportunity.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers scan the internet for remote desktop services that are open to everyone.
  2. They try stolen or weak passwords until one account lets them into the network.
  3. Once inside, they create services or scheduled tasks so their access survives a restart.
  4. They use common administration tools to move between computers and find valuable data.
  5. Files are collected in one place and then copied out of the network in large amounts.
  6. The group sends a payment demand and threatens to publish the stolen information.

Real-world example

Imagine a warehouse with a side door that staff keep unlocked because it is convenient. Nobody robs the place by force. Someone simply walks in during the day, photographs the stock list, and later offers to sell it back to the owner. FIN10 works like that. The open side door is an exposed remote desktop service, and the photographs are the stolen files.

Key takeaways

  • Close remote access services that do not need to face the internet.
  • Use strong authentication and review remote login records regularly.
  • Data theft alone can support extortion, even without ransomware.

In more detail

FIN10 is a financially motivated group described in public reporting as targeting gaming, casino and hospitality organisations, mainly in Canada and the United States. Reporting describes a comparatively simple playbook: obtain access, often by abusing internet-facing remote desktop services or stolen credentials, establish persistence with publicly available tools, steal data, then demand payment under threat of publishing it or disrupting operations. Because the tooling is widely available, signature detection adds little and behavioural analysis carries most of the weight. Evidence appears in remote desktop session logs, the creation of new services or scheduled tasks, and bulk data staging shortly before exfiltration. A common misunderstanding is that extortion requires ransomware; reporting on this group shows that data theft plus a credible threat can be enough. Names of individual operators are generally not published, so the label describes a cluster of activity rather than a known team.

What you will practise

  • Describe FIN10 as a financially motivated group and its target sectors.
  • Explain how exposed remote desktop services lead to full compromise.
  • Identify persistence and data staging evidence in host logs.
  • Explain why extortion can succeed without any ransomware.
  • Outline access controls that reduce internet-facing remote login risk.
