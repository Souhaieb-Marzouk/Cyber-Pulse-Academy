T1606 Forge Web Credentials

T1606Credential AccessExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Websites often keep you signed in using a small piece of data, such as a cookie or a token. This technique describes an attacker creating those pieces of data from scratch. The site believes they are real, so the attacker gets in without a password.

Why should you care?

Multi factor authentication checks you only when you sign in. A forged cookie or token skips that check completely, because the site trusts the item itself. One stolen signing key can therefore let an attacker act as anyone. The access can look completely normal in the application log, which makes it very hard to spot.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker obtains a secret that the service uses to validate credentials.
  2. That secret may be a signing key, a private key or a shared password.
  3. They build a brand new cookie or token and sign it with that secret.
  4. They choose the identity, the permissions and the lifetime that they want.
  5. The service checks the signature, accepts the item and then grants access.
  6. The session then appears with no real sign in or second factor behind it.

Real-world example

Think of a members only club where the doorman checks the wax seal on your card. A real member signs in at the desk, then receives a card with the club seal. A forger cannot copy the card of a member, but he can steal the seal stamp from the back office. Now he can press that seal onto any card he likes, writing whatever name and access level he chooses. The doorman looks at the seal, sees it is correct and waves him through. Forging web credentials is that seal. The club issued nothing, yet every card the forger makes passes inspection.

Key takeaways

  • Protect token signing keys and certificates like master passwords.
  • Alert on sessions that begin with no matching sign in event.
  • Rotate signing keys when you suspect they were exposed.

In more detail

Forge Web Credentials covers the creation of new authentication material for web applications and internet services, rather than the theft or interception of material that already belongs to a user. Web services commonly authenticate through session cookies, signed tokens or federation assertions, all of which are only trustworthy because they carry a value that the service can verify with a secret. If an adversary obtains the signing key, private key, password or seed behind that verification, they can mint credentials that the service accepts as genuine, for any identity and often with any set of permissions or lifetime. Because the forged material is validated by the same code path as real material, it can pass multi factor authentication entirely: the second factor was completed by the legitimate user at some earlier point, and the adversary simply manufactures a credential that sits behind it. Artefacts depend on the token type but the shared theme is a session with no corresponding authentication event. Useful signals include sessions appearing without a preceding sign in, token issuer or signature inconsistencies, cookie values that fail strict validation, permissions or roles in the token that the account never held, impossible travel between the token creation and its use, and configuration changes to signing certificates or federation trusts. Legitimate credentials are always issued by the identity provider after a recorded authentication; forged ones are not.

What you will practise

  • Explain how web services trust cookies and tokens to identify a user.
  • Describe why forging credentials differs from stealing them.
  • Identify the secret material an adversary needs in order to forge credentials.
  • Explain how forged credentials bypass multi factor authentication.
  • Recognise telemetry that shows a session with no authentication event.
  • List the artefacts that reveal tampering with signing keys or federation settings.
