Rogue Access Point

Network SecurityHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

A rogue access point is a wireless device added to a network without permission. It may be a cheap router that someone plugs in for better signal. It may also be a device an attacker hides in order to reach the network. Either way it creates a door that your team did not approve.

Why should you care?

A rogue access point can connect strangers to your internal network. It can also trick your staff into joining a fake network with a familiar name. Rules and firewalls at the network edge may not see traffic that enters this way. Finding one is hard once it is hidden among many devices. Catching it early keeps your network boundary honest.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Someone connects an unauthorised wireless device to a network port or shares a laptop connection.
  2. The device broadcasts a network name, or clones a real one, and offers addresses to clients.
  3. Staff devices may join it automatically, and their traffic passes through the unknown device.
  4. Sensors compare the wireless signals they can see with the list of approved access points.
  5. Switches report unknown hardware addresses, which reveals a device that should not be there.
  6. The team locates the device, disconnects it, and checks what it may have reached.

Real-world example

Picture a shared office building where each company has its own floor. A stranger sets up a small router in the corridor and names it after the coffee shop downstairs. Your phone remembers that name and connects by itself. From that moment your messages travel through a device you do not own. The fix is the same as a reception desk that checks every visitor badge.

Key takeaways

  • A rogue access point matters because it bridges wireless clients into the wired network.
  • Compare what is on the air and on the wire against an approved list.
  • A cloned network name defeats detection that only looks for strange names.

In more detail

A rogue access point is any wireless access point connected to an organisation's network without authorisation, whether it is a cheap router that a staff member plugged in for better coverage, a laptop sharing its connection, or a device an attacker planted to bridge wireless clients into the wired local area network. The security problem is not the radio itself but the bridge, because once the rogue device is on the internal network it bypasses perimeter controls, and clients that associate with it may be served false addresses and have their traffic inspected. Detection depends on comparing what is on the air and on the wire against an approved inventory, using wireless intrusion detection, switch port monitoring and network access control. The classic mistake is to look only for a foreign network name, since an attacker can clone the legitimate name and hide in plain sight.

What you will practise

  • Define what makes an access point rogue and give common examples.
  • Explain how a rogue device can bypass controls at the network edge.
  • Describe how wireless monitoring and switch data help detect unauthorised devices.
  • Explain why checking only for unfamiliar network names is not enough.
  • Outline the steps to locate and remove a rogue access point safely.
