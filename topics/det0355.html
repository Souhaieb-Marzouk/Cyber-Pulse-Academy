DET0355 Detection Strategy for Email Bombing

DET0355Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This detection finds a mailbox that suddenly receives a very large number of messages. The sender is often a normal mailing list or newsletter signup page. The goal of the attacker is to bury one important message under hundreds of useless ones.

Why should you care?

People notice a password reset message. They do not notice it when three hundred newsletters arrive in the same hour. While the mailbox fills up, the intruder can approve a payment change or reset an account. The victim may also lose real mail that is pushed out of the inbox.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The mail gateway records every accepted message, including the recipient address and the sender domain.
  2. A rule counts messages per recipient over a short window, such as ten minutes.
  3. It also counts how many different sender domains wrote to that recipient in the same window.
  4. If both counts cross a threshold, the mailbox is flagged as flooded.
  5. The rule then looks for account or payment changes for the same user and raises the alert with both facts joined.

Real-world example

Imagine a shop till receipt that is stamped three hundred times in one hour for tiny amounts. The one real purchase is hidden in the middle of the roll. The cashier has to search through the whole roll to find it.

Key takeaways

  • Email bombing is usually cover for another action, not the action itself.
  • Volume alone is normal; volume plus a change to the account or a payment is the real signal.
  • Ask the user what they were doing when the flood started, because the useful detail is often only in their memory.

In more detail

This strategy detects email bombing, the delivery of a large volume of messages to one mailbox in a short time so that the real message is buried. It maps to T1667 Email Bombing. The technique is interesting because the flood is rarely the goal: it hides a password reset, a new payee confirmation or a purchase receipt from the person reading the inbox. Detection relies on mail gateway and mailbox telemetry that reports per-recipient message rates, sender diversity and subscription confirmations, joined to identity and finance audit events in the same window. The main failure mode is that the flood is delivered by legitimate bulk senders, so a volume rule alone pages the service desk about marketing mail while the actual fraudulent account change passes unnoticed.

What you will practise

  • Define email bombing and explain why the message flood is a distraction technique
  • Identify per-recipient volume and sender-diversity metrics available from a mail gateway
  • Correlate a mailbox flood with password reset, mailbox rule and payment change events for the same user
  • Distinguish bulk marketing mail from an abusive signup flood using sender reputation and domain age
  • Choose thresholds that fit the size and normal volume of each mailbox group
