CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities

Vulnerability ManagementHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

The CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is a public list of security weaknesses that attackers are already using in real attacks. It is kept by a United States government cyber agency. Each entry names the affected product and a date by which it should be fixed. Anyone can read the list for free.

Why should you care?

Companies often have thousands of weaknesses to fix and not enough time. A plain severity score does not say whether anyone is actually attacking that weakness. This list gives proof of real attacks, so you can fix the truly urgent ones first. It also gives you a date to aim for and a way to explain the priority to managers. Missing from the list does not mean a weakness is safe.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. Security teams and researchers report weaknesses that are being used in real attacks.
  2. The agency checks the evidence and adds the weakness to the public catalog.
  3. Each entry lists the product affected and a deadline for fixing it.
  4. You compare the catalog with your own list of software and versions.
  5. Anything that matches is treated as urgent and scheduled for patching first.
  6. If a fix is impossible, you apply other controls and record the decision.

Real-world example

Think of a neighbourhood watch board at the local shop. When a house is broken into, the details go on the board. Neighbours then check that same weak window in their own homes first. You would not ignore the board and instead paint your fence. The board shows where real break-ins happen, so it guides where you spend your time.

Key takeaways

  • This catalog lists weaknesses that attackers already use, not just severe ones.
  • Use it with your own asset list to decide what to patch first.
  • A weakness missing from the list can still be dangerous, so keep other sources.

In more detail

The CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is a public list, maintained by the United States Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency, of vulnerabilities that have been observed being exploited in real incidents rather than merely being theoretically severe. Each entry records an identifier, the affected product, a short description, and required remediation dates that bind United States federal agencies under a binding operational directive, which makes the catalog both a threat intelligence source and a compliance driver that many other organisations adopt voluntarily. Its value in prioritisation is the evidence of exploitation: a vulnerability with a moderate severity score can sit above a higher-scoring one that no one is attacking, because the catalog answers a different question from a scoring system. It is not complete, since it only contains what has been publicly reported or observed, so an absence from the list is not evidence that a flaw is safe, and very recent exploitation may take time to appear. The common mistake is to use the catalog as the only patching input, which ignores gaps in visibility and leaves internally significant or unpatchable issues unmanaged, or to treat the federal deadlines as optional for systems that carry the same exposure.

What you will practise

  • Explain what the CISA Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog is and who maintains it.
  • Describe how evidence of exploitation differs from a severity score when prioritising.
  • Explain how to compare the catalog against an asset and software inventory.
  • Describe the role of remediation deadlines and why organisations adopt them.
  • State the limitations of the catalog, including reporting gaps and delay.
  • Outline what to do when a listed vulnerability cannot be patched immediately.
