DET0335 Detect Abuse of XPC Services (T1559.003)

DET0335Detection StrategyVery hardDetection Strategy

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This detection watches helper programs on Apple computers that work for other programs. A helper can do things a normal program cannot, such as changing system settings. Attackers talk to a helper and ask it to do the work for them.

Why should you care?

Helpers often run with high permissions, so they can act on behalf of anyone who asks correctly. That makes them a shortcut to more control over the machine. Local traffic like this is easy to overlook, because it never touches the network. Watching who calls which helper closes an important privilege gap.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The system records which programs start, who started them and how they are signed.
  2. The detection builds a list of helper services and the programs that normally connect to them.
  3. It flags helper connections from programs that are unsigned or that live in unusual folders.
  4. It watches the files that register a helper, because adding a new one is a persistence step.
  5. It checks whether the helper performs a sensitive action, such as writing a system setting.
  6. Related events from the same session are grouped, so the analyst sees the full chain.

Real-world example

Think of a hotel with a concierge who can open any door for guests. Guests asking for help is normal, and the concierge never questions a request. Now imagine a person with no room key and no uniform badge. This person keeps asking the concierge to open the manager's office. The front desk will want to know why.

Key takeaways

  • Local helper services are powerful and easy to overlook, so baseline them.
  • Signing status and file location of the calling program are your best signals.
  • A new or modified helper definition is a persistence clue worth alerting on.

In more detail

This strategy detects abuse of interprocess communication services (T1559.003) on macOS, where a local process talks to a privileged helper through a service interface and convinces it to perform actions on the caller's behalf. Attackers use this to escalate privileges, bypass user prompts and reach capabilities they are not entitled to, all from what appears to be ordinary local traffic. The detection depends on process creation with parent-child relationships, service registration and launch records, file writes of service definition files outside approved locations, and signed process identity on both ends of the connection. It fails when service definitions and their clients are not baselined, because hundreds of legitimate helpers exist on a normal system and only the pairing and signing status separate abuse from routine behaviour.

What you will practise

  • Explain how a privileged helper service can be abused by a local process
  • Identify the process, signing and service definition telemetry available on macOS
  • Baseline approved helper clients and their installed locations
  • Detect new or modified service definitions as a persistence indicator
  • Describe why network-focused rules cannot see this behaviour
