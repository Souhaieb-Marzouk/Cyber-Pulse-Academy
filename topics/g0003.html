G0003 Cleaver

G0003Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Cleaver is the name for a group of attackers who broke into companies in the Middle East. Researchers also call it Threat Group 2889. The group spied on governments, energy firms and defence companies. Experts think it may be linked to a national government, but they are not certain.

Why should you care?

This group entered through public websites, which most companies must keep open to the internet. Once inside, they stole passwords and reused them to reach deeper systems. If you ignore web application security, one small flaw can open the whole network.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attackers look for a public website with a flaw, such as a login form that accepts unexpected input.
  2. They use that flaw to run commands or to upload a small file to the web server.
  3. From the server they collect saved passwords, browser data and email login details.
  4. They sign in to email and remote access services with the stolen details, often from unusual places.
  5. Inside the network they install back doors and add their own accounts so they can return later.
  6. They copy documents quietly over months, using normal business traffic to hide their actions.

Real-world example

Picture a hotel where the side door for the kitchen is always unlocked. A stranger walks in, copies the master key from the hook, and then uses it whenever they like. The front desk sees nothing unusual, because the stranger always enters like a member of staff. Nobody changes the lock either, because the key was never reported as missing. The hotel keeps running normally while every room becomes available to the visitor.

Key takeaways

  • Public websites are doors into your network, so patch and review them often.
  • Stolen passwords let attackers look like normal employees.
  • Watch for logins from unusual places or at unusual times.

In more detail

Cleaver, also tracked as Threat Group 2889, is an espionage cluster that public reporting has linked to targets in the Persian Gulf, the wider Middle East and, to a lesser degree, North America. Reporting from 2014 described two overlapping methods: direct intrusion into public-facing web servers through flaws in web applications, and compromise of sites frequented by the target population. The operators harvested webmail and virtual private network credentials, then reused those credentials to reach internal systems, which is why one exposed service could become an entry point for an entire organisation. Persistence came from custom back doors and, in some cases, from abuse of legitimate administrative tools. Detection evidence tends to appear in web server logs as unusual parameters or uploads, in authentication logs as logins from unexpected regions, and on hosts as new services and scheduled tasks. Attribution to a specific state is reported with moderate confidence and has been contested.
