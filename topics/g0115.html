G0115 GOLD SOUTHFIELD

G0115Enterprise GroupExtremeThreat Group

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

GOLD SOUTHFIELD is the name for a criminal group that runs a ransomware service. Public reports also call it Pinchy Spider. It has been active since about 2018. The group builds the tools and recruits other criminals to break into companies and use them.

Why should you care?

The group does not only lock your files. Since about 2020 it copies data first, then threatens to publish it. So paying to unlock files does not end the problem. Its partners get in through ordinary doors, such as remote access left open to the internet. Those doors are often tools your own team installed and trusts. If you do not know which of those tools are reachable from outside, you cannot defend them. Good backups and watching what leaves your network are what limit the damage.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The group builds the harmful software and the payment systems, then recruits partners online.
  2. A partner breaks in using an open remote access service or a harmful email attachment.
  3. The partner explores the network, finds valuable files, and gathers working logins.
  4. The stolen files are copied out to storage space controlled by the criminals.
  5. They delete the recovery copies that a business would normally use to restore data.
  6. Finally they lock the files with harmful software and demand payment for both files and silence.

Real-world example

Imagine a moving company that arrives days before you plan to leave. Workers photograph every room, catalogue your jewellery, and take a copy of the house keys. They do nothing yet. On moving day they take the furniture and also post your photographs online for anyone to see. You can replace furniture, but you cannot unshare the pictures. That is how copying data before locking files changes the game. The theft is finished before you notice the crime, so the only real defences are keeping the copy from leaving and keeping your own spare set safe.

Key takeaways

  • Stealing data before locking files means backups alone do not solve this problem.
  • Remote access services that anyone on the internet can reach are a favourite way in.
  • Watch what data leaves your network, not just what gets locked inside it.

In more detail

GOLD SOUTHFIELD, tracked elsewhere as Pinchy Spider, is a financially motivated group that MITRE ATT&CK describes as active since at least 2018 and as operating a ransomware as a service programme. The distinction matters: rather than a single closed team, the group is reported to supply the back end, payment handling and negotiation infrastructure while recruiting affiliates on criminal forums to carry out the actual intrusions, including high value deployments. That structure explains the variability analysts see between incidents: the access method, the dwell time and the level of operator skill differ because different affiliates are doing the work, while the encryptor and the extortion process stay recognisable. The entry also records a shift in business model: from early 2020 the group began stealing data before encryption and using the threat of publication as a second source of pressure, which is why data theft evidence and backup destruction belong in the same investigation as the encryption event. Reported access routes are mostly unglamorous and concern services rather than exploits: remote desktop exposed to the internet, remote monitoring and management tools that were reachable, and commodity malicious email, with exploitation of an application server weakness and a compromised software distribution route also described. Because remote management software is legitimate and often already approved, its abuse by an affiliate is a strong argument for inventorying which such tools exist in your estate and which accounts can reach them. The commonly repeated misunderstanding is that data exfiltration happens only after encryption is impossible; in this pattern the copy is taken first, so backup integrity and outbound transfer visibility are the controls that determine whether extortion works.

What you will practise

  • Explain what a ransomware as a service model is and why incidents vary between victims.
  • Describe how extortion changes when data is stolen before files are encrypted.
  • Identify the internet facing services that public reporting associates with initial access.
  • Explain why legitimate remote management software is difficult to ban and must instead be governed.
  • Prioritise controls that limit both data theft and recovery destruction by an affiliate.
