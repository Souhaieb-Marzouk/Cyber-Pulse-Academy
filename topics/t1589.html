T1589 Gather Victim Identity Information

T1589ReconnaissanceExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is how attackers learn about the people who work at a target company. They collect names, job titles, email addresses, and sometimes passwords. It happens before any break in, so you usually see nothing on your own network.

Why should you care?

A message that uses your real name and your manager's name is far more convincing. Attackers who know your email format can guess hundreds of addresses quickly. When passwords or one time codes leak, an attacker can sign in as a real person instead of breaking in.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker picks a target organisation and decides which departments are worth attacking.
  2. They read the public sources, such as company pages, social media, and news articles.
  3. They may probe sign in services to learn which usernames really exist.
  4. They piece the details together into a list of people and contact points.
  5. They may also obtain stolen passwords and session records from criminal markets.
  6. They use the list to write believable messages or attempt account sign ins.

Real-world example

Think of a receptionist at a large office. Anyone can walk past and read the staff board in the lobby, noting names and job titles. That is not a break in. But those names make a later phone call sound completely normal, because the caller already knows who to ask for.

Key takeaways

  • Public staff details are raw material for convincing attacks.
  • Watch sign in logs for many failed usernames, which signals enumeration.
  • Reduce what your organisation publishes about its people and systems.

In more detail

Gather Victim Identity Information is the reconnaissance behaviour of collecting facts about the people inside a target organisation before contact. The material includes ordinary personal detail such as names, job titles, work email addresses, and answers to security questions, and it extends to sensitive authentication material such as passwords, session tokens, and the multi-factor authentication methods registered to an account. Collection happens in three broad modes. Passive collection reads what is already published, including social media profiles, corporate websites, press coverage, and breach datasets. Active collection sends probes and reads the responses, for example testing whether a username exists or which authentication methods an account offers. Elicitation persuades a person to hand information over directly, often through a conversation that appears routine. The resulting picture is used to build target lists, choose credible lures, guess email formats, and prepare account compromise. For defenders there is little to see on the collection side, because much of it never touches your systems. What you can see is the consequence and the probing: authentication failures spread across many accounts, enumeration patterns against sign-in endpoints, help desk social engineering attempts, and new multi-factor authentication registrations. The durable controls are reducing the public identity footprint, hardening the authentication edge, and monitoring for enumeration rather than waiting for a successful login.

What you will practise

  • Define victim identity information and list the kinds of detail it includes.
  • Describe the difference between passive collection, active probing, and elicitation.
  • Explain how stolen credentials and session tokens extend this reconnaissance into access.
  • Identify the authentication log patterns that suggest identity enumeration.
  • Describe how reducing public staff information lowers phishing risk.
