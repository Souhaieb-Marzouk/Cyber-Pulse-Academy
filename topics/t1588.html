T1588 Obtain Capabilities

T1588Resource DevelopmentExtremeTechnique

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

This is how attackers get the tools they need for an intrusion. They buy them, download them for free, or steal them. They do not always write anything themselves.

Why should you care?

Attackers who buy or steal tools do not need deep skills, so more people can run intrusions. Stolen certificates and licences let their software look legitimate and trustworthy. When a supplier is breached, your systems may be affected even though you did nothing wrong.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. The attacker decides which capability the operation needs, such as a backdoor or an exploit.
  2. They look for it in criminal markets, public repositories, or specialist vendors.
  3. They pay for it, download it free, or steal it from another party.
  4. They test the capability against security tools before using it in an operation.
  5. They use the tool during the intrusion, often reusing it across many victims.
  6. Defenders learn about it later from intelligence, vendor breach notices, or revocation.

Real-world example

Think of a shared flat where nobody owns a drill. One person buys a cheap drill, another borrows a neighbour's, and a third simply takes one from a toolbox left in the hallway. The hole in the wall looks the same in every case. Only the story of where the drill came from tells you how much skill the person really had.

Key takeaways

  • Attackers often reuse off-the-shelf or stolen capability rather than writing new tools.
  • Stolen certificates and licences let malicious activity look legitimate.
  • Use threat intelligence and supplier breach alerts, since victims see little directly.

In more detail

Obtain Capabilities is the resource development behaviour of acquiring the tools of an intrusion from outside the adversary's own team, instead of building them in house. The acquisition channels are buying, downloading, and stealing. Purchases flow through criminal marketplaces, exploit brokers, malware developers, and commercial software vendors, including licences for red team frameworks. Free downloads come from open repositories and public proof-of-concept code. Theft targets capability instead of data: stealer logs, cracked licence keys, and certificates lifted from a compromised vendor. This technique is the counterpart to Develop Capabilities, and analysts use the distinction to profile an intrusion. A group that buys capability tends to reuse widely available tooling with known signatures and known infrastructure patterns. A group that steals a signing certificate leaves a full compromise of a third party in its wake, which is often how the intrusion is eventually discovered. Because this activity happens outside the victim's estate, there is no direct victim telemetry. Detection instead depends on threat intelligence, marketplace and dark web monitoring, vendor breach notifications, and timely revocation of stolen credentials and certificates. The response is governance as much as engineering: knowing which certificates and licences your suppliers hold, and being ready to revoke them.

What you will practise

  • Explain the difference between obtaining capabilities and developing them.
  • Describe the three main acquisition channels and give an example of each.
  • Explain why stolen code signing certificates are valuable to an adversary.
  • Identify the intelligence sources that reveal capability acquisition.
  • Describe what an organisation should monitor and revoke after a supplier breach.
