EPSS

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

EPSS stands for Exploit Prediction Scoring System. It gives each known software weakness a score from near zero to one. That score estimates how likely attackers are to use it within the next month. A higher number means a greater chance.

Why should you care?

A company may have thousands of software weaknesses to fix, and the team cannot fix them all this week. Guessing the order wastes effort on flaws nobody attacks. EPSS uses real evidence to show which ones are most likely to be used soon. That helps the team fix the dangerous ones first. It is a probability, not a promise.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. A weakness in a popular product becomes public and gets an identifier.
  2. The model gathers evidence, such as public code that demonstrates an attack.
  3. It also looks at how often similar products are attacked in the real world.
  4. All of this produces a probability that the weakness will be used within a month.
  5. The score is refreshed often, so it can rise quickly after new evidence appears.
  6. Teams use the score with severity and asset value to decide what to fix first.

Real-world example

Think of a city with a hundred broken street lights and only two repair crews. Counting broken bulbs is not enough. The city also asks where fights and accidents actually happen. A dark alley behind a busy station needs fixing tonight. A dead lamp on a quiet lane can wait until next month, even though both lamps are equally broken.

Key takeaways

  • EPSS estimates the chance of exploitation, not the size of the damage.
  • Use it together with severity and the importance of the affected system.
  • A low score lowers the priority; it never proves the risk is zero.

In more detail

The Exploit Prediction Scoring System, or EPSS, is a data-driven model that estimates the probability a published software vulnerability will be exploited in the wild within the next thirty days. It exists because severity ratings answer a different question. A severity score describes how bad the consequences would be if a flaw were exploited, while EPSS estimates how likely exploitation actually is, using signals such as whether public exploit code exists, how attractive the affected product is to attackers, and observed exploitation activity. The output is a probability between zero and one with a percentile, refreshed daily as new evidence appears. In practice it belongs in the prioritisation step of vulnerability management, where it is combined with severity, asset criticality and exposure rather than replacing them. The mistake people usually make is treating a low score as permission to ignore a finding, when a probability is not a guarantee and a targeted attacker may care about exactly the system everyone else ignored.

What you will practise

  • Explain what the Exploit Prediction Scoring System estimates and over what time window.
  • Describe how likelihood differs from severity and why a vulnerability programme needs both.
  • Explain how evidence about public exploit code and observed activity feeds the score.
  • Apply a probability score sensibly alongside asset criticality and exposure when ranking work.
  • Recognise the error of treating a low probability as proof that no risk exists.
