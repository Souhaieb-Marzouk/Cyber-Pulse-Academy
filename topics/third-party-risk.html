Third-Party Risk Management

Risk & ResilienceHardKeyword

New to this? Start here

Explained in plain language

Written for beginners. No security background needed.

Simple definition

Third party risk management means watching the danger that comes from other companies you work with. A third party is any supplier, contractor or software vendor outside your own organisation. These companies may hold your data or connect to your systems. This work checks how safe they are and what happens if they fail.

Why should you care?

You can have strong protection and still be breached through a supplier who is weaker than you. Attackers know this and target smaller companies that serve large ones. A supplier outage can also stop your own business for days. Clear contracts and security rules make responsibilities visible before trouble starts. Without this work, you discover how much you depend on a supplier only during an incident.

How it works, in plain steps

  1. List every outside company that touches your data, your systems or your money.
  2. Sort the list by how much harm a failure at that company would cause you.
  3. Ask each important supplier to show how they protect data and detect attacks.
  4. Put clear security duties, reporting times and audit rights into the written agreement with each supplier.
  5. Check the supplier again on a schedule and after any major change or breach.
  6. Plan how you would keep working if that supplier suddenly stopped trading.

Real-world example

Think of a restaurant that serves a famous sauce made by one small kitchen. The restaurant is clean and well run, but it never inspects that kitchen. One day the small kitchen has a hygiene problem and the restaurant must close too. Careful owners visit the kitchen, agree standards in writing and keep a second supplier ready. Third party risk management is that diligence for every company your business depends on.

Key takeaways

  • Your security is only as strong as the weakest supplier that holds your data or reaches your systems.
  • Check suppliers before you sign, and keep checking them afterwards.
  • Know your suppliers of suppliers, because they often hold the same data.

In more detail

Third party risk management is the discipline of identifying, assessing and continuously monitoring the security and resilience exposure created by suppliers, contractors, software vendors and service providers. It runs as a lifecycle: inventory the relationships, classify them by the data and access they involve, assess controls before onboarding, write security requirements into contracts, then monitor for change such as breaches, ownership shifts or new subcontractors. In real environments it appears as vendor questionnaires, right to audit clauses, concentration risk reviews and supply chain incident response planning. The mistake organisations make is treating it as a procurement checkbox driven by a questionnaire that is answered once: a point in time answer says nothing about the supplier's current posture, and it usually ignores fourth parties, the suppliers of your suppliers, who often handle the same data under weaker controls.

What you will practise

  • Explain what third party risk management covers and why it exists.
  • Describe how suppliers are classified by the data and access they involve.
  • Identify contract terms that make security duties clear.
  • Explain why a one time questionnaire is not enough evidence of safety.
  • Describe how to monitor a supplier for changes after onboarding.
  • Explain what fourth party risk means in a supply chain.
