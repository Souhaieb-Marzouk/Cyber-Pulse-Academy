CISA ISACA CISA

CISAISACAExtremeCertificationUnofficial Study Resource

The Certified Information Systems Auditor is ISACA's established credential for professionals who audit, assure and control information systems, and it remains the reference qualification for IT audit roles in banking, government, healthcare and large enterprise internal audit functions. It suits external and internal auditors, control and compliance analysts, security and risk practitioners who support audits, and technology managers responsible for the control environment. To an employer it signals that the holder can plan and scope an audit engagement, evaluate whether controls are designed and operating effectively, test evidence rather than accept assertions, assess the governance and management of IT, judge whether systems development and operations protect the organisation, and report findings with proportionate, practical recommendations. The exam is a single multiple-choice assessment of roughly one hundred and fifty questions with a four-hour allowance, scored on a scaled model where a minimum scaled score of 450 out of 800 is required, and it is deliberately written from the auditor's perspective, so the correct answer is usually the one that obtains sufficient appropriate evidence, preserves independence, or applies professional scepticism instead of the fastest technical fix. Its five domains cover the information systems audit process, IT governance and management, information systems acquisition, development and implementation, information systems operations and business resilience, and protection of information assets. Hands-on practice matters because audit judgement is a skill: reading a change management log to find a control that was bypassed, tracing a population of changes to sampled evidence, spotting an independence conflict in a review plan, and turning an exception into a finding with a clear risk statement and a proportionate recommendation.

Exam domains and objectives

  • Information Systems Auditing Process: Plan, scope and conduct audits by applying risk-based approaches, IS audit standards and guidelines, and professional ethics and independence requirements.
  • Information Systems Auditing Process: Gather and evaluate evidence through sampling, interviewing, observation and control testing, then document, report and follow up on findings and management responses.
  • IT Governance and Management: Evaluate whether IT governance structures, strategy, policies and decision rights align with enterprise objectives and deliver value while managing risk and resources.
  • IT Governance and Management: Assess IT organisational structure, roles, outsourcing and third-party arrangements, performance monitoring, and compliance with laws, regulations and contractual obligations.
  • Information Systems Acquisition, Development and Implementation: Evaluate business case justification, project governance and risk, and whether acquisition and development activities deliver solutions that meet requirements and remain auditable.
  • Information Systems Acquisition, Development and Implementation: Review development methodologies, testing, changeover, data migration and post-implementation review, including controls over change and configuration.
  • Information Systems Operations and Business Resilience: Assess service management, infrastructure operations, scheduling, capacity, and the controls that keep production services reliable, recoverable and appropriately monitored.
  • Information Systems Operations and Business Resilience: Evaluate backup, restoration testing, disaster recovery and business continuity arrangements against recovery objectives and dependencies, including for outsourced services.
  • Protection of Information Assets: Evaluate security architecture, identity and access management, encryption, network and endpoint controls, and physical and environmental safeguards.
  • Protection of Information Assets: Assess security programme management, incident response and monitoring, awareness and training, data classification and privacy obligations, and the adequacy of security testing.
