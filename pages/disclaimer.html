Educational and defensive purpose only

This site exists to teach defence. Every exercise is written to help a defender recognise, triage, contain, investigate or remediate activity, and to help a learner practise in a lab they control.

For that reason, the site deliberately does not contain:

  • working exploit code
  • real credentials, keys, tokens or passwords
  • live malware, or hashes of live malware
  • live targets, real hostnames of real victims, or addresses of systems the site owner does not control

Every indicator that appears in the material is defanged: addresses, domains, hashes and identifiers are altered, bracketed or otherwise made non-functional so they cannot be used as-is. Where a technique is described, it is described at the level needed to detect or defend against it, not as a recipe for misuse. Nothing here is a penetration testing service, an attack service, or a source of access to anything.

Your responsibility

You are responsible for how you use what you learn here, and for complying with the law in your own jurisdiction and in any jurisdiction you are working from or against.

Cyber security law differs from country to country. Accessing a system without authorisation is a criminal offence in many of them, and in some it is an offence even when nothing is damaged or copied. Rules on interception, monitoring, data handling, encryption, export and disclosure also vary. It is your job to know which ones apply to you.

The practical rule this site teaches and expects is simple: never test, scan, probe, exploit or monitor anything you do not own, or for which you do not hold clear written permission from someone authorised to give it. Statistics and privacy

This section only points you to the detail. The two pages that matter are the Privacy Policy and the Cookies and local storage page, and both are written to be read rather than skimmed.

In short: the site sets no cookies at all. It uses your browser's local storage for the keys listed in the Privacy Policy, and nothing else. Statistics ship in local mode, which means counters stay on your device and never reach the site owner. Remote mode is opt-in by the site owner, and even then it transmits a truncated hash rather than your username. Off mode collects nothing at all. The footer has an Edit identity button and a Delete my data button that removes every cm.* key from this browser.
