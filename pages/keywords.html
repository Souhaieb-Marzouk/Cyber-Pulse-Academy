Cyber Security Keywords

This is the vocabulary layer of the site: the terms, controls and concepts that exams, interviews and incident bridges assume you already hold before the conversation starts. Each entry explains the thing itself, where it fails in production, what the telemetry looks like when it misbehaves, and what a defender does next. The catalog holds 302 keyword topics grouped into eleven themes, and every one of them reserves three exercise batches, so the reading is only half of the work.

Community-built study content. Not official vendor material.

Every topic reserves three batches, and each one attacks the same subject from a different angle: batch 1 is detection and triage, batch 2 is hands-on response and configuration, and batch 3 is the adversarial batch written from the attacker's side. The full catalog accounts for 7805 planned batches, of which 66 are published and 7739 are still open. A topic with no published batch is not an empty page: it keeps its full study entry, and it says honestly that the exercises are not written yet. If you want to fill one of those gaps, the process, the generation prompt and the quality bar are in the about page.

How to use this catalog

Read the topic before you open a batch

The study page carries the plain-language explanation, the failure modes and the sources. The batches assume that context instead of restating it, so skipping the reading turns a hard exercise into a guessing game.

Take batch 1 early, even expecting a low score

Batch 1 is the detection and triage lens: it asks you to recognise the term in logs and alerts rather than recite it. That is where most people who can define a term cleanly are weakest, and the score is a better study plan than any reading order.

Leave the adversarial batch until last

Batch 3 is written from the attacker's side and assumes you can already hold the defensive explanation. Attempting it first produces a confusing result rather than a useful one.

Re-sit a failed batch after reading the sources

A score below the pass mark is a pointer to what to read, not a verdict. Read the sources listed on the topic page, then re-sit. Repeating a batch without reading in between trains recall of the question set, not the subject.
