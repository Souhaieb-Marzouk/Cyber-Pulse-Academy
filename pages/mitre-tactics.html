MITRE ATT&CK Tactics

A tactic is an adversary goal, not a method. Reconnaissance is the goal of learning about a target; the technique is whatever behaviour or tool gets there. That separation is what makes the framework usable for defenders, because you can reason about what a log source shows without first arguing about which binary was involved. CyberPulseAcademy covers all 15 Enterprise tactics.

Read the grouping as a two-step path. Each technique topic names its parent tactic, and each tactic topic carries a "Techniques in this tactic" cross-reference block that resolves to the technique pages underneath it. So you start from a goal, follow it down to the concrete behaviours that achieve it, then follow each behaviour out to the mitigations and detection strategies aimed at it. The tactic is the chapter heading; the technique is the material.

Why the ordering matters

The list runs Reconnaissance, Resource Development, Initial Access, Execution, Persistence, Privilege Escalation, Defense Evasion, Credential Access, Discovery, Lateral Movement, Collection, Command and Control, Exfiltration, Impact. Read top to bottom it looks like a sequence, and that is exactly the mistake to avoid. The order is a presentation order that groups related goals together. ATT&CK does not claim that an intrusion visits them in turn, and an incident report that draws them as a straight line has quietly invented a timeline.

Real intrusions loop. An operator who loses a foothold re-enters at Initial Access with a different vector. An operator who finds an interesting share during Discovery goes back to Credential Access for the account that can reach it, then back to Lateral Movement. Credential Access almost always happens more than once, because every new host holds new secrets. And two of the tactics are not stages at all: Persistence and Defense Evasion run alongside everything else for as long as the operator intends to stay.

That has two practical consequences for defenders. First, do not reason about how far along an intrusion is from the tactic it was spotted in; Impact-stage evidence can appear in the first hour and Collection-stage evidence can appear in the third month. Second, use the tactic list as a coverage checklist rather than a detection pipeline: walk the fourteen goals, ask which one you could not currently see, and fix that gap. The loops are where dwell time accumulates, so the repeated tactics — Credential Access, Discovery, Privilege Escalation — are the ones worth the most instrumentation.
