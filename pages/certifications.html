Certifications

CyberPulseAcademy catalogues 76 certification tracks across twelve vendor and standards families. Each entry summarises the published exam domains in our own words, links every domain to the keyword and ATT&CK topics that actually teach it, and reserves three exercise batches: a detection and triage batch, a hands-on response and configuration batch, and an adversarial batch written from the attacker's side. This is independent study material, not vendor training, and it contains no reproduced or recalled exam questions.

Unofficial Study Resource — CyberPulseAcademy is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by CompTIA, ISC2, ISACA, OffSec, EC-Council, GIAC, Microsoft, AWS, Google, Cisco, or any other vendor. Objectives summaries are paraphrased from publicly available exam objectives.

Community-built study content. Not official vendor material.

The same compact notice travels with every card in the grid below, next to the Unofficial Study Resource badge that the renderer attaches to each certification entry. Nothing on this site is gated, and nothing depends on you buying a course, a voucher or a subscription.

One rule is absolute: no vendor logos appear anywhere on this site. A logo printed beside a certification name reads as an endorsement, and there is no endorsement here to claim. Because that rule matters more than any reviewer's memory, it is enforced in code rather than documented and hoped for: assets/js/catalog-render.js inspects every certification card before it reaches the page and strips any <img>, <picture> or <object> element it finds, so a logo cannot be reintroduced by editing a template or a data file. Vendor names are carried as text, never as marks.

How to study for a certification with this site

Map the exam domains to topics before you read anything else

Open the certification entry, read the domain summary, then follow each domain through to the keyword and ATT&CK topics behind it. Half an hour of mapping saves days of reading material that the exam does not test.

Use the batch 1 detection lens as a diagnostic

Batch 1 asks you to recognise the domain in logs, alerts and telemetry rather than recite it. Take it early, accept a low score, and treat the questions you missed as the syllabus you actually need.

Do the adversarial batch last

Batch 3 is written from the attacker's side and assumes the defensive explanation is already solid. It is the sharpest test of whether you understand a domain or merely recognise its vocabulary, and it is wasted as a first attempt.

Re-sit a failed batch after reading its sources

The pass mark is a pointer, not a verdict. Every topic page lists the sources behind it; read the ones covering the questions you missed, then re-sit the batch. Repeating a batch without reading in between trains recall of the question set, not the domain.

Check the coverage dashboard before you plan a study week

The coverage dashboard shows which topics have published batches and which are still empty, so you can see whether a vendor family has exercises yet and plan around the gaps instead of finding them mid-session.

Where these objectives come from

Every certification entry cites the vendor's official objectives page in its sources list, and the domain headings on the study page follow that published structure. The wording is paraphrased, never copied, and the study page says which objective version it was written against. Exam objectives are versioned documents that change between releases, so the vendor's own page is always the source of record: if this site and the vendor disagree, the vendor is right and this site has a bug worth reporting.

Accuracy reports require a citation. If a domain summary here is wrong, outdated or misleading, the useful report names the vendor page, the section and what that page says, because that is what makes the correction verifiable rather than a matter of opinion. The citation requirement, the batch format and the review expectations for a pull request are all described on the about page.
