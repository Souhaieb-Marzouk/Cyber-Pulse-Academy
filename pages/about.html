About CyberMastery

CyberMastery is an independent, community-run and strictly non-commercial study platform for cyber security. It exists because reading about security is easy and applying it under pressure is hard, and almost nothing on the internet trains the second skill honestly. No account is required, there are no adverts, there is no third-party tracking, and no part of the content sits behind a payment.

What this project is

CyberMastery publishes free study material for people who already work in security or are training to. Every topic carries three independently written hands-on exercise batches, and each batch is judged against a pass mark. Progress is stored in your own browser. The platform serves 246 topics drawn from 125 vocabulary entries, 28 certification objective summaries, 14 ATT&CK tactics, 33 techniques, 18 mitigations, 12 detection strategies and 16 tracked threat groups, reserving 738 exercise batches in total.

Theory is easy to read and hard to apply. A definition of lateral movement takes thirty seconds to memorise; deciding, at 03:00 with three alerts open, which of four authentication events actually matters is a different skill entirely. Textbooks, video courses and multiple-choice question banks all optimise for recognition. Recognition is not the skill that gets tested when something is on fire. The exercises here are written to be difficult on purpose: they ask you to read telemetry, choose a containment action, justify it against the evidence, and notice what the evidence does not say.

Who it is for

SOC analysts

Tier 1 and Tier 2 work is repetitive until it is not. The detection and triage batches are built around the judgement calls that separate a closed ticket from an escalation, including the cases where the correct answer is to keep looking.

Incident responders

Response and hands-on batches cover containment order, evidence preservation, scoping and the trade-offs between speed and certainty, the decisions that are hardest to rehearse in a live incident.

Threat hunters

Hypothesis-driven hunting depends on knowing which telemetry would confirm or refute a theory. The detection strategies and technique topics are cross-linked so a hypothesis can be followed from tactic to concrete data source.

GRC analysts

Control language is only useful when it maps to something observable. Risk, control and compliance topics are written so that a framework clause and the technical behaviour it is supposed to govern appear in the same place.

Pentesters

The adversarial batch is written from the attacker side, which is exactly why it is useful to a defender: understanding the cheapest path through a control is how you decide whether that control is worth its cost. Engagement scoping and rules of engagement are treated as first-class material, not as paperwork.

Cloud engineers

Identity is the new perimeter, and most cloud incidents are authorization incidents. Policy, key management, workload identity and logging topics assume you build the environment rather than only defend it.

Students

If you are studying for a first certification or a degree module, the certification summaries give you the shape of the syllabus and the keyword catalog gives you the vocabulary examiners assume. Nothing here requires a paid account anywhere.

What all of them get

A single, consistent place to practise, in the browser, on a phone or on a locked-down work laptop, with no sign-up and no telemetry leaving the device.

How the exercises are generated and why that matters

Every topic reserves three exercise batches, and the three are deliberately different from one another rather than three variations of the same quiz.

Batch 1

Detection and triage

You are given evidence and asked what it means. The batch concentrates on reading telemetry, separating benign from malicious, prioritising correctly and stating what the evidence does not support. Answers are judged on the reasoning path, not only on the final choice.

Batch 2

Response and hands-on

You are asked to do something. Containment, configuration, control selection, ordering of actions, and the consequences of getting the order wrong. This is the batch that most often exposes the gap between knowing a control exists and knowing how it fails in production.

Batch 3

Adversarial and cross-domain

The same subject seen from the other side, then connected to a neighbouring domain. If batch 1 and batch 2 teach you to recognise a pattern, batch 3 asks what an operator with a goal would do instead, and what that means for the defences you just configured.

Every batch is one standalone HTML file

A batch ships as a single self-contained HTML document with its own styles, its own questions, its own scoring and its own navigation back to the topic. It does not require the rest of the site to be loaded, it has no server dependency, and it can be opened from a local folder, from a USB stick or from a locked-down environment that permits nothing else. That constraint is also what makes the batches reproducible: each one is produced from a published prompt, so a contributor anywhere can generate a batch in the same shape and submit it for review, and the result will look and behave like the batches already published. The catalog is the index of which batches exist; the published manifest is the single source of truth for their status.

The full exercise generation prompt

This is the canonical prompt, reproduced verbatim from docs/exercise-generation-prompt.md. It is the whole reason a stranger can generate a missing exercise batch without asking anyone's permission: copy it, fill in the topic block, and the output is a batch that matches every other batch on the site. The same text is reproduced in CONTRIBUTING.md; if the two ever disagree, the copy in docs/ is authoritative and the disagreement is a bug worth reporting. 

## THE PROMPT

You are generating ONE exercise batch for CyberPulseAcademy, a free, non-commercial cyber security
study platform. The output must be a single, complete, standalone HTML file that runs by
double-clicking it, with no build step, no server, and no network access.

### TOPIC BLOCK

```
topicId        = <id, for example t1059>
topicTitle     = <title, for example Command and Scripting Interpreter>
externalId     = <ATT&CK id or exam code, for example T1059, or leave blank>
batch          = <1, 2, or 3>
batchLens      = <see the lens definitions below>
difficulty     = <hard, very-hard, or extreme>
questionCount  = <20 unless told otherwise>
passMark       = <70 unless told otherwise>
audience       = <for example SOC analyst tier 2, incident responder, cloud security engineer>
```

### LENS DEFINITIONS, use the one that matches the batch number

* **Batch 1, Detection and Triage.** Log and alert analysis, SIEM-style queries, prioritisation
  between competing alerts, triage decisions, and the blue-team perspective. The learner is
  looking at telemetry and deciding what it means and what to do first.
* **Batch 2, Response and Hands-on.** A terminal emulator, configuration and policy completion,
  decision trees, spot-the-mistake, and incident timeline ordering. The learner is doing
  something with their hands and being judged on whether the action is correct and safe.
* **Batch 3, Adversarial and Cross-domain.** The red-team perspective, evasion reasoning,
  dual-perspective pairs where the learner must answer the same scenario as attacker and then as
  defender, ATT&CK mapping, risk ranking, and report drafting. The learner must reason about both
  sides of the same event.

### HARD REQUIREMENTS

1. **One file.** All CSS in `<style>`, all JavaScript in `<script>`. No external requests of any
   kind: no CDN, no web font, no icon library, no analytics, no remote image. Inline SVG only.
2. **Under 250 KB.** Aim for 60 to 120 KB. Never embed base64 images.
3. **Declare the metadata block** so the hosting page can render an accurate header:
   ```html
   <script type="application/json" id="cm-exercise-meta">
   { "topicId": "<topicId>", "batch": <batch>, "version": "1.0.0",
     "title": "<a specific descriptive title>", "focus": "<one-line lens summary>",
     "difficulty": "<difficulty>", "questionCount": <questionCount>,
     "passMark": <passMark>, "estimatedMinutes": <a realistic integer>,
     "supportsScoreBridge": true, "resultsSelector": "#cm-results" }
   </script>
   ```
4. **Emit the score contract.** When the results screen is shown, post exactly once:
   ```js
   try {
     parent.postMessage({
       type: "CYBERPULSEACADEMY_SCORE", topicId: "<topicId>", batch: <batch>,
       score: <number correct>, total: <questionCount>,
       percent: <0-100 integer>, passed: <boolean>
     }, location.origin);
   } catch (e) { /* works standalone too */ }
   ```
5. **Accept the host handshake and ignore what you do not need:**
   ```js
   window.addEventListener("message", function (ev) {
     if (ev.data && ev.data.type === "CYBERPULSEACADEMY_INIT") {
       // ev.data.username, ev.data.country, ev.data.theme, ev.data.locale, ev.data.batch
     }
   });
   ```
   Apply `ev.data.theme` ("dark" or "light") to your own `html` element. The batch must work
   perfectly when opened directly, with no handshake ever arriving.
6. **The results container must have `id="cm-results"`** and must be hidden until the results
   screen is shown.
7. **Question quality over quantity.** Every question must be genuinely difficult and realistic
   for the stated audience. For a 20-question batch, include a deliberate spread:
   * at least 6 multiple-choice questions where more than one option is correct, and the question
     text says so explicitly
   * at least 3 questions presenting a short SIEM-style or audit-log excerpt in a `<pre>` block,
     asking for the most likely ATT&CK technique or the correct next triage step
   * at least 3 prioritisation questions, for example which of four alerts to work first and why
   * at least 2 questions whose correct answer is "this is benign activity", with a
     confident-sounding false positive as the main distractor
   * at least 2 detection-engineering questions, for example which telemetry source would actually
     let you detect a behaviour, or why a given rule would drown an analyst in false positives
   * free-text questions must accept a documented set of reasonable answers, matched
     case-insensitively and with obvious synonyms tolerated
8. **Explain every answer.** After each question is answered, show why the correct answer is
   correct and why each distractor is wrong. This is a study tool: the explanation carries more
   value than the score.
9. **Results screen** with score, total, percent, pass or fail against the pass mark, a
   per-question review linking back to the explanation, and a retry that resets cleanly.
10. **Accessibility, WCAG 2.1 AA, non-negotiable:**
    * one `<h1>`, ordered headings, a `<main>` landmark
    * every input has a real `<label>`; group choices with `<fieldset>` and `<legend>`
    * a visible focus ring that is never removed
    * `aria-live="polite"` on the score and feedback region
    * progress conveyed by text, for example "Question 7 of 20, 35 percent", never by colour or a
      bar alone
    * honour `prefers-reduced-motion`
    * fully keyboard operable, with no interaction that requires a pointer; if you use drag and
      drop anywhere, provide a click-to-select fallback
    * text contrast of at least 4.5 to 1
    * touch targets of at least 44 by 44 pixels
11. **Work on iOS Safari and Android Chrome.** Use Pointer Events if you need pointer input, guard
    every `localStorage` write in `try/catch` and fall back to an in-memory object, and never rely
    on hover.
12. **Visual continuity.** Use the CyberPulseAcademy palette so the batch does not look foreign inside
    the host iframe:
    background `#070d16`, surface `#0e1a28`, raised surface `#101d2e`, border `#1e3350`,
    primary text `#e8f1fb`, secondary text `#b3c6db`, accent `#22d3ee`, warning `#fbbf24`,
    success `#34d399`, failure `#f87171`. Provide a light theme applied by
    `html[data-theme="light"]` using background `#f6f9fc` and text `#0d1b2a`.

### CONTENT SAFETY, NON-NEGOTIABLE

* Defensive training only. No working exploit code.
* No real credentials, no real API keys, no real tokens.
* No live malware samples and no hashes of live samples.
* Nothing that crosses into how to actually attack a real target. No procedures to follow.
* Every indicator must be defanged: `hxxp://evil[.]example`, `10[.]0[.]0[.]1`.
* Use only the RFC 5737 documentation ranges `192.0.2.0/24`, `198.51.100.0/24`, `203.0.113.0/24`
  and the RFC 2606 example domains `example.com`, `example.net`, `example.org`.
* Log excerpts must be analyst-facing telemetry presented as data to investigate, such as command
  lines, process trees, parent-child relationships, encoded parameters, scheduled task creation
  and audit log entries. They must never read as instructions.
* If a question genuinely needs to reference an attack technique, describe the behaviour and what
  it leaves behind. Do not write the procedure.

### OUTPUT FORMAT

Return **only** the complete HTML file, from `<!DOCTYPE html>` to `</html>`. No commentary before
or after. No markdown fences.

## After you have the file

1. Save it as `exercises/<topic-id>/batch-<n>.html`.
2. In `data/<type>/<topic-id>.json`, set that batch's `"status"` to `"published"`.
3. Run the build and the gate:

   ```
   python scripts/build_catalog.py
   python scripts/generate_pages.py
   python scripts/validate.py
   ```

4. Preview with `python -m http.server 8000`, open `http://localhost:8000/topics/<topic-id>.html`,
   click Start on your batch, and confirm the score reaches the page. The hub shows a toast and
   updates "your recent attempts" when it does.
5. Also open the batch directly, outside the iframe, and confirm it still works. It must never
   depend on the host.

## Why the batch contract is shaped this way

The site does **not** duplicate the exercise UI. The batch owns its own interface; the hub only
frames it, passes identity in, and listens for a score. That keeps three hundred exercise files
from each re-implementing navigation, and it means a batch is useful on its own, which is what
lets a contributor generate one in isolation.

Because batches are generated separately and may be written before this contract existed,
`assets/js/exam-hub.js` also injects a small bridge script into the same-origin iframe on load.
That bridge watches for a results screen and for score-shaped `localStorage` writes, and degrades
silently when it finds nothing. It is a safety net, not a substitute: a batch that emits the
contract itself is always more reliable.

The prompt ends with the submission checklist. It is reproduced in full, including the build gate, so nothing about the contract between a contributor and this site is hidden behind a repository login.

Batch availability is published honestly

All 738 batches are reserved by the manifest. As of this build, 1 are published and 737 are still open. Nothing is marked available before it exists, which is why the coverage dashboard is the most useful page on the site for anyone who wants to help.

The technology

The stack is deliberately boring, because a study resource that breaks is worse than a study resource with fewer features.

Static HTML, CSS and vanilla JavaScript

No framework, no bundler, no transpiler, no runtime server, no CDN, no web font and no icon library. Icons are inline SVG. The only JavaScript executed is the code in this repository, and the site works from a plain file server as well as from GitHub Pages.

One manifest as the source of truth

data/catalog.json is the single source of truth for every topic, its metadata, its cross-links and the status of its three batches. Nothing about the catalog is hand-copied into a page: a client-side renderer reads the manifest and builds the lists, filters and counts. Change the manifest and every listing page follows.

Standard-library build scripts and a validation gate

The Python build scripts use the standard library only, so a contributor needs no environment beyond Python itself. A validation pass runs on every change and fails the build on broken cross-references, duplicate ids, missing manifest entries and size violations. The gate is not advisory: continuous integration turns red and the change does not land.

A 250 KB budget per file, enforced

No single shipped file may exceed 250 KB. The budget exists because the site is aimed at people on locked-down work laptops, older phones and poor connections, and because a large file is usually the symptom of content pasted where content should have been referenced. Continuous integration fails the build when a file goes over, so the limit cannot quietly erode.

A service worker for offline study

A service worker caches the shell, the manifest and batches you have already opened, so a commute, a flight or a filtered network is not a reason to stop. Cache updates are explicit rather than silent, so a stale page cannot sit in front of you indefinitely without your knowledge.

Hosted on GitHub Pages

Deployment is a push. There is no application server, no database and no runtime secret anywhere in the project, which removes an entire class of operational risk and is also why hosting costs are close to zero. The site is served over HTTPS from a static origin.

Accessibility is not optional

The target is WCAG 2.1 level AA, applied to the site shell and to every exercise batch. A study platform that excludes people is not a study platform.

  • Pointer Events, with a click-to-place fallback. Where an exercise asks you to drag or to place something, the interaction uses Pointer Events and also offers an explicit select-then-place path, so it works with touch, with a mouse and with a keyboard alone. A drag-only interaction is not accessible, and pretending otherwise by adding a tooltip is not a fix.
  • Reduced motion is honoured. When the operating system asks for reduced motion, animations and transitions are removed rather than shortened. Motion is never load-bearing: no information appears only as a result of an animation.
  • Colour is never the only signal. Correct and incorrect, published and missing, pass and fail all carry text or a shape in addition to colour. The same rule applies to the charts and to the coverage grid.
  • Focus management and live regions. Focus moves deliberately when something opens or completes and returns where it came from when it closes. Timers, scores and errors are announced through polite live regions, and a skip link is the first focusable element on every page.
An honest limit on that claim

The site shell is tested directly. The generated exercise batches are produced separately by contributors and by language models, and each one is reviewed by a human against this standard before it is published. They are reviewed, not machine-verified: there is no automated conformance suite that can certify an arbitrary batch as WCAG 2.1 AA. If you find a batch that fails, that is a bug and reporting it is welcome.

Roadmap

The manifest already reserves fields for the next four content types. They are reserved and empty today: no page, no renderer and no content depends on them yet, and nothing on the site pretends otherwise. The renderer ignores fields it does not recognise, so these can be filled in later without touching user-interface code.

Manifest fields reserved on the roadmap, what they will hold, and their state today
Reserved field What it will hold State today
courses Ordered study paths that group existing topics into a sequence with a start and an end. Reserved, empty
labs Guided, sandboxed practical exercises that go beyond a single batch, with a defined environment. Reserved, empty
glossary Short canonical definitions linked from the keyword catalog, so a term has one authoritative entry. Reserved, empty
quizzes Short retrieval-practice sets for spacing and revision, distinct from the three deep exercise batches. Reserved, empty

Because the renderer ignores unknown fields, adding these later is a content change rather than a rewrite. That is deliberate: the schema was designed so that the roadmap does not require a migration.

Who this is appropriate for, and what it does not teach

This is defensive security training, written for adults who work in security or are studying it formally, and for older students working towards a security qualification with supervision. The subject matter is incident response, detection engineering, control design and risk. It assumes the reader can handle material about real intrusions, real malware families and real breaches described in professional language.

What it does teach

  • How to read telemetry and decide what it does and does not prove.
  • How to choose, order and justify a response action.
  • How controls are implemented, and how they fail in production.
  • How attacker behaviour maps to defensive coverage, using public ATT&CK data.
  • How to reason about risk, evidence and confidence in writing.

What it does not teach, on purpose

  • It does not teach how to attack real systems, and it contains no working exploit code.
  • It contains no real credentials, no live malware and no functional payloads.
  • All indicators, addresses, domains, hashes and hostnames in examples are defanged or fictional.
  • Nothing here is a live target. There is no lab that reaches a real environment.
  • Nothing here replaces your employer's policy, your legal obligations or an authorised engagement.

A word on judgement: the difference between understanding an intrusion and being able to reproduce one is mostly intent and authorisation. This platform teaches the first. If you want to practise the second, do it inside a lab you own or with written authorisation, because unauthorised access is a criminal offence in most jurisdictions regardless of what you intended to learn.

Why the split exists

The code is infrastructure and there is no reason to restrict it: the more people who reuse the renderer, the shell and the build scripts, the more useful the work becomes. The content is different. It is written by named contributors in their own time, it is reviewed for accuracy, and it is meant to stay free for learners. The non-commercial and share-alike terms are what stop a third party from repackaging this material behind a paywall while the project itself asks for voluntary support to keep going.

One consequence is worth stating plainly: relicensing later would require contacting every contributor whose work is included and obtaining their agreement, because a share-alike licence cannot simply be withdrawn for material already published under it. Contributions are accepted into the project under these terms, which is exactly why the terms are stated before you contribute rather than after.

How to contribute

The most valuable contribution is an exercise batch for a topic that does not have one yet. Writing one batch is a bounded piece of work, and the coverage dashboard shows exactly where the gaps are. Contributions of corrections, accessibility fixes and clearer explanations are equally welcome.
