Cookies and local storage

This site sets no cookies. None. Every key it keeps is listed below, in full, with what it holds and how long it lasts.

Last updated: 1 June 2025

Blunt version: this site sets no cookies. Zero. Not first-party cookies. Not third-party cookies. Not advertising cookies. Not analytics cookies. No pixels, no beacons, no fingerprinting. All persistence is browser local storage, which never travels with your requests unless the page sends it deliberately, and this page tells you exactly when that happens.

The difference between a cookie and local storage

These two things are often talked about as if they were the same. They are not, and the difference is the whole reason this page exists.

How a cookie differs from browser local storage
Property Cookie Local storage
Sent to a server automatically Yes. A cookie is attached to every matching HTTP request, without the page doing anything. No. It stays in the browser until a script on the page deliberately reads it.
Who can see it The site that set it, and any server it is sent to, including third parties when the cookie is not restricted. Only pages from the same origin. Other websites cannot read it.
Typical size limit Roughly 4 KB per cookie. Roughly 5 to 10 MB per origin, depending on the browser.
Lifetime Until it expires, or until the session ends for a session cookie. Until it is deleted, by you, by the site, or by the browser clearing site data.
Used by this site No, never. Yes, for the ten keys in the table below, and nothing else.

This site uses local storage only, and the reason is simple: the data it keeps is your data, for your own use. Your theme choice, your language choice, your chosen name, your country and your own scores have no business being attached to every request the page makes. Local storage keeps them where they belong. In the shipped default mode nothing about them is ever sent anywhere at all.

Every local storage key this site uses

Ten keys, listed in full. There are no others. Anything the site stores begins with cm., which is what makes the footer's Delete my data button able to remove exactly this site's data and nothing else in your browser.

Every local storage key CyberPulseAcademy uses, what it holds, why it exists and how long it lasts
Key What it holds Why Lifetime
cm.identity.v1 The username you chose, the two-letter country code you picked, a consent flag, and the timestamps for when you first consented and when you last changed it. So your scores are attached to a name you recognise, so the country breakdown has something to show, and so there is a record that you agreed. The name is one you invent; it is never your real name unless you choose to type it. Until you use Delete my data in the footer, or clear site data in your browser. It does not expire on its own.
cm.attempts.v1 Your last 500 exercise attempts. Each one holds the topic id, the batch number, the score, the total, the percentage, whether you passed, the country code, a truncated username hash and a timestamp. To show your own history, your best score per batch, and where you left off. It powers "continue where you left off" and is never transmitted, in any mode. The most recent 500 attempts. Once a 501st is recorded the oldest is dropped. Removed entirely by Delete my data or by clearing site data.
cm.stats.users.v1 A truncated username hash mapped to a country code, with a first-seen and a last-seen timestamp for each hash. To count distinct users rather than runs, so one person repeating a batch ten times does not look like ten people. The hash is the first 16 characters of SHA-256 over a public salt plus your username. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data. It does not expire on its own.
cm.theme.v1 Your theme choice: dark or light. To apply the theme you picked before the first paint. Without it a dark site flashes white on every load, which is a real problem for photosensitive visitors, not just an aesthetic one. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data.
cm.locale.v1 Your language code. To remember the language you selected, so the site does not ask again on every page. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data.
cm.consent.v1 That you acknowledged the storage notice, which version you saw, and when. So the notice is not shown again for the same version, and so there is a record that it was shown and acknowledged. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data.
cm.notice.dismissed.* One entry per notice you closed, for example a storage warning or a one-off site message. So the site does not nag you about the same notice every visit. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data.
cm.celebrate.dismissed.* One entry per celebration card you closed, such as a congratulation after a pass. So a card you have already dismissed does not come back. Until you use Delete my data or clear site data.
cm.stats.userSent.v1
remote mode only		 The last truncated username hash that was already announced to the site owner's endpoint. To avoid announcing the same visitor more than once per deployment, so the distinct-user count is not inflated. Until you use Delete my data, clear site data, or the site owner ships a new deployment hash. Not present at all in local or off mode.
cm.stats.queue.v1
remote mode only		 Statistics events that could not be sent, held as a queue of at most 50 entries. So a flaky connection does not silently lose a legitimate attempt. The queue is drained on the next successful send and the key is removed when it is empty. Until the events are sent, until the queue is replaced, or until you use Delete my data. Not present at all in local or off mode.

The two .* keys are prefixes: each dismissal adds its own key under that prefix, and all of them are removed together by the footer delete button. The .v1 suffix is a version marker, so a future format change can be made without misreading data written by an older version of the site.

What this site does not use

To state it as plainly as possible, CyberPulseAcademy has:

  • no third-party advertising cookies, and no advertising of any kind
  • no tracking cookies, first-party or third-party
  • no analytics service, and no analytics library loaded from anywhere
  • no tracking pixels and no web beacons
  • no social media widgets, no embedded posts, no share buttons that load a third-party script
  • no embedded third-party fonts, and no web fonts at all: the site uses the fonts already on your device
  • no embedded third-party scripts, no CDN, no tag manager, no icon library
  • no session recording, no heat maps, no A/B testing service
  • no device fingerprinting, and no attempt to identify you across websites

The site does link out to other websites, including the MITRE ATT&CK site, NIST publications and vendor documentation. Those links are ordinary links. They load nothing, set nothing and request nothing until you click them. Once you do click, you are on the other site, and that site's own cookie and privacy policies apply to you, not this one.

How to clear this data

The fastest route is the button this site provides. The browser routes below do the same job, and also remove anything the site left behind under a key you do not recognise.

Using the button in the footer

  1. Scroll to the footer at the bottom of any page.
  2. Select Delete my data.
  3. Confirm in the dialog.
  4. All ten keys listed above are removed from this browser, including queued events.

Chrome

  1. Open Settings, then Privacy and security, then Third-party cookies.
  2. Select "See all site data and permissions".
  3. Search for this site, then select Delete for that entry.

Firefox

  1. Open Settings, then Privacy & Security.
  2. Under Cookies and Site Data, select Manage Data.
  3. Search for this site, select it, then select Remove Selected.

Safari

  1. Open Settings, then Privacy.
  2. Select Manage Website Data.
  3. Find this site in the list and select Remove.

Safari private browsing may block local storage writes entirely. When that happens the site shows a notice and works for the session without saving anything. Nothing is lost from your device because nothing was ever written to it, and your scores last only until you close the tab.

Edge

  1. Open Settings, then Cookies and site permissions.
  2. Select "Manage and delete cookies and site data".
  3. Find this site and select Delete, or use "See all cookies and site data" to search for it.

On a phone or tablet

  1. iOS Safari: Settings, then Safari, then Advanced, then Website Data, then find this site and delete it.
  2. Android Chrome: menu, then Settings, then Site settings, then Storage, then find this site and clear its data.

Menu names move between browser versions and languages. Look for the section called site data or storage; local storage always lives there.

The consent notice

The first time you visit, a small notice appears under the page header. It says that CyberPulseAcademy stores your chosen name, country, theme and exercise scores in this browser only, and that there are no advertising or tracking cookies. It offers a button to accept it and a link to this page.

That notice is about local storage, not about cookies. It is there because the honest thing to do is tell you what is being kept on your device, even though none of it leaves your device in the default mode.

  • It is dismissible: accept it, or close it, and it stays closed for that version.
  • It is non-blocking: it does not cover the content, does not trap focus, and does not stop you reading anything.
  • Your choice is stored in cm.consent.v1, together with the version of the notice you saw and the time you saw it.
  • Declining to interact with it does not restrict any content. Every page, every topic and every catalog stays readable, because nothing is collected that would need a gate.
  • Only starting an exercise asks for anything, and what it asks for is a name you choose, a country, and a tick to confirm you have read the Privacy Policy.

You can withdraw at any time. Delete my data in the footer removes the consent record along with everything else, and the notice will appear again on your next visit, as if the site had never seen you.
