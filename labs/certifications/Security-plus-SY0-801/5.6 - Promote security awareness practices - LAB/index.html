Why the Human Layer

Read this once, then keep the glossary handy. Covers objective 5.6's core: what awareness practices are and why attackers target people.

The stack, and where it breaks

Harborline's mail gateway, patching and endpoint tools are all current. Attackers know that, so they attack the one layer that can approve their request: a person. Awareness practices turn that layer from the easiest bypass into an active sensor and a real control.

Tap a layer to see how this quarter's attack would meet it.

Where it sits in the attack chain

RECON ─▶ WEAPONIZE ─▶ [DELIVER] ─▶ EXPLOIT ─▶ INSTALL ─▶ C2 ─▶ ACT awareness works on DELIVER + the human EXECUTION step

Almost everything Lesson 5.6 defends against is Initial Access tradecraft. In MITRE ATT&CK terms the practices you will configure defend against: T1566 Phishing (and its spearphishing attachment/link/service/voice variants), T1534 Internal Spearphishing, T1621 MFA request generation (“push bombing”), T1110.004 Credential Stuffing, T1091 Replication Through Removable Media and T1200 Hardware Additions.

Documented real-world cases

APT29 (G0016) — diplomatic and think-tank targets compromised with spearphishing attachments; the technique MITRE documents under T1566.001.
APT41 (G0096) — spearphishing links, including COVID-19 lures, against organizations worldwide (T1566.002).
2020 Twitter incident — employees socially engineered by phone; attackers reached internal admin tools. Voice-channel phishing is why callback verification is drilled.
2022 Uber breach — a contractor's credentials obtained by social engineering plus repeated MFA push requests — the pattern behind MFA-fatigue guidance.

Cases are documented public incidents (MITRE ATT&CK group pages and public reporting); details are simplified for teaching.

Variants and pivots you will meet in the intake

Phishing flexes across channels and targets: mass phishing → spear phishing (researched, personal) → whaling/BEC (executive impersonation for money movement) → vishing (voice) and smishing (SMS) when mail filters improve → quishing (QR codes) → attacks on the controls themselves (MFA fatigue). The intake dock holds one of each pattern so you can tell them apart on sight.
