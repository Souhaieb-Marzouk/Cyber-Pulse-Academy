Watch Handover — Scenario Briefing

C1 · Scenario briefing · fictional exercise

Tuesday, 18:42. The night guard logs: “Two people through the IDF-2 corridor door on one badge swipe — the second carried a tool bag.” By Friday, Harborview’s booking portal is drowning in SYN traffic, dispatchers complain their browser redirects to a fake portal, and three staff laptops have “joined” a Wi-Fi network that isn’t ours.

The campaign on this watch floor is a tour of Lesson 4.7: it begins with social engineering (tailgating, then dumpster diving and shoulder surfing for context), plants a rogue device, and from there works up the stack — MAC flooding, ARP poisoning and VLAN hopping at layer 2, a rogue DHCP server, DNS poisoning at the resolvers, an evil twin in the breakroom, a phishing mail that harvests the stolen credentials, and a DDoS flood that keeps the response team busy while everything else lands.

Every console on this floor shows a slice of the same week. Work the Mission Ledger on the right: each mission names the console to inspect. Day Watch teaches as you go; Night Watch expects you to find it yourself, among the red herrings.

Why it matters for the exam: N10-009 objective 4.2 asks you to summarize each of these attacks *and its impact on the network*; objective 4.3 asks you to pick the right hardening in a scenario. This lab rehearses both, in order, on one incident.
