2 · Knowledge core

The four instruments, in five minutes

Open what you need. Everything here is fair game for the bench and the quiz.

Protocol analyzers (PacketLens)▸

A protocol analyzer captures frames passing a network point and decodes them, so you can watch real conversations: DNS queries, TCP handshakes, ARP chatter. It is passive — it observes without interrupting, like imaging a patient without surgery.

How to work a capture

1) Start the capture on a relevant point. 2) Reproduce the issue. 3) Stop and filter (for example dns, tcp, icmp). 4) Open the interesting frames and read the exchange — who asked, who answered, what came back.

What analyzers prove

Where name resolution actually goes; whether a TCP session completes its SYN / SYN-ACK / ACK handshake; retransmissions (lost packets being re-sent — a reliability smell); unexpected or unauthorized traffic. On a switch you capture your own traffic or use a mirrored (SPAN) port.

Command-line toolkit▸
CommandWhat it proves
ipconfig /all · ifconfig -aIP, mask, gateway, DHCP state, and which DNS servers the host really uses
pingReachability + round-trip time; reply TTL hints at the remote OS family (128 Windows, 64 Unix-like)
tracertThe path hop by hop — shows where packets stop

netstat -nOpen TCP/UDP sessions and local ports
route printThe routing table and default gateway in use

The classic divide

ping an IP succeeds but ping the name fails? Connectivity is fine — the problem lives in name resolution. That one pair of tests saves an hour.

Cable testers and wire maps▸

A wire-map tester checks that all eight pins land where T568A/B says. Three faults to know cold:

  • Split pair — continuity passes on every pin, but transmit/receive pairs are split across twisted pairs. Causes crosstalk and intermittent errors; only a tester with split-pair detection catches it.
  • Open — a conductor does not reach the far end. Short — two conductors touch.
  • Over length — a copper channel is limited to 100 m (about 90 m permanent link + 10 m patches). Longer runs mean attenuation and loss, no matter how good the cable is.

Hardware cousins: TDR measures distance to a fault in copper; OTDR does the same for fiber; a tone generator and probe traces one cable in a bundle; a multimeter checks voltage/continuity; a spectrum analyzer hunts non-Wi-Fi interference.

Wi-Fi analyzers▸

A Wi-Fi analyzer surveys the RF room: which SSIDs/BSSIDs are visible, on which channel and band, how strong, and with what security. It finds channel overlap, dead zones and rogue APs without ever connecting to them.

2.4 GHz: only three clean lanes

20 MHz channels sit 5 numbers apart, so only channels 1, 6 and 11 do not overlap. Two of your APs on 1 and 3 talk over each other at peak. Signal strength uses dBm (negative numbers): closer to 0 is stronger.

ReadingMeaning
−30 dBmExcellent — practically on top of the AP
−50 dBmStrong, nothing to fix
−60 dBmGood for most uses
−67 dBmMinimum for voice/video apps — below this, calls suffer
−70 dBmBrowsing only, drops likely
−80 dBmUnreliable, effectively a dead zone

An open (no-auth) SSID that nobody can match to the asset log is a rogue-AP candidate — flag it for security review.

The 7-step method (N10-009 5.1)▸
  1. Identify the problem — gather information, question users, identify symptoms, identify changes, duplicate the problem, and consider multiple potential causes (divergence!).
  2. Establish a theory of probable cause — question the obvious; if it is not obvious, try top-to-bottom, bottom-to-top or divide-and-conquer.
  3. Test the theory to determine the actual cause.
  4. Establish a plan of action to resolve the problem.
  5. Implement the solution — or escalate if it is not yours to fix.
  6. Verify full system functionality and implement preventive measures.
  7. Document findings, actions, outcomes and lessons learned.

Divergence is the discipline in step 1–2: keep several live hypotheses until evidence kills them. That is exactly what this bench rewards.

Preventive measures (step 6 of 7)

  • Ban ad-hoc static DNS on workstations; use DHCP reservations when a host must be pinned.
  • Document the channel plan and re-survey after every AP change.
  • Certify new runs with a wire-map tester before they carry voice.
  • Sweep for rogue SSIDs quarterly — open + unknown BSSID goes to security.
