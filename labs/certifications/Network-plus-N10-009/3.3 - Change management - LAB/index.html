Scenario Briefing — Shift Handover

Meridian Freight & Logistics moves freight through an HQ campus in Columbus and three regional depots (North, East, West) joined by site-to-site VPN. Last quarter, an auditor’s question — “who approved this change?” — took the team two days to answer. Management has mandated the change-management process from CompTIA Network+ objective 3.1: every network change is requested, tracked, approved, scheduled, validated and documented — and anything that bypasses that process is treated as a fault, not a favor.

You are the duty engineer. The dispatch queue holds seven change requests — a firmware security patch, a failed switch swap, an open-source upgrade, an incomplete re-addressing plan, a redundancy build-out, an emergency ransomware block, and one request that should make you suspicious — plus one routine service request and a monitoring alert that does not match any request at all. Work the gates in order; the evidence locker has everything you need.

Chapter focus: request process tracking — the lifecycle of a change request from intake to close, the audit trail it leaves, and how its absence creates configuration drift. Syllabus reference: N10-009 objective 3.1 “Explain the purpose of organizational processes and procedures” (Domain 3.0 Network Operations, 19% of the exam).
